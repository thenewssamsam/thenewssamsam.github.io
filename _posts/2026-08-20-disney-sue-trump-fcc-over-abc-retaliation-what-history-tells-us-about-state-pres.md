<h2 id="what-happened">What Happened: Disney Takes the FCC to Court</h2>
<p>On Tuesday, Disney filed a lawsuit in the DC District Court alleging that the Trump administration and FCC Chairman Brendan Carr are waging a retaliatory campaign against ABC. The catalyst for the legal showdown involves increased regulatory scrutiny over ABC stations, early license renewal demands, inquiries into daytime talk shows like "The View," and probes regarding diversity, equity, and inclusion (DEI) policies.</p>

<p>According to the complaint, the pressure has already altered daily broadcasting operations. ABC reports that "The View" has grown circumspect about booking political candidates, avoiding guest appearances since February, and deliberately omitting video clips that could trigger regulatory retaliation. While conservative supporters of the administration argue that broadcasters should not act as partisan arms, Disney's leadership has drawn a hard line, with CEO Josh D'Amaro stating that the company will not be told how to run its editorial business.</p>

<h2 id="the-chilling-effect">The Mechanics of State Pressure Across Eras</h2>
<p>History demonstrates that direct censorship is rarely necessary when subtle economic and regulatory pressures can achieve the same result. In past eras, government officials realized that threatening broadcast licenses, initiating sudden tax audits, or questioning corporate compliance under broad statutory headings could prompt self-censorship among editors and producers.</p>
