    <p style="font-size: 1.125rem; color: #4b5563; margin: 0;">

  <section id="introduction" style="margin-bottom: 32px;">
    <p>
      When federal data reveals dramatic shifts in enforcement policy, the numbers often overshadow the human realities happening on the ground. Recently released figures show that U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) arrested a record high of more than 800 people without criminal records per day on average in July. As the administration pushes toward ambitious daily targets, the debate over how immigration laws are enforced has intensified across towns, cities, and state capitals.
    </p>
    <p>
      This development is not merely a logistical or political headline; it strikes at the core of how society balances the rule of law with fundamental questions of fairness and compassion. To understand this moment fully, we must examine what the data actually shows, how communities are responding, and what timeless principles of justice suggest about our treatment of one another.
    </p>
  </section>
