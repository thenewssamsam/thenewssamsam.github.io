  <section id="what-happened" class="ncg-section">
    <h2>What Happened: The Six-Month Pivot</h2>
    <p>When military operations launched in late February, initial estimates anticipated a four-to-five-week engagement. Half a year later, the conflict has settled into a grinding test of endurance. A promising June memorandum of understanding designed to pave the way toward a resolution has stalled out, and the U.S. administration has found itself pivoting away from heavy initial airstrikes toward long-term economic containment.</p>
    
    <p>Crucially, the objectives themselves have mutated. While the Pentagon has achieved significant tactical success in degrading Iran's conventional military infrastructure—including its air force, navy, and missile production base—new and unintended vulnerabilities have taken center stage. Chief among them is the status of the Strait of Hormuz, a narrow maritime corridor through which roughly twenty percent of the world's global oil supply traditionally flows.</p>
  </section>
