While state leaders argue that urgent economic momentum is slipping through California's fingers—pointing to a lost $3 billion defense contract that went to Texas—local officials are pushing back against what they view as a rushed legislative maneuver tied to the controversial California Forever utopian city project. This high-stakes standoff exposes the delicate balancing act between statewide economic competitiveness and grassroots local control.</p>

<section class="ncg-section" id="what-happened">
<h2 id="what-happened-heading">What Happened: The Collinsville Standoff</h2>
<p>The political drama unfolded late Tuesday night when the Solano County Board of Supervisors debated draft legislation designed to fast-track maritime and shipbuilding initiatives in Collinsville. Rather than giving the framework immediate backing, the board voted 3-2 to oppose the Collinsville-focused legislation for the current legislative cycle, pushing further talks to later in the year.</p>
<p>The decision drew an immediate rebuke from Governor Newsom on Wednesday morning during a press event at Mare Island in Vallejo, where he announced $48.5 million in statewide job-creation funding, including $8.2 million designated for Mare Island and Cal Poly Maritime. Responding to the supervisors' hesitation, Newsom did not mince words: "Continue to do what you've done, you'll get what you got. It's easy to punt something, but there's going to be some reckoning."</p>
