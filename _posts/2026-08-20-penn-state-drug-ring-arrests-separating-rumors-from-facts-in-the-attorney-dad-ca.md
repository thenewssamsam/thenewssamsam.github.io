    <h2 id="what-happened">What Happened: The Penn State Drug Ring Charges</h2>
    <p>The Pennsylvania Office of Attorney General recently announced sweeping charges against fourteen individuals tied to an alleged cocaine trafficking operation operating out of two major campus organizations: the Delta Upsilon and Sigma Chi fraternities at Penn State University. According to investigators, the illicit operation involved cutting, packaging, and distributing cocaine primarily to other students on campus, with pledges allegedly tasked with handling the preparation work.</p>
    
    <p>While the vast majority of those charged are students or young adults connected to campus life, one high-profile name stood out in the police affidavits. Paul Robinson, a managing attorney at the Pittsburgh law firm Meyer, Darragh, Buckler, Bebenek, and Eck, P.L.L.C., became the only nonstudent arrested in the sweep. Investigators charged Robinson with felony tampering with evidence and hindering apprehension, accusing him of trying to conceal a safe containing drugs and cash belonging to one of the alleged ringleaders—who happens to be his son, Thomas Robinson.</p>
