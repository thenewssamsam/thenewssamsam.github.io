    <h2>What Happened at the Strait of Hormuz?</h2>
    <p>The diplomatic window for a ceasefire between the United States and Iran has officially closed without a deal. Following the expiration of a 60-day memorandum of understanding, the primary points of contention—Iran’s nuclear program and the U.S. naval blockade of the Strait of Hormuz—remain unresolved. For residents in local communities across the U.S., this isn't just a distant geopolitical headline; it is a development that touches the cost of living, energy security, and regional economic stability.</p>
  </section>

  <section id="local-impact">
    <h2>Why This Matters to Your Local Economy</h2>
    <p>The Strait of Hormuz is not just a waterway; it is arguably the most critical "chokepoint" for the global energy supply, with roughly one-fifth of the world’s oil and liquefied natural gas passing through its narrow passage. When traffic slows or stops due to conflict, the immediate ripple effect is felt at local gas stations and in the heating bills of everyday families.</p>
