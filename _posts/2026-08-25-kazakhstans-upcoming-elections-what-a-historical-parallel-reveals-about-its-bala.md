  <p class="ncg-lead">As Kazakhstan prepares for parliamentary elections under a newly transformed single-chamber system, the outcome of the vote itself feels almost anticlimactic. Yet, beneath the political consolidation of power lies a high-stakes geopolitical balancing act between Russia, China, and a pragmatic European Union.</p>

  <h2 id="what-happened">What Happened in Kazakhstan?</h2>
  <p>On August 23, Kazakhstan will hold parliamentary elections following the adoption of a sweeping new constitution. The legislative body is shifting from a traditional two-chamber framework to a single-chamber assembly known as the Kurultai. Voters will elect 145 members exclusively through party lists, while independent candidates have been barred from participating under the revised electoral code.</p>
  
  <p>Political analysts widely agree that President Kassym-Jomart Tokayev’s ruling party, Adilet (meaning "Justice"), is virtually guaranteed victory. According to experts interviewed by international observers, the vote is primarily designed to further consolidate presidential authority while systematically sidelining the remnants of the political bloc led by former President Nursultan Nazarbayev, who governed from 1991 until 2019.</p>

While Astana has managed to remain officially neutral—neither endorsing nor actively opposing Western sanctions against Moscow—it faces immense gravitational pull from both Russia and China.</p>
