<section id="what-happened">
    <h2>What Happened</h2>
    <p>Ukraine is officially shifting from political agreements to the hard, practical work of building a robust ballistic missile defense network. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy recently outlined two critical tracks currently underway: the collaborative pan-European Freyja shield project and an ambitious domestic manufacturing initiative to produce Ukrainian analogues for Patriot system interceptor missiles in partnership with American firm Raytheon and Ukrainian manufacturer Fire Point.</p>
    
    <p>This industrial pivot comes as intelligence estimates indicate Russia aims to ramp up ballistic missile production to roughly 1,300 units per year, with current stockpiles sitting around 600 to 630 missiles. To counter this looming winter threat, Ukrainian leadership is actively pursuing diplomatic channels to secure a portion of U.S. Patriot stockpiles—roughly 300 to 360 missiles—while simultaneously expanding production infrastructure abroad, including a major solid rocket fuel plant planned in Denmark by late 2027. It is a race against time, engineering, and manufacturing scale.</p>
</div>
