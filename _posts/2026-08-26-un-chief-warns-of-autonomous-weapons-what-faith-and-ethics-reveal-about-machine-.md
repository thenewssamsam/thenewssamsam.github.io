  <section id="what-happened" class="ncg-section">
    <h2>What Happened: The UN's Urgent Warning</h2>
    <p>In a joint appeal that cuts straight to the heart of modern technological advancement, United Nations Secretary-General António Guterres and International Committee of the Red Cross (ICRC) chief Mirjana Spoljaric issued a stark warning this week: countries must urgently ban or severely restrict the use of lethal autonomous weapons. Speaking out against the rapid militarization of artificial intelligence, Guterres cautioned that the world is inching dangerously close to crossing a profound moral red line—allowing machines to independently target and extinguish human life.</p>
    
    <p>The urgency of the UN's appeal was amplified by recent reports emerging from modern conflict zones. Press releases and investigative reporting, including a recent account by the New York Times, highlight unconfirmed yet deeply alarming instances of AI-guided drones operating on battlefields, such as an incident in Ukraine. These systems represent a fundamental shift in military technology, removing direct human agency from the calculus of combat and replacing it with automated algorithmic calculations.</p>

    <h2>Crossing a Moral Red Line: The Rise of AI-Guided Combat</h2>
    <p>Throughout history, warfare has been defined by human agency, human error, and crucially, human conscience. Even in the darkest moments of armed conflict, combatants possess the capacity for mercy, hesitation, and moral discernment. When a soldier looks across a battlefield, they see a fellow image-bearer of God, however distorted that reality becomes in the fog of war. Automated weapon systems, by design, strip away this moral friction.</p>

When accountability is outsourced to software, war becomes sterilized for the aggressor while becoming infinitely more arbitrary and terrifying for those caught in the crossfire.</p>
