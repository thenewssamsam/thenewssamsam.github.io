<h2>What Happened to the Swift Rescue Mission?</h2>
<p>Space exploration is rarely a straightforward march of unbroken triumphs. Sometimes, even the most innovative and carefully planned rescue operations run straight into the unforgiving realities of orbital mechanics. NASA and Katalyst Space announced that the Link spacecraft will no longer capture or boost the Swift telescope to a higher altitude due to an ongoing attitude control issue.</p>

<p>Instead of the planned orbital rescue, the Link spacecraft will pivot to conducting rendezvous and proximity operations to collect valuable data for future space missions. Launched in 2004 to study gamma-ray bursts, the Neil Gehrels Swift Observatory far outlived its original two-year planned lifespan. However, a recent, intense surge in solar activity heated up Earth’s upper atmosphere, causing it to expand and drag the satellite down into a dangerously low orbit.</p>
