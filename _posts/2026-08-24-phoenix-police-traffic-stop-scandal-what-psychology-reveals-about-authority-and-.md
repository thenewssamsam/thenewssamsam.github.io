    <h2 id="psychology-authority">The Psychology of Authority and Group Dynamics</h2>

    <p>To understand how a routine traffic stop transforms into a criminal act, we have to look past the individual actors and examine the psychological underpinnings of institutional authority. Human beings possess a deeply ingrained tendency to defer to authority, a phenomenon famously demonstrated in classic psychological experiments like the Milgram obedience study. When individuals are granted a uniform, a badge, and systemic immunity or insulation, a psychological shift often occurs.</p>

When groupthink takes root within a specific precinct or squad, aberrant and abusive behaviors can become normalized. A culture of silence allows small transgressions to snowball into catastrophic abuses of power.</p>
