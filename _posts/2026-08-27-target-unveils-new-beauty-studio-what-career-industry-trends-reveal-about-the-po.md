<section id="what-happened">
<h2>What Happened: Inside Target’s Independent Beauty Play</h2>
<p>The high-profile shop-in-shop collaboration between Target and Ulta Beauty, which debuted to immense fanfare in 2021, has officially come to an end. While the partnership expanded to 610 store locations, intense competition from rival partnerships—such as Sephora’s footprint inside Kohl’s and Amazon's aggressive moves into prestige beauty—created mounting pressures. Rather than renewing or seeking another massive co-branding venture, Target leadership chose a different path: going it alone.</p>

<p>According to Amanda Nusz, Target’s senior vice president of merchandising for essentials and beauty, the upcoming Target Beauty Studio is anchored entirely on curation and discovery rather than sheer volume. More than two-thirds of the 1,600-item assortment will feature brands new to Target. Skincare additions feature names like Clearstem, First Aid Beauty, and Sunday Riley, while hair care brings in Briogeo, Joico, Nioxin, and Remilia Hair. The retailer is also expanding deeper into the popular K-Beauty category with brands like Eqqualberry, Amuse, Kaja, Rom&nd, and Purito Seoul.</p>

<h2>The Industry Perspective: Navigating the Post-Partnership Landscape</h2>
<p>From a career and industry trend perspective, Target’s pivot highlights a crucial lesson in modern retail merchandising: the delicate balance between brand equity and operational control. When multi-category retailers partner with specialty giants, they borrow instant credibility. However, they also share margins, floor space, and strategic flexibility with external corporate entities.</p>
