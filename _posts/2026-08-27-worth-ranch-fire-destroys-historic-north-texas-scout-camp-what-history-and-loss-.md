<h2 id="what-happened">What Happened at Worth Ranch?</h2>
<p>For nearly a century, the rugged Palo Pinto hills of North Texas served as a sacred landscape for generations of Scouts. Known simply as Worth Ranch, this sprawling camp on the Brazos River was more than just a collection of cabins and mess halls; it was a crucible of character. Young people learned to paddle turbulent waters, climb Kyle Mountain, and forge lifelong friendships.</p>

<h2 id="the-weight-of-history">The Weight of History in the Palo Pinto Hills</h2>
<p>To understand the grief surrounding the Ross Fire, one must grasp how deeply rooted Worth Ranch is in regional heritage. Longtime scouter Kent Couch, dedicated to preserving the camp's archives, notes that original stone structures were constructed in 1930 using native stone hauled in by wagon. The camp's legacy stretches even further into wartime history; its swimming pool famously served as a training ground for military swim instruction during World War II.</p>
