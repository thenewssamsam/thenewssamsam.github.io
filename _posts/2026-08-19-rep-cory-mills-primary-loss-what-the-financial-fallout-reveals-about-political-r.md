<section id="introduction">
<p>Republican Representative Cory Mills has lost his Florida primary, a result that highlights the fragile intersection of personal scandal, donor confidence, and political viability. As voters in Florida’s 7th District opted for challenger Ryan Elijah, the defeat serves as a case study in how mounting investigations can shift the financial landscape of a congressional campaign.</p>
</section>

<section id="what-happened">
<h2>The Shift in Florida’s 7th District</h2>
<p>With over 80% of the vote counted, news outlets project that Ryan Elijah defeated the incumbent, Rep. Cory Mills, securing 46% of the vote compared to Mills’ 34%. Despite receiving a high-profile endorsement from President Donald Trump, Mills faced significant headwinds stemming from multiple ongoing investigations, including a House Ethics Committee probe into campaign finance and sexual misconduct allegations, as well as a Justice Department inquiry. Mills has consistently denied any wrongdoing.</p>
</section>

<section id="financial-impact">
<h2>The Financial Mechanics of a Campaign in Crisis</h2>
<p>From a financial perspective, Mills' campaign struggled to maintain the momentum required for a competitive re-election bid. While he raised nearly $900,000 by late July, that figure significantly lagged behind the fundraising averages of most House incumbents. This "under-funding" creates a compounding effect: when a candidate is embroiled in controversy, institutional donors and PACs often step back to mitigate risk, leaving the campaign to rely on a dwindling base.</p>
