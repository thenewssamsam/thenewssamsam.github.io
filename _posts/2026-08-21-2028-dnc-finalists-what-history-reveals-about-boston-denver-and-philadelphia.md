  <section id="what-happened">
    <h2>What Happened</h2>
    <p>The Democratic National Committee announced that Boston, Massachusetts; Denver, Colorado; and Philadelphia, Pennsylvania are the final three contenders vying to host the 2028 Democratic National Convention, which is scheduled to take place from August 7 to August 10, 2028. The selection process, first reported by The Associated Press, comes after a months-long review of requests for proposals initiated in September 2025 and subsequent site visits in April and May.</p>
    
    <p>Notably absent from the shortlist are Atlanta and Chicago—the latter of which hosted the 2024 DNC. The exclusion of Atlanta sparked immediate pushback from municipal leaders, with Atlanta Mayor Andre Dickens expressing sharp criticism in a letter obtained by CBS News Atlanta, arguing that the decision overlooks the critical role of Georgia in contemporary national politics.</p>

That convention aimed to project traditional New England stability combined with forward-looking charisma around nominee John Kerry. Historically, bringing a convention to Boston signals an appeal to the intellectual, historical roots of the party, though it also invites questions about regional insularity and high local operational costs.</p>

However, political history also reminds us that convention cities do not guarantee state-level electoral victories; Pennsylvania remains one of the most fiercely contested political battlegrounds in modern history.</p>
