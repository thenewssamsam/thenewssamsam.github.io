    <h2 id="what-happened">What Happened in the San Francisco Muni Train Crash?</h2>
    <p>On the morning of July 31, near the intersection of 47th Avenue and Wawona Street just north of the San Francisco Zoo, two Muni trains collided. Newly obtained video, released by the group NorCal Bodycam and shared with ABC7 Bay Area, captures the startling moments before impact. The operator appears asleep as the train plows into the rear of another stationary train, shattering window glass.</p>
    
    <p>Fortunately, both trains were empty of passengers, and the San Francisco Municipal Transportation Agency (SFMTA) confirmed that no injuries were reported. However, the luck that prevented human casualties does nothing to calm rising anxieties about transit safety. In an official statement, the SFMTA called the incident "simply unacceptable." Officials added that while formal investigations remain ongoing, equipment and infrastructure failures have been entirely ruled out, leaving operator fatigue as the primary culprit.</p>

When a safety-critical worker falls asleep at the controls, we are rarely looking at simple laziness. Instead, we are witnessing the predictable collision between biological limits and high-demand operational environments.</p>

Chronic sleep deprivation compounds this, impairing cognitive function long before an individual realizes they are about to drift off.</p>

Following that event, the SFMTA launched a fatigue-awareness education campaign. Yet, the recurrence of a nearly identical failure mode suggests that educational brochures alone cannot overcome systemic scheduling pressures, inadequate rest periods, or the lack of automated train protection.</p>
