  <section id="what-happened" class="ncg-section">
    <h2>What Happened: Virginia Joins Apple Wallet</h2>
    <p>Starting today, Virginia residents can store a secure, digital version of their driver's license or state ID directly inside the Apple Wallet app on their iPhone or Apple Watch. Announced by MacRumors, this update brings Virginia into a growing cohort of states—including California, Arizona, Ohio, and Georgia—that officially support digital identity verification.</p>
    
    <p>The rollout comes with specific capabilities and limitations. According to the report, travelers can use their digital IDs at Transportation Security Administration (TSA) checkpoints across more than 250 U.S. airports. Select businesses and apps also accept the digital credentials. However, the adoption rate among everyday merchants remains relatively slow, and law enforcement agencies do not currently accept Apple Wallet IDs during routine traffic stops. Consequently, carrying a physical plastic card remains a legal necessity.</p>

    <h3>1. The Friction of Change and Normalcy Bias</h3>
    <p>Most of us exhibit a heavy dose of normalcy bias: the tendency to assume that because things have always worked a certain way (pulling out a physical card from a wallet), they always will. When a new technology arrives, our first instinct is often skepticism. We worry about security breaches, dead batteries, or technological glitches. Yet, once a frictionless habit takes hold—like tapping a phone instead of digging for a debit card—our behavioral baseline shifts rapidly.</p>

    <h3>2. The Illusion and Reality of Control</h3>
    <p>Security features like biometric authentication (Face ID) tap into our deep psychological need for control. When handing a physical license to a TSA agent or a bouncer, you expose your home address, exact birth date, height, and eye color—far more data than the person actually needs to verify your age. Apple Wallet utilizes a system where only the requested data point (such as "is this person over 21?") is shared. Understanding this mechanism helps reframe the anxiety: technology isn't just taking away our privacy here; in some ways, it gives us granular control over what we disclose.</p>
