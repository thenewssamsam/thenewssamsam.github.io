    <h1>Trump's Saudi Nuclear Deal Sent to Congress: What History and Strategy Reveal About the Middle East</h1>
  </header>

  <p class="ncg-lead">When the Trump administration formally transmitted its civil nuclear cooperation agreement with Saudi Arabia to Capitol Hill, it ignited an immediate debate over nuclear nonproliferation, Middle Eastern diplomacy, and executive authority. But beneath the immediate political headlines lies a deeper pattern of high-stakes geopolitical bargaining that echoes decades of American foreign policy in the region.</p>

  <h2 id="what-happened">What Happened: The 123 Agreement Moves to Capitol Hill</h2>
  <p>The Trump administration has officially sent its signed civil nuclear agreement&mdash;commonly known as a 123 agreement&mdash;to Congress for its mandated 90-day review period. According to the Atomic Energy Act, the deal will automatically go into effect in three months unless both houses of Congress pass resolutions of disapproval.</p>
  
  <p>The agreement includes provisions that could eventually pave the way for uranium enrichment on Saudi soil under a joint US-Saudi study mechanism. Energy Secretary Chris Wright confirmed that the deal provides Riyadh with a pathway to domestic enrichment, though he stressed that such steps are many years away and subject to stringent bilateral safeguards developed in coordination with the International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA).</p>

  <p>However, the path forward is clouded by unusual complications. A source familiar with the matter told CNN that the transmitted text is classified, which experts describe as an unprecedented level of secrecy for a civil nuclear accord. Furthermore, the White House has maintained that the agreement is tied to a strict geopolitical condition: Riyadh must join the Abraham Accords and normalize diplomatic relations with Israel for the deal to ultimately move forward, creating a complex diplomatic chess match between nuclear energy, regional security, and executive oversight.</p>

Traditionally, civil nuclear cooperation pacts include only a small annex with a detailed State Department nonproliferation analysis classified from public view. Classifying the entire text of a 123 agreement, as reported in this instance, has stunned nonproliferation experts.</p>
