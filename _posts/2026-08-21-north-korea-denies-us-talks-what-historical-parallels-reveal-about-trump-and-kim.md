<h2>What Happened: The Diplomatic Disconnect</h2>
<p>The latest diplomatic friction began when President Donald Trump told reporters he had been in touch with North Korean leader Kim Jong Un, claiming the regime had responded "very positive[ly]" to his overtures for renewed talks. Trump framed these efforts around his frustration with South Korea's reluctance to assist with US military operations in Iran, pointing out that Washington maintains roughly 28,500 troops on the peninsula and is demanding increased financial contributions from Seoul.</p>

<h2>What History Teaches About Posturing vs. Reality</h2>
<p>There is a profound difference between tactical engagement and strategic disarmament. During the Singapore and Hanoi summits in 2018 and 2019, dramatic photo-ops at the Demilitarized Zone created immense media spectacles. Yet, as analysts have repeatedly pointed out, those meetings did not halt North Korea’s nuclear program; instead, they granted Pyongyang international legitimacy while it continued developing its military arsenal.</p>
