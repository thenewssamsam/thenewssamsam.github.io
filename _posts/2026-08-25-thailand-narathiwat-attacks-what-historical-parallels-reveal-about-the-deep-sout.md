    <p>When fifty-one coordinated arson and incendiary attacks ripped through Thailand’s restive south late Saturday night, leaving local government buildings, a convenience store, and vehicles ablaze, it felt to many like an abrupt escalation. But for those who study the long, persistent rhythms of regional conflict, the explosions and fires across Narathiwat, Yala, and Pattani were part of an entrenched historical echo.     <h2 id="what-happened">What Happened in the Narathiwat Attacks?</h2>
    <p>According to the military's Internal Security Operations Command (ISOC) and local media reports, the wave of 51 synchronized incidents unfolded between 8:00 PM and midnight on Saturday, August 22. The attacks targeted multiple locations across Thailand’s southernmost border provinces, with the highest concentration occurring in Narathiwat province. Two civilians sustained injuries, and authorities promptly instituted an overnight curfew lasting until Sunday morning.</p>
    
    <p>Among the properties targeted were a local government office building, a 7-Eleven convenience store, and various telecommunications towers and electrical poles. Insurgents also made off with a pickup truck from a subdistrict administrative organization office in Narathiwat, later abandoning the burned-out vehicle on a roadway. Narathiwat Governor Boonchuay Homyamyen confirmed that authorities spent Sunday inspecting the damage sites. The timing of the violence coincides with preparations for Thai Prime Minister Anutin Charnvirakul and his cabinet members to visit nearby Songkhla province to discuss regional investment projects and security enhancements.</p>
