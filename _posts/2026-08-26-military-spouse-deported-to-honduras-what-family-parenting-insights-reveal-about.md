<section id="what-happened">
<h2>What Happened at Fort Bliss</h2>
<p>The sequence of events unfolded rapidly in July 2026. Villafranca-Trejo, who entered the United States in 2016 at the age of 18 and carries no known criminal record, was detained on July 11. According to the Department of Homeland Security (DHS), she had received a final order of removal back in 2017 after missing an immigration hearing—a notice her family maintains she never received. The reality only came to light years later when the couple married in 2022 and began the process of applying for her green card.</p>

<p>Turcios Juarez, who immigrated from Honduras, enlisted in the Army and earned his U.S. citizenship in 2024. While he was actively serving his adopted country, his wife was waiting for a decision on her application for military parole-in-place—a discretionary program traditionally utilized to protect immediate family members of service members from deportation while they seek legal status. Despite those pending appeals and congressional inquiries led by figures like Rep. Veronica Escobar, DHS executed the removal order, sending Villafranca-Trejo back to Honduras.</p>
