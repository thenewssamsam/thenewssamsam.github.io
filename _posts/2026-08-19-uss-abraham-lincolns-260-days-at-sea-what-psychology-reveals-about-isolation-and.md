  <section id="what-happened">
    <h2>What Happened to the USS Abraham Lincoln?</h2>
    <p>The USS Abraham Lincoln (CVN-72) has been away from homeport since December, serving as a floating base for aerial combat operations in the Arabian Sea during Operation Epic Fury. However, supply lines were severely disrupted on February 28 when an Iranian attack heavily damaged Naval Support Activity Bahrain, a critical logistics hub in the Persian Gulf located about 870 miles from the central Gulf of Oman.</p>

    <h2>A Psychology Perspective: The Toll of Extended Isolation</h2>
    <p>Looking at a 260-day deployment through the lens of psychology reveals a profound friction between modern military technology and ancient human biology. Human beings are hardwired for rhythm, change of scenery, and social variety. When those boundaries vanish, the psychological toll begins to compound.</p>

    <p>Psychologists studying extreme confinement often point to concepts like <strong>normalcy bias</strong> and <strong>sensory monotony</strong>. Without a port call to reset the psychological clock, the human brain struggles to register the passage of time in a healthy way. Days blur together under the constant hum of aircraft, artificial lighting, and confined quarters. Even when basic physical needs like water and calories are met, the absence of psychological decompression can lead to cognitive fatigue, irritability, and anxiety.</p>
