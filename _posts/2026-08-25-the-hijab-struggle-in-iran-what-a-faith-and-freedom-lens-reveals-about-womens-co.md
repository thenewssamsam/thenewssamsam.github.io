  <section id="what-happened" class="ncg-section">
    <h2>What Happened: The Unfolding Reality in Iran</h2>
    <p>Recent reports from major news outlets highlight a persistent, escalating social divide across Iran regarding the mandatory hijab. Against a backdrop of severe economic strain, military friction with the United States, and internal restructuring under the new leadership of Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei, public defiance by women has not receded. Instead, women in Tehran and at least six other major cities continue to walk public spaces, ride bicycles, and navigate daily life without headscarves.</p>

    <p>The Iranian government finds itself walking a precarious tightrope. While conservative clerics and the Tehran prosecutor's office have clamped down on commercial establishments—closing cafes and restaurants for failing to enforce dress codes, and handing down harsh judicial sentences to cultural figures like singer Parastou Ahmadi—the broader state response has been remarkably calculated. Rather than launching a uniform, full-scale street crackdown, authorities are currently prioritizing national security, economic survival, and regime consolidation. For many ordinary citizens, the daily reality on the street tells a story of quiet, stubborn resilience.</p>
