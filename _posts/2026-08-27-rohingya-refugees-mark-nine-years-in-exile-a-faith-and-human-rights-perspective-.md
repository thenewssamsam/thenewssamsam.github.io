<h2 id="what-happened">What Happened: Nine Years in the Camps</h2>
<p>Tens of thousands of Rohingya refugees gathered across sprawling camps in Cox’s Bazar, Bangladesh, on Tuesday to mark a solemn milestone: the ninth anniversary of their mass exodus from Myanmar. Demonstrators chanted slogans, held peaceful rallies, and called on the international community to secure a safe, dignified return to their homeland in Rakhine state. For the roughly 1.2 million refugees currently residing in these congested camps, the anniversary serves as a painful reminder of a decade spent waiting for justice, safety, and a place to truly call home.</p>

<p>The crisis erupted in August 2017 following a brutal military crackdown in Myanmar’s Rakhine state in response to attacks by insurgent groups. The resulting violence drove over 700,000 Rohingya—a predominantly Muslim ethnic minority—across the border into Bangladesh on foot and by boat. They joined hundreds of thousands who had fled earlier waves of persecution. Today, despite repeated diplomatic efforts and international inquiries, repatriation remains stalled as escalating civil conflict and ongoing violence in Rakhine make safe returns nearly impossible.</p>

<h2 id="the-human-cost">The Human Cost and Diplomatic Realities</h2>
<p>As the refugees marked what they termed &ldquo;Rohingya Genocide Day,&rdquo; diplomatic missions from 15 countries and the European Union issued a joint statement praising Bangladesh for hosting the displaced population for nearly a decade. However, the international representatives stressed that conditions in Rakhine state—marked by ongoing fighting between the Myanmar military and the insurgent Arakan Army, airstrikes on civilian infrastructure, and severe aid restrictions—remain far too volatile for voluntary or safe repatriation.</p>
