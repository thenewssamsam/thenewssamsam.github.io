<h1 style="font-size: 32px; color: #1a202c; line-height: 1.2; margin-top: 0; margin-bottom: 15px;">Forget 8 Hours: What New Sleep Research and Ancient Wisdom Reveal About Rest</h1>
<p style="font-size: 18px; color: #4a5568; margin: 0;">For generations, the eight-hour sleep rule has been treated as an absolute commandment for modern health. But fresh scientific reviews and a look at our ancestral habits suggest that the golden rule of shut-eye might actually be more flexible than we think.</p>
</header>

<section style="margin-bottom: 40px;">
<p>If you have ever felt guilty for waking up naturally after seven hours of sleep—or conversely, felt completely exhausted despite logging a full eight—you are not alone. Modern life has standardized our nighttime routines around alarms, digital devices, and a rigid 40-hour workweek. Yet, our bodies may have been designed for something entirely different.</p>

<p>A recent review published in <em>Brain Medicine</em> challenges the universal assumption that every adult needs exactly eight hours of nocturnal rest. By looking at how humans slept long before the invention of electric lighting and smartphones, researchers are painting a much more nuanced picture of human rest. As we navigate the pressures of modern exhaustion, exploring what science and wisdom say about rest can help us find genuine peace and physical renewal.</p>
</section>

<p>The recent review in <em>Brain Medicine</em> suggests that human sleep patterns in pre-industrial societies were remarkably flexible. Researchers studying hunter-gatherer communities in places like Tanzania, Namibia, and Bolivia—populations with little to no artificial light exposure—discovered that adults typically slept between 5.7 and 7.1 hours per night.</p>
