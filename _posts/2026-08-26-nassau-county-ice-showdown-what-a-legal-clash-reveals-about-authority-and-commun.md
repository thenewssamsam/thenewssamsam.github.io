<section id="what-happened">
<h2>What Happened: The New York ICE Ban Showdown</h2>
<p>The legislative battle lines in New York hardened significantly when a state deadline forced local police departments and sheriff's offices to wind down any formal civil immigration enforcement agreements with federal authorities. Under the terms of the Local Cops, Local Crimes Act, state and local employees are legally prohibited from questioning individuals solely for immigration purposes—unless explicitly mandated by a federal judicial warrant. Furthermore, the statute restricts government employees from allowing ICE agents into sensitive locations, such as schools, without proper judicial authorization.</p>

<p>According to reports from Albany, 53 of New York’s 62 counties never signed cooperation agreements with ICE in the first place, and others quietly wound down their programs ahead of the deadline. However, Nassau County and Rensselaer County stood firm as prominent holdouts. Nassau County Executive Bruce Blakeman, who has long championed a comprehensive local partnership with federal immigration enforcement—including deputizing officers and renting jail space to ICE—has defended the cooperative stance as vital for solving local crimes. In response to the county's refusal to comply, the state Attorney General’s Office of Immigrant Trust swiftly issued subpoenas to Nassau's sheriff and police departments.</p>
