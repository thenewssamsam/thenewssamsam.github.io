<h2>What Happened: A Brutal Primary Night</h2>
<p>Nearly 300,000 Republican voters headed to the polls in Wyoming and two congressional districts in Florida, delivering a stark message that reverberated across the political landscape. Just 28% of those voters cast their ballots for the candidates endorsed by President Donald Trump. This single Tuesday marked a brutal culmination of an election season where the political gravity surrounding the former president's endorsements appears to be shifting significantly.</p>

<p>According to comprehensive tracking, Republican primary voters have rejected 10 Trump-endorsed candidates for statewide office or Congress this primary season alone—matching the combined total of losses from 2018, 2020, and 2024. High-profile defeats include Wyoming gubernatorial candidate Megan Degenfelder and Florida U.S. Representatives like Cory Mills, joining a growing list that features Minnesota's Mike Lindell and Tennessee's Andy Ogles.</p>

<h2>The Psychology of Political Loyalty and Fatigue</h2>
<p>Why do powerful political endorsements suddenly lose their luster? From a psychological standpoint, political loyalty is heavily anchored in identity, social proof, and emotional momentum. When a leader enjoys sweeping electoral victories, endorsing a candidate triggers a powerful wave of <strong>social proof</strong>—voters eagerly align with the perceived winning team to share in collective triumph.</p>
