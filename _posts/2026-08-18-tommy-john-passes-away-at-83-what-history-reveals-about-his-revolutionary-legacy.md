<section id="historical-perspective">
  <h2>The Historical Parallel: How One Surgery Changed Sports Forever</h2>
  <p>To understand the magnitude of Tommy John's legacy, we have to look back at sports history before 1974. Back then, a torn ulnar collateral ligament (UCL) in a pitcher's throwing elbow was essentially a career death sentence. Pitchers threw until their arms gave out, and once the joint blew, they retired. There was no playbook for reconstruction.</p>
  <p>When Dr. Frank Jobe approached Tommy John with an experimental proposition after John's pitching arm essentially collapsed in 1974, the odds of success were roughly 100-to-1. John famously agreed, knowing it might end his playing days entirely. Instead, he returned to win 164 more games, pitching until he was 46 years old.</p>
  <p>History is full of accidental breakthroughs born out of desperation. Much like the rapid medical innovations spurred by wartime crises or technological leaps during industrial transitions, the invention of "Tommy John surgery" proved that human limits are often just temporary boundaries waiting to be crossed. John's willingness to step into the unknown transformed a catastrophic injury into a routine recovery, altering the economic and competitive landscape of baseball for the next half-century.</p>

    <p>Tommy John did not just pitch a baseball; he pitched open a door to the future, turning an absolute finish line into a second chance for generations of athletes.</p>

      <summary>Who was Tommy John in baseball history?</summary>
      <p>Tommy John was a legendary left-handed MLB pitcher who won 288 games over a 26-year career. He is best known for giving his name to the revolutionary elbow ligament replacement surgery first performed on him in 1974.</p>
    </details>
    <details>
      <summary>What is Tommy John surgery?</summary>
      <p>Tommy John surgery (UCL reconstruction) is a surgical procedure in which a ligament in the medial elbow is replaced with a tissue graft from elsewhere in the body or a donor, allowing pitchers to recover from career-threatening elbow injuries.</p>
    </details>
    <details>
      <summary>How did Tommy John's surgery change sports?</summary>
      <p>Before Dr. Frank Jobe performed the procedure on Tommy John in 1974, a torn elbow ligament usually ended a pitcher's career. The surgery successfully established a precedent that extended the careers of thousands of athletes across baseball and other sports.</p>
    </details>
