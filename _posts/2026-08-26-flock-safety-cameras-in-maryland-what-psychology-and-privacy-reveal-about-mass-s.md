<section id="psychology-surveillance">
    <h2 class="ncg-section-h2">The Psychology Behind Constant Monitoring</h2>
    <p class="ncg-p">
        To truly understand why the proliferation of Flock cameras causes such visceral unease, we have to look past the political debates and examine human psychology. When people know—or even strongly suspect—that their movements are being continuously recorded, cataloged, and archived by algorithms, their behavior subtly changes. Psychologists call this the Hawthorne effect: individuals alter their habits simply because they realize they are being observed.

<section id="normalization-bias">
    <h2 class="ncg-section-h2">Normalcy Bias and Creeping Expansion</h2>
    <p class="ncg-p">
        Another powerful psychological mechanism at play here is normalcy bias—our innate tendency to underestimate the likelihood of danger or the severity of gradual changes because things have "always been this way." When camera networks expand incrementally—one intersection at a time, justified by public safety appeals—communities rarely experience a single, shocking moment of transition. Instead, they sleepwalk into total coverage.
    </p>
