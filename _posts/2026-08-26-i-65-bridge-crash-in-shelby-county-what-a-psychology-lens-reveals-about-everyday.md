<section id="what-happened">
<h2>What Happened on I-65</h2>
<p>Shortly before noon on a Tuesday that started like any other, a routine commute on Interstate 65 in Shelby County turned into a chaotic scene. A flatbed truck hauling a heavy trackhoe excavator failed to clear an overhead structure, slamming directly into the County Road 87 bridge at the 233.9-mile marker. According to senior reporter Carol Robinson of AL.com, the impact heavily damaged the bridge and sent falling debris crashing down onto traffic below, resulting in one person being rushed to UAB Hospital with a head injury.</p>

<p>Emergency responders from Alabama State Troopers, Alabaster police, and local fire departments rushed to secure the scene, while the Alabama Department of Transportation began evaluating the structural integrity of the overpass. While state troopers lead the ongoing investigation, drivers are left asking an unsettling question: how does a driver misjudge height clearances so drastically on a major interstate?</p>

<h3 style="margin-top: 20px;">The Trap of Normalcy Bias</h3>
<p>Human beings are creatures of habit. When drivers travel familiar routes day after day, the brain begins to economize energy by shifting from active processing to automatic pilot. Known in psychological circles as normalcy bias, this mental shortcut assumes that because yesterday's route was clear, today's route will be identical. A driver transporting oversized equipment can fall victim to this cognitive blind spot, failing to recalculate clearances because routine has lulled their active vigilance to sleep.</p>

<p>Modern driving requires processing an immense amount of visual and auditory data. When too many demands compete for attention, the brain practices selective attention—tunneling in on immediate traffic flow while inadvertently filtering out peripheral risks, such as the vertical clearance of an upcoming overpass.</p>
