    <h1 class="ncg-title">OG Anunoby and the Knicks: What Scripture Teaches About Glory and Moving Forward</h1>
    <p class="ncg-subtitle">Fresh off breaking a 53-year championship drought, New York's quiet hero is ready for what's next. His stoic refusal to dwell on past glory points us toward a deeper spiritual truth about how we handle success and life's fleeting triumphs.</p>
  </header>

  <section class="ncg-section" id="what-happened">
    <h2 id="what-happened-heading">What Happened in New York?</h2>
    <p>For the New York Knicks, the summer of 2026 has been an exhausting, exhilarating blur. After ending a agonizing 53-year championship drought with an impossible comeback in the NBA Finals—capped off by forward OG Anunoby's legendary game-winning tip-in against the San Antonio Spurs—the franchise has been living in a whirlwind of parades, talk show appearances, and endless celebration.</p>
    <p>Yet, in a recent interview with CNN Sports, Anunoby looked less like a conqueror basking in eternal glory and more like someone ready to clock in for his next shift. Wearing a black hoodie and radiating his trademark stoicism—jokingly dubbed a "raging stoic who plays like a whirling dervish"—Anunoby made headlines by casually noting that he is already "over" his iconic moment.</p>
    <p>Clarifying his remarks, the 10-year veteran explained that he isn't ungrateful for the historic milestone. Rather, he is simply ready for the next season. In a city where sports success is treated as a birthright and the pressure to defend a title is an immense albatross, Anunoby's ability to turn the page offers a fascinating window into human nature—and, quite unexpectedly, into timeless spiritual truths.</p>
