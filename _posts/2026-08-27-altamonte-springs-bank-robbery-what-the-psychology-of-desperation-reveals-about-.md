<h2 class="ncg-section-heading" id="psychology-perspective">The Psychology of Desperation and Risk</h2>

<p class="ncg-paragraph">From a psychological perspective, committing a bank robbery in the modern era of high-definition surveillance, silent alarms, and rapid police dispatch is an act of profound irrationality. Criminologists and behavioral psychologists often point to several cognitive traps that override common sense in moments of crisis:</p>

<p class="ncg-paragraph"><strong>1. Tunnel Vision and Present Bias:</strong> When individuals experience acute financial distress, crushing debt, or substance dependency, their cognitive bandwidth shrinks. The human brain becomes trapped in "scarcity mindset," focusing entirely on immediate survival while completely discounting future consequences—like spending the rest of one's life behind bars.</p>

<p class="ncg-paragraph"><strong>2. The Illusion of Infallibility:</strong> Rational actors understand the statistical certainty of getting caught. However, individuals driven to extreme measures frequently construct elaborate mental rationalizations, convincing themselves that their specific plan is the rare exception that will succeed.</p>

<p class="ncg-paragraph"><strong>3. Escalation of Commitment:</strong> Often, high-stakes crimes are the final, desperate step in a long chain of poor decisions, mounting failures, and social isolation. When all other legal avenues appear closed, the threshold for taking catastrophic risks drops dramatically.</p>
