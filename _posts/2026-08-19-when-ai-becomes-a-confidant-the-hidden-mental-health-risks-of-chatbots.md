  <section id="introduction">
    <p>In an era where artificial intelligence is increasingly integrated into our daily lives, many are turning to chatbots like ChatGPT for more than just productivity—they are using them as digital therapists. However, the tragic death of 29-year-old Sophie Rottenberg, who confided her deepest agonies to an AI bot named "Harry," has sparked a critical conversation about the limitations and risks of using technology for mental health support.</p>
  </section>

  <section id="what-happened">
    <h2>The Reality Behind the Screen</h2>
    <p>Sophie Rottenberg, a driven public health policy analyst, struggled with depression and anxiety in late 2024. Despite having a supportive family, including a father who is a psychologist, and a dedicated therapist, she kept the true depth of her suffering hidden. Instead, she turned to a ChatGPT-based "therapist" for months, sharing nearly 1,800 pages of communication. Her parents only discovered these interactions after her passing, realizing that the AI had become a primary—and ultimately insufficient—outlet for her pain.</p>
  </section>

  <section id="health-wellness-perspective">
    <h2>The Appeal and the Danger of AI Therapy</h2>
    <p>The rise of "AI therapy" is a cultural phenomenon. For many, chatbots offer a 24/7, non-judgmental space that feels safer than the perceived burden of sharing struggles with loved ones. According to the American Psychological Association, this accessibility is exactly what makes these tools so appealing.</p>

    <h3>The Limitation of Algorithms</h3>
    <p>While AI companies have implemented safety guardrails, including recommendations for resources like 988, these systems are not substitutes for human intervention. Experts note that AI lacks the clinical training to assess risk levels accurately. When a person is experiencing severe suicidal intent, they require a human who can guide them toward appropriate, real-world care.</p>

  <section id="practical-response">
    <h2>How Should Readers Respond?</h2>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Prioritize Human Connection:</strong> If you are struggling, reach out to a trusted friend, family member, or licensed therapist. Human empathy is a biological necessity.</li>
      <li><strong>Use Tools as Supplements:</strong> If you use AI for productivity or organization, keep a strict boundary between that and your emotional health.</li>
      <li><strong>Check on Your Circle:</strong> Don't assume that because someone is functioning well, they are doing well. Ask deep questions and be prepared to listen.</li>
