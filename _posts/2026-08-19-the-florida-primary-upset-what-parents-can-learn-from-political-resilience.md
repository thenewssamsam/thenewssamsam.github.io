    <h3>1. Defining Value Beyond "Resources"</h3>
    <p>Children often see the world through a lens of "who has the most"—the most toys, the most followers, or the most money. Nixon’s victory over a candidate with a massive fundraising advantage is a tangible example that money cannot buy everything. Use this to talk to your kids about how hard work, clear values, and genuine community connections can often outweigh external advantages.</p>

Talk to your teens about why it is important to stand by what you believe, even when it isn't the "popular" or "easy" path.</p>

  <section id="practical-response">
    <h2>How Should Parents Respond?</h2>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Start the Conversation:</strong> Ask your older children, "What do you think makes someone successful in a competition?" Use current events to open up these dialogues.</li>
      <li><strong>Model Persistence:</strong> When you face a challenge at work or home, talk about it openly (in an age-appropriate way) so your kids see how you handle obstacles.</li>
      <li><strong>Value Community:</strong> Encourage your kids to participate in local activities. Show them that being part of a team or a neighborhood group is where real impact starts.</li>
    </ul>

    <h2>Closing Takeaway</h2>
