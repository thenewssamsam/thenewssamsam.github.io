Hours later, an immigration judge dismissed the case, and the 48-year-old handyman returned to his Key West home. Yet, beneath the sudden legal resolution lies a deeper human story about duty, family anxiety, and the intersecting pressures of national policy and personal sacrifice.</p>

<section id="what-happened">
<h2>What Happened: A Family Divided at Home While a Sailor Serves Overseas</h2>
<p>According to reports from The Associated Press, Luis Manuel Aviles—a Nicaraguan native who has lived in the United States for 19 years and holds a valid work permit—was arrested on Saturday as he left his house to take his car to a mechanic. His wife, Argelia Aviles, watched as the sudden detention upended their household. The arrest occurred amidst a broader wave of high-profile immigration enforcement actions targeting immediate family members of enlisted U.S. service members, prompting swift bipartisan concern and a congressional investigation.</p>

<p>The timing could hardly have been more agonizing. His son, Joshua Aviles, was entering his ninth month aboard the <em>USS Abraham Lincoln</em>. The aircraft carrier has made headlines for setting a U.S. military record for uninterrupted time at sea, a deployment that has strained supply chains and tested the mental health and endurance of the sailors on board. To learn that a loved one back home had been detained while executing grueling, demanding labor on a floating military base highlights a profound friction between administrative enforcement and the emotional realities of military families.</p>
