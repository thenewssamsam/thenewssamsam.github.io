  <section id="what-happened" class="ncg-section">
    <h2>What Happened: The Shift in Public Sentiment</h2>
    <p>Five months ago, the political establishment and technology executives met the proposal for an AI infrastructure freeze with immediate skepticism. Sen. Bernie Sanders (I-Vt.) and Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez (D-N.Y.) introduced the AI Data Center Moratorium Act in March, aiming to halt new power-hungry data center projects requiring at least 20 megawatts until Congress established meaningful regulatory safeguards.</p>
    
    <p>At the time, major tech figures—including Tesla and SpaceX CEO Elon Musk, alongside Y Combinator CEO Garry Tan—pushed back hard, arguing that such measures would handicap American technological dominance. Major political figures like President Donald Trump defended the expansion, pointing to job creation, massive capital investment, and local tax revenues, even while admitting the industry needed better public relations.</p>

    <div class="ncg-quote-box">
      <p>"Five months ago, AOC and I called for a moratorium on new AI data centers. We were called 'radical,' 'crazy' and 'extremists.' Today, 75% of Americans agree and hundreds of candidates are responding." — Sen. Bernie Sanders</p>

    <p>Why did public opinion flip so decisively in less than half a year? To understand this rapid polarization, we have to look past the political rhetoric and examine the underlying human behavior and cognitive patterns at play.</p>

    <h3 class="ncg-h3">1. Normalcy Bias Meets Tangible Disruption</h3>
    <p>For years, artificial intelligence lived largely in the digital realm—confined to chat windows, software updates, and abstract promises of future efficiency. Human beings possess a strong trait called normalcy bias, where we assume tomorrow will look much like yesterday. But when massive industrial warehouses requiring tens of megawatts of power start rising next to suburban neighborhoods—humming with industrial cooling fans, drawing immense grid capacity, and threatening local water supplies—the abstract becomes physical.</p>

    <h3 class="ncg-h3">2. The Cost of Arrogance and Loss Aversion</h3>
    <p>Loss aversion teaches us that the psychological pain of losing something tangible (quiet neighborhoods, reliable electricity, stable water tables) outweighs the abstract pleasure of future gains. Futurum Group CEO Daniel Newman recently noted on X that the rebellion isn't necessarily against artificial intelligence itself, but rather against "certain spokespeople who have come across as callous, self-serving and/or diabolical" regarding job displacement and existential risks.</p>
