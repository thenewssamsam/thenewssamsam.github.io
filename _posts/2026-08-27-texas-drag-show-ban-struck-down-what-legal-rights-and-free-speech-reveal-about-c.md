<section id="what-happened">
  <h2>What Happened: The Fall of Senate Bill 12</h2>
  <p>A federal judge in Texas has officially struck down Senate Bill 12, a state law passed in 2023 designed to penalize businesses hosting drag performances and criminalize certain public entertainers. U.S. District Judge David Hittner, a Reagan appointee, ruled that the measure improperly restricts speech and violates the First Amendment of the U.S. Constitution.</p>
  
  <p>The ruling directly prevents Texas Attorney General Ken Paxton from enforcing the statute and denies his motion for a new trial. Under the invalidated law, business owners faced fines up to $10,000, while performers could have faced Class A misdemeanors carrying up to a year in jail. Judge Hittner noted that the statute was overly broad and vague, potentially sweeping in common public occurrences such as cheerleading, dancing, and live theater.</p>

<section id="legal-perspective">
  <h2>A Legal Rights Perspective on Free Speech</h2>
  <p>From a legal standpoint, the Texas ruling highlights the high constitutional bar required to restrict expressive conduct. The First Amendment protects not only political speech and written text but also artistic performance, costuming, and theatrical presentation, provided they do not cross into legally unprotected categories such as obscenity under the established <em>Miller v. California</em> standard.</p>
