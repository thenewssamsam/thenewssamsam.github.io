<section id="what-happened">
<h2>What Happened: Navigating a $40 Trillion Reality</h2>
<p>America has officially crossed a staggering economic milestone: the national debt has hit $40 trillion. Appearing on CBS News' <em>Face the Nation with Margaret Brennan</em>, Minneapolis Federal Reserve President and CEO Neel Kashkari broke down the state of the American economy, addressing the monumental debt load, rising Treasury yields, and persistent inflation pressures that continue to challenge central bankers.</p>

<p>The interview comes at a critical juncture for U.S. financial markets. Following a notable bond sell-off and intervention by Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, economists and investors are debating whether the recent jump in borrowing costs represents routine market volatility or the dawn of a profound structural shift. Against this backdrop, Kashkari offered a measured look at the mechanics driving government borrowing, global economic growth, and the delicate path ahead for monetary policy.</p>
</section>

<section id="financial-perspective">
<h2>The Financial Perspective: Decoding Debt, Yields, and Markets</h2>
<p>When Brennan pressed Kashkari on whether a $40 trillion debt burden signals an immediate crisis, the Fed official pointed to the broader mechanics of the Treasury market. While acknowledging that 10-year Treasury yields hovering around 4.7% are high relative to recent history, he placed them in a longer historical context. In the early 2000s and the 1990s, yields were often at or meaningfully above these levels without triggering systemic market dysfunction.</p>
