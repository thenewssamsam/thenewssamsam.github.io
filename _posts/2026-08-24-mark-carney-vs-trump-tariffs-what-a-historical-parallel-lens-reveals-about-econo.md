<section id="what-happened">
<h2>What Happened: The Brink of a Trade Rupture</h2>
<p>The economic standoff between Washington and Ottawa has reached a critical boiling point. Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney has walked away from high-stakes trade negotiations after concluding that the United States was demanding unacceptable terms in exchange for tariff relief. In response, the U.S. administration slapped 50 percent duties on roughly $20 billion worth of Canadian goods. Carney, maintaining a defiant stance, announced dollar-for-dollar retaliatory measures scheduled to begin soon.</p>

<p>At the heart of the dispute is a clash over national sovereignty. Carney revealed that final-hour U.S. demands would have severely restricted Canada's ability to forge independent trade agreements with other nations. British Columbia Premier David Eby warned that accepting such a clause would reduce Canada to the economic equivalent of the fifty-first state—a status President Donald Trump has frequently mused about. With nearly three-quarters of Canadian exports heading south to an economy ten times its size, Carney is taking a massive gamble: risking severe economic pain rather than capitulating to superpower pressure.</p>
