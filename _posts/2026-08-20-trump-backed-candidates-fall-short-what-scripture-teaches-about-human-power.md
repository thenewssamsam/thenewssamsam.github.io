<h2 style="font-size: 1.5rem; font-weight: 700; color: #1a202c; border-bottom: 1px solid #e2e8f0; padding-bottom: 8px; margin-bottom: 20px;">
What Happened: A Brutal Night for Endorsements
</h2>
<p>
The numbers coming out of the recent Republican primary elections tell a striking story. Nearly 300,000 voters turned out in Wyoming and two congressional districts in Florida, yet just 28% of them cast their ballots for the candidates endorsed by former President Donald Trump. 
</p>
<p>
Among the notable casualties were Wyoming gubernatorial candidate Megan Degenfelder and Florida U.S. House incumbents and contenders like Rep. Cory Mills and Catalina Lauf, both of whom registered remarkably low shares of the vote. These losses are not isolated incidents. Republican primary voters have already rejected 10 Trump-endorsed candidates for statewide office or Congress this season alone—matching the combined losses from 2018, 2020, and 2024, and creeping toward the record of 12 set in the turbulent 2022 midterms.
</p>
<p>
As political analysts sift through the data points, it becomes increasingly clear that primary voters are exercising their independent judgment with greater frequency. Whether driven by local issues, fatigue, or a desire for fresh representation, the electorate is showing that high-profile endorsements no longer guarantee automatic victory.
</p>

Yet, the 2026 primaries highlight a growing disconnect between central party figures and grassroots voters who are registering discontent on key issues.
