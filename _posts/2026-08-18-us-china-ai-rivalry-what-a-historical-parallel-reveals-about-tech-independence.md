<section id="what-happened">
    <h2>What Happened: The Push for Standards</h2>
    <p>As restrictions tighten and global competition accelerates, the US-China AI rivalry has entered a critical new phase. According to recent commentary from Zheng Yongnian, dean of the school of public policy at the Chinese University of Hong Kong (Shenzhen), external pressure is forcing a fundamental reassessment of how technological autonomy is achieved.</p>

    <p>Writing in his preface to <em>The Rise of Atlas</em> &mdash; a new book chronicling the development of Huawei Technologies' Atlas 950 SuperPoD computing system &mdash; Zheng argued that hardware accomplishments are merely symptoms of a larger institutional ambition. The true challenge for any nation navigating severe external constraints is not simply possessing advanced components, but building the industrial and institutional capacity to define international standards, shape ecosystems, and seamlessly coordinate hardware, software, and applications.</p>
    <p>&ldquo;True technological autonomy is not simply about 'having chips', but about 'having the ability to define the technological path',&rdquo; Zheng wrote.</p>

<section id="historical-perspective">
    <h2>A Historical Perspective: Beyond Hardware</h2>
    <p>History repeatedly shows that economic blockades, technological embargoes, and export controls rarely succeed in stopping a determined industrial power over the long run. Instead, they act as a severe catalyst for self-reliance. But manufacturing a substitute component is only half the battle; the historical graveyard of technology is filled with superior hardware platforms that failed simply because they could not establish a compatible ecosystem or a recognized global standard.</p>
    
    <div class="ncg-historical-box">
        <h3>The Power of Standards Over Equipment</h3>
        <p>Consider past industrial shifts, such as the telecommunications and railway booms of the 19th and 20th centuries. Nations and corporations that controlled raw manufacturing capacity often lost out to those that successfully established the underlying protocols and standards that everyone else had to adopt to participate in the market. Owning the factory floor matters, but owning the technical rulebook determines long-term dominance.</p>
    </div>

    <p>In the context of the modern US-China AI rivalry, projects like Huawei's Atlas 950 SuperPoD represent more than just engineering workarounds to export curbs. They signal an institutional effort to build parallel or independent technical ecosystems. When a nation is cut off from established international frameworks, it is forced to create its own benchmarks for software compatibility, data processing efficiency, and network architecture.</p>

    <div class="ncg-quote-box">
        <p>&ldquo;True technological autonomy is not simply about having chips, but about having the ability to define the technological path.&rdquo;</p>
    </div>
</section>

<section id="how-readers-respond">
    <h2>How Should We Understand This Shift?</h2>
    <p>For ordinary observers, tech professionals, and business leaders watching the escalating US-China AI rivalry from the outside, this dynamic carries broader lessons about global economic fragmentation.</p>
    <ul>
        <li><strong>Look Beyond the Headlines:</strong> Media coverage often reduces high-tech competition to single components like advanced semiconductors. In reality, the true friction lies in software ecosystems, cloud infrastructure, and regulatory standards.</li>
        <li><strong>Anticipate Market Bifurcation:</strong> As different regions develop distinct technological paths to bypass trade restrictions, companies operating globally may increasingly have to adapt to multiple, non-interoperable technical standards.</li>
        <li><strong>Recognize the Catalyst Effect:</strong> External pressure frequently accelerates native innovation and structural reform far faster than comfortable, open-market conditions would ever prompt on their own.</li>
    </ul>
</section>

<section class="ncg-closing-box">
    <h3>One Last Thought</h3>
    <p>The US-China AI rivalry is reshaping the future of global innovation not because of what products are blocked, but because of what new paths are forcibly carved out in response. When nations and industries are denied access to existing systems, they do not simply stop moving forward &mdash; they build entirely new roads. And the rules of those new roads may govern the next century of technology.</p>
    <p><strong>The future belongs not just to those who make the fastest processor, but to those who write the standard everyone else must eventually follow.</strong></p>
