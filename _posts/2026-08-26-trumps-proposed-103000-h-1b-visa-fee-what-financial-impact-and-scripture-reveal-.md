  <section id="what-happened" style="margin-bottom: 30px;">
    <h2 style="font-size: 24px; color: #0f5132; border-bottom: 1px solid #e9ecef; padding-bottom: 8px; margin-top: 0;">What Happened: A Six-Figure Surcharge</h2>
    <p>
      Mere months after a federal judge struck down a previous attempt to impose a six-figure price tag on high-skilled immigration, the Trump administration is taking another aggressive step. On Tuesday, the Department of Homeland Security (DHS) formally proposed a new $103,265 surcharge for employers looking to hire foreign workers under the H-1B visa program. This fee sits entirely on top of existing standard filing costs.
    </p>
    <p>
      According to the official proposal, the department itself estimates that this upcharge will create a &ldquo;significant economic impact&rdquo; for 11,051 small businesses—representing a staggering 76 percent of the small entities the agency analyzed. While the policy must travel through a 30-day public comment period before final enactment, it threatens to fundamentally alter the financial mechanics of a visa program relied upon heavily by technology companies, specialized consulting firms, and early-stage startups.
    </p>
    <p>
      DHS defends the policy by pointing to the rising administrative expenses of vetting and managing lawful immigration programs. Zach Kahler, a spokesperson for U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services, noted that the federal government spends roughly $8.8 billion annually on immigration-related functions. Dividing that figure by the annual cap of 85,000 available H-1B visas yields the exact mathematical basis for the proposed $103,265 fee. Over a ten-year span, the rule is projected to cost employers an eye-watering $74.9 billion.
    </p>
