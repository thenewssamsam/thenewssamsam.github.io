    <p style="font-size: 1.15rem; color: #4a5568; font-weight: 400;">A contentious legal battle over modernizing the White House East Wing reaches the Supreme Court, prompting a deeper look at the timeless balance between civic protection, executive stewardship, and the heavy responsibilities of leadership.</p>

  <section id="what-happened" style="margin-bottom: 40px;">
    <h2 style="font-size: 1.5rem; font-weight: 700; color: #2b6cb0; border-bottom: 2px solid #ebf8ff; padding-bottom: 8px; margin-bottom: 20px;">What Happened in the White House Construction Dispute</h2>
    <p>The Department of Justice has officially petitioned the United States Supreme Court to intervene in an unfolding dispute regarding the renovation of the White House East Wing. Following lower court rulings that imposed a restrictive construction schedule overseen by a federal judge, Chief Justice John Roberts is reviewing an application to stay the appellate decision.</p>
    <p>Commentators and legal analysts, including radio host and law professor Hugh Hewitt, have argued that micromanaging executive branch facilities amid a modern threat matrix undermines core presidential responsibilities. At issue is not merely architectural preference, but the urgent need to update subterranean emergency facilities originally built during the Roosevelt administration in 1942—long before the advent of modern drone swarms, hypersonic threats, and sophisticated digital surveillance.</p>

      <p style="font-size: 1.1rem; font-style: italic; color: #2d3748; margin: 0;">"To ignore the rapidly evolving threats to the White House and any facility used frequently by any president against this reality would be reckless beyond reason and defy common sense."</p>
    </div>
  </section>
