    <h1>Jared Kushner and Hakeem Jeffries Meet: What Historical Parallels Reveal About Divided Washington</h1>
    <p class="ncg-subtitle">Behind closed doors in New York, an unexpected political dialogue points to a long tradition of pragmatic deal-making amidst deep partisan division.</p>
  </header>

  <section class="ncg-intro">
    <p>When unexpected political actors sit down across from one another away from the public eye, it usually signals that the calculus in Washington is shifting. Recently, Jared Kushner—the president’s son-in-law and close adviser—held a private meeting in New York with House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries. Their agenda: exploring ways the White House and congressional Democrats might collaborate on immigration, housing, and the soaring cost of living.</p>
    <p>For casual observers, the pairing seems paradoxical. Jeffries has positioned himself as a fierce critic of the second Trump administration, while Kushner operates outside formal day-to-day government channels. Yet, looking back through the lens of American political history, such back-channel diplomacy during periods of intense legislative gridlock is far more common—and necessary—than many realize.</p>
  </section>

  <section class="ncg-news-summary">
    <h2 id="what-happened">What Happened Behind Closed Doors?</h2>
    <p>According to reports confirmed by CNN, Kushner and Jeffries gathered quietly several weeks ago. The conversation centered on substantive domestic concerns, including housing affordability, immigration policy, and cost-of-living pressures affecting everyday Americans. During the meeting, Kushner reportedly suggested that Jeffries also open lines of communication with White House Chief of Staff Susie Wiles.</p>
