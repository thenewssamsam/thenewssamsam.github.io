  <h2 id="what-happened">What Happened</h2>
  <p>Target recently issued a public apology and removed a children's Halloween costume from its seasonal Hyde and EEK Boutique brand following intense online backlash. The item, listed as the "Kids' Glows Under Blacklight Circus Clown Halloween Costume," drew immediate criticism across platforms like Threads and X, with users accusing the retailer of selling merchandise that evoked 19th-century minstrel shows and blackface imagery.</p>
  <p>Company spokesperson Brian Harper-Tibaldo addressed the controversy directly in a statement to FOX Business, acknowledging the gravity of the oversight. "As a company, we know we got this wrong, and we are deeply sorry. The costume is offensive and should never have been part of our assortment. It is no longer available for sale," Harper-Tibaldo stated. He further noted that the company is actively investigating how the product cleared its review pipelines to ensure such an oversight does not happen again.</p>

  <h3 id="groupthink-and-homogeneity">Groupthink and Cultural Blindness</h3>
  <p>Groupthink occurs when the desire for harmony or conformity in a decision-making group results in an irrational or dysfunctional outcome. In large corporations, product development teams often work in streamlined siloes where internal validation replaces external reality testing. If a design concept passes through initial approvals without anyone raising a hand, subsequent reviewers frequently assume someone else already vetted it. The presence of diverse perspectives is often the primary safeguard against these cultural blind spots, making any reduction in inclusive review pipelines acutely risky.</p>

  <h3 id="normalcy-bias">Normalcy Bias</h3>
  <p>Normalcy bias causes people to underestimate the possibility of a disaster or cultural misstep because things have always operated a certain way in the past. Merchandising cycles move quickly, and routine approvals can easily turn into rubber-stamp exercises. When teams rely strictly on past templates for circus themes or novelty items without re-examining cultural connotations, offensive designs slip past the finish line unnoticed.</p>
