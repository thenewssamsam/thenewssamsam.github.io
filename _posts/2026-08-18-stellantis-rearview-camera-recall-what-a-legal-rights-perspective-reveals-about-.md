  <h2 id="what-happened">What Happened in the Stellantis Recall?</h2>
  <p>Automaker Stellantis has issued a massive recall impacting approximately 848,511 vehicles in the United States, alongside more than 100,000 units internationally, primarily in Canada. The core issue stems from radio software that can intermittently block the rearview camera image from appearing on the central media display screen.</p>
  
  <p>The affected lineup spans almost the entire modern American stable of the manufacturer, including:</p>

Furthermore, the company anticipates remedying the defect through an over-the-air (OTA) software update, meaning many owners may bypass traditional dealership service appointments altogether.</p>

  <h2 id="legal-perspective">A Legal Rights Perspective on Vehicle Recalls</h2>
  <p>From a legal and regulatory standpoint, large-scale automotive recalls represent a delicate balance between consumer protection statutes, federal safety mandates enforced by agencies like the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA), and corporate liability management.</p>

  <div class="ncg-notice-box">
    <p><strong>Important Disclaimer:</strong> This is general information, not legal advice. Consult a licensed attorney about your specific situation if you have been involved in an automotive accident or dispute.</p>
  </div>

  <p>Under federal consumer protection standards, manufacturers are legally obligated to remedy safety-related defects at no cost to the consumer once a formal recall is declared. While physical component replacements traditionally required dealership visits—and sometimes brought disputes over loaner vehicles or service delays—modern connected vehicles introduce a new digital layer to warranty and recall law.</p>

  <p>Over-the-air software updates streamline the remediation process, but they also raise important questions regarding digital liability. When a safety feature relies entirely on software code rather than a mechanical link, the manufacturer's duty to provide prompt, secure, and reliable patches becomes paramount. Consumers maintain the right to expect that digital fixes will not introduce secondary vulnerabilities or impair other vehicle systems.</p>

  <div class="ncg-quote-box">
    <p>“A vehicle recall is not just a logistical hurdle for an automaker; it is a legally binding commitment to restore baseline safety for every driver on the road.”</p>
  </div>

  <h2 id="what-owners-should-do">What Affected Drivers Should Do Next</h2>
  <p>While waiting for official notification notices to arrive by mail beginning in September, vehicle owners are not left entirely in the dark. Responsible vehicle operation requires adapting temporarily to the software limitation:</p>
  
  <ol>
    <li><strong>Rely on Physical Mirrors:</strong> Until the software patch is installed, drivers must explicitly rely on traditional side and rearview mirrors and direct physical check-arounds when reversing.</li>
    <li><strong>Watch for Update Prompts:</strong> Keep an eye on your vehicle infotainment screen for an over-the-air update prompt, and ensure your vehicle has an active network connection to receive it.</li>
    <li><strong>Verify Your VIN:</strong> Owners can proactively check whether their specific vehicle is included in the campaign by visiting <a href="https://recalls.mopar.com" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">recalls.mopar.com</a> or calling customer care directly at 1-800-853-1403.</li>
  </ol>

  <div class="ncg-faq">
    <details>
      <summary>Do I have to pay for the Stellantis camera software update?</summary>
      <p>No. Under federal recall guidelines, manufacturers must provide safety-related defect repairs free of charge to vehicle owners.</p>
    </details>
    <details>
      <summary>Will I receive a formal notice in the mail?</summary>
      <p>Yes. Stellantis has indicated that affected drivers will receive official recall notification letters starting in September.</p>
    </details>
