<h2 id="what-happened">What Happened to the Swift Observatory?</h2>
<p>For more than two decades, the Swift Observatory has served as one of NASA's most productive assets, keeping an uninterrupted watch on gamma-ray bursts—the most powerful explosions in the universe. But residing in low-Earth orbit meant Swift was always vulnerable to atmospheric drag. Driven by an intense cycle of increased solar storms since the fall of 2024, the satellite began plummeting back toward Earth much faster than mission planners originally anticipated.</p>

<p>Facing the inevitable loss of the telescope, NASA greenlit an innovative, high-risk solution. Under a $30 million contract, Arizona-based startup Katalyst Space developed an 880-pound robotic vehicle named LINK. Launched atop a Northrop Grumman rocket on July 3, LINK's ambitious objective was to rendezvous with Swift, grab it with robotic arms, and boost it to a safer 370-mile-high orbit.</p>
