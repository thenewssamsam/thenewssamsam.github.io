<section id="what-happened">
    <h2>What Happened: Twelve Days in the Dark</h2>
    <p>For nearly two weeks, thousands of residents across northwestern Indiana have lived without the most basic modern convenience: electricity. Following a violent, destructive storm on August 11, 2026—preliminarily identified by the National Weather Service as a derecho with winds topping 100 mph—communities like Gary, Munster, and Portage have faced an arduous recovery. While utility provider NIPSCO reports that hundreds of thousands of accounts have been reconnected since the initial 374,500 outages peaked, roughly 16,000 customers remained in the dark as of August 23.</p>

Mayor Melton noted in a video update that 12 days is entirely too long for families to worry about their children, for businesses to bleed revenue, and for residents to wonder when relief will arrive. Meanwhile, utility crews working alongside the mobilized Indiana National Guard continue clearing downed trees and repairing shattered lines, with full restoration promised for late August—though individual homeowners like Denise Bairns have faced staggering estimates stretching into September.</p>

    <h3>The Test of Community Resilience</h3>
    <p>Extended crises reveal the true character of a neighborhood. While frustration with utility companies is entirely valid, the devastation also opens doors for neighborly love in action. When power fails, porch lights may go dark, but the light of human kindness often shines brighter. Sharing generators, checking on elderly neighbors, and offering a cool drink to linemen working in the August heat embody the practical call to love one's neighbor.</p>
