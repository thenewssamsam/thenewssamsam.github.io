  <section id="what-happened">
    <h2>What Happened: The White House Ballroom Legal Battle</h2>
    <p>The controversy centers on a plan announced by the Trump administration to tear down part of the White House East Wing to construct a sprawling 90,000-square-foot ballroom designed to host substantially larger gatherings than current spaces allow. Proponents argue that presidents have long desired a grand event space to accommodate official functions on behalf of the American people.</p>
    
    <div style="background: #fffdf5; border-left: 4px solid #d4af37; padding: 15px; margin: 20px 0; border-radius: 4px;">
      <p style="margin: 0; font-size: 0.95rem; color: #555;"><strong>Quick Take:</strong> Lawyers for the National Trust for Historic Preservation have urged the Supreme Court to leave in place a lower court ruling temporarily blocking the project, accusing the administration of racing to finish construction before judicial review can catch up.</p>
    </div>

    <p>However, the National Trust for Historic Preservation intervened in federal court, arguing that the project is proceeding without the necessary approval from the National Capital Planning Commission, the Commission of Fine Arts, and—most critically—without explicit funding authorization from Congress. While a federal district judge initially ordered a pause on above-ground construction, that order was temporarily lifted, leading to a divided three-judge panel of the D.C. Circuit upholding the restrictions while giving the government 14 days to appeal to the Supreme Court.</p>

    <h2>The Stakes: Speed, Authority, and Permanence</h2>
    <p>At its heart, this high-profile litigation is not simply about architectural styles or the scheduling of concrete pours. It is a fundamental contest over institutional jurisdiction. The National Trust framed the core question in its 40-page filing as a matter of constitutional order: who ultimately decides whether a major permanent structure can be erected on federal property belonging to the public?</p>
