    <h2>What Happened in Forest Grove</h2>
    <p>This past weekend, a horrific domestic violence incident unfolded just outside of Forest Grove, Oregon, claiming the lives of five individuals. Among the victims were three generations of the Kimsey family, including 60-year-old Rowan Kimsey, her mother Mary, and her daughter Stella, alongside family friends Linsey Haraldsen and Alvin Chu. According to authorities, the 36-year-old suspect, Benjamin Parker, later died by suicide in Washington state.</p>
    
    <p>For the broader Portland metropolitan area, and specifically the historic Montavilla neighborhood on Southeast 81st Avenue, the news arrived as a devastating blow. Rowan Kimsey was not merely a local resident; she was a vibrant fixture of the community whose art, empathy, and fierce dedication to lifting others up left an indelible mark on everyone she encountered.</p>

    <h3>Understanding Domestic Violence Dynamics</h3>
    <p>Psychological experts emphasize that domestic violence rarely emerges in a vacuum; it is driven by patterns of power, control, and often severe, untreated mental health struggles. While the public frequently asks "how could this happen?", behavioral specialists point out that perpetrators often mask their instability behind closed doors, leaving friends and family members blindsided. Recognizing early behavioral warning signs—such as extreme isolation, threats, or escalating volatility—remains one of the few preventative tools available to society.</p>
