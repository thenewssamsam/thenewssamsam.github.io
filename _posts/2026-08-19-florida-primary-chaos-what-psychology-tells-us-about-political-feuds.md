    <h2>The Psychology of Political Tribalism</h2>
    <p>When we observe the intensity of these primary feuds, we are seeing classic psychological phenomena in action. Why do candidates—and voters—often become more aggressive toward their own party members than their political opponents?</p>

    <h3>The "Black Sheep" Effect</h3>
    <p>Psychologically, groups often exhibit heightened hostility toward members who deviate from perceived ideological purity. This is known as the "Black Sheep Effect." In the current Florida GOP primaries, candidates who are viewed as "insufficiently loyal" or "DEI Republicans" face harsher vitriol than opposition party members might receive. The human brain tends to categorize these "traitors" as greater threats to group cohesion than external rivals, triggering a more intense defensive reaction.</p>

    <h3>Groupthink and Moral Panic</h3>
    <p>We also see the influence of <strong>groupthink</strong>, where the desire for harmony or conformity in a group results in an irrational or dysfunctional decision-making outcome. As candidates attempt to out-signal one another to satisfy their specific base, they often escalate to inflammatory rhetoric. This creates a feedback loop of <strong>moral panic</strong>, where voters feel that the survival of their values is at stake, justifying increasingly aggressive tactics against their own party's incumbents.</p>

  <section id="practical-response">
    <h2>How Should Readers Respond?</h2>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Practice Media Literacy:</strong> Recognize when headlines are designed to trigger "in-group/out-group" biases. Pause before sharing content that uses dehumanizing language.</li>
      <li><strong>Focus on Policy, Not Personality:</strong> When evaluating candidates, look past the "feud of the week" and examine their actual track record on issues that impact your daily life.</li>
      <li><strong>Engage Locally:</strong> Primary elections are won by the most motivated voters. Your individual vote carries significantly more weight in a primary than in a general election.</li>

  <section id="closing-takeaway">
    <h2>One Last Thought</h2>
    <p>Political primaries are, by definition, the messy process of a party deciding what it stands for. While the current level of discord may feel unprecedented, it is a reflection of a society grappling with changing identities and intense polarization. By understanding the psychological forces at play, we can move from reactive emotional responses to more intentional civic participation. The goal of any election should be to find the most capable representative, not simply the loudest voice in the room. Stay informed, stay critical, and remember that your vote is a tool for the future, not just a weapon for the present.</p>
