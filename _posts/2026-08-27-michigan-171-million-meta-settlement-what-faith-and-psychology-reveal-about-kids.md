  <section id="what-happened" style="margin-bottom: 40px;">
    <h2 style="font-size: 1.5rem; color: #2d3748; border-left: 4px solid #3182ce; padding-left: 12px; margin-bottom: 20px;">What Happened in the Historic Meta Settlement</h2>
    <p>Michigan is set to receive at least $171 million from Meta Platforms, the parent company of Facebook and Instagram, as part of a monumental multi-state settlement. Attorney General Dana Nessel announced the agreement, which represents Michigan's share of up to $17.1 billion provided nationwide to settle sprawling litigation involving 47 states, the District of Columbia, and three U.S. territories.</p>
    
    <div style="background: #f7fafc; border: 1px solid #e2e8f0; border-radius: 8px; padding: 20px; margin: 20px 0;">
      <h3 style="margin-top: 0; color: #2d3748; font-size: 1.1rem;">Key Settlement Highlights:</h3>
      <ul style="margin-bottom: 0; padding-left: 20px; color: #4a5568;">
        <li><strong>Financial Recovery:</strong> Michigan secures a minimum of $171 million to address the youth digital crisis.</li>
        <li><strong>Structural Safeguards:</strong> Mandatory new platform rules including age verification and parental controls.</li>
        <li><strong>Time Restrictions:</strong> Automatic notification pauses during school hours and "nighttime blocks" from midnight to 6 a.m. for younger users.</li>
        <li><strong>Accountability:</strong> Resolves core allegations that Meta intentionally designed addictive loops to ensnare young internet users.</li>
      </ul>
    </div>

    <p>The legal battle began in earnest when 29 states filed suit against the tech giant, alleging that Meta deliberately engineered its algorithms to "entice, engage and ultimately ensnare" minors. As the trial began in federal court, the consent judgment brought a sudden, sweeping resolution. While the financial injection is substantial, the true battle lies in changing how technology interacts with developing human minds.</p>
  </section>

  </div>
