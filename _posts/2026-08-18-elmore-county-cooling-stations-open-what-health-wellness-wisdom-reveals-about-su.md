<section id="health-perspective" style="margin-bottom:28px;">
<h2 style="font-size:1.5rem; color:#1e293b; margin-bottom:12px; border-bottom:2px solid #e2e8f0; padding-bottom:6px;">A Health &amp; Wellness Perspective on Extreme Heat</h2>
<p>From a general health and wellness perspective, extreme heat places an extraordinary burden on the human body. When ambient temperatures soar, our cardiovascular system works overtime to cool the body down by redirecting blood flow to the skin and inducing perspiration. If humidity is also high, sweat cannot evaporate effectively, impairing the body's primary cooling mechanism.</p>
<p>Recognizing early signs of heat-related stress—such as excessive fatigue, dizziness, heavy sweating, or a rapid pulse—can prevent a minor discomfort from escalating into heat exhaustion or heat stroke. Public cooling stations serve as critical circuit breakers against this physiological stress, offering the body a chance to lower its core temperature before severe complications arise.</p>
</section>

<section id="how-to-respond" style="margin-bottom:28px;">
<h2 style="font-size:1.5rem; color:#1e293b; margin-bottom:12px; border-bottom:2px solid #e2e8f0; padding-bottom:6px;">How Residents Can Stay Safe and Support Neighbors</h2>
<p>When severe weather warnings are active, proactive habits make all the difference. Drink plenty of water throughout the day even before you feel thirsty, wear lightweight and light-colored clothing, and limit strenuous outdoor activities during peak afternoon hours.</p>
<p>Furthermore, extreme heat is a community vulnerability test. Check in on elderly neighbors, families with young children, and anyone without reliable air conditioning. Sometimes a simple phone call or a knock on the door is all it takes to connect a vulnerable neighbor to life-saving local resources.</p>
<div class="ec-medical-notice" style="background:#f8fafc; border:1px solid #cbd5e1; padding:14px 18px; border-radius:6px; margin-top:16px; font-size:0.9rem; color:#475569;">
<strong>Important Notice:</strong> This is general information, not medical advice. If you or someone you know is experiencing severe symptoms of heat stroke—such as confusion, a high body temperature, or loss of consciousness—call 911 immediately.
