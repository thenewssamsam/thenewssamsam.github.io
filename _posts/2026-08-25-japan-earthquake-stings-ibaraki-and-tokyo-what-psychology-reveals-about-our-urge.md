<h2>What Happened in the Japan Earthquake</h2>
<p>Early on Sunday, residents across eastern Japan experienced a violent awakening. A 5.9-magnitude earthquake struck the southern part of the Ibaraki Prefecture at a depth of roughly 68 kilometers, sending powerful shockwaves toward Tokyo and the surrounding regions. According to the Japan Meteorological Agency (JMA), there was thankfully no immediate concern about tsunamis, sparing the coastal communities from a secondary crisis.</p>

<p>However, the shaking was robust enough to cause disruptions. <h2>The Psychology of Sudden Shocks</h2>
<p>Earthquakes occupy a unique psychological space compared to other natural disasters. Unlike typhoons or blizzards, which often provide hours or days of tracking warnings, an earthquake offers zero advance notice. The ground beneath our feet—our most fundamental symbol of permanence and security—becomes untrustworthy in a fraction of a second.</p>
