<p>When an individual commits a severe crime, the initial psychological shock is often followed by an instinctive survival response: flight. Fleeing across state lines—such as traveling from Oregon into the quiet expanses of Washington—represents an attempt to regain control. By changing physical environments, the fleeing individual engages in a psychological defense mechanism, subconsciously hoping that distance equates to safety or a reset button on reality.</p>
