    <h3>Comparing Eras</h3>
    <p>Historically, mid-decade adjustments—while controversial—have often been used to bypass traditional decennial census cycles to secure partisan advantages. This mirrors the "Gerrymander" phenomena of the 19th century, where politicians sought to "pack" or "crack" districts to ensure electoral dominance. While technology and data analytics have made these efforts more precise today, the fundamental goal remains the same: shaping the legislature to fit a specific vision of the future.</p>
