<section id="what-happened">
<h2>What Happened: A Historic Federal Sentence</h2>
<p>The federal justice system delivered a landmark ruling in Knoxville, Tennessee, handing down a 77-year prison sentence to 27-year-old Kyle William Spitze of Friendsville. According to the Department of Justice, this represents the longest federal prison sentence ever given to a member of a nihilistic violent extremist network. Spitze operated as an administrator and early member of decentralized online groups known as "764" and "HarmNation." These digital syndicates use social media platforms to target vulnerable minors, leveraging extortion, blackmail, and coercion to force young victims into acts of self-harm and graphic abuse.</p>

<p>Federal investigators began unraveling the network in December 2023 after tracing abusive media back to Spitze's online channels. Court documents revealed that the defendant uploaded graphic videos of minor victims who had been manipulated into severe self-mutilation, sometimes carving his online monikers into their skin. In February 2024, law enforcement executed a search warrant and uncovered definitive evidence involving underage victims, leading to Spitze admitting to operating with a terrorist motive. Attorney General Todd Blanche called the crimes "the worst of the worst," while FBI Director Kash Patel highlighted a 500% surge in arrests connected to nihilistic violent extremist groups over the past year.</p>
