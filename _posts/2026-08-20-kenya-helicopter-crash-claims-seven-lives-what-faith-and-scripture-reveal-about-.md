    <section id="news-summary" style="margin-bottom: 40px;">
        <h2 style="font-size: 1.5rem; color: #2b6cb0; border-left: 4px solid #3182ce; padding-left: 12px; margin-bottom: 20px;">What Happened in Northern Kenya</h2>
        <p>On a Wednesday morning that promised breathtaking aerial views of northern Kenya's dramatic landscapes, a routine charter flight transformed into a scene of devastating loss. A Eurocopter EC130 B4, operated by local company Lady Lori Helicopters and carrying guests from the travel company +Beyond, crashed in a rugged area near the foothills of Mount Ololokwe in Samburu county. The aircraft was traveling from the Loisaba Conservancy to the Ewaso Nyiro area when it went down at approximately 9:13 a.m.</p>
        
        <p>Local authorities, including Samburu police commander David Nkoroi, confirmed that all seven people onboard—six passengers and the pilot—lost their lives. The United States State Department subsequently verified that five of the victims were American citizens. Among those tragically killed were José Suárez, 55, president and general manager of several Telemundo stations in Florida, and Michele Sensi-Contugi, the director of Ecuador’s national intelligence center, who was visiting Kenya as a tourist alongside his wife, Stephany Hollihan Vasconez, who also perished in the crash.</p>

        <p>While local safari operators such as Tropic Air Kenya swiftly dispatched rescue helicopters to aid search and rescue operations, the harsh terrain of Mount Ololokwe underscored the isolation of the disaster. The Kenya Civil Aviation Authority has launched a formal investigation to determine the exact cause of the accident, casting a somber light on the safety of domestic tourism carriers in East Africa.</p>
