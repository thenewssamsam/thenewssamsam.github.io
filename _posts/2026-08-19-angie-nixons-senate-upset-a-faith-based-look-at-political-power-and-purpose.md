    <p>In a surprising turn for Florida politics, state Representative Angie Nixon has secured the Democratic nomination for the U.S. Senate, defeating heavily favored candidate Alex Vindman. As Floridians look toward the general election against incumbent Senator Ashley Moody, the race highlights a deepening divide in American political priorities—and invites us to reflect on the nature of leadership, community, and the stewardship of our influence.</p>

  <section id="what-happened">
    <h2>What Happened in the Florida Primary?</h2>
    <p>Angie Nixon, a member of the Democratic Socialists of America, overcame a significant fundraising deficit to defeat Alex Vindman, a retired lieutenant colonel and former National Security Council aide. While Vindman held the backing of the Democratic Senatorial Campaign Committee and raised over $16 million, Nixon’s campaign—focused on Medicare for all, universal child care, and affordable housing—resonated with voters who favored her grassroots approach.</p>
    <p>The race now shifts to November, where Nixon will face Republican Senator Ashley Moody. The contest is expected to be contentious, as both parties draw stark lines regarding the future of Florida and the nation.</p>
