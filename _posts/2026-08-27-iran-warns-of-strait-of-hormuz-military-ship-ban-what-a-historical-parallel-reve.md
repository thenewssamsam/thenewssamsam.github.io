  <h2 id="what-happened" style="font-size: 1.5rem; font-weight: 700; color: #1e293b; margin-top: 30px; margin-bottom: 15px;">What Happened in the Strait of Hormuz?</h2>
  
  <p>
    According to reports from state media via the Associated Press, Iranian Deputy Foreign Minister Kazem Gharibabadi announced late Tuesday night that Iran and Oman have forged a preliminary understanding regarding the management of ship traffic through the Strait of Hormuz. The vital waterway has remained largely paralyzed since the Iran war erupted nearly half a year ago.
  </p>
  
  <p>
    Under the proposed framework, commercial shipping would follow heavily regulated corridors: incoming traffic from the Gulf of Oman would pass exclusively through Iranian territorial waters, while outbound vessels would split transit between Iranian waters and Oman's jurisdiction. However, the most explosive element of the understanding is a sweeping prohibition: <em>“If the understanding becomes binding, no military vessel will be permitted to pass through the Strait of Hormuz,”</em> Gharibabadi stated bluntly. <em>“No military vessel at all.”</em>
  </p>
