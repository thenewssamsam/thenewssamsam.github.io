<section id="what-happened">
<h2>What Happened</h2>
<p>When shifts at the top of global superpowers send ripples across regional security architectures, smaller nations are often forced to adapt overnight. That reality came into sharp focus this week in Seoul, where South Korean President Lee Jae Myung called for stronger independent defense capabilities following a directive from U.S. President Donald Trump to substantially reduce joint military exercises.</p>
<p>The developments began unfolding as U.S. Forces Korea commander Gen. Xavier Brunson visited South Korea's Defense Ministry. The discussions coincided with the annual Ulchi Freedom Shield exercises, scheduled to run through August 27. President Lee addressed a Cabinet meeting by emphasizing that while the U.S.-South Korea alliance remains the cornerstone of regional security, Seoul must accelerate its own military readiness, speed up its nuclear-powered submarine program, and prepare for a worst-case scenario. At the same time, South Korean officials expressed hope that Washington's outreach to Pyongyang might help restart diplomatic dialogue on the Korean Peninsula.</p>
</section>

<h2>One Last Thought</h2>
