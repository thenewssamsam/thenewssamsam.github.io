  <section id="what-happened">
    <h2>What Happened</h2>
    <p>United States President Donald Trump has seen his approval rating plunge to 33 percent—matching the lowest point of his entire political career—as an overwhelming majority of Americans grow deeply anxious over the prolonged US-Israel war on Iran and the resulting spike in fuel costs. According to a recent Reuters/Ipsos poll, 64 percent of respondents disapprove of his job performance, driven largely by persistent inflation at the gasoline pump and fears that the conflict has no clear endpoint.</p>
    <p>Trump originally campaigned on promises to tame inflation and steer clear of protracted foreign entanglements, initially assuring the public that any military engagement with Iran would wrap up in a matter of weeks. Instead, the conflict has stretched on, keeping oil and gas exports through the crucial Strait of Hormuz largely bottled up. While the administration argues that enduring higher fuel prices is a necessary sacrifice to prevent a hostile regime from acquiring a nuclear weapon, polling indicates that even bedrock supporters are starting to feel the economic pinch. A recent Politico survey showed that only a third of self-identified MAGA voters believe the economic costs of the war are justified.</p>
  </section>

When a administration enters a conflict with promises of a swift victory, the public is often willing to give the benefit of the doubt. But history shows that patience evaporates the moment abstract geopolitical goals translate into tangible, daily financial pain for working families.</p>
