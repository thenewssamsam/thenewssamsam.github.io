<section id="what-happened">
<h2>What Happened in Hawaii</h2>
<p>Communities across Hawaii's Big Island are bracing for yet another severe weather threat as Tropical Storm Moke swirls past the islands, bringing the prospect of up to 15 inches of rain. This fresh soaking comes directly on the heels of Hurricane Lala, which left behind washed-out roads, widespread mudslides, and power outages affecting over 6,500 customers. With two reported deaths already linked to the previous storm, local officials—including Hawaii County Mayor Kimo Alameda and Governor Josh Green—have urged residents to shelter in place, stay off the roads, and restock essential supplies.</p>

<p>While meteorologists track Moke passing south of the island chain, the psychological and physical toll on families living in vulnerable areas is mounting. When consecutive storms strike vulnerable communities, the disruption extends far beyond flooded streets; it reaches directly into family routines, childhood security, and parental peace of mind. Preparing for such events requires more than just bottled water and canned goods—it demands a calm, intentional approach to family communication.</p>
</section>
