<section id="what-happened">
<h2>What Happened: The $50 Medicare Bridge Program</h2>
<p>By statutory law, Medicare has long been barred from paying directly for weight loss drugs. However, facing mounting pressure over rising obesity rates and chronic disease burdens among older adults, the administration engineered a clever administrative workaround: the Bridge program.</p>
<p>Starting in July, the pilot program allowed qualifying Medicare beneficiaries to obtain obesity treatments for a flat copay of $50 a month. To qualify, patients must meet specific clinical criteria, such as battling obesity combined with hard-to-control high blood pressure. Notably, beneficiaries with sleep apnea or Type 2 diabetes are excluded from the Bridge program because Medicare Part D already provides coverage pathways for GLP-1 medications under those specific diagnoses.</p>

Nonpartisan health research group KFF estimated that the 18-month Bridge program could cost taxpayers anywhere between $1.3 billion and $10 billion, depending entirely on participation rates among the nearly 4 million potentially eligible beneficiaries.</p>
