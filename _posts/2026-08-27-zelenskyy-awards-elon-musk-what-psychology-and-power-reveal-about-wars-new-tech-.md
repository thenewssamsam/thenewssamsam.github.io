    <h2>What Happened: The Order of Freedom</h2>
    <p>In a move that highlights the bizarre and unprecedented intersection of Silicon Valley entrepreneurship and high-stakes geopolitics, Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy has awarded Elon Musk Ukraine's prestigious Order of Freedom. The state honor recognizes Musk's "outstanding personal merits" in protecting human life and freedom through the deployment of Starlink during Russia's full-scale invasion.</p>
    
    <p>Since the war began in 2022, SpaceX’s satellite internet network has served as an indispensable lifeline for Ukraine's military. Commanders and frontline soldiers rely on continuous connectivity to coordinate tactical movements, manage drone warfare, and sustain morale. However, the relationship is fraught with tension. Musk retains unilateral control over how the network operates, refusing to permit its use for offensive long-range drone strikes inside Russian territory out of fear of dangerous escalation.</p>

    <h2>The Psychology of Private Power in Statecraft</h2>

Historically, military sovereignty and geopolitical leverage were the exclusive monopolies of nation-states. Today, modern infrastructure relies on private corporations whose leaders answer to shareholders, personal ideologies, or whims rather than voters.</p>

<section id="the-paradox-of-dependency">
    <h2>The Paradox of Digital Dependency</h2>
    <p>Human beings—and by extension, complex government institutions—struggle immensely with systemic dependency. When an external entity provides an absolute good (like uninterrupted internet access in a warzone), recipients quickly experience a psychological phenomenon known as habituation. The lifeline rapidly transforms in the collective mind from a generous gift into an entitlement, making any restriction or policy disagreement feel like an active betrayal.</p>
