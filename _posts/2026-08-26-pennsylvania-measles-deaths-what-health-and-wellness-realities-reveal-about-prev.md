<section id="what-happened">
<h2>What Happened in Pennsylvania?</h2>
<p>The Pennsylvania Department of Health confirmed that two unvaccinated residents of Lancaster County have died from complications related to measles. These tragic losses mark the first US deaths from the virus in 2026 and the first in Pennsylvania in over three decades. State health officials reported that the local outbreak has climbed toward 400 confirmed cases across 28 counties, with roughly half of those infections concentrated in Lancaster County.</p>

<p>State Health Secretary Dr. Debra Bogen expressed deep sympathies for the affected families while emphasizing the safety and efficacy of the MMR vaccine. Meanwhile, political leaders have clashed over the broader public health environment. Gov. Josh Shapiro publicly criticized federal vaccine skepticisms and misinformation, noting that real-world public health decisions carry profound human consequences when communities lose access to clear, science-backed guidance.</p>

<h2>Understanding Transmission and Protection</h2>
<p>Measles is among the most contagious infectious agents known to science. It spreads easily when an infected person breathes, coughs, or sneezes, and the virus can linger in the air and on surfaces for up to two hours after an infected individual has left a room. Early signs often mimic a severe cold or flu: fever, cough, runny nose, and red, watery eyes, followed by a characteristic rash that starts on the head and works its way down.</p>
