  <section id="what-happened" class="ncg-section">
    <h2>What Happened: A Soldier's Family Split Apart</h2>
    <p>On a Monday that shattered an Army family's fragile stability, Cristy Maryori Villafranca Trejo was deported to Honduras. For her husband, Army Sgt. Hedar Leonel Turcios Juarez, the moment marked the tragic culmination of months of anxiety, detention, and bureaucratic heartbreak. Sgt. Turcios Juarez, who became a U.S. citizen in 2024 following a demanding nine-month deployment to the Middle East, had trusted that his honorable service and ongoing legal petitions would shield his wife from the sweeping machinery of modern immigration enforcement.</p>

    <p>Instead, ICE agents detained Villafranca outside a local Walmart in July while their six-year-old daughter looked on in confusion and terror. According to the Department of Homeland Security, Villafranca had entered the United States unlawfully in 2016 while fleeing violent crime in Honduras—long before she met her husband. Yet, her removal is far from an isolated incident. An investigation by the Associated Press reveals that President Donald Trump's intensified deportation campaign has swept up more than 50 parents and spouses of active-duty service members during his second term alone, with at least six already deported.</p>
    <p>This reality directly collides with the administration's stated mission of targeting only the "worst of the worst" criminal elements. State and federal records reviewed by journalists show no criminal histories for the military spouses caught in these sweeps. As military advocacy groups point out, these sudden actions leave troops scrambling to take emergency leave, pulling their focus away from national defense to care for terrified children in the shadows.</p>

For generations, green card holders have worn the uniform, and non-citizen family members have found a protected corner within an otherwise rigid immigration bureaucracy. Enlistment incentives dating back to the Civil War have always operated on a tacit social contract: you risk your life for the nation, and the nation guards your home front.</p>
