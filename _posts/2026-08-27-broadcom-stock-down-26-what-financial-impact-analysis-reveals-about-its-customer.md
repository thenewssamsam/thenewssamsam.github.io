<section id="what-happened">
    <h2>What Happened</h2>
    <p>Broadcom Inc. finds itself in a fascinating position in the late-summer 2026 markets. Trading near $356 per share, the stock sits roughly 26% below its 52-week high. For many retail investors and momentum traders, a double-digit pullback in a proven artificial intelligence winner looks like a clearance sale. But financial analysis of the company's recent regulatory disclosures and management guidance tells a more nuanced story.</p>

Even more striking, the fiscal Q3 2026 guidance projects $16 billion in AI semiconductor revenue inside a consolidated $29.4 billion top line, pushing that segment to roughly 54% of total revenue. Broadcom is no longer simply a diversified enterprise software and legacy chip supplier; it has rapidly transformed into a specialized AI infrastructure engine.</p>

Behind Broadcom's booming $30 billion AI booking pipeline and long-term deployment commitments—including a multi-generation TPU agreement, 1.3 gigawatts for OpenAI in 2027, and Meta’s MTIA X rollout through 2028—lies a remarkably short list of buyers.</p>

<section id="valuation-reality">
    <h2>Valuation Reality: Paying Peak Multiples on a Narrow Base</h2>
    <p>Market corrections often create genuine bargains, but pricing context matters immensely. Despite sliding 15.3% over the past three months, Broadcom's stock still trades at roughly 22.4 times sales. That valuation sits very close to its ten-year high of 26.0, placing it comfortably in the top decile of its historical decade range.</p>
