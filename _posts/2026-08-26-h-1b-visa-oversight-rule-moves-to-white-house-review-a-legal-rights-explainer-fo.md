  <section id="what-happened">
    <h2>What Happened: The H-1B Rule Enters OIRA Review</h2>
    <p>The Department of Homeland Security proposal, designated under regulatory identifier <strong>RIN 1615-AD00</strong>, has landed at the Office of Information and Regulatory Affairs (OIRA), an arm of the Office of Management and Budget. Once OIRA concludes its interagency review, the draft proposal will be published for public inspection and commentary.</p>
    
    <p>According to reports from Bloomberg Law, this oversight initiative runs parallel to separate administration discussions regarding steep financial adjustments for employers utilizing the visa program, including potential proposals that would dramatically alter the cost structure for filling jobs under the annual 85,000 H-1B cap. Together, these regulatory movements signal a concerted effort by federal regulators to alter both the compliance burdens and financial realities of high-skilled immigration sponsorships.</p>

At the same time, changes to cap exemption criteria could impact non-profit research institutions and institutions of higher education that have historically enjoyed streamlined hiring paths.</p>

The move to OIRA review is merely a preliminary step in a structured federal rulemaking process governed by the Administrative Procedure Act (APA). This means stakeholders will have formal opportunities to voice concerns, submit empirical data, and challenge ambiguities during the subsequent public comment window.</p>
