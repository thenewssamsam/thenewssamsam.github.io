<section id="what-happened" class="ncg-section">
<h2 class="ncg-h2">What Happened: The  Million Walkaway</h2>
<p>In an era where tech companies routinely buy up large tracts of rural land to power the next generation of artificial intelligence, one family's choice made national headlines. Delsia Bare and her mother, Ida Huddleston, initially agreed to sell their Kentucky property for a combined total of  million. But once they discovered the land would host a sprawling data center to fuel machine learning infrastructure, they pulled out of the deal completely.</p>
<p>Bare's response to the prospective buyers was blunt and unforgettable: <q class="ncg-inline-quote">Kick rocks and don’t come back.</q></p>

<p>According to reports from the <em>Wall Street Journal</em> and the <em>New York Post</em>, the prospective buyer is believed to have ties to Meta, though the tech giant noted it has not finalized any specific local development plans. The land itself has been in the family for two centuries. For Bare and Huddleston, parting with two hundred years of agricultural heritage for a server farm simply crossed an ethical line, with Bare noting she feared being <q class="ncg-inline-quote">held accountable</q> by a higher power if she went through with the sale.</p>

<section id="faith-perspective" class="ncg-section">
<h2 class="ncg-h2">A Faith Perspective: Land, Legacy, and Stewardship</h2>
<p>In our modern economy, everything has a price tag. Land is frequently viewed purely as a commodity—a bundle of square footage waiting to be optimized, zoned, or digitized. Yet a traditional biblical worldview offers a very different paradigm regarding the earth and our responsibility toward it.</p>
<p>Scripture reminds us that human beings are not absolute owners of the creation entrusted to us, but temporary caretakers. The Book of Leviticus establishes a foundational principle regarding the management of land:</p>
<blockquote class="ncg-verse-box">
<p>The land must not be sold permanently, because the land is mine and you reside in my land as foreigners and strangers.</p>
<cite>— Leviticus 25:23 (NIV)</cite>
</blockquote>
<p>While this Old Testament statute specifically governed ancient Israel's economic and tribal inheritance, its theological underlying principle remains clear: creation has intrinsic value anchored in its Creator, not merely its utility to the highest bidder. When Bare expressed a fear of being held accountable by a higher power, she was tapping into an ancient intuition—that what we do with the physical earth matters, and that some inheritances carry a moral weight that transcends bank accounts.</p>

<div class="ncg-shareable-box" role="region" aria-label="Shareable Quote">
<p class="ncg-shareable-text">&#8220;When land becomes just a commodity, we risk trading our deepest historical and moral roots for temporary digital convenience. True stewardship asks what we are leaving behind, not just what we are cashing out for.&#8221;</p>
</div>

<p>A second vital biblical theme here is the danger of unrestrained accumulation. In Luke 12:15, Jesus issues a direct warning:</p>
<blockquote class="ncg-verse-box">
<p>Watch out! Be on your guard against all kinds of greed; life does not consist in an abundance of possessions.</p>
<cite>— Luke 12:15 (NIV)</cite>
</blockquote>
<p>Walking away from  million sounds irrational to a tech market driven by exponential growth. Yet the family's choice testifies to a counter-cultural truth: integrity, family legacy, and a clear conscience cannot be balanced out by a ledger.</p>
</section>

<section id="the-broader-grid" class="ncg-section">
<h2 class="ncg-h2">Why the AI Infrastructure Clash Is Spreading</h2>
<p>This Kentucky skirmish is not an isolated incident. Across the United States, data centers have faced intense pushback from local communities. These massive facilities—numbering nearly 5,000 nationwide—require staggering amounts of electrical energy and water to cool high-performance servers.</p>
<p>Local residents and lawmakers are increasingly pushing to slow or temporarily halt new developments until grid capacities and water supplies can be thoroughly studied. The tension highlights a friction point in modern technological progress: the physical footprint of the digital world often collides directly with the physical realities of the neighborhoods that host it.</p>
</section>

<section id="how-should-christians-respond" class="ncg-section">
<h2 class="ncg-h2">How Should Christians Respond?</h2>
<p>How should believers evaluate rapid technological shifts like the AI boom?</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Practice Intentional Stewardship:</strong> Whether managing a small backyard garden, a family business, or corporate assets, view your resources through the lens of long-term responsibility rather than short-term extraction.</li>
<li><strong>Value People Over Efficiency:</strong> Technological advancement is a neutral tool, but it should never override human dignity, community well-being, or ethical boundaries.</li>
<li><strong>Cultivate Discernment:</strong> Just because an industry offers unprecedented sums of money does not mean participation is morally neutral. Ask what values are being advanced behind the corporate pitch.</li>
</ul>
</section>
