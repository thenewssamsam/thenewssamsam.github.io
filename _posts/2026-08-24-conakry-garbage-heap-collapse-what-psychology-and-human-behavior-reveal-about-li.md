  <h2 id="what-happened" style="font-size: 1.5rem; color: #2d3748; margin-top: 40px; margin-bottom: 15px;">What Happened at the Dar Es Salam Landfill?</h2>
  
  <p style="margin-bottom: 20px;">
    According to reports from The Associated Press and local sources, the catastrophic landslide struck on a Sunday morning. Torrential overnight rains washed over the massive refuse heap at Dar Es Salam, the largest waste dump in Conakry. The sheer weight of the saturated waste overwhelmed the site, causing a structural collapse that buried nearby homes and crushed a three-story building.
  </p>

  <p style="margin-bottom: 20px;">
    At least 30 people lost their lives, and six others sustained serious injuries. Rescue teams, alongside local residents using heavy machinery like excavators, immediately mobilized to search the wreckage for survivors trapped beneath the towering mass of debris. 
  </p>

  <p style="margin-bottom: 20px;">
    Officials noted that the landfill was slated to close precisely on the day of the disaster due to recognized safety hazards. Lt. Col. Baldé Mamadou Bailo, a sanitation officer with the military engineering battalion, pointed out the escalating risks. Furthermore, authorities had previously issued eviction notices to nearby residents. Yet, despite these warnings and memories of a 2017 disaster that killed eight people, many families remained in the high-risk zone. Dienabou Touré, the minister of territorial administration and decentralization, visited the scene to coordinate relief and promised the relocation of remaining families, but for many, the intervention came too late.
  </p>

This normalcy bias deadens our internal alarm systems until a sudden catastrophic shift shatters the illusion.
