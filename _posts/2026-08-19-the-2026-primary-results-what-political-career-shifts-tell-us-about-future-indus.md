    <h3>The Shift Toward Specialized Professional Backgrounds</h3>
    <p>Candidates who lean into their professional expertise—such as Hageman’s focus on natural resources law or Barlow’s ranching background—are finding success by positioning themselves as industry experts rather than just party loyalists. For professionals in any sector, this trend suggests that "domain expertise" is becoming a critical currency in public influence. It is no longer enough to be a generalist; voters are looking for individuals who understand the technical mechanics of the industries they intend to regulate.</p>

    <h3>Volatility and the "Endorsement" Risk</h3>
    <p>The string of losses for candidates tied strictly to high-profile national endorsements highlights a shift in industry "hiring" preferences. Voters are behaving more like discerning board members, evaluating candidates on their specific platform and regional feasibility rather than just their "brand recognition." For those looking at a career in public policy or advocacy, this suggests that building a localized, sustainable network is a more reliable long-term career strategy than hitching one's wagon to a single national figure.</p>
