  </header>

  <section class="ncg-section" id="what-happened">
    <h2>What Happened in Niger State?</h2>
    <p>Last Friday, a coordinated and devastating series of attacks struck four remote villages—Dekara, Gidan Zana, Sabon Gida, and Masaka—in the Borgu area of Niger state, Nigeria. According to local residents and security officials speaking to the BBC, armed men dressed in military-style clothing launched simultaneous assaults on local communities, targeting mosques during prayer times.</p>
    <p>Police spokesman Wasiu Abiodun reported that more than 40 people were killed, though the final death toll remains unconfirmed. Meanwhile, a local youth leader estimated that as many as 500 individuals may have been abducted and marched into nearby forests. The crisis took a surreal and agonizing turn when the kidnappers uploaded a video to social media showing the captives sitting in a remote forest area. While the footage brought profound relief to families who spotted their loved ones alive, it underscored the brazen impunity of the attackers.</p>
    <p>Adding to the horror, local officials reported that retreating gunmen planted improvised explosive devices (IEDs) along escape routes. A teenage boy lost his life when an explosive detonated as he attempted to flee. In response to the massacre and mass abductions, Nigerian President Bola Tinubu issued a stern directive ordering an immediate, aggressive manhunt for the perpetrators, condemning them as "enemies of peace and enemies of humanity."</p>

    <h2>The Escalating Crisis: Bandits, Jihadists, and Regional Insecurity</h2>
    <p>To understand why these attacks occurred, one must look at the broader security landscape of north-western Nigeria. For years, authorities categorized the primary threat in these remote regions as "bandits"—loose criminal networks engaged in cattle rustling, village looting, and mass kidnappings for ransom.</p>
