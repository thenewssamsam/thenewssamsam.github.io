<section id="what-happened">
<h2>What Happened in the Persian Gulf?</h2>
<p>For weeks, headlines across the globe have focused almost exclusively on maritime traffic moving through the Strait of Hormuz. As military tensions flared and naval blockades reshaped maritime trade, analysts, politicians, and everyday consumers watched tanker counts like a barometer for global economic stability. When a memorandum of understanding was signed and tanker traffic showed signs of resuming, many breathed a collective sigh of relief, assuming the worst of the energy crisis had passed.</p>

<p>Yet veteran petroleum geologist and energy consultant Art Berman argues that obsessing over tankers misses the more consequential half of the story. According to recent assessments from both the International Energy Agency (IEA) and the U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA), roughly 5.5 to 8.3 million barrels per day of Persian Gulf oil production remain shut in. While political agreements can theoretically reopen a shipping lane overnight, restarting a shuttered oil field is an entirely different matter.</p>

<section id="upstream-vs-midstream">
<h2>The Upstream Problem: Why Closed Wells Do Not Just Turn Back On</h2>
<p>To understand the true financial stakes of the current crisis, market analysts distinguish between the midstream and upstream segments of the oil industry. The midstream problem involves logistics—moving oil safely across open water through choke points like the Strait of Hormuz. Once security is established, tankers can theoretically move again relatively quickly.</p>

<p>The upstream problem, however, involves the physical reservoirs deep underground. As Berman noted in interviews with industry analysts, shutting in an active oil well is practically a last resort for operators. Maintaining production requires delicate pressure balances between surface equipment and subterranean rock formations. When a well sits idle for extended periods, sediment can shift, water intrusion can occur, and reservoir pressure can drop.</p>
