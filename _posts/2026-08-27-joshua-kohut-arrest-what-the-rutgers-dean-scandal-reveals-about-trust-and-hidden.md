  <div class="ncg-content">
    <p>When a respected academic leader with nearly three decades of service at a major university is suddenly arrested on criminal charges, it sends shockwaves through a community. The recent arrest of Rutgers University dean and professor Joshua Kohut on charges of invasion of privacy has left colleagues, students, and observers grappling with a painful question: How can someone trusted with high authority fall into such profound moral failure?</p>

    <h2 id="what-happened">What Happened in the Joshua Kohut Case?</h2>
    
    <p>According to the New Jersey Attorney General's Office, 51-year-old Joshua Kohut was arrested and charged with fourth-degree invasion of privacy. Prosecutors allege that Kohut used his mobile phone to secretly record "upskirt" videos of a victim's intimate parts without consent. Investigators executed a search warrant at his residence, recovering electronic devices that allegedly contained the unauthorized recordings.</p>

    <p>Kohut has been a fixture at Rutgers University since starting as a doctoral student in physical oceanography in 1997. Over the decades, he climbed the academic ranks, eventually being named dean of research for the School of Environmental and Biological Sciences in 2025. Following his arrest, Rutgers officials swiftly placed him on administrative leave and banned him from campus pending the investigation. If convicted, he faces up to 18 months in prison and a fine of up to $10,000.</p>
