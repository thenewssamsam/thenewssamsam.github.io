<h1 id="top">Florida Primary Results: What Historical Parallels Tell Us About the 2026 Midterms</h1>

<p>Florida’s 2026 primary elections have officially drawn the battle lines for a November showdown that will decide who succeeds outgoing Governor Ron DeSantis and test the state's broader political identity. U.S. Representative Byron Donalds secured the Republican nomination, while former Republican-turned-Democrat David Jolly claimed the Democratic nod. For a state that has evolved dramatically over the past decade, these nominations are more than just a typical news cycle—they reflect a repeating cycle in American politics where parties constantly reinvent themselves to capture shifting voter bases.</p>

<h2 id="what-happened">What Happened in the Florida Primaries?</h2>

<p>Tuesday night's primary results reshaped Florida's upcoming general election ballot. Byron Donalds, a 47-year-old conservative congressman banking on Donald Trump's endorsement, defeated a crowded GOP field that included former Lieutenant Governor Jay Collins. Donalds' victory positions him to make a historic run as he attempts to become Florida's first Black governor, anchoring his platform on tax cuts, property insurance overhauls, and healthcare price transparency.</p>

<p>On the Democratic side, former GOP Representative David Jolly secured the nomination after officially switching parties last year. Jolly's platform embraces a blend of traditional fiscal conservatism and targeted social moderation, consciously distancing himself from culture-war politics while appealing to moderate voters. Meanwhile, in high-profile congressional and Senate races, progressive state Representative Angie Nixon won the Democratic nomination to challenge appointed Republican Senator Ashley Moody, and longtime Democratic Representative Debbie Wasserman Schultz held off primary challengers in a newly redrawn South Florida district.</p>
