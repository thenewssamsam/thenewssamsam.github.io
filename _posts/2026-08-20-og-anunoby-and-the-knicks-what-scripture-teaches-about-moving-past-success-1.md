    <h1>OG Anunoby and the Knicks: What Scripture Teaches About Moving Past Success</h1>
    <p class="ncg-subtitle">When a historic victory ends a 53-year drought, the hardest challenge isn't winning the prize—it's refusing to let yesterday's glory trap tomorrow's potential.</p>
  </header>

  <section class="ncg-intro">
    <p>For most athletes, hitting a game-winning shot to secure a franchise's first title in over half a century is a moment to marinate in indefinitely. But for New York Knicks forward OG Anunoby, the summer of celebration following their epic NBA Finals victory has been less of a victory lap and more of an interruption. Citing his desire to simply get ready for the next season, Anunoby's seemingly indifferent reaction to instant fame offers a surprisingly profound mirror for how we handle our own milestones, achievements, and seasons of transition.</p>
  </section>

  <section class="ncg-section" id="what-happened">
    <h2>What Happened in New York?</h2>
    <p>The New York Knicks are reigning NBA champions, having capped off a grueling season with a miraculous comeback against the San Antonio Spurs in the 2026 NBA Finals. The defining moment belonged to OG Anunoby, whose clutch tip-in during Game 4 cemented his legacy in Madison Square Garden lore and earned his game-winning heroics the playful, tongue-in-cheek moniker of the "Hand of God."</p>
    <p>Yet, amidst a whirlwind summer of parades, television appearances, and brand pop-ups, Anunoby made headlines by stating he was essentially "over" his big moment. Far from showing ingratitude, his comments revealed a pragmatic, levelheaded focus on the horizon. With training camp looming and the target of defending a title firmly on their backs, Anunoby and his veteran-laden teammates understand a fundamental truth of competitive life: yesterday's victory cannot win tomorrow's games.</p>
  </section>

  <section class="ncg-section" id="scripture-perspective">
