    <h2>What Happened: The Proposed EPA Rule Shift</h2>
    <p>As the artificial intelligence boom strains national power grids, the Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) under the Trump administration has proposed a rule that would dismantle public notification requirements for datacenter air pollution. Under the Clean Air Act, projects classified as "minor" air polluters have historically been required to disclose projected emissions to the public via state agencies before permits are issued.</p>

    <p>The newly proposed regulation would make these disclosures voluntary at the discretion of state and local officials. Supporters of the move argue that reducing administrative hurdles will accelerate permit approvals, foster energy dominance, and cut red tape for companies building out essential infrastructure for artificial intelligence. However, public health advocates and environmental lawyers warn that eliminating mandatory public notification allows permits to be issued behind closed doors, leaving local communities in the dark about nitrogen oxides, carbon monoxide, and heavy metals released by backup diesel generators and gas turbines.</p>

Environmental law clinics and local watchdog groups rely heavily on permit data to monitor compliance and, when necessary, hold operators accountable through the courts. Without publicly available disclosure, communities lack the baseline data required to challenge proposed industrial installations.</p>
