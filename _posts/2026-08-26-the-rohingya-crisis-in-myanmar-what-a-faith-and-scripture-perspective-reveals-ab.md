  <p class="ncg-intro">As the world marks another grim anniversary of the 2017 genocidal campaign against the Rohingya in Myanmar, more than one million people remain displaced, trapped in brutal limbo or facing a renewed wave of violence. This ongoing erasure of an entire ethnic and religious community raises a devastating question: What does justice mean when the victims have nowhere left to return?</p>

  <h2 id="what-happened">What Happened in Myanmar and Bangladesh?</h2>
  <p>August 25 marks the painful anniversary of the 2017 military crackdown in Myanmar's Rakhine state that forced more than 700,000 Rohingya—a Muslim-majority ethnic group—to flee across the border into Bangladesh. Today, roughly 1.2 million Rohingya languish in overcrowded, underfunded camps in Cox's Bazar, while international humanitarian aid is being sharply reduced.</p>
  
  <p>Compounding this tragedy, the balance of power in Rakhine state has shifted. The Arakan Army (AA), an ethnic armed group, now controls large portions of the region. However, according to reports from human rights advocates and leaders like Tun Khin of the Burmese Rohingya Organisation UK (BROUK), the persecution has not stopped. The AA has been implicated in forced labor, restricted movement, mass killings, and a deliberate strategy to drive the remaining Rohingya from their ancestral lands. Meanwhile, international legal proceedings—such as universal jurisdiction cases in Argentina and ongoing proceedings at the International Court of Justice (ICJ)—offer glimmers of accountability, but legal victories mean little to families fighting for survival today.</p>

  <h2 id="scripture-perspective">A Biblical Reflection on Justice and Human Dignity</h2>

Scripture has a great deal to say about the plight of the vulnerable, the displaced, and those who suffer under the heavy hand of powerful oppressors.</p>

  <h2 id="the-pattern-of-impunity">The Pattern of Impunity and Human Nature</h2>
  <p>The tragedy in Rakhine state also lays bare a recurring pattern of human behavior: the ease with which one oppressed group, once empowered, can turn around and inflict similar suffering on others. The anti-Rohingya racism that fueled the Myanmar military's atrocities is echoed in the actions of the Arakan Army today. It is a stark reminder of the persistent brokenness of human nature.</p>
