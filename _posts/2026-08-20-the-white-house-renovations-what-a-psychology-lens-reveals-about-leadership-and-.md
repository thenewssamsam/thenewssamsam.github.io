    <h2>The Psychology of the "Builder" Identity</h2>
    <p>Psychologically, the desire to "build" is often linked to a need for tangible evidence of impact. For a former real estate developer, the White House is not just a seat of power; it is a physical asset that requires improvement. When a leader faces complex, abstract problems like a  trillion debt or international warfare, the human brain often experiences "cognitive overload."</p>

  <section id="legacy-and-normalcy-bias">
    <h2>The Trap of Normalcy Bias</h2>
    <p>Critics, including Senator Jon Ossoff, suggest that this focus on aesthetics represents a detachment from the severity of current events. From a behavioral standpoint, this may be a manifestation of <em>normalcy bias</em>—the tendency to act as though things are proceeding as usual even when facing extreme circumstances. By treating the White House like a luxury property, the president maintains a sense of normalcy that may be intended to reassure his base, even as it alienates those focused on the urgent crises of the day.</p>
