<section id="what-happened">
<h2>What Happened: The Megabank M&amp;Incentive</h2>
<p>For years, major banking institutions operated under strict regulatory scrutiny that effectively froze transformative merger and acquisition activity. However, recent policy shifts have dismantled Biden-era restrictions at the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC) and reinstated expedited FDIC merger reviews. This regulatory thaw has created what financial experts call the best environment for banking deals since the 2008 financial crisis.</p>

<p>Yet, while banking giants like JPMorgan Chase and Bank of America are legally barred from large-scale domestic bank deals because they already control more than 10% of the nation's total deposits, two massive players remain eligible: Citigroup and Wells Fargo. Both institutions have spent recent years navigating compliance penalties—Citi through consent orders and Wells Fargo under asset growth restrictions—but have now cleared those hurdles and find themselves eager for strategic growth.</p>
