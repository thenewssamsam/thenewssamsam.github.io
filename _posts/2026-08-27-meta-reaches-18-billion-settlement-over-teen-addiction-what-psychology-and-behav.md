<h1 class="ncg-main-title">Meta Reaches $18 Billion Settlement Over Teen Addiction: What Psychology and Behavior Reveal About Social Media Design</h1>
<p class="ncg-subtitle">When a tech giant pays billions to settle state lawsuits over youth mental health, it forces a hard look at the invisible psychological architecture that keeps young minds glued to glowing screens.</p>
</header>

<section class="ncg-section" id="what-happened">
<h2 id="what-happened-heading">What Happened: A Landmark Legal Agreement in Oakland</h2>
<p>Meta has agreed to pay up to $18 billion and implement sweeping child-safety measures on Facebook and Instagram, bringing an abrupt halt to a high-stakes federal trial in Oakland, California. The agreement, announced on Wednesday, resolves claims brought by nearly every state in the U.S. seeking to hold the tech giant accountable for its role in the youth mental health crisis.</p>
<p>The lawsuit accused Meta of deliberately engineering features designed to hook young users, hiding internal research regarding the platform's psychological toll, and collecting data on children under 13 without parental consent. Under the proposed settlement, Meta will introduce mandatory two-hour daily time limits for minors, disable push notifications during school hours, remove "like" counts, and curb harmful content related to eating disorders and self-harm.</p>
<p>Notably, about 30% of the settlement payout is contingent on rivals TikTok and YouTube adopting similar safety standards. While child-safety advocates and grieving parents welcomed the accountability, critics and former Meta insiders warn that an $18 billion price tag is a fraction of the company's annual revenue and that true safety requires ongoing, rigorous enforcement.</p>
