<section id="what-happened">
<h2>What Happened in Florida's 7th District</h2>
<p>According to Associated Press race calls, third-term incumbent Rep. Cory Mills was defeated in Florida's 7th congressional district Republican primary by challenger Ryan Elijah. Mills entered the race carrying substantial political baggage, including an ongoing House Ethics Committee investigation into allegations of sexual misconduct and campaign finance violations, both of which he has denied.</p>
<p>The political fallout was swift. While President Donald Trump endorsed Mills back in February, he conspicuously omitted him from late social media endorsement rounds. Meanwhile, Florida Governor Ron DeSantis publicly stated he neither endorsed nor supported the incumbent. Voter sentiment split clearly between loyalty to Mills' past legislative delivery of funds and anxiety over electability. With nonpartisan forecasters like the Cook Political Report shifting the district from "solid Republican" to "likely Republican," the financial stakes for both parties escalated overnight.</p>
</section>

<section id="financial-impact">
<h2>The Financial Impact of Campaign Turmoil</h2>
<p>From a financial and economic perspective, a bruising primary election driven by scandal creates massive capital inefficiencies. Congressional campaigns require millions of dollars in donor contributions, advertising investments, and operational overhead. When an incumbent becomes mired in investigations regarding campaign finance violations, every dollar spent defending against ethics inquiries or repairing public trust is a dollar diverted from actual constituent service or policy advocacy.</p>
