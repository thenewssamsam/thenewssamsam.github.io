<section id="news-hook">
<p>As the trial of Lindsay Clancy enters its fourth week, the focus remains locked on the tragic events of January 2023. While the legal debate centers on criminal responsibility versus a psychotic break, the testimony has inadvertently pulled back the curtain on a fragmented mental healthcare system. For families and taxpayers alike, the case raises uncomfortable questions about the economic and structural costs of mismanaged psychiatric care.</p>
</section>

<section id="what-happened">
<h2>The Core of the Dispute</h2>
<p>The facts of the case are not in dispute: Lindsay Clancy strangled her three children before attempting to take her own life. The prosecution argues this was a premeditated act, while the defense contends that Clancy was suffering from severe, untreated postpartum psychosis fueled by an overmedicated and mismanaged psychiatric journey. The trial has highlighted a startling lack of coordination between various providers, revealing a system where critical data—such as calls to suicide hotlines—was not shared between the professionals responsible for Clancy’s care.</p>
</section>

<h3>The Hidden Price of "Siloed" Care</h3>
<p>In the modern medical landscape, fragmentation is a major driver of cost. When providers operate without a unified electronic health record (EHR) or a shared care plan, duplication of services and missed diagnoses become inevitable. For the average family, this means paying for multiple consultations that fail to address the root cause, leading to long-term economic instability and personal tragedy. The testimony in this trial suggests that Clancy's providers were essentially working in the dark, unable to see the full trajectory of her deterioration.</p>
