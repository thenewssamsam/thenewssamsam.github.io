<section id="what-happened">
<h2>What Happened: The E-Commerce Warehouses Burn</h2>
<p>In a sharp escalation of economic targeting, Ukraine launched a wave of drone strikes hitting four more warehouses belonging to Ozon, Russia's retail titan second only to Wildberries. Ozon reported strikes across southern Russia—bringing its total targeted facilities to six within just three days. Logistics hubs in Makhachkala (Dagestan), Enem (Krasnodar), Adygeysk (Adygeya), and Nevinnomyssk (Stavropol) suffered fires and operational halts, compounding earlier strikes in the Samara and Orenburg regions.</p>

<p>These warehouse attacks did not happen in a vacuum. They coincide with broader infrastructure strikes, including a shutdown at the Perm oil refinery—Russia's seventh-largest—after multiple drone attacks paralyzed its primary refining units. For everyday consumers and market watchers, the message is clear: the friction of war is now directly impacting the supply chains of daily commerce.</p>

<h2>Financial Impact: Debt, Shares, and State Interventions</h2>
<p>The financial fallout for corporate Russia has been swift and severe. Following the news of the warehouse fires, Ozon's shares plummeted by nearly 30% on Monday. Financial analysts immediately drew stark contrasts between Russia's retail giants. While privately owned Wildberries enjoys robust backing from the state-owned bank VTB, Ozon’s primary shareholder, AFK Sistema, carries a heavy debt burden that leaves it ill-equipped to provide substantial financial cushion during a crisis.</p>
