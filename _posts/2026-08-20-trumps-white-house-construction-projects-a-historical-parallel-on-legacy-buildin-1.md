    <h2>The Builder-in-Chief: A Historical Echo</h2>
    <p>Throughout American history, the urge to physically mark one’s tenure has been a recurring theme among leaders. President Trump’s focus on the "White House image" echoes the actions of past administrations that sought to leave a tangible imprint on the capital.</p>

    <p>Historically, leaders often turn to architecture to project strength and permanence. For instance, the expansion of the West Wing or the renovation of the East Wing were frequently framed by past presidents as essential modernizations. Yet, there is a distinct difference between functional renovations and the creation of personal monuments. In the past, when presidents engaged in significant alterations, they often sought to align their projects with the symbolic dignity of the office. Trump’s approach, characterized by his background as a real estate developer, shifts the focus toward personal aesthetic and material durability.</p>
