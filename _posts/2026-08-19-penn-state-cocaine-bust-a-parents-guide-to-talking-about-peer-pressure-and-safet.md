  <section id="the-news">
    <h2>What Happened at Penn State?</h2>
    <p>Pennsylvania Attorney General Dave Sunday recently announced the arrest of 14 individuals, including current and former Penn State University students, in connection with an alleged large-scale cocaine distribution ring. Investigators claim that members of two off-campus fraternities—Delta Upsilon and Sigma Chi—were involved in cutting, packaging, and distributing illegal drugs. Perhaps most disturbing is the allegation that some fraternity pledges were required to participate in the drug-packaging process as a form of "indoctrination" or hazing.</p>
    <p>This news has sent shockwaves through the university community and beyond, raising urgent questions for parents about the environment their children are stepping into when they head off to college.</p>
  </section>

  <section id="parenting-perspective">
    <h2>Navigating the Conversation: A Parenting Perspective</h2>
    <p>For parents of college students or high schoolers preparing for the transition, this story is a sobering reminder that "peer pressure" doesn't end at the high school gates. In the college environment, the stakes—and the potential for long-term legal and personal consequences—are significantly higher.</p>

    <h3>Age-Appropriate Communication</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>For High Schoolers:</strong> Focus on the "why" behind the rules. Discuss how social groups, even those that seem prestigious, can sometimes demand conformity that compromises personal values. Role-play "exit strategies" for when they find themselves in an uncomfortable situation.</li>
      <li><strong>For Current College Students:</strong> Shift the tone from parental instruction to peer-level consultation. Ask open-ended questions like, "What are you seeing on campus regarding the social scene?" or "How do you handle it when friends push boundaries you aren't comfortable with?"</li>
    </ul>
