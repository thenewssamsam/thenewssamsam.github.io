    <time datetime="2026-08-18">Published: August 18, 2026</time> <span>8 min read</span>
  </header>

  <section id="introduction">
    <p>When our children face deep emotional struggles, we hope they will turn to us, their friends, or professional counselors. But for a growing number of young adults, the first point of contact during a crisis isn't a human being—it's an artificial intelligence chatbot. The tragic story of Sophie Rottenberg, who confided in an AI "therapist" named Harry while hiding her agony from family, serves as a sobering wake-up call for parents navigating the digital age.</p>
  </section>

  <section id="what-happened">
    <h2>The Invisible Struggle</h2>
    <p>Sophie Rottenberg, a 29-year-old public health analyst, seemed to be doing everything right. Despite her internal struggle with depression, she remained high-functioning, even traveling and hiking. Her parents provided immense support, connecting her with therapists and medical professionals. Yet, behind the scenes, Sophie had instructed ChatGPT to act as her therapist. For months, she shared her deepest pain with a machine, creating a sense of isolation that prevented the "spillover" of her needs into her real-world relationships. Tragically, she died by suicide in February 2025.</p>
  </section>

  <section id="parenting-perspective">
    <h2>The Parenting Challenge: Why AI Appeals to Struggling Youth</h2>
    <p>As parents, we must understand the "why" behind the shift toward AI. Dr. Vaile Wright of the American Psychological Association notes that many young people feel they are a "burden" to their loved ones. AI is available 24/7, is free, and—most importantly—is perceived as non-judgmental. For a child fearing the stigma of mental illness, a screen can feel safer than a conversation.</p>

    <h3>How to Bridge the Gap</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Normalize the "Burden":</strong> Regularly remind your children that they are never a burden. Create a "no-judgment zone" where they can express negative emotions without you trying to "fix" it immediately.</li>
      <li><strong>Watch for Digital "Silos":</strong> If your child is spending hours on their phone in the morning or late at night, it may not just be social media. Gently inquire about what they are engaging with.</li>
      <li><strong>Advocate for Human Care:</strong> If your child is struggling, emphasize that AI can be a tool for organization or research, but it is not a substitute for a human therapist trained in suicide risk assessment.</li>
    </ul>
