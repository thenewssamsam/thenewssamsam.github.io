    <p>President Donald Trump’s presidency rests on a foundational premise: that American power is so absolute that allies and adversaries alike must ultimately submit to his administration's will. Top aide Stephen Miller famously articulated this worldview, telling reporters that "iron laws" dictate a real world "governed by strength, that is governed by force, that is governed by power." But this hardline philosophy is currently facing its most severe stress test yet in simultaneous, deepening showdowns with Iran and Canada.</p>

The stunning parallel places Canada—traditionally one of America's most steadfast allies—in a similar state of economic friction as Iran, one of Washington’s most entrenched adversaries.</p>

    <p>Throughout modern history, comprehensive sanctions and trade embargoes achieve their stated aims only when backed by a unified global coalition. As former IMF chief economist Kenneth Rogoff noted regarding Iran, sanctions require widespread international buy-in to truly suffocate a target economy. When major economic players—such as China regarding Iranian oil—refuse to fall in line, unilateral measures often leak, creating friction with friendly nations while failing to achieve total regime compliance.</p>

    <h3>2. The Cost of Alienating Traditional Allies</h3>
    <p>Historical cycles show that empires and dominant states maintain their standing largely through durable alliances built on shared trust and institutional norms rather than pure transactional coercion. When trade pressures cross into cultural or sovereignty disputes—such as Washington allegedly attempting to restrict Canada's third-party trade deals or touching upon constitutional bilingualism—allies are forced into nationalist self-preservation. Historically, pushing loyal neighbors into economic corners accelerates the diversification of their trade away from the dominant power.</p>

When a superpower signals an "economic onslaught" but hesitates to trigger secondary sanctions out of fear of blowing up the global financial system, adversaries and allies alike take note of those hesitation markers.</p>
