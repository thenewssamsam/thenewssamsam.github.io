  <section id="what-happened">
    <h2>What Happened: The Surge in ICE Arrests and Policy Shifts</h2>
    <p>According to a recent report by Hayes Brown on MS NOW, immigration enforcement has accelerated dramatically following a turbulent start to the year. Federal data indicates that ICE arrested nearly 50,000 people in July, setting a new monthly high since the administration took office. This surge is not driven by a single change, but rather by a synchronized strategy involving traffic stops, local law enforcement partnerships, and a concerted effort to strip legal protections from long-term residents.</p>

    <div class="ncg-quote-box" style="border-left: 4px solid #d4af37; background: #fdfdfd; padding: 15px; margin: 20px 0; border-radius: 4px;">
      <p style="margin: 0; font-style: italic; color: #333;">"The Great Delegalization effort forces individuals who have lived and worked peacefully in communities for years into sudden legal limbo and eventual displacement."</p>
    </div>

    <p>Among the most significant shifts highlighted in the reporting is the revocation of Temporary Protected Status (TPS) for hundreds of thousands of individuals, including roughly 300,000 Haitian nationals. Additionally, the State Department has initiated efforts to scrutinize asylum seekers who entered on tourist or business visas between 2016 and 2026, while proposed rules aim to introduce steep fees for H-1B visas. For families, communities, and businesses across the United States, these compounding changes signal a profound turning point in federal immigration practice.</p>
  </section>

The current policy landscape touches directly on ancient biblical themes of justice, hospitality, and the treatment of the stranger.</p>
