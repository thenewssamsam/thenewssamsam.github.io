    <h3>The Nero Precedent</h3>
    <p>The most famous historical echo is that of Emperor Nero, who oversaw extensive rebuilding of Rome—including his own sprawling Golden House—following the Great Fire. While the city suffered, Nero’s obsession with his architectural legacy became a focal point for his critics, who argued he was detached from the suffering of his people. Like the current criticism directed at the White House, the historical lesson is that when a leader prioritizes physical structures over the immediate welfare of the citizenry, they risk losing the narrative of their own administration.</p>

    <h3>The Versailles Effect</h3>
    <p>Similarly, the expansion of the Palace of Versailles under Louis XIV served to centralize power and project an image of absolute authority. While the opulence was intended to intimidate and impress, it eventually became a symbol of the disconnect between the ruling class and the financial reality of the French people, contributing to long-standing resentment. While the White House renovations are on a vastly different scale, the psychological impact on a public struggling with inflation and fuel costs remains a consistent historical variable.</p>
