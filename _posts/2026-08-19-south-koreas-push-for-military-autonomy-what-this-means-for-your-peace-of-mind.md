  <section id="introduction">
    <p>Following President Trump's recent directive to substantially scale back joint US-South Korean military drills, South Korean President Lee Jae Myung has accelerated calls for the nation to regain independent control over its own wartime military operations. While this is a complex geopolitical development, it also highlights a universal human challenge: how to maintain a sense of calm and stability when the security arrangements we rely on begin to shift.</p>

  <section id="what-happened">
    <h2>The Shift in Defense Dynamics</h2>
    <p>The decision to reduce the "Ulchi Freedom Shield" exercises, which were designed to test South Korea’s readiness to assume wartime operational control (Opcon), has sent ripples through the international community. For decades, the authority to direct South Korean forces in the event of war has rested with the US-led Combined Forces Command. President Lee argues that strengthening South Korea’s own defense capabilities, including the development of nuclear-powered submarines, is essential for a mature, mutually reinforcing alliance.</p>
