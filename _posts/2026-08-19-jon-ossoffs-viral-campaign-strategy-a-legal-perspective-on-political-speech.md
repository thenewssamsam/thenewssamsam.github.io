        <h2>The Legal Boundaries of Political Speech</h2>
        <p>In the United States, political speech enjoys the highest level of protection under the First Amendment. However, these protections are not absolute. When candidates discuss public figures or private individuals, they must navigate the complex waters of defamation law.</p>

        <h3>The Public Figure Doctrine</h3>
        <p>Under the landmark <em>New York Times Co. v. Sullivan</em> decision, public figures—including government officials and those who thrust themselves into the public eye—must prove "actual malice" to successfully sue for defamation. This means they must show that the speaker knew the statement was false or acted with reckless disregard for the truth.</p>
