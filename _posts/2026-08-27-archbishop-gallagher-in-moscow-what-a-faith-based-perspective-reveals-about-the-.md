      <h2 class="vncg-h2" id="what-happened">What Happened in Moscow?</h2>
      <p class="vncg-p">
        In a diplomatic engagement held at the Foreign Ministry’s representative office in Moscow, Archbishop Paul Richard Gallagher met with Russian Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov. The high-level bilateral meeting, which lasted over an hour and was joined by Apostolic Nuncio Archbishop Giovanni d’Aniello, addressed international tensions, humanitarian initiatives, and the ongoing conflict between Russia and Ukraine. Both sides identified areas of convergence and underscored the ongoing importance of diplomatic channels between the Holy See and the Russian Federation.
      </p>
      <p class="vncg-p">
        During the post-meeting press statements, Archbishop Gallagher delivered a direct message on behalf of Pope Leo XIV, reiterating that the ongoing hostilities must come to an immediate halt. Emphasizing that military solutions alone cannot resolve the crisis, the Vatican official called for the establishment of genuine political and diplomatic processes. He urged both nations to demonstrate goodwill, protect vulnerable civilian populations, safeguard critical infrastructure, and respect international humanitarian law regarding prisoners of war and the wounded.
      </p>

      <h2 class="vncg-h2" id="vatican-diplomacy">The Vatican's Approach to Global Conflict</h2>
      <p class="vncg-p">
        The presence of a high-ranking Vatican diplomat in Moscow underscores a unique approach to international relations. Unlike secular political entities driven primarily by economic leverage or geopolitical dominance, the Holy See operates under a moral and spiritual mandate. The core of this diplomatic framework rests on the belief that human dignity remains sacred, regardless of national borders or wartime hostilities. When Archbishop Gallagher states that "this war must end" and that hatred must be deprived of oxygen, he is speaking not merely as a political strategist, but as a moral witness to a suffering world.
      </p>

      <div class="vncg-quote-box">
        "To be clear, this war must end... It is urgent and necessary to end the hostilities and plan future relations so as not to remain prisoners of the wrongs of the past." — Archbishop Paul Richard Gallagher
      </div>

      <p class="vncg-p">
        History shows that protracted conflicts often breed generational bitterness, where retaliatory cycles feed upon themselves until peace becomes almost unimaginable to those living through the daily trauma. The Vatican's insistence on restoring a minimum level of trust through humanitarian cooperation—such as upcoming missions led by figures like Cardinal Zuppi—demonstrates a practical theology of reconciliation. Small humanitarian gestures build the fragile stepping stones necessary for larger political breakthroughs.

      </p>
