<h2>What Happened in the Markets Today</h2>
<p>Gold prices experienced a minor pullback on Tuesday morning, yet they remain tightly anchored near their highest marks since mid-May. Spot gold was recently recorded falling a modest 0.1% to $4,647.05 per ounce, while gold futures ticked up marginally to $4,694.50 per ounce.</p>

<section id="financial-mechanics">
<h2>The Financial Mechanics: Why a Soft Dollar and Buybacks Matter</h2>
<p>To understand why gold is surging, we have to look past the shiny metal itself and examine the plumbing of global finance. Gold is priced globally in U.S. dollars. When the greenback loses ground—as the dollar index has, dropping 0.8% so far this month—foreign currencies suddenly possess greater purchasing power. This inverse relationship makes greenback-priced assets like bullion much more attractive to international buyers.</p>
