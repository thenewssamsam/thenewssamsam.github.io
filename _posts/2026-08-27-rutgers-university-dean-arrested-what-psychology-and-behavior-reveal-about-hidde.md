<section>
    <p>When a prominent academic leader is suddenly led away in handcuffs, the collective reaction is rarely just surprise—it is a jarring dissonance between public prestige and private actions. The recent arrest of a high-ranking Rutgers University official has left students, faculty, and the broader New Brunswick community grappling with difficult questions about trust, power, and the hidden behaviors of individuals we assume are beyond reproach.</p>
    
    <p>News broke that 51-year-old Josh Kohut, serving as the Dean of Research at the School of Environmental and Biological Sciences, was arrested following allegations of invasion of privacy. According to the New Jersey Attorney General's office, Kohut allegedly used his cellular device to record unauthorized "upskirt" videos of a victim while on campus. The swift institutional response—placing Kohut on administrative leave and banning him from campus grounds—highlights the gravity of the charges, but it also opens a much deeper conversation about why individuals in positions of high status engage in clandestine, destructive behaviors.</p>
</section>
