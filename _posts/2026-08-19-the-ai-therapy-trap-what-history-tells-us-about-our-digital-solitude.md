<h1>The AI Therapy Trap: What History Tells Us About Our Digital Solitude</h1>
<time datetime="2026-08-18">Published: August 18, 2026</time> | 8 min read
</header>

<section id="introduction">
<p>In a world of 24/7 connectivity, many are finding themselves more isolated than ever, turning to artificial intelligence as a surrogate for human companionship. The tragic story of Sophie Rottenberg, who sought solace from a ChatGPT-based "therapist" in her final months, highlights a growing crisis. When we replace the messy, demanding, but vital presence of other people with the frictionless convenience of an algorithm, we aren't just using a new tool—we are repeating a historical pattern of withdrawing from the very communities that keep us anchored.</p>
</section>

<section id="what-happened">
<h2>The Reality of Digital Seclusion</h2>
<p>Sophie Rottenberg, a 29-year-old public health analyst, died by suicide in early 2025. Her parents later discovered that she had engaged in an 1,800-page dialogue with a custom-prompted ChatGPT "therapist" named Harry. Despite having a loving family and a professional therapist, Sophie had compartmentalized her agony, sharing her deepest struggles only with the machine. Her story is not an isolated incident; millions are now using AI for mental health support, often bypassing professional care for the perceived safety of a non-judgmental, always-available algorithm.</p>
</section>

<section id="historical-parallel">
<h2>The Illusion of the Mechanical Confessor</h2>
<p>History teaches us that humans have always looked for "outsourced" wisdom when faced with the discomfort of real-world relationships. In the 18th century, the rise of the novel was often criticized for creating "solitary readers" who spent more time in the company of fictional characters than their own neighbors. Later, the introduction of the telephone and then the radio were met with warnings that we were losing the "human touch."</p>

History shows that technology often provides a temporary reprieve from social friction, but it rarely—if ever—replaces the stabilizing influence of genuine, face-to-face community.</p>

<h2>A Note on Human Connection</h2>
<p>This situation brings to mind the wisdom of Proverbs 27:17: "Iron sharpens iron, and one man sharpens another." It serves as a reminder that we were not created to carry our heaviest burdens in the silence of a digital void. There is a profound, God-given dignity in being seen—truly seen—by another person. When we hide our struggles behind a screen, we miss the opportunity for the messy, sharpening process of community that is so vital to our health.</p>

<section id="how-to-respond">
<h2>How Should Readers Respond?</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Audit Your Digital Habits:</strong> If you find yourself turning to an AI for emotional support, ask why. Is it because you fear being a "burden" to others? That fear is exactly what needs to be shared with a trusted human.</li>
<li><strong>Prioritize "Friction":</strong> Seek out interactions that require you to be present and vulnerable—coffee with a friend, a support group, or a long-form conversation that cannot be paused or edited.</li>
<li><strong>Know the Resources:</strong> If you or someone you know is in crisis, reach out to human-led services like the 988 Suicide and Crisis Lifeline. AI is a tool, not a substitute for a trained professional.</li>
</ul>
</section>

<section id="closing-takeaway">
<h2>One Last Thought</h2>
<p>The tragedy of Sophie Rottenberg is a sobering reminder that while technology can mimic the language of empathy, it cannot provide the presence of a soul. We must be wary of the comfort that costs us our connection to reality. If you are struggling, please choose the discomfort of speaking to a human over the silence of the machine. Your life is worth the friction of being known.</p>
