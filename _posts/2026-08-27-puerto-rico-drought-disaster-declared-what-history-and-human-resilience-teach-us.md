  <section id="what-happened">
    <h2>What Happened: The Anatomy of the Puerto Rico Drought</h2>
    <p>The U.S. Department of Agriculture's disaster declaration covers 26 of Puerto Rico's 78 municipalities, granting eligible farmers and agricultural producers access to emergency loans. The crisis stems from a potent mix of natural factors and long-standing infrastructural challenges. Meteorologists point to a natural warming cycle, El Niño, as a primary driver of the persistent dry conditions affecting both Puerto Rico and the neighboring U.S. Virgin Islands.</p>
    
    <p>The human toll on the ground is immediate and severe. Farmers cultivating avocados, plantains, lemons, and cassava report that crops are shriveling and fruits are dropping prematurely in the unrelenting heat. Irrigation canals, such as the one in Lajas that once supplied water three times a week, have been restricted to a single weekly supply. Beyond crop production, livestock breeders are struggling to feed animals, leading some farmers to temporarily abandon agriculture for alternative work, such as mechanics, just to make ends meet.</p>
