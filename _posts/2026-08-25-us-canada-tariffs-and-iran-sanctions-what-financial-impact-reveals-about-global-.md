  <section id="what-happened">
    <h2>What Happened: The Collapse of Trade Talks and Escalating Sanctions</h2>
    <p>Over the weekend, economic tensions between Washington and Ottawa reached a boiling point. Trade negotiations between the neighboring nations completely collapsed, leading the U.S. government to levy immediate 50% tariffs on select Canadian goods. Both governments pointed fingers, blaming each other for the breakdown in dialogue.</p>
    <p>Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney swiftly announced that Ottawa would retaliate dollar-for-dollar, with reciprocal duties on U.S. goods scheduled to take effect on September 8. President Donald Trump fired back, asserting that Canada desired the financial advantages of becoming an American state without accepting the political responsibilities.</p>
    <p>Simultaneously, the U.S. Treasury Department is turning its economic leverage toward the Middle East. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent announced a sweeping sanctions package targeting Iran, describing the measures as "the single greatest financial offensive ever marshalled against an adversary." By cutting off international business ties with Tehran, the administration aims to cripple Iran's economy as an alternative to major military intervention. However, Iranian officials have condemned the move as an overreach of extraterritorial sovereignty.</p>

    <h2>The Financial Mechanics: Who Pays for Tariffs and Rising Yields?</h2>
    <p>From a purely financial perspective, tariffs are frequently misunderstood by the general public. While political rhetoric often frames tariffs as a tax paid by foreign nations or companies, the underlying economic mechanics tell a different story. Importers, businesses, and ultimately everyday retail consumers shoulder the financial burden.</p>

To protect profit margins, companies must either absorb the loss or pass those higher expenses down to the consumer. For households already battling elevated living costs, retaliatory duties and import restrictions translate directly into higher prices at the grocery store, the gas pump, and the hardware store.</p>
