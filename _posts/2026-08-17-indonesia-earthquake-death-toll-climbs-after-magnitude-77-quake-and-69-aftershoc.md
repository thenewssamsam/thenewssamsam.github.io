    <h1>Indonesia Earthquake Death Toll Climbs After Magnitude 7.7 Quake and 6.9 Aftershock Hit Flores Region</h1>
    <p class="na-subtitle">At least 53 people are dead and hundreds have evacuated after powerful earthquakes struck Indonesia's East Nusa Tenggara province, triggering brief tsunami concerns and widespread structural damage.</p>
  </header>

  <div class="na-quick-take">
    <h3>Quick Take</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>What happened:</strong> A magnitude 7.7 earthquake followed by a magnitude 6.9 aftershock struck off the coast of eastern Indonesia, killing at least 53 people.</li>
      <li><strong>The impact:</strong> Over 300 houses and 100 public facilities were damaged or destroyed, with thousands evacuating over initial tsunami alerts.</li>
      <li><strong>Current status:</strong> Tsunami warnings have since expired, while military and emergency crews conduct search, rescue, and damage assessment operations.</li>
    </ul>
  </div>

  <section id="what-happened">
    <h2>What Happened in Indonesia</h2>
    <p>At least 53 people have died and dozens of others sustained injuries after a powerful magnitude 7.7 earthquake struck off the coast of eastern Indonesia, followed hours later by a magnitude 6.9 aftershock [Confirmed — U.S. Geological Survey / BNPB].</p>
    
    <p>The initial tremor struck shortly before 5 a.m. local time, hitting approximately 60 kilometers north-northwest of Ende in Indonesia's Flores region at a shallow depth of about 10 kilometers [Confirmed — USGS]. The shallow depth significantly amplified the intensity of the shaking, which was strongly felt across the entire province of East Nusa Tenggara [Confirmed — BNPB].</p>

    <p>According to Indonesia's National Disaster Management Agency (BNPB), the consecutive seismic events brought down numerous residential buildings and triggered widespread panic. Emergency crews, including members of the Indonesian National Armed Forces, were quickly deployed to conduct evacuations and assess structural damage [Confirmed — BNPB].</p>
  </section>

  <section id="damage-and-evacuations">
    <h2>Damage and Evacuations Across the Flores Region</h2>
    <p>The destructive force of the earthquakes left a heavy toll on local infrastructure. More than 300 houses were either completely destroyed or heavily damaged across the affected zone [Confirmed — BNPB].</p>

    <p>Public infrastructure also suffered extensive losses. More than 100 public facilities sustained structural damage, encompassing:</p>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>87 educational facilities</strong>, disrupting local schooling.</li>
      <li><strong>18 health facilities</strong>, straining regional medical response capabilities.</li>
      <li><strong>6 administrative offices</strong>.</li>
      <li><strong>5 places of worship</strong>.</li>
    </ul>

    <p>The tremor's marine epicenter initially prompted severe concerns regarding a potential sea surge. Indonesia's Meteorology, Climatology and Geophysics Agency issued urgent alerts instructing residents to stay away from beaches and move to higher ground [Confirmed — Associated Press]. In the Nagekeo Regency on Flores island alone, roughly 2,000 residents executed self-evacuations as a precautionary measure before tsunami warnings eventually expired [Confirmed — BNPB / AP].</p>
  </section>

  <blockquote class="na-quote-box">
    <p>"The shallow 10-kilometer depth of the magnitude 7.7 mainshock maximized surface shaking energy, resulting in severe local structural failures and triggering widespread precautionary self-evacuations across Flores island."</p>
  </blockquote>

  <section id="seismic-context">
    <h2>Seismic Context: Why Indonesia Experiences Frequent Earthquakes</h2>
    <p>[Analysis] Indonesia sits directly atop the Pacific Ring of Fire, one of the most geologically active and seismically volatile zones on the planet. The region experiences frequent tectonic collisions between the Eurasian, Indo-Australian, Pacific, and Philippine Sea plates.</p>

    <p>Shallow megathrust earthquakes and intraplate faults frequently generate high-consequence tremors in this archipelago. Because many structures in rural and semi-urban Indonesian islands lack robust earthquake-resistant engineering, even moderately deep or offshore events can result in significant casualties and structural collapses.</p>
  </section>

  <section id="what-happens-next">
    <h2>What Happens Next</h2>
    <p>[Analysis] As search and rescue operations progress over the coming days, several developments are expected:</p>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Aftershock monitoring:</strong> The U.S. Geological Survey and local agencies note that a series of strong aftershocks—including a 6.1 and a 5.9 tremor near Ende—will likely continue, posing ongoing risks to already compromised structures.</li>
      <li><strong>Humanitarian relief:</strong> BNPB and military units will scale up temporary shelter, clean water, and medical supply distribution for the hundreds of displaced residents.</li>
      <li><strong>Infrastructure rehabilitation:</strong> Rebuilding damaged schools and health clinics will likely require extended federal assistance to restore baseline community services in East Nusa Tenggara.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
