But while digital-age data fabrication feels distinctly modern, the temptation to manufacture public opinion is an ancient political tactic.</p>

  <h2 id="what-happened">What Happened: The Median Strategies Hoax</h2>
  <p>Median Strategies shocked political observers by announcing it was closing its doors after confessing that its widely circulated polls were a hoax. The firm had previously released surveys indicating that Los Angeles Mayor Karen Bass and Wisconsin Democratic gubernatorial candidate Francesca Hong held massive leads over their rivals. These fabricated numbers quickly entered the mainstream information ecosystem, being picked up by social media, local TV newscasts, and online reporting outlets before voters headed to the polls.</p>
  
  <p>In reality, the actual election outcomes diverged significantly from the firm's manufactured numbers. In Los Angeles, Mayor Bass narrowly edged Nithya Raman by five points rather than the claimed twelve-point spread. In Wisconsin, David Crowley defeated Francesca Hong in a razor-thin margin, directly contradicting the twenty-point lead Median Strategies had publicized. The company stated that its intent was to study how unverified polling information spreads, yet the fallout has left voters and analysts questioning the reliability of modern data streams.</p>
