<section id="what-happened" class="ncg-section">
  <h2>What Happened: The Collision of Politics and Policy</h2>
  <p>Xavier Becerra spent decades championing the expansion of health coverage. From his time as a U.S. congressman helping pass the Affordable Care Act in 2010, to his tenure as California attorney general defending it, and finally serving as President Biden's Health and Human Services Secretary, Becerra's political identity is deeply intertwined with government-backed healthcare.</p>
  
  <p>Now, as the frontrunner running to succeed term-limited Gov. Gavin Newsom in California, Becerra faces a stark irony. Polling suggests he is cruising toward victory in November against Republican challenger Steve Hilton. Yet if he wins, he will inherit what health economists describe as the steepest potential decline in health insurance coverage in a generation.</p>

  <p>According to a May analysis by the UC Berkeley Labor Center, federal cuts and recently enacted state and federal policy rollbacks could nearly double the number of uninsured Californians under 65 by 2030—surging from 2.4 million to 4.6 million people. Federal funds account for roughly one-third of California's overall state budget and over 60 percent of spending for Medi-Cal, the state's Medicaid program. With the Trump administration and a GOP-led Congress shrinking the federal safety net—including roughly $900 billion in Medicaid spending cuts over a decade and the expiration of enhanced Obamacare tax credits—California's progressive healthcare model is staring down a massive fiscal cliff.</p>
