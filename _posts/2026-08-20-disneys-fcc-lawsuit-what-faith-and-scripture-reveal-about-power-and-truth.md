  <section class="ncg-section" id="what-happened">
    <h2>What Happened: The Clash Over ABC Licenses</h2>
    <p>In a high-stakes legal confrontation that bridges corporate media and federal regulation, Disney has filed a lawsuit in federal court against the Federal Communications Commission (FCC) and its leadership. The media conglomerate alleges that the Trump administration has waged a targeted, "retaliatory campaign" against its television network, ABC.</p>
    
    <p>At the heart of the dispute is an unusual early-renewal review of eight station licenses owned directly by Disney. While FCC license renewals typically occur quietly every eight years and are routinely approved, FCC Chairman Brendan Carr ordered ABC to submit early paperwork following clashes over network programming, including late-night comedy sketches and political commentary on daytime talk shows like <em>The View</em>. The administration has cited concerns regarding diversity, equity, and inclusion (DEI) policies and equal-time rules, while critics view these proceedings as political pressure aimed at chilling speech.</p>

    <p>Disney’s lawsuit asks the court for a temporary restraining order and preliminary injunction to halt the license challenge, arguing that the federal government is unconstitutionally punishing a broadcaster for its editorial content. As this public tug-of-war unfolds, it invites observers to look past the political headlines and examine the deeper ethical principles at stake.</p>
