  <section id="introduction">
    <p>In the quiet corners of Ohio, a massive industrial transformation is meeting a wall of grassroots resistance. As AI data centers proliferate across the state, they have become an unexpected "anchor" in the Senate race between Republican Senator Jon Husted and Democratic challenger Sherrod Brown. For voters, these facilities represent noise, massive energy consumption, and landscape disruption; for politicians, they have become a high-stakes liability that threatens to shift the balance of power in Washington.</p>
  </section>

  <section id="the-backlash">
    <h2>The Political Cost of Infrastructure</h2>
    <p>Recent polling shows Husted trailing Brown by eight percentage points, a gap the National Republican Senatorial Committee (NRSC) explicitly attributes to the data center controversy. The NRSC’s leaked memo suggests that Brown is effectively leveraging local frustrations, framing the tech giants behind these projects as disconnected from the needs of the average resident.</p>

    <h2>The AI Industry's Growing Pains</h2>
    <p>From a career and industry perspective, this backlash highlights a critical disconnect. The AI sector often views data centers purely through the lens of capacity, power, and technological necessity. However, the labor and economic arguments—such as construction jobs and tax revenue—are often insufficient to placate communities dealing with the immediate externalities of these facilities.</p>
