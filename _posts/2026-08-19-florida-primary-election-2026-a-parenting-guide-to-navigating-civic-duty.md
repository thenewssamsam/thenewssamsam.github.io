    <h2>What Happened Today?</h2>
    <p>Today, August 18, 2026, Florida voters are heading to the polls for the state’s primary election. From 7 a.m. to 7 p.m., residents are casting ballots for critical races, including U.S. Senate, U.S. House of Representatives, and various state-level positions. These results will determine the final candidates for the general election this November.</p>
  </section>

  <section id="parenting-perspective">
    <h2>A Parenting Perspective: Turning News into Lessons</h2>
    <p>Election days can feel chaotic, but they offer a unique window into the mechanics of our society. For parents, the Florida primary election is not just a news event—it is an opportunity to demystify the democratic process for our children.</p>

    <h3>Engaging Different Age Groups</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>For Younger Children:</strong> Focus on the "helper" aspect. Explain that voting is how communities make decisions together, much like how a family decides on a weekend activity. Keep it simple: "We are choosing the people who help make the rules for our town."</li>
      <li><strong>For Teens:</strong> This is the time for deeper discussion. Look at the specific races—from the U.S. Senate to local school board seats—and talk about how these roles affect their daily lives, such as school funding or environmental policy.</li>
    </ul>

  <section id="how-to-respond">
    <h2>How Should Parents Respond?</h2>
    <p>Rather than focusing solely on the "winners" and "losers," focus on the process. Take the time to explain to your children that every vote represents a person with their own hopes and concerns. If the results don't go the way you hoped, use it as a lesson in resilience and the importance of continued civic involvement.</p>
