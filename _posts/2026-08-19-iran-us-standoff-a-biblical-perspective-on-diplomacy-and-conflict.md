    <p>The breakdown of the 60-day memorandum of understanding highlights the fragility of international diplomacy. Washington continues to demand unrestricted navigation through the Strait of Hormuz—a vital artery for global energy—while Tehran insists on retaining authority over the waterway as a strategic lever. With neither side willing to yield on core demands, the reality of the situation is one of increasing exhaustion and mutual suspicion.</p>

    <h2>The Search for True Peace</h2>
    <p>In moments of geopolitical friction, it is easy to become consumed by the rhetoric of victory and surrender. Yet, the biblical narrative often invites us to look past the immediate horizon of human power plays. When we read about nations clashing, we are reminded of the inherent brokenness of a world apart from divine order. James 4:1-2 asks a piercing question: <em>"What causes quarrels and what causes fights among you? Is it not this, that your passions are at war within you?"</em> This insight invites us to consider that the conflicts between nations are frequently reflections of deep-seated desires for control, security, and supremacy that exist within the human heart.</p>
