Consequently, the administration is now focusing on "Operation Economic Fury," an attempt to sever Iran's remaining financial lifelines. The threat extends to any nation, business, or financial institution providing support to Tehran, signaling a potential clash with major global powers like China.</p>

    <h2>A Historical Parallel: When Economic Pressure Meets Reality</h2>
    <p>History offers a complex view of total economic blockades. The strategy of using trade isolation to force political capitulation is not new, and it rarely produces the swift results proponents often expect. Looking back at the Continental System of the early 19th century or the mid-20th-century embargoes, we see a recurring pattern: when a nation is pushed to the brink of economic survival, it often develops "shadow" networks to bypass sanctions.</p>

    <h3>The Limit of Sanctions</h3>
    <p>Just as in the past, Iran has already begun utilizing sophisticated tanker networks to move oil despite existing restrictions. History teaches us that the effectiveness of sanctions is inversely proportional to the number of alternative markets available. If China—Iran's largest trading partner—chooses to maintain its current commercial ties, the "crushing" nature of the operation may be significantly diluted, much like the failure of the 1807 Embargo Act in the United States, which hurt domestic exporters more than the intended foreign targets.</p>
