        <h3>For Incoming Freshmen</h3>
        <p>Focus on the concept of "situational awareness." Encourage your child to identify their "exit strategy" before they go out. If a situation feels off, they need to know they can leave without judgment. Remind them that true friends will never demand they compromise their health or safety to "prove" themselves.</p>

        <h2>A Moment of Reflection</h2>
        <p>The pressures students face often stem from a deep, human desire to belong—to be accepted by a group. Yet, Proverbs 13:20 reminds us: <em>"He who walks with wise men will be wise, but the companion of fools will be destroyed."</em> This passage invites us to consider the gravity of the company our children keep. As parents, we can pray for our students to find communities that build them up rather than tear them down, and for the wisdom to discern between true fellowship and destructive conformity.</p>

    <section id="how-to-respond">
        <h2>Practical Steps for Families</h2>
        <ul>
            <li><strong>Define Boundaries:</strong> Discuss specific scenarios. What would they do if they were at a party and saw drugs being used? What would they do if someone was pressured to drink?</li>
            <li><strong>Review Campus Resources:</strong> Familiarize yourselves with the university’s anonymous reporting lines for hazing.</li>
            <li><strong>Keep the Door Open:</strong> Ensure your student knows that if they get into a situation that goes wrong, they can call you—no matter the time—and you will focus on their safety first, rather than reprimand.</li>
        </ul>
    </section>

    <section id="closing-takeaway">
        <h2>One Last Thought</h2>
        <p>College is a time of immense growth, but that growth requires a foundation of safety. By keeping the lines of communication open and focusing on values rather than just rules, we empower our students to make decisions that honor their health and future. Let’s encourage them to seek out groups that add value to their lives, not those that demand their compromise.</p>
