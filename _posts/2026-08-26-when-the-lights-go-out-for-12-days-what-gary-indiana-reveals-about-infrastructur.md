<p>When severe storms swept across northern Indiana on August 11, 2026, they knocked out power for hundreds of thousands of residents. But while most communities saw their lights return within days, residents in the majority-Black city of Gary endured nearly two weeks of suffocating darkness, with some households waiting twelve agonizing days for power to be restored. The prolonged blackout laid bare a harsh reality: when natural disasters strike, the duration of the suffering often depends less on the wind speeds and more on the zip code.</p>

<h2 id="what-happened">What Happened in Gary?</h2>
<p>The storms that hit Gary on August 11 were part of a wider weather front that left a trail of downed trees, shattered power poles, and tangled lines across the region. According to Northern Indiana Public Service Co. (NIPSCO), more than 370,000 customers initially lost electricity. While the utility worked rapidly to restore service to the vast majority of the grid, progress lagged in specific pockets, most notably in Gary.</p>

<p>By day twelve, Mayor Eddie Melton called the pace of restoration "unacceptable," pointing out that families had spent nearly two weeks without refrigeration, air conditioning, or reliable lighting in the sweltering August heat. Meanwhile, neighboring Porter County residents filed a lawsuit accusing NIPSCO of negligence regarding vegetation management around power lines, prompting Indiana Governor Mike Braun to direct the state consumer advocate to petition for a formal investigation into the utility's preparedness and infrastructure maintenance.</p>
