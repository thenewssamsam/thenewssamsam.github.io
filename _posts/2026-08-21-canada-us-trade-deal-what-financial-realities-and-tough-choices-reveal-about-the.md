    <h2>What Happened: The 15% Auto Tariff Compromise</h2>
    <p>Following intense negotiations in Washington, Prime Minister Mark Carney's government faces a ticking clock to finalize a trade agreement with the U.S. administration. While U.S. President Donald Trump temporarily postponed looming 50 per cent levies on $20-billion worth of Canadian goods, the prospective deal brings steep compromises. Sources indicate the proposed pact would lower U.S. tariffs on Canadian autos from an punitive 25 per cent down to 15 per cent.</p>

    <h2>The Financial Mechanics: Who Really Pays the Tariffs?</h2>
    <p>From a purely financial perspective, tariffs are rarely paid directly by the exporting nation; instead, they function as a tax absorbed by domestic importers, manufacturers, and ultimately, everyday consumers. When the cost of cross-border auto parts rises due to a 15 per cent tariff barrier, automotive assembly plants on both sides of the border face increased input costs.</p>
