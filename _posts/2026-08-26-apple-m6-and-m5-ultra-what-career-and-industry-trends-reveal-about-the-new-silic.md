  <section id="what-happened">
    <h2>What Happened: Apple Unveils M6 and M5 Ultra</h2>
    <p>On August 25, 2026, Apple made a major hardware announcement, introducing the <strong>M6</strong> chip—its very first 2-nanometer processor—alongside the powerhouse <strong>M5 Ultra</strong>. Debuting in the latest Mac mini and Mac Studio models, these processors represent a significant generational leap in both raw performance and on-device artificial intelligence compute capabilities.</p>
    <p>The M6 features a larger 12-core CPU complex, a 12-core GPU equipped with Neural Accelerators, a Dual 16-core Neural Engine, and up to 170GB/s of unified memory bandwidth. Meanwhile, the M5 Ultra leverages next-generation UltraFusion technology to create a quad-die architecture boasting up to a 36-core CPU, an 80-core GPU, and a staggering 1.2TB/s of unified memory bandwidth with support for up to 512GB of unified memory. For developers, video editors, and data scientists, these specs point toward an era where massive local workloads no longer require cloud server offloading.</p>
  </section>
