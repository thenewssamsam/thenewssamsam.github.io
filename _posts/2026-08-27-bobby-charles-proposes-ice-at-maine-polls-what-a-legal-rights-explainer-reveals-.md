<h2>What Happened: The Audio Recording</h2>
<p>When election season rolls around, debates over security, access, and voter verification invariably take center stage. But a recent report from the <em>Bangor Daily News</em> brought a remarkably sharp edge to the 2026 Maine gubernatorial race. According to leaked audio obtained from an appearance before young Republicans in Arundel, Republican candidate Bobby Charles outlined an unorthodox proposal: he plans to contact Trump administration border czar Tom Homan three to four weeks before the election to request Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) and U.S. Marshals at Maine voting booths.</p>

<p>"I'm going to make an appeal for ICE and U.S. Marshals to be at our voting booths," Charles was reportedly heard saying. "Do you think the illegals who are being paid money are going to like that?"</p>

However, election officials across Maine and federal jurisdictions emphasize that there is no credible evidence showing widespread noncitizen voting or organized payment schemes to encourage illegal ballots.</p>

<p>Maine law requires individuals to be U.S. citizens, residents of Maine, and at least 18 years old to register and vote.
