<h2>What Happened? The New Hormuz Transit Corridor</h2>
<p>Global energy markets received a complex jolt on Tuesday when a top Iranian official announced that Tehran and Oman had reached an agreement on a new temporary shipping route through the strategic Strait of Hormuz. According to Kazem Gharibabadi, Iran's deputy foreign minister for legal and international affairs, the proposed transit corridor would be seven miles (11.3km) wide, with parts of both the entry and exit paths passing through Iranian territorial waters.</p>

<p>However, the announcement comes with a heavy caveat: despite the bilateral pact with Oman, Iran insists that the vital waterway will not fully reopen until the United States fulfills its commitments under an interim peace agreement signed back in June. The Strait of Hormuz has largely been shut down since the US-Israel conflict with Tehran began in February, severing a chokepoint that previously handled roughly one-fifth of global oil and liquefied natural gas (LNG) shipments.</p>
