  <section id="what-happened">
    <h2>What Happened: The Rollout and the Retraction</h2>
    <p>When the Mamdani administration first issued "you may be subject to" letters to roughly 17,000 property owners across New York City, it touched off immediate panic and frustration. The tax targets second homes valued over $5 million, along with co-ops and condos valued over $1 million. However, administrative friction surfaced almost immediately. Property owners who insist they are full-time residents suddenly found themselves forced to navigate a time-consuming challenge process to prove their residency.</p>
    
    <p>Facing a lawsuit led by attorney Randy Mastro on behalf of impacted homeowners, the administration has now reduced the pool of potential taxpayers to 10,800 and pushed the challenge deadline back to October 6. Mayor Mamdani defended the initial blunder by pointing to a lack of up-to-date state tax records at the time the letters were dispatched. Critics, meanwhile, argue that administrative overreach and rushed implementation created unnecessary financial anxiety for thousands of residents.</p>
  </section>
