  <section id="what-happened">
    <h2>What Happened: SLO County Joins the OCS Program</h2>
    <p>At its August 18 meeting, the San Luis Obispo County Board of Supervisors voted 3-2 to approve a $25,000 financial contribution to Santa Cruz County, which is leading a renewed statewide effort to counter federal oil leasing proposals. The funds secure SLO County’s participation in the Local Government Outer Continental Shelf (OCS) Coordination Program.</p>

    <p>This coalition originally formed in 1980 in response to Reagan-era plans for expanded energy leasing. It successfully coordinated local resistance until offshore leasing halted and the Monterey Bay National Marine Sanctuary was established, leading to the program's disbandment in 1994. Now, Santa Cruz County has revived the initiative under 3rd District Supervisor Justin Cummings to push back against the U.S. Department of the Interior’s 11th National Outer Continental Shelf Program.</p>

    <p>The Bureau of Ocean Energy Management (BOEM) draft leasing program proposes up to six offshore lease sales. Under the current schedule, waters off Northern California could open by 2029, the Central Coast from 2027 to 2029, and Southern California from 2027 to 2030. In response, SLO County joined Monterey, Marin, San Mateo, and Sonoma counties, along with the cities of Monterey and Santa Cruz, bringing total coalition contributions to $192,000.</p>

  <section id="blue-wall">
    <h2>Rebuilding California's Blue Wall</h2>
    <p>The historical success of California's anti-drilling movement in the 1980s and 1990s relied heavily on what became known as "California’s Blue Wall"—a decentralized network of local land-use ordinances passed by coastal counties and voters. These rules restricted or entirely prohibited the onshore support facilities (such as pipelines, processing terminals, and supply depots) without which offshore drilling rigs cannot economically operate.</p>
