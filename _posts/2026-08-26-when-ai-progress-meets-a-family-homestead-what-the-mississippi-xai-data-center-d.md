<p>For generations, a quiet two-acre plot of land sitting right along the Mississippi-Tennessee state line was more than just real estate for Krystal Polk and her family. It was a hard-won sanctuary. Here, they raised chickens and pigs, harvested fresh onions and herbs, and traded homegrown peaches for pears in a simple, self-reliant rhythm of life. But that generational endurance finally broke under an unrelenting industrial hum. The sudden arrival of a massive power plant built to run Elon Musk's artificial intelligence data centers in the Memphis area ultimately drove the family from their longtime home, transforming a generational refuge into a largely abandoned site overshadowed by mechanical noise.</p>

<p>This situation in Southaven, Mississippi, brings into sharp relief a mounting tension in the modern technological landscape: the massive physical footprint of artificial intelligence. While tech giants race to secure power and compute capacity to feed soaring AI models, rural and residential communities are increasingly left to grapple with the immediate environmental and personal fallout. This piece examines the unfolding situation through a local and community lens, exploring what happens when hyper-modern technological expansion collides with deep-rooted local history.</p>

<h2 id="what-happened">What Happened in Southaven, Mississippi?</h2>

<p>According to reports from CBS News and local coverage, Krystal Polk's family recently felt forced to abandon their two-acre homestead after decades of weathering adversity. The property, located near the Mississippi-Tennessee state line, held profound historical significance. Polk noted that her ancestors acquired the land during an era when people of color faced severe restrictions on property ownership. When her parents first moved onto the homestead decades ago, they even encountered explicit racial hostility, finding intimidating graffiti scrawled at the bottom of the hill.</p>
