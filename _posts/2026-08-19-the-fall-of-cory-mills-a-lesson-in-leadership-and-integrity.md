  <section id="introduction">
    <p>In a significant shift for Florida’s 7th Congressional District, incumbent Republican Rep. Cory Mills has been defeated in his primary election. As voters head to the polls, the outcome serves as a stark reminder that public trust is not a permanent entitlement but a fragile commodity earned through consistent character. The loss, coming amidst serious allegations of misconduct, invites us to reflect on the biblical standard for those who hold positions of influence.</p>
  </section>

  <section id="the-news">
    <h2 style="color: #2c3e50;">A Shifting Political Landscape</h2>
    <p>The Associated Press confirmed that former local television news anchor Ryan Elijah secured the Republican nomination, defeating Mills by a margin of 12 percentage points. The race, which had been considered a safe seat for the GOP, saw its status downgraded to "likely Republican" by analysts as concerns over Mills' "baggage"—including House Ethics Committee investigations—grew among the electorate.</p>

  <section id="practical-response">
    <h2 style="color: #2c3e50;">How Should We Respond?</h2>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Prioritize Character:</strong> When evaluating candidates, look beyond the party label. Seek evidence of consistency between their public promises and private conduct.</li>
      <li><strong>Practice Discernment:</strong> Be wary of "baggage" that could compromise the ability to govern effectively. A candidate’s personal life is not irrelevant to their public duty.</li>
      <li><strong>Seek Accountability:</strong> Support systems that demand transparency in government, whether it is through ethics committees or local investigative journalism.</li>
