    <section id="what-happened" style="margin-bottom: 32px;">
        <h2 style="font-size: 1.5rem; color: #1a202c; border-bottom: 2px solid #edf2f7; padding-bottom: 8px; margin-bottom: 16px;">What Happened in the Oklahoma Runoff?</h2>
        <p>
            Politics often defies expectations, but few observers predicted the outcome of the recent Democratic Senate runoff in Oklahoma. N'Kiyla Jasmine Thomas, a nurse with zero prior experience running for public office, defeated lawyer and nonprofit executive Jim Priest to secure the Democratic nomination. Having previously captured roughly 45 percent of the vote in a crowded five-person primary, Thomas capitalized on a wave of anti-establishment sentiment.
        </p>
        <p>
            Yet, the general election math in Oklahoma presents an entirely different stratosphere of difficulty. Voters in the state haven’t sent a Democrat to the U.S. Senate in over thirty years, and President Donald Trump carried the state by nearly 35 points in 2024. Her general election opponent, Republican Rep. Kevin Hern, boasts an imposing war chest peaking near $10 million, contrasted sharply by Thomas's modest fundraising total of roughly $60,000. 
        </p>
        <p>
            Rather than tacking to the center to appeal to conservative or moderate Oklahoma voters, Thomas leaned aggressively into her convictions. During an interview with NPR affiliate KOSU, she famously declared that she would work diligently within her first 100 days to "impeach, remove and convict" Donald Trump, adding metaphorically that voters would have to pry her pen out of her "cold, dead hands." It is a high-stakes gamble that breaks every conventional rule of political survival in a deeply red state.
        </p>

        <p>
            From a behavioral perspective, Thomas's rise and uncompromising messaging highlight several classic psychological mechanisms at play in contemporary elections. When a candidate enters the political arena without a traditional background, voters often project their own frustrations onto that blank canvas. This phenomenon is closely tied to <strong>cognitive dissonance</strong> and the desperate human desire for authenticity in public life.
        </p>
