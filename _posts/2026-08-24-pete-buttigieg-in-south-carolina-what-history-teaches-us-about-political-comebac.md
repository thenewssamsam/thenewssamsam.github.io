<h2 id="what-happened">What Happened in South Carolina?</h2>
<p>During a weekend swing through Florence, Orangeburg, and Columbia, Buttigieg addressed party activists, held roundtables at historically Black colleges and universities (HBCUs), and attended Sunday services. His itinerary was no accident. Following the Democratic National Committee's reaffirmation of South Carolina as the first-in-the-nation presidential primary, candidates are once again recognizing that a viable path to the nomination runs directly through the Palmetto State's substantial Black electorate.</p>

<p>In 2020, Buttigieg struggled mightily with Black voters, winning just 3% of their support in the South Carolina primary while Joe Biden captured 61% with the crucial backing of Rep. Jim Clyburn. Six years later, Buttigieg is pointing to his tenure as Biden's transportation secretary—highlighting tangible federal investments like a $23 million infrastructure grant connecting Orangeburg's HBCUs—to demonstrate a track record of delivery rather than mere promises.</p>
