<section id="introduction">
<p>Pennsylvania Attorney General Dave Sunday recently announced criminal charges against 14 individuals—mostly current and former Penn State students—linked to an alleged narcotics operation involving two off-campus Greek organizations. As Penn State moves to suspend Delta Upsilon and investigate the fallout, the incident raises an urgent question: how do large institutions balance the independence of student organizations with the necessity of safety? History suggests this is a cycle universities have struggled with for over a century.</p>
</section>

<section id="what-happened">
<h2>The Current Situation</h2>
<p>The charges involve a sophisticated narcotics operation spanning 2023 and 2024. Penn State’s administration has responded by placing Delta Upsilon on interim suspension, while noting that Sigma Chi currently operates outside of university oversight. Vice President for Student Affairs Andrea Dowhower expressed horror at the allegations, emphasizing that the university is cooperating fully with law enforcement while initiating its own internal conduct investigations.</p>
</section>
