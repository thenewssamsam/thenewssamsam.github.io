    <p>The Colorado River basin is facing a historic turning point. As of mid-August 2026, Lake Powell and Lake Mead—the lifeblood of the American Southwest—have officially hit record-low water levels. For residents across Arizona, this isn't just a headline about distant reservoirs; it is a shift that will touch our monthly utility bills and the long-term stability of our local power grid.</p>

  <section id="the-current-situation">
    <h2>The State of the Reservoirs</h2>
    <p>The numbers are stark. Lake Powell has dropped over 20 feet since the beginning of 2026, teetering just 30 feet above the "dead pool" threshold where hydroelectric turbines can no longer function. Meanwhile, Lake Mead, located further downstream, has also plummeted to its lowest recorded elevation.</p>
