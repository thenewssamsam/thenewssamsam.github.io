<p>Politics is rarely a game of confession. Candidates are expected to project unshakeable confidence, brandishing policy briefs like shields and speaking with absolute certainty on everything from local zoning laws to complex geopolitical flashpoints in the South China Sea. That is why a recent moment on a South Carolina debate stage caught voters and commentators entirely off guard.</p>

<p>Facing off against ultra-conservative Representative Ralph Norman in the Republican primary runoff to succeed her late brother, the veteran foreign policy hawk Lindsey Graham, fill-in Senator Darline Graham was asked a direct question about whether Taiwan and the South China Sea threaten American national security. Instead of dodging or bluffing with rehearsed talking points, she leaned into the microphone and offered a rare political commodity: raw, unvarnished honesty.</p>

<p>"I'm just going to be honest here," she told the opera house audience. "I'm not that informed on national security, so—but I do support the military." When gasps and boos rippled through the crowd, she doubled down, stating plainly, "I'm not a polished politician up here. National security is not my thing, not my area of expertise, but I do support the military."</p>
