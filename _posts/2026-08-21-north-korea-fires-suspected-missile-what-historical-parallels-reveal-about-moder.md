  <section id="what-happened" style="margin-bottom: 30px;">
    <h2 style="font-size: 1.5rem; font-weight: 700; color: #2d3748; border-bottom: 1px solid #e2e8f0; padding-bottom: 8px; margin-bottom: 15px;">What Happened on the Korean Peninsula</h2>
    <p style="margin-bottom: 15px;">
      According to reports from Tokyo and Seoul, the launch occurred on Thursday morning, involving what Japanese authorities identified as a suspected ballistic missile. South Korean military sources confirmed an unidentified projectile was fired off the country's eastern coastline. 
    </p>
    <p style="margin-bottom: 15px;">
      This development arrived on the heels of major strategic announcements. On Wednesday, U.S. and South Korean militaries announced they were scaling back their 11-day Ulchi Freedom Shield exercises. The reduction followed instructions from U.S. President Donald Trump, who directed the Pentagon to substantially cut back the drills, citing existing relationships and broader geopolitical frustrations, including South Korea's stance regarding the war in Iran.
    </p>
    <p style="margin-bottom: 15px;">
      However, the diplomatic outreach hit a brick wall almost instantly. Kim Yo Jong, the influential sister of North Korean leader Kim Jong Un, publicly dismissed the gesture. She declared that the scaled-back nature of the drills did not alter their fundamentally provocative and aggressive character, noting that Washington would not receive the desired cooperative answer simply by framing reduced drills as an act of good faith. She further highlighted ongoing military modernization efforts by Seoul, including nuclear-powered submarine initiatives, as ongoing points of friction.
    </p>
  </section>

    <p style="margin-bottom: 15px;">
      History shows us that whenever an administration attempts a unilateral de-escalation or signals a willingness to compromise, adversary regimes rarely respond with immediate concession. Instead, they engage in tactical stress-testing.

    <p style="margin-bottom: 15px;">
      During the height of Cold War negotiations, superpowers frequently encountered the "escalation-for-concession" paradox. When one side reduced military posture to demonstrate peaceful intent, the opposing side often interpreted the move not as magnanimity, but as a potential vulnerability or political calculation driven by domestic pressures. 
    </p>
    <p style="margin-bottom: 15px;">
      In North Korea's specific institutional history, projecting strength during moments of potential diplomatic engagement is a foundational strategy. By pairing diplomatic rejections from officials like Kim Yo Jong with physical missile tests, Pyongyang establishes maximum leverage. They ensure that any future negotiation table is approached from a position of perceived technological advancement and unyielding posture rather than perceived weakness.
    </p>
