<h2>What Happened: A Historic Heat Dome Settles In</h2>
<p>More than 45 million people across the United States south-west are bracing for dangerous, record-breaking temperatures as a powerful heat dome settles into the region through late August and early September. According to the National Weather Service (NWS), daytime highs are expected to surge between 110F and 115F (43-46C) in parts of the south-west and 100F to 110F across the Southern Plains, accompanied by minimal overnight cooling relief.</p>

<p>A heat dome forms when a strong upper-level high-pressure system traps hot air over a large geographic area, pushing down and preventing cooler air from moving in. This stagnant atmospheric trap not only intensifies ground-level heat but also overlaps with ongoing wildfire threats and monsoonal moisture from Texas to California. The NWS has categorized these conditions as Extreme HeatRisk, warning of severe health hazards, especially for vulnerable populations such as older adults, young children, and those without proper hydration or air conditioning.</p>
