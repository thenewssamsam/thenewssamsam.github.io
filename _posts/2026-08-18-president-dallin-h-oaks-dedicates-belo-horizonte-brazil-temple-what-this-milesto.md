<h1 class="ncg-title">President Dallin H. Oaks Dedicates Belo Horizonte Brazil Temple: What This Milestone Means for Global Faith</h1>
</header>

<div class="ncg-body-content">
<p class="ncg-intro">When President Dallin H. Oaks dedicated the Belo Horizonte Brazil Temple on a sunlit Sunday in August 2026, he marked more than just a geographic expansion for The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints. For over 1.5 million members across Brazil, the opening of the Church's 220th operating temple represents the end of grueling overnight bus rides and the beginning of a closer, more accessible walk of faith.</p>

<h2 id="what-happened">What Happened in Belo Horizonte</h2>
<p>On Sunday, August 16, 2026, President Dallin H. Oaks—just days after celebrating his 94th birthday—offered the dedicatory prayer for the Belo Horizonte Brazil Temple. Traveling with his wife, Sister Kristen M. Oaks, alongside General Authority Seventies and local leaders, the President of the Church performed his first international temple dedication since being set apart in October 2025.</p>
<p>The new sacred structure is the 12th operating temple in Brazil and the 220th worldwide, followed immediately by the Cleveland Ohio Temple as the 221st. For decades, Latter-day Saints in Belo Horizonte and the surrounding state of Minas Gerais faced arduous journeys of up to eight hours overnight by bus to reach the distant houses of the Lord in São Paulo or Campinas. Now, members in the region have a dedicated sanctuary right in their capital city, transforming both their regular worship and their connection to sacred ordinances.</p>

<h2 id="a-faith-perspective">A Faith &amp; Scripture Perspective: The House of the Lord</h2>
<p>In his dedicatory remarks, President Oaks reminded the assembled Saints that temples are far more than architectural achievements or statistical milestones. They are houses of God designed to prepare individuals for eternal life through sacred covenants centered on Jesus Christ.</p>
<p>This emphasis on sacred spaces and covenant-keeping echoes throughout the scriptural narrative. When King Solomon dedicated the ancient temple in Jerusalem, he acknowledged the transcendence of God while honoring the physical sanctuary dedicated to His name:</p>
<blockquote class="ncg-scripture-box">
<p><em>"But will God indeed dwell on the earth? behold, the heaven and heaven of heavens cannot contain thee; how much less this house that I have builded?"</em> — <strong>1 Kings 8:27 (KJV)</strong></p>
<p class="ncg-verse-note">Solomon's humility reminds believers that physical temples do not contain the infinite Creator, but rather serve as consecrated places where human hearts are drawn heavenward in worship and covenant.</p>
</blockquote>
<p>In the New Testament, the Apostle Paul expands this imagery, reminding believers of their personal accountability as living temples, anchored always to the Chief Cornerstone:</p>
<blockquote class="ncg-scripture-box">
<p><em>"Now therefore ye are no more strangers and foreigners, but fellowcitizens with the saints, and of the household of God; And are built upon the foundation of the apostles and prophets, Jesus Christ himself being the chief corner stone."</em> — <strong>Ephesians 2:19-20 (KJV)</strong></p>
<p class="ncg-verse-note">This passage underscores that the brick-and-mortar houses of the Lord dedicated today exist to strengthen the spiritual foundation of individual disciples, binding communities together in Christ.</p>
</blockquote>
<p>Furthermore, temple worship calls believers to a higher standard of devotion and daily living. As the prophet Micah declared when outlining what God truly requires of His people, holy places should inspire justice, mercy, and a humble walk with the Divine:</p>
<blockquote class="ncg-scripture-box">
<p><em>"He hath showed thee, O man, what is good; and what doth the Lord require of thee, but to do justly, and to love mercy, and to walk humbly with thy God?"</em> — <strong>Micah 6:8 (KJV)</strong></p>
<p class="ncg-verse-note">Temple ordinances are not meant to isolate believers from the world, but to equip them to carry justice, mercy, and service back into their families and communities.</p>
</blockquote>

<p>"Temples are essential to our Heavenly Father's plan—reminding us to stay worthy of His blessings and to translate sacred knowledge into service for our fellowman."</p>

<h2 id="how-should-christians-respond">How Should Believers Respond?</h2>
<p>The dedication of a new house of worship in Brazil invites believers everywhere—regardless of denomination or geography—to reflect on their own spiritual commitments. Here are three practical ways to apply this moment:</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Evaluate Personal Devotion:</strong> Just as Brazilian Saints sacrificed hours of travel to attend sacred spaces, modern disciples can evaluate how intentionally they prioritize spiritual quietness and communion with God amidst busy daily schedules.</li>
  <li><strong>Strengthen Local Fellowship:</strong> Physical distance once separated the believers of Belo Horizonte from their nearest sanctuary. In our own lives, we can bridge relational distances by reaching out to support neighbors, family members, and fellow congregants in need of encouragement.</li>
  <li><strong>Focus on Covenant Living:</strong> President Oaks emphasized that temple covenants are designed to translate eternal knowledge into active service. Believers can look for daily, practical opportunities to practice patience, charity, and integrity where they live.</li>
</ul>

<div class="ncg-closing-box">

Oaks and all leaders who minister across the earth. Help us to build our lives upon the solid foundation of Thy truth, that we may serve our neighbors with compassion and walk humbly in Thy sight.
