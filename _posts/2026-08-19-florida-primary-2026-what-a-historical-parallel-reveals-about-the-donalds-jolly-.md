<p>Republican U.S. Rep. Byron Donalds and Democrat David Jolly have secured their respective party nominations in Florida's crowded primary election, setting the stage for a high-stakes November showdown to succeed term-limited Governor Ron DeSantis. Following months of polling that pointed directly to this matchup, voters have narrowed a massive field of 17 major-party candidates down to a classic two-party general election contest in a state that has fundamentally transformed over the past three decades.</p>

<h2 id="what-happened" style="font-size:22px; margin-top:32px; margin-bottom:12px; color:#202124;">What Happened: The Primary Results</h2>
<p>The August 18 primary brought a definitive end to months of fierce campaigning across Florida. On the Republican side, Naples-area congressman Byron Donalds dominated the field, capturing 49% of the vote in late July polling and securing the backing of President Donald Trump. Donalds outpaced a crowded field that included Lieutenant Governor Jay Collins—appointed by DeSantis in 2025 to preserve the outgoing administration's record—as well as former Florida House Speaker Paul Renner and businessman James Fishback.</p>

<p>Meanwhile, the Democratic primary saw former U.S. Rep. David Jolly separate himself from rivals like State Rep. Dotie Joseph and educator Dayna Marie Foster. Jolly, who represented the Tampa Bay area as a Republican before leaving the GOP in 2018 and joining the Democrats in 2025, has made the soaring cost of living, housing affordability, and property insurance central themes of his campaign. He enters the general election alongside running mate former U.S. Rep. Gwen Graham, aiming to break a gubernatorial losing streak for Democrats that stretches back to Lawton Chiles's 1994 reelection victory.</p>
