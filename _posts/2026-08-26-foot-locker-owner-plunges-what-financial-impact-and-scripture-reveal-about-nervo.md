<h2>The Financial Impact: Understanding the Retail Pullback</h2>
<p>To understand why a retail stock plunge matters to everyone—even those who never shop at Foot Locker—we have to look at the underlying financial mechanics. Retail is often considered the canary in the coal mine for the broader consumer economy. Consumer spending accounts for roughly two-thirds of economic activity in major Western nations, meaning that when shoppers hit the brakes, the entire economic engine slows down.</p>

<h3 id="who-pays-and-who-profits">Who Pays and Who Profits in a Retrenchment?</h3>
<p>When retail demand softens, the financial burden cascades down the supply chain. Retailers must mark down inventory to move sluggish products, crushing profit margins. Manufacturers see order volumes cut, which eventually leads to margin compression, hiring freezes, or cost-cutting measures. On the flip side, discount retailers and deep-value stores often see temporary inflows as consumers trade down from mid-tier mall brands to budget alternatives.</p>
