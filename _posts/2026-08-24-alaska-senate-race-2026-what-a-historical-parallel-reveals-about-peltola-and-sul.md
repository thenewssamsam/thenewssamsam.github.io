<section id="what-happened" class="ncg-section">
<h2>What Happened in the Alaska Primary?</h2>
<p>Preliminary results from Alaska's statewide primary election released early Wednesday morning show a familiar face stepping back into the spotlight. Former U.S. Rep. Mary Peltola, a Democrat, secured an early lead over incumbent Republican U.S. Sen. Dan S. Sullivan. With nearly 94% of precincts reporting and 132,746 ballots counted, Peltola captured roughly 48% of the vote (63,364 ballots), while Sullivan trailed with 42.8% (56,510 votes).</p>

<p>Thousands of mail-in and absentee ballots remain to be tabulated in the coming days under Alaska's unique open primary system. The top four vote-getters from a crowded field of 16 candidates will advance to the November general election, where the state's ranked-choice voting system will ultimately decide the winner.</p>

<p>This race is far from settled. As Sen. Sullivan noted to reporters, a very similar scenario unfolded in 2024 when Peltola also led on primary night before ultimately losing her House seat to Republican Nick Begich. Yet, the stakes in 2026 are exceptionally high. With control of the United States Senate hanging in the balance, national political action committees, heavy outside spending, and high-profile endorsements—including one from President Donald Trump urging support for the incumbent—have turned Last Frontier politics into a national battleground.</p>
