  <section id="what-happened" class="ncg-section">
    <h2>What Happened: The 72-Hour Tariff Pause</h2>
    <p>Late Tuesday stateside, just hours before aggressive new duties were set to take effect at midnight, President Trump announced on Truth Social that the imposition of 50 percent tariffs on specific Canadian goods had been paused. According to the administration, the reprieve is "based on the fact that Canada and the U.S.A., subject to the finalization of documents, have a DEAL!"</p>
    <p>The proposed duties—targeting items like Canadian hockey sticks, wine, and various consumer goods—were being invoked under Section 338 of the Tariff Act of 1930. This Great Depression-era provision has been largely dormant for decades. The intended scope covered roughly $20 billion in Canadian imports, designed to retaliate against what U.S. officials termed trade discrimination in motor vehicle, alcohol, and dairy sectors. While $20 billion is only a fraction of the broader $382 billion in goods imported from Canada last year, the severe 50 percent rate threatened to price specific regional sellers entirely out of the U.S. market.</p>
