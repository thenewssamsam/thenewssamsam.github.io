<p class="ncg-intro">More than two years after the tragic killing of five-year-old Palestinian child Hind Rajab shook the international community, the Israeli military has officially admitted to firing on the civilian vehicle she and her family were trapped in, launching a formal criminal investigation. As global human rights groups and grieving family members grapple with this delayed admission, the case forces a profound global examination of civilian protection, systemic impunity, and the deep human cry for true accountability.</p>

<section id="what-happened">
<h2>What Happened to Hind Rajab?</h2>
<p>On January 29, 2024, five-year-old Hind Rajab and several members of her extended family attempted to follow evacuation orders, fleeing their neighbourhood in Gaza City inside a black Kia car. Her mother, Wissam Hamada, had already been separated from her earlier in the day. As the vehicle moved through the streets, Israeli forces unleashed a barrage of more than 300 bullets into the car, instantly killing six relatives, including Hind's 15-year-old cousin Layan, who had managed to speak with emergency dispatchers before being fatally shot.</p>

<div class="ncg-quote-box">
<p>“I’m so scared… Please come.” — Hind Rajab's final whispered plea to emergency dispatchers.</p>
</div>

<p>Wounded and trapped alongside the bodies of her family members, the young girl survived for hours. A clearly marked Palestine Red Crescent Society (PRCS) ambulance was dispatched to rescue her after receiving explicit coordination clearance. Yet, despite these safety protocols, the rescue vehicle was struck by a tank round, instantly killing the two paramedics inside. Investigations by groups like Avaaz and Al Jazeera later pointed to a deliberate double-tap tactic, leaving Hind to pass away from her injuries later that evening.</p>

However, relentless investigative work by organizations like the Hind Rajab Foundation, Forensic Architecture, and international media outlets produced satellite, audio, and visual evidence pointing directly to military units operating in the immediate vicinity.</p>
