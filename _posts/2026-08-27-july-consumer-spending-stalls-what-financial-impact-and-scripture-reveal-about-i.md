<h2>What Happened: The July PCE Report Breakdown</h2>
<p>According to the Commerce Department's report released Wednesday, consumer spending when adjusted for inflation was entirely flat in July. This represents a sharp deceleration from the 0.4% gain recorded in June. Meanwhile, the Federal Reserve's preferred inflation gauge—the PCE price index—rose 0.2% on a monthly basis, keeping the annual inflation rate locked at 3.7%.</p>

<p>Economists surveyed by FactSet had anticipated a slightly cooler monthly increase of 0.1% and an annual rate slowing down to 3.6%. When stripping out volatile food and energy costs, the core PCE index also ticked up 0.2% monthly, sitting at 3.3% year-over-year. Although after-tax personal income grew by a solid 0.4% in July, the savings rate ticked up from a four-year low of 2.6% in June to a more cautious 3.0% as families chose to preserve cash rather than open their wallets.</p>
