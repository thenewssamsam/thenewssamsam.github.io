  <section id="what-happened">
    <h2>What Just Happened?</h2>
    <p>President Donald Trump recently announced a three-day pause on 50% tariffs against Canadian imports, narrowly avoiding a massive trade disruption just hours before they were set to take effect. While this temporary "deal" buys time for negotiators, it leaves many families in a state of limbo, wondering how shifting trade policies might eventually hit their grocery bills and household budgets.</p>
  </section>

  <section id="parenting-perspective">
    <h2>Navigating Uncertainty as a Family</h2>
    <p>When headlines are dominated by trade wars and talk of 50% tariffs, it is natural for parents to feel a sense of anxiety. We are responsible for the financial stability of our homes, and when international relations become volatile, we often feel that pressure at the kitchen table. How we handle this uncertainty matters, not just for our own peace of mind, but for how our children learn to process the world.</p>

    <p><strong>For Younger Children:</strong> Keep it simple. They don't need to know the specifics of Section 338 of the Tariff Act of 1930. Focus on the concept of "working out problems." Explain that sometimes countries have disagreements, just like friends or siblings, and that leaders are taking extra time to talk and find a solution that helps everyone.</p>

  <section id="practical-response">
    <h2>How Should Parents Respond?</h2>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Focus on what you can control:</strong> Instead of obsessing over trade negotiations, focus on your family's budget. Are there areas where you can reduce waste?</li>
      <li><strong>Limit the "doom-scrolling":</strong> Constant exposure to breaking news alerts keeps our nervous systems in a state of fight-or-flight. Set specific times to check the news, and keep the screens off during family meals.</li>
      <li><strong>Teach resilience:</strong> Use the "wait and see" nature of this tariff delay to talk to your kids about patience and the value of thorough negotiation over quick, impulsive reactions.</li>
    </ul>
