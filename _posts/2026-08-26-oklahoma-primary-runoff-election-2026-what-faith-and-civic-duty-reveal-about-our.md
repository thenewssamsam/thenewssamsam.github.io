  <h2 id="what-happened-in-the-runoffs" style="color: #2c3e50; margin-top: 40px;">What Happened in the Oklahoma Primary Runoffs?</h2>
  <p>
    The August 25, 2026, primary runoff election brought closure to several contentious primary battles across the Sooner State while setting the stage for the upcoming November general election. According to reports from KOCO, voters decided crucial nominations across executive offices, legislative seats, and municipal propositions.
  </p>
  <p>
    In the marquee Republican gubernatorial runoff, Attorney General Gentner Drummond and former state Senator Mike Mazzei went head-to-head after tightly contested June primaries where both candidates garnered roughly 26 percent of the vote. Meanwhile, the Republican race for state superintendent saw veteran educator Robert Franklin defeat pastor and teacher James Taylor, positioning Franklin to face Democratic nominee Janetta Marshall in November. Franklin brings over 40 years of classroom experience to the table, highlighting an election cycle heavily focused on educational standards and institutional stability.
  </p>

  <h2 id="state-questions-and-ballot-measures" style="color: #2c3e50; margin-top: 40px;">State Questions: SQ 846 Passes and SQ 844 Fails</h2>
  <p>
    Oklahomans also weighed in on significant state questions that carried direct policy implications. State Question 846 successfully passed, amending the state constitution to explicitly require identification to be presented while voting. While presenting ID at the polls was already statutory law in Oklahoma, this constitutional measure cements the requirement into the foundational laws of the state, reflecting a strong public desire for electoral transparency and security.
