  <section id="introduction">
    <p>In a significant shift for regional security, South Korean President Lee Jae Myung has signaled a move toward bolstering the nation's independent defense capabilities. This strategic pivot follows reports that the U.S. will scale back joint military exercises with Seoul, prompting a reassessment of how South Korea manages its own security infrastructure and the associated economic costs.</p>
  </section>

  <section id="the-financial-shift">
    <h2>The Economic Weight of Strategic Autonomy</h2>
    <p>Moving toward a more independent defense posture is not merely a military decision; it is a profound fiscal one. For decades, the reliance on joint U.S.-South Korea exercises provided a cost-sharing benefit, allowing Seoul to maintain a high level of readiness while leveraging the logistical and technological infrastructure of the U.S. military. As these drills scale back, the burden of maintaining that readiness shifts more directly onto the South Korean taxpayer.</p>
