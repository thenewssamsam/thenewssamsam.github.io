  <section id="what-happened">
    <h2>What Happened: The Stakes in Nvidia's Earnings Report</h2>
    <p>Nvidia has reigned as the undisputed profit engine of the artificial intelligence boom, filling data centers worldwide with the advanced graphics processing units (GPUs) that power modern AI models. Yet, as investors gear up for the company's fiscal second-quarter earnings report following the closing bell on Wednesday, a subtle wave of skepticism has begun dragging down the stock. Shares recently slid 2.9%, marking a seventh consecutive losing streak—the longest since 2022.</p>
    
    <p>The root of Wall Street's anxiety is not Nvidia's technical capability, but rather customer concentration. A massive share of Nvidia's staggering revenue depends on a handful of tech "hyperscalers"—Amazon, Google, and Microsoft—alongside major buyers like Meta and SpaceX. These companies buy GPUs in bulk for internal workloads and rent out excess capacity. However, as capital expenditures soar, the free cash flow of these vital buyers is beginning to contract sharply, raising hard questions about the near-term sustainability of the AI spending spree.</p>
  </section>

  <section id="financial-perspective">
    <h2>The Financial Mechanics: Cash Flow Pressures and Customer Concentration</h2>
    <p>To understand the financial tightrope Nvidia is walking, we have to look closely at the balance sheets of its primary customers. In the second quarter, Amazon and Alphabet both turned cash flow negative, while Meta's cash generation dwindled by more than 90% compared to the previous year. Even Elon Musk's public ventures, SpaceX and Tesla, reported negative free cash flow as they aggressively pursued their own massive AI expansions.</p>

    <h3>The Shift Toward the Long Tail</h3>
    <p>The core financial challenge for Nvidia is transitioning from a narrow base of hyper-funded cloud giants to a broad, diversified customer ecosystem. While the hyperscalers helped build a $5 trillion market cap, waiting for new buyers to arrive with cash in hand is not a passive strategy Jensen Huang is willing to adopt. Because most ordinary enterprises lack the massive cash reserves or balance sheets required to purchase expensive rack-scale AI systems upfront, market mechanisms must evolve.</p>
