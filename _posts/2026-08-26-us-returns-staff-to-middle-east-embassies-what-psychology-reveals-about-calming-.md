Following joint US-Israeli strikes on Iran that began on February 28, the United States pulled personnel from diplomatic missions across the Middle East. Now, sources report that the Donald Trump administration is beginning to send staff back to key posts in Lebanon, Israel, Saudi Arabia, and Baghdad, Iraq, while also allowing family members to return to Riyadh.</p>

<h2 id="what-happened">What Happened in the Middle East Missions?</h2>
<p>According to reports from Reuters, Washington sees a lower risk of immediate regional escalation in its ongoing conflict with Iran, prompting the phased return of diplomatic staff. However, the move is calculated and cautious: select embassies will initially operate at no more than 75 to 85 per cent of authorized staff levels. The return of personnel is scheduled to begin as early as this week, representing a measured step away from full crisis mode toward a managed, stable diplomatic presence.</p>

<p>This phased approach reflects a delicate balancing act. Governments cannot simply flip a switch from total evacuation to business as usual without risking credibility or exposing personnel to sudden volatility. By capping capacity at 85 per cent, decision-makers are acknowledging that while the immediate precipice of all-out regional war may have receded, the underlying geopolitical landscape remains complex and unpredictable.</p>
