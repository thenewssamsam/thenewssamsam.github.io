<h2>What Happened in the U.S.-Iran Strategy Shift?</h2>
<p>Secretary of State Marco Rubio recently informed foreign counterparts that the United States is shifting its strategy in the ongoing conflict with Iran. Rather than initiating new military strikes, Washington is focusing on aggressive economic pressure, anchored by a new Treasury Department sanctions initiative and an effective naval blockade. According to U.S. officials, the clearing of mines from the Strait of Hormuz by the U.S. Navy represents a watershed moment, largely neutralizing Iran's primary source of geopolitical and economic leverage.</p>

<p>This major strategic pivot comes as the White House believes it is effectively squeezing Tehran's economic lifelines while restoring safe passage for energy tankers through global shipping lanes. With oil moving out of the Persian Gulf and into international markets, Iran's main export hub at Kharg Island has seen virtually no tanker traffic over the past two weeks. For everyday consumers and global markets alike, this marks a profound transition from active kinetic warfare to high-stakes economic containment.</p>

For months, restricted oil flow through the strait created immense volatility in global energy markets, threatening households and businesses worldwide with soaring fuel costs.</p>
