  <section id="what-happened">
    <p style="font-size: 1.1rem; font-weight: 500; color: #222;">The second quarter earnings season is nearing its finish line, and it is carrying numbers the market hasn't seen in half a decade. When Nvidia delivered another blockbuster Q2 earnings beat, it acted as a massive keystone for a remarkably robust stretch of corporate financial reports.</p>
    
    <p>According to fresh data from FactSet, second quarter earnings for S&P 500 companies are currently on pace to climb an astonishing 50% year-over-year. That marks the highest growth rate our markets have experienced since 2021. But beneath those broad market indexes lies a very specific growth engine: artificial intelligence. Bank of America strategists point directly to AI as the primary catalyst lifting corporate revenues, turning what could have been a standard reporting season into a historic financial event.</p>

    <p>To understand the financial mechanics of this quarter's surge, we have to look past the top-line numbers and examine how money is actually flowing through the tech and retail ecosystems. On the same Wednesday Nvidia anchored the markets, a slew of software and cybersecurity stalwarts—including Salesforce, CrowdStrike, and Okta—delivered reports that sent their stocks surging by double digits in extended trading.</p>
    
    <p>From a general financial literacy perspective, this type of market movement illustrates how capital reallocation works during technological shifts. When enterprise clients prioritize digital resilience and automated efficiency, dollars move rapidly away from legacy operations and concentrate heavily on platforms providing scalable AI infrastructure and cloud security.</p>
