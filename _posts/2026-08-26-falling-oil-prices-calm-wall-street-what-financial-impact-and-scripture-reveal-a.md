As Brent crude pulled back 3.6% to $87.27 on Tuesday, Wall Street responded with relief—spurring gains across the S&P 500, the Dow Jones Industrial Average, and the Nasdaq composite while reining in runaway Treasury yields.</p>

  <section class="ncg-section" id="what-happened">
    <h2>What Happened in the Markets</h2>
    <p>Financial markets have spent much of the summer riding a turbulent rollercoaster dictated largely by energy costs, geopolitical tensions in the Middle East, and persistent inflation anxieties. But Tuesday brought a welcome shift. Even as the U.S. government announced fresh economic sanctions against Iran, the price of a barrel of Brent crude tumbled for the second consecutive session, following an aggressive run of 13 gains in 14 trading days.</p>
    
    <p>This downward correction in energy markets provided an immediate psychological and financial cushion for investors. The S&P 500 edged up 0.3% toward its recent record highs, the Dow climbed 160 points (0.3%), and the tech-heavy Nasdaq rose 0.7%. Chipmaker Nvidia gained 2.2%, helping offset a sharp 30.7% plunge for Dick's Sporting Goods after the retailer trimmed its full-year profit forecast and reported softer-than-expected sales.</p>

    <p>In response to rising inflation expectations, bond investors demand higher yields on U.S. Treasury bonds to protect their capital against purchasing power erosion. Through the summer months, the yield on the 10-year Treasury climbed significantly. When Treasury yields rise, borrowing costs follow suit:</p>
    
    <ul>
      <li>Mortgage rates tick upward, pricing prospective homebuyers out of the market.</li>
      <li>Auto loans and credit card interest rates become more punishing for working families.</li>
      <li>Corporate borrowing costs increase, potentially slowing business expansion and hiring.</li>
    </ul>

    <p>Tuesday's drop in oil prices helped reverse this stressful chain reaction. The yield on the 10-year Treasury fell from 4.70% to 4.63%, offering immediate relief to a bond market that had been stretched to its limits. While yields remain well above their pre-conflict baseline of 3.97%, the downward pressure eased immediate fears of a harsher economic slowdown.</p>
