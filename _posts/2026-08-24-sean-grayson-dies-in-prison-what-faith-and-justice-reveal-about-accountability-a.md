<section id="what-happened">
<h2>What Happened: The Final Chapter of a Tragic Case</h2>
<p>Sean Grayson, the former Sangamon County Sheriff's deputy convicted of the 2024 murder of Sonya Massey, died in prison on August 23, 2026, according to his defense attorney Daniel Fultz. Grayson was serving a 20-year sentence after a Peoria County jury found him guilty of second-degree murder following the fatal shooting in Massey's Illinois home. Just three weeks prior to his death, the Illinois Prisoner Review Board had unanimously denied Grayson's request for early release under a medical statute, as he had been battling terminal Stage 4 cancer.</p>

<p>The news brings a definitive, yet somber, close to a legal battle that shocked the nation. Massey, a 36-year-old Black mother of two, had called 911 for help regarding a suspected intruder. Instead of assistance, she encountered an officer with a troubled work history—having bounced through six agencies in four years—who fatally shot her after a brief verbal exchange about a pot of boiling water. While the legal proceedings resulted in a conviction and maximum sentence, many community members felt the second-degree murder verdict fell short of true justice. Now, with Grayson's passing, earthly judicial appeals have ended, leaving behind profound questions about accountability, systemic failure, and the complex human desire for restitution.</p>
