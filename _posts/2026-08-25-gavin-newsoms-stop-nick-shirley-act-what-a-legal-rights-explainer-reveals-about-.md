<h2 id="what-happened">What Happened: Inside AB 2624</h2>
<p>The legislative battle centers around a privacy expansion measure introduced by Democratic Assemblymember Mia Bonta. Proponents of the law argue it is a necessary protective measure designed to shield immigration-service workers from targeted threats, harassment, and doxing. In an era where public-facing workers frequently face online hostility, supporters maintain that personal data safeguards are essential to worker safety.</p>

<p>However, critics see an entirely different motive. The nickname "Stop Nick Shirley Act" points directly to conservative citizen journalist Nick Shirley, who has gained widespread attention for publishing videos investigating government-funded programs and confronting facility workers. Opponents contend that by cloaking certain workers in expanded address protections, the state is effectively raising legal and practical barriers for investigative journalists, independent auditors, and taxpayers attempting to examine how public funds are utilized.</p>

The First Amendment protects not only the right to publish information but also, crucially, the freedom to gather news and scrutinize public officials and entities operating with taxpayer funds.</p>
