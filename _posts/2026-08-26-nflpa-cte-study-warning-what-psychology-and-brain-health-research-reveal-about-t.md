    <h2>A Psychology Perspective: The Weight of Anticipatory Anxiety</h2>
    <p>When professional athletes read headlines about progressive neurodegenerative diseases, they do not process the information as detached observers. They read it through the lens of lived experience. Every forgotten name, every morning stiffness, and every lingering headache suddenly carries a heavy, existential weight. This phenomenon taps directly into well-documented psychological principles.</p>

    <h3>Normalcy Bias Versus Catastrophizing</h3>
    <p>For years, human psychology allows us to practice <em>normalcy bias</em>—the tendency to underestimate the likelihood of a disaster or long-term consequence happening to us personally, even when the statistical risk is clear. Athletes must maintain this mental armor just to step onto the gridiron. However, when a definitive study shatters that protective illusion, the mind can swing violently in the opposite direction toward catastrophizing. Every minor cognitive slip becomes magnified into an irreversible fate.</p>

    <h3>The Power of Action Over Helplessness</h3>
    <p>Psychological research consistently demonstrates that perceived lack of control is one of the primary drivers of severe anxiety and depression. When players feel that neurodegenerative decline is an inevitable, untreatable freight train, hopelessness sets in. This is precisely why the NFLPA's inclusion of Dr. Daneshvar's commentary was so critical. By emphasizing that many overlapping symptoms are treatable and that specialists understand how to care for athletes, the communication shifts players away from helpless resignation and back toward agency.</p>
