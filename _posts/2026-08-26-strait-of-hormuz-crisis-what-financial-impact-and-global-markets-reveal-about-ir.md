<h2>What Happened in the Strait of Hormuz?</h2>
<p>The geopolitical chessboard in the Middle East shifted slightly on Tuesday when Iran and Oman announced a joint framework for a temporary shipping corridor through the critical Strait of Hormuz. Following weeks of intense negotiations between Tehran and Muscat, the proposal aims to clear mines and establish a controlled transit route through the waterway, which has served as a primary flashpoint during six months of regional conflict.</p>

<p>However, the diplomatic maneuver comes with serious friction. According to senior Iranian officials, the proposed transit arrangement would effectively close a separate, UN-authorized shipping channel that currently hugs the coast of Oman—a route that Iran has vehemently opposed. Meanwhile, the United States insists the strait remains international waters, with President Donald Trump warning against interference and asserting that American naval forces are closely monitoring every square inch of the corridor.</p>
