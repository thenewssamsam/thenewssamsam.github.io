<section id="what-happened">
    <h2>What Happened in Phoenix</h2>
    <p>When the people sworn to uphold the law cross the line into alleged criminality, it sends shockwaves through a community. In Phoenix, Arizona, two former police officers—identified as Luis Vasquez Jr. and Antonio Felix—were arrested and immediately fired following a disturbing internal investigation. According to Phoenix Police Chief Matt Giordano, the officers face a slew of serious felony charges brought by the Maricopa County Attorney's Office, including aggravated assault, aggravated robbery, kidnapping, threatening and intimidating, causing criminal damage, and tampering with evidence.</p>
    
    <p>The case came to light after a man and a woman reported that they were pulled over late at night on August 5 by an SUV equipped with lights and sirens. What was supposed to be a routine traffic stop allegedly devolved into a violent nightmare. The victims told investigators that the on-duty officers punched them, restrained them, and deployed a stun gun while they were still trapped inside their vehicle. In a terrifying escalation, one of the suspects allegedly pulled a handgun, counted to three, and threatened to shoot the man if he did not exit the car. The woman was reportedly handcuffed and pushed face-down onto the pavement.</p>

When an officer acts completely outside their official capacity, fails to report an interaction, and uses unauthorized violence, the legal framework pivots from qualified immunity debates into straightforward criminal prosecution.</p>
