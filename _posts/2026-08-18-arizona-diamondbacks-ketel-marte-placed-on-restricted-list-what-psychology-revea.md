    <h1 style="font-size: 2.25rem; line-height: 1.2; margin-bottom: 1rem; color: #1a1a1a;">
      Arizona Diamondbacks' Ketel Marte Placed on Restricted List: What Psychology Reveals About Team Resilience
    </h1>
    <p style="font-size: 1.15rem; line-height: 1.6; color: #444;">
      When Arizona Diamondbacks star Ketel Marte unexpectedly missed a crucial game against the Boston Red Sox, resulting in his placement on the restricted list, teammate Geraldo Perdomo offered a masterclass in professional compartmentalization. But beneath the calm exterior of a post-game interview lies a fascinating look at how groups process sudden disruption and maintain focus under pressure.
    </p>
  </header>

  <section id="what-happened" style="margin-bottom: 2rem;">
    <h2 style="font-size: 1.5rem; border-bottom: 2px solid #3b82f6; padding-bottom: 0.5rem; margin-bottom: 1rem; color: #1e3a8a;">What Happened in Arizona?</h2>
    <p style="line-height: 1.7; color: #333; margin-bottom: 1rem;">
      The Arizona Diamondbacks found themselves navigating an unexpected roster shock when star infielder Ketel Marte failed to report for their game against the Boston Red Sox. The sudden absence prompted the organization to place Marte on the restricted list, leaving both fans and media scrambling for answers. 
    </p>
    <p style="line-height: 1.7; color: #333; margin-bottom: 1rem;">
      Following the game, infielder Geraldo Perdomo addressed reporters in a brief, measured exchange. When pressed about Marte’s whereabouts, team chemistry, and potential distractions, Perdomo maintained a strict boundary: he hadn't spoken to Marte, he didn't know the specifics of the situation, and his sole focus was stepping onto the field to play baseball. 
    </p>
  </section>

  <section id="psychology-perspective" style="margin-bottom: 2rem;">
    <h2 style="font-size: 1.5rem; border-bottom: 2px solid #3b82f6; padding-bottom: 0.5rem; margin-bottom: 1rem; color: #1e3a8a;">The Psychology of Workplace Disruption</h2>
    <p style="line-height: 1.7; color: #333; margin-bottom: 1rem;">
      When an unexpected crisis hits any organization—whether it is a Major League Baseball clubhouse or a corporate office—human behavior tends to follow predictable psychological patterns. Two key concepts help explain how Perdomo and the Diamondbacks managed the moment: <strong>compartmentalization</strong> and <strong>normalcy bias management</strong>.
    </p>
    <p style="line-height: 1.7; color: #333; margin-bottom: 1rem;">
      Compartmentalization is often viewed with skepticism in modern self-help culture, but in high-stakes environments, it is an essential survival mechanism. When a team loses a key performer without warning, spiraling into speculation or rumination degrades immediate performance. By repeatedly returning to the core mandate—&quot;we came here to play baseball&quot;—Perdomo demonstrated a healthy cognitive boundary. He separated the unknown administrative drama from his immediate professional duty.
    </p>
    <p style="line-height: 1.7; color: #333; margin-bottom: 1rem;">
      Furthermore, Perdomo's refusal to speak for the rest of the clubhouse (&quot;You can ask those guys... I can talk about myself&quot;) reflects a psychological awareness of groupthink avoidance. In high-stress scenarios, groups often adopt a collective panic or a shared narrative before the facts are clear. By refusing to speculate or project emotional reactions onto his peers, Perdomo resisted the pull of speculative contagion.
    </p>
    
    <div class="ncg-quote-box" style="background-color: #f8fafc; border-left: 4px solid #3b82f6; padding: 1.5rem; margin: 1.5rem 0; border-radius: 0 8px 8px 0;">
      <p style="font-size: 1.1rem; font-style: italic; color: #1e3a8a; margin: 0; line-height: 1.5;">
        &quot;In high-pressure environments, true resilience is not the absence of disruption, but the disciplined refusal to let the unknown paralyze your immediate purpose.&quot;
      </p>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section id="how-teams-respond" style="margin-bottom: 2rem;">
    <h2 style="font-size: 1.5rem; border-bottom: 2px solid #3b82f6; padding-bottom: 0.5rem; margin-bottom: 1rem; color: #1e3a8a;">How to Navigate Unexpected Shocks in Your Own Life</h2>
    <p style="line-height: 1.7; color: #333; margin-bottom: 1rem;">
      Most of us will never play in a Major League Baseball game, but everyone experiences sudden workplace disruptions, team member departures, or abrupt crises. Perdomo's straightforward reaction offers three practical takeaways for maintaining stability when the unexpected hits:
    </p>
    <ul style="line-height: 1.7; color: #333; margin-left: 1.5rem; margin-bottom: 1rem;">
      <li style="margin-bottom: 0.5rem;"><strong>Acknowledge your circle of control:</strong> Perdomo freely admitted what he didn't know rather than inventing narratives. Focus your energy only on the variables within your direct control.</li>
      <li style="margin-bottom: 0.5rem;"><strong>Protect your primary mission:</strong> When chaos erupts around you, anchor yourself to the core task at hand. </li>
      <li style="margin-bottom: 0.5rem;"><strong>Balance support with boundaries:</strong> Reaffirming team unity (&quot;we are a family and we're gonna support everybody&quot;) doesn't require compromising your personal focus or engaging in gossip.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>

  <section id="closing-takeaway" style="margin-bottom: 2rem;">
    <h2 style="font-size: 1.5rem; border-bottom: 2px solid #3b82f6; padding-bottom: 0.5rem; margin-bottom: 1rem; color: #1e3a8a;">Closing Takeaway</h2>
    <p style="line-height: 1.7; color: #333; margin-bottom: 1rem;">
      The Arizona Diamondbacks will ultimately sort out the details surrounding Ketel Marte's placement on the restricted list through proper channels. Until then, the clubhouse's ability to absorb a sudden blow without unraveling stands as a reminder of how professional maturity operates under the bright lights.
    </p>
  </section>

    <p style="line-height: 1.7; color: #333; margin: 0;">
      Uncertainty is an inevitable part of any team dynamic, whether on the baseball diamond or in daily life. When plans dissolve and questions outnumber answers, the most productive response is often the simplest one: show up, do your job, and let tomorrow handle itself.
