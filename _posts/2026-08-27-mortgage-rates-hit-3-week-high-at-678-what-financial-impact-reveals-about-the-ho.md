  <section id="what-happened">
    <h2>What Happened in the Mortgage Market?</h2>
    <p>According to data released by the Mortgage Bankers Association (MBA) for the week ending in late August 2026, the average contract interest rate for 30-year fixed-rate mortgages with conforming loan balances (up to $832,750) increased to 6.78%. This is up slightly from 6.77% the prior week, accompanied by rising points averaging 0.66 from 0.65 for loans backed by a 20% down payment.</p>
    <p>The immediate consequence of these elevated borrowing costs was a contraction in borrower demand. Total mortgage application volume dropped 1% on a seasonally adjusted basis. Refinance applications, which react almost instantaneously to weekly rate shifts, fell 2% for the week and sat a steep 17% lower than the same week one year prior—when benchmark rates were lower by 9 basis points.</p>

When interest rates hover near 6.78%, the cost of borrowing capital expands significantly over a 30-year horizon. A home buyer purchasing a median-priced home with a conventional loan sees thousands of dollars shifted away from their personal savings and directly into bank interest payments.</p>
