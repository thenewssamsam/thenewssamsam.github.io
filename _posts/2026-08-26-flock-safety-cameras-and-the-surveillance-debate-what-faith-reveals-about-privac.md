<section id="what-happened">
<h2>What Happened: The Rise of Flock Cameras</h2>
<p>Across towns, cities, and suburban neighborhoods nationwide, a subtle new infrastructure has taken root. Small, solar-powered black cameras mounted on traffic poles and light fixtures quietly photograph passing vehicles, recording license plate numbers, timestamps, and vehicle characteristics. Among the leading providers of this automated license plate reader (ALPR) technology is Flock Safety, a company whose systems have become ubiquitous fixtures on American roadways.</p>

<p>While proponents and law enforcement officials defend the technology as an invaluable tool for solving crimes, locating missing children, and enhancing community safety, a growing backlash is making waves in both local town halls and the halls of Congress. Recent reports of misuse—such as an instance where a former police officer in Florida allegedly used the system to track a single license plate hundreds of times—have brought simmering privacy anxieties to a full boil.</p>

<p>Technology has marched far beyond simple license plate reading. Today, advanced artificial intelligence capabilities integrated into these systems allow for deeper data collection, sparking intense debates about the boundaries of state surveillance, individual liberty, and the Fourth Amendment. As lawmakers on both the political right and left begin pushing back against unchecked deployment, ordinary citizens are left wondering where convenience ends and an Orwellian reality begins.</p>
