  <p>Crude oil prices slid by more than 2% in early Asian trading on Monday, pulling back from a sharp multi-day rally as global energy markets brace for what traders are calling Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent's "Economic D-Day." While WTI futures slipped to around $85.18 per barrel and Brent crude retreated to $92.32, the sudden correction reflects a tense mix of profit-taking and high-stakes anticipation over impending U.S. sanctions on Tehran. For everyday consumers and investors watching energy markets, this volatility isn't just a headline about commodities—it is a direct window into how macroeconomic policy, geopolitical conflict, and market psychology collide.</p>

  <h2 id="what-happened">What Happened in the Energy Markets?</h2>
  <p>The sudden downturn on Monday followed a tumultuous week that saw both major oil benchmarks surge by more than 5%. That earlier rally was driven by escalating rhetoric between Washington and Iran, plunging Iranian crude exports, and a near-halt in commercial tanker traffic through the critical Strait of Hormuz. CENTCOM reported over the weekend that a U.S. blockade of Iranian ports had redirected roughly 70 commercial vessels and disabled three, while Iranian authorities warned ships against violating new transit protocols.</p>
  
  <p>Despite these lingering supply bottlenecks and a lack of confirmed safe-passage guarantees, traders chose Monday morning to lock in profits from last week's gains. The primary driver behind the sudden pause, however, was not improving geopolitical stability, but the looming press conference scheduled by U.S. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent to unveil aggressive new economic measures against Iran. Markets are holding their breath to see how hard the financial hammer will fall.</p>
