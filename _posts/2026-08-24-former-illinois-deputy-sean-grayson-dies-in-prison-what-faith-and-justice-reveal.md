<section id="what-happened">
<h2>What Happened: The Final Chapter of a Tragic Case</h2>
<p>Sean Grayson’s death in custody marks the end of a high-profile legal battle that began in July 2024. Grayson, then a deputy for Sangamon County, Illinois, responded to a 911 call placed by 36-year-old Sonya Massey, who reported a suspected intruder at her home in Springfield. Body camera footage later revealed a horrifying escalation: after asking Massey to tend to a pot of boiling water on her stove, Grayson directed her to walk away, threatened to shoot her, and subsequently fired three shots into her face when she ducked behind a counter holding a red oven mitt.</p>
<p>The incident sparked widespread national outrage, demands for police reform, and profound grief. In October, a jury convicted Grayson of second-degree murder, and in January, he was sentenced to 20 years behind bars. Last month, Grayson sought early release, citing his stage 4 colon cancer diagnosis. However, the Illinois Prisoner Review Board denied the request by a 3-0 vote, supported by prosecutors who argued his volatile history posed an ongoing threat. Following news of Grayson's death, attorneys Ben Crump and Antonio Romanucci, representing the Massey family, issued a statement reiterating that Sonya's unjustifiable death and her family's enduring pain cannot be erased by terminal illness, while offering standard prayers of peace for all grieving families.</p>
