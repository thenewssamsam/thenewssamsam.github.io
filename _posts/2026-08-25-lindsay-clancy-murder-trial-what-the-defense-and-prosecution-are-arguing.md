    <p style="font-size: 1.1em; margin-bottom: 16px;">
      Few criminal cases in recent New England history have stirred the raw, agonizing collision between profound mental illness and the cold mechanics of the American legal system quite like the trial of Lindsay Clancy. The Duxbury, Massachusetts mother stands at the center of a closely watched legal proceeding that forces jurors to look past the undeniable horror of what happened and confront a deeply complicated question: When severe psychiatric crisis strips a person of their reality, where does legal culpability lie?
    </p>
    <p style="margin-bottom: 16px;">
      As opening statements and testimonies unfold, the courtroom has become a battleground of medical experts, legal definitions, and public grief. It is a trial that demands not just a verdict on a set of charges, but an examination of how society understands the breaking point of the human mind.
    </p>

  <section id="what-happened" style="margin-bottom: 24px;">
    <h2 style="font-size: 1.5em; color: #2d3436; border-left: 4px solid #6c5ce7; padding-left: 10px; margin-bottom: 14px;">What Happened and Where the Case Stands</h2>
    <p style="margin-bottom: 16px;">
      According to reports from NBC News and other outlets covering the proceedings, the tragic events in Duxbury left three young children dead and shook a quiet suburban community to its core. Lindsay Clancy, a labor and delivery nurse with no prior criminal record, was hospitalized following the incident after surviving a severe fall. 
    </p>
    <p style="margin-bottom: 16px;">
      The core of the trial is not a dispute over physical actions—the defense does not contest what Clancy did—but rather her state of mind at the exact moment she did it. Prosecutors and defense attorneys are presenting starkly contrasting narratives to the jury, turning the courtroom into an exploration of the limits of human endurance and the legal thresholds of criminal responsibility.
    </p>
  </section>

    <h2 style="font-size: 1.5em; color: #2d3436; border-left: 4px solid #6c5ce7; padding-left: 10px; margin-bottom: 14px;">A Psychology and Behavior Perspective: When the Mind Fractures</h2>
    <p style="margin-bottom: 16px;">
      From a psychological standpoint, cases like this often force us to confront concepts like cognitive dissonance, acute psychological decompensation, and the terrifying reality of postpartum psychosis. Unlike standard criminal motives driven by anger, greed, or malice, psychiatric emergencies involve a profound severing from reality.
    </p>
    <p style="margin-bottom: 16px;">
      Psychologists and behavioral experts emphasize that severe mental illnesses—such as postpartum psychosis—are medical emergencies that can drastically distort perception, impulse control, and rational thought. When a person is experiencing delusions or command hallucinations, their brain is operating under a completely altered framework of survival and logic.
    </p>

    <h3 style="font-size: 1.2em; color: #2d3436; margin-top: 16px; margin-bottom: 8px;">The Burden of Proof and Psychiatric Defense</h3>
    <p style="margin-bottom: 16px;">
      The legal threshold for an insanity defense or a lack of criminal responsibility is notoriously difficult to meet. Juries are asked to look past the visceral horror of the crime and evaluate whether the defendant understood the nature of their actions or was capable of conforming their conduct to the requirements of the law. This creates a deep tension for observers: balancing deep compassion for acute medical suffering with the demand for accountability and justice for the victims.
    </p>
