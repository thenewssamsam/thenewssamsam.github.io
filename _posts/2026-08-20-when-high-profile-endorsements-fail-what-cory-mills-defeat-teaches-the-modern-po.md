<section class="ncg-intro">
    <p>In modern politics, a high-profile endorsement is often treated as political armor—a shield capable of deflecting almost any institutional scandal or legislative misstep. But on Tuesday night in Florida's 7th Congressional District, that conventional wisdom suffered a major fracture. Rep. Cory Mills, a Republican incumbent backed by Donald Trump, lost his primary bid to former TV news anchor Ryan Elijah, proving that personal brand stability and institutional trust can ultimately override even the most coveted backing in American politics.</p>
    <p>For observers watching labor markets, organizational leadership, and high-stakes career management, the race offers a fascinating case study. When professional misconduct allegations collide with performance metrics, how resilient is any individual's career capital? Let us break down what this surprising primary upset reveals about reputation management, institutional accountability, and the limits of external patronage in high-visibility roles.</p>

He had secured the endorsement of Donald Trump, which has historically served as a decisive catalyst in Republican primaries across the country. However, Mills was weighed down by mounting turbulence, including an ongoing House Ethics Committee investigation and unconfirmed allegations of a federal probe—pressures that dogged his campaign for months.</p>
