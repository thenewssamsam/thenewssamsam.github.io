  <p class="ncg-lead">When the U.S. government reopened a border crossing in Douglas, Arizona, to livestock from Mexico, it was billed as a decisive step toward tackling the record-high grocery bills plaguing American families. But behind the political headlines, agricultural economists are offering a sobering reality check: your steak and ground beef prices aren't dropping anytime soon. Here is a look at the economic mechanics driving the cattle market, why a border reopening takes time to ripple down to supermarket aisles, and what this means for household budgets.</p>

  <h2 id="what-happened">What Happened at the Border?</h2>
  <p>The U.S. Department of Agriculture announced that concerns over the spread of the New World screwworm had lessened sufficiently to permit livestock movements through a crossing in Douglas, Arizona. Mexican President Claudia Sheinbaum confirmed during a morning press conference in Mexico City that the border in Sonora was officially open for livestock, with cattle crossing by mid-afternoon.</p>
  
  <p>This reopening is part of a broader federal effort to cool soaring beef costs. President Donald Trump announced plans to allow up to 331,000 tons of imported ground beef into the U.S. tariff-free over a 90-day window. The White House originally closed the border to livestock imports from Mexico more than a year ago to contain the flesh-eating parasite—a move that successfully protected livestock health but severely tightened an already strained supply of cattle available for slaughter in the U.S.</p>

  <h2 id="supply-crunch">The Anatomy of a 75-Year Low in U.S. Herds</h2>
  <p>The resistance of beef prices to falling is rooted in a much deeper structural deficit. On January 1, the USDA reported that the U.S. cattle herd shrank to 86.2 million head—the lowest figure recorded in 75 years. This contraction has been driven by a combination of persistent droughts across major cattle-producing regions and nearly two decades of low cattle prices that forced ranchers to liquidate their herds.</p>
