    <h3>The Familiarity Bias</h3>
    <p>Incumbency, such as we see with Sen. Moody, often triggers "familiarity bias." Humans have a natural tendency to prefer what they already know. This "mere exposure effect" creates a psychological comfort zone that makes challengers work twice as hard to overcome the status quo.</p>

    <h3>Group Identity and In-Group Favoritism</h3>
    <p>In the Democratic primary, the divergence between Nixon and Vindman highlights "in-group favoritism." Voters often align with candidates who mirror their specific social or ideological identity. When a candidate articulates the values of a voter’s perceived "tribe," the psychological reward is higher than that of a candidate who might be seen as an outsider, regardless of policy specifics.</p>
