  <section id="what-happened">
    <h2>What Happened: The Sudden Shift in US-South Korea Drills</h2>
    <p>South Korea's defense ministry recently announced that its annual joint military exercises with the United States—known as the 2026 Ulchi Freedom Shield—would be trimmed down in both scale and duration. Originally scheduled to run through August 27, the drills will now wrap up on August 21 following a direct request from Washington.</p>
    
    <p>According to reports from Seoul correspondents Tabby Wilson and Jake Kwon, neither South Korean nor US military officials were given advance warning about the decision. President Donald Trump explicitly linked the curtailment to South Korea's refusal to join US-led actions regarding Iran, stating in the Oval Office that maintaining 39,000 American troops in South Korea warrants mutual cooperation on other fronts. Meanwhile, leaders in Pyongyang and Washington are discussing potential future diplomatic meetings, leaving regional allies such as Japan and Taiwan watching closely as foundational defense pacts experience sudden friction.</p>

    <p>For adults following international relations, it is a masterclass in realpolitik. But for families living in an era where global news flashes across pocket screens instantly, macro-level political maneuvering inevitably filters down into household anxiety.</p>
