<section id="what-happened">
<h2>What Happened in the Energy Markets?</h2>
<p>Crude oil prices experienced a turbulent mid-week session, dropping for three consecutive days before making a sudden, volatile U-turn. U.S. crude oil fell as low as $79 per barrel early Wednesday—its lowest point since mid-August—while international benchmark Brent crude dipped toward $85 a barrel. However, those lower prices proved short-lived. Markets quickly reversed course following reports of potential Russian escalation in Ukraine, ultimately settling with U.S. crude at $82.23 and Brent at $87.84 per barrel.</p>

<p>This rollercoaster ride was driven by a push-and-pull between hopeful diplomatic signals in the Middle East and renewed anxieties in Eastern Europe. On the positive side, reports of renewed talks between Iran and Oman regarding the reopening of the critical Strait of Hormuz, alongside statements from Secretary of State Marco Rubio that the administration does not expect to resume military strikes on Iran, initially injected optimism into the markets. President Donald Trump also posted on social media that mines had been cleared from international waters in the Strait, though shipping analytics firm Kpler reported that actual vessel crossings remained heavily subdued at just a fraction of pre-war averages.</p>

<p>As oil prices trended lower over the multi-day slide, bond yields moved downward in tandem. Mortgage lenders responded to the falling yields by trimming rates, with the average 30-year fixed mortgage rate settling at 6.74% early Wednesday.</p>
