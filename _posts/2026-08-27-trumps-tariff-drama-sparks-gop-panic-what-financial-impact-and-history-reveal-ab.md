  <section id="what-happened">
    <h2>What Happened: The 10-Week Tariff Shock</h2>
    <p>The political calendar leaves little room for error when control of Congress hangs in the balance. With voters heading to the polls in November, the administration's sudden pivot back to aggressive protectionism caught Capitol Hill off guard. Talks between Washington and Ottawa collapsed following late demands and competing signals, leading to new threats of punitive tariffs and a September 8 deadline for potential Canadian retaliation.</p>
    
    <p>Compounding the trade friction, the administration advanced plans to increase ground beef imports temporarily. The stated goal is addressing domestic supply crunches and lowering grocery bills before voters cast their ballots. However, the political reality on the ground has proven remarkably hostile. Lawmakers from ranching strongholds like Wyoming, Arkansas, Nebraska, and Texas swiftly denounced the move, arguing that any short-term relief for consumers will come at the devastating expense of American cattle producers.</p>

When a trade dispute escalates with a primary trading partner like Canada, the immediate financial ripple effects hit cross-border supply chains, manufacturing inputs, and retail shelves.</p>
