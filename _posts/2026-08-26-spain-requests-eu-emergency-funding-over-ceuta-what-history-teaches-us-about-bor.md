  <section class="ncg-section" id="what-happened">
    <h2>What Happened in Ceuta?</h2>
    <p>Spain has formally petitioned the European Commission for emergency financial assistance to cope with an unprecedented migration crisis in Ceuta, its North African enclave. The move follows a chaotic late-July surge where more than 70,000 individuals rushed the border, fueled by intense poverty and misinformation.</p>

    <p>Tragically, official numbers indicate at least 90 lives were lost on both sides of the border during the rush, with human rights organizations citing even higher tolls. While the majority of those who crossed have since returned to Morocco, an estimated 3,500 to 7,000 people remain in Ceuta. This remaining population includes unaccompanied minors who cannot legally be deported under current international and domestic frameworks.</p>
    <p>In response to the bottleneck, the Spanish cabinet approved a "single command" structure led by Territorial Policy Minister Angel Victor Torres to expedite returns while managing humanitarian obligations. Meanwhile, European Commission spokesman Markus Lammert confirmed that Brussels received the formal request and is prioritizing a swift assessment.</p>
