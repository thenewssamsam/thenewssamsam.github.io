    <h1 style="font-size: 2.2rem; color: #002855; line-height: 1.3; margin: 0 0 10px 0;">Penn State Drug Ring Arrests: What Pittsburgh and State College Communities Are Facing Now</h1>
    <p style="font-size: 1.15rem; color: #444444; font-weight: 400; margin: 0;">When a prominent Pittsburgh attorney is accused of hiding evidence for a campus drug ring allegedly run by his son, the shockwaves instantly cross regional lines, shaking both a major university town and a corporate legal community.</p>
  </header>

  <section id="ncg-intro" style="margin-bottom: 30px;">
    <p>News travels fast between the rolling hills of Centre County and the urban corridors of Western Pennsylvania, but few recent headlines have bridged those geography points quite as jarringly as the unfolding Pennsylvania Office of Attorney General case involving a Penn State fraternity drug ring. Fourteen individuals were charged in a sweeping bust that revealed cocaine was being cut, packaged, and distributed directly out of campus fraternity houses—specifically involving the Delta Upsilon and Sigma Chi chapters.</p>
    
    <p>Yet among the cluster of student names and young adult suspects, one specific individual stands out in stark relief: Paul Robinson, a seasoned managing attorney at a prominent Pittsburgh law firm. According to investigators, Robinson is the sole nonstudent charged in the sweep, accused of felony tampering and hindering apprehension after allegedly attempting to conceal a safe containing drugs and cash. For the communities of Pittsburgh and State College, this case is no longer just a routine campus disciplinary matter or a localized drug bust. It has morphed into a complex local impact story raising urgent questions about parental complicity, professional ethics, legal accountability, and the dark underbelly of Greek life systems.</p>
  </section>

    <p>According to official statements released by the Pennsylvania Office of Attorney General, the operation was structured with alarming organizational efficiency. Cocaine was brought into the Delta Upsilon and Sigma Chi fraternity houses, where university pledges were allegedly tasked with cutting and packaging the narcotics. From there, the illicit substances were distributed primarily to other students across the campus.</p>
