        <h2 style="font-size: 1.5rem; font-weight: 700; color: #1f2937; margin-bottom: 12px;">What Happened: A Fragile Pause in the Strait of Hormuz</h2>
        <p style="margin-bottom: 16px;">Nearly a month after active fighting came to a halt, the geopolitical standoff between the United States and Iran has entered a new, highly financialized phase. While physical naval battles have paused, high-stakes economic pressure and international diplomacy are moving to center stage. Qatari Prime Minister Sheikh Mohammed bin Abdulrahman al-Thani is heading to Tehran to restart stalled negotiations, aiming primarily to restore normal commercial navigation in the vital Strait of Hormuz and safeguard global energy and food supply chains.</p>

        <p style="margin-bottom: 16px;">Despite the cessation of direct military fire, diplomatic breakthroughs remain elusive. The U.S. government maintains that Tehran has violated the existing memorandum of understanding by targeting commercial shipping vessels. In response, Washington has reinstated a naval blockade against Iranian ports and pivoted aggressively toward economic warfare. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent rolled out a fresh sanctions campaign aimed at dismantling the financial networks and third-party corporate entities helping Iran generate revenue and bypass international restrictions.</p>
        <p style="margin-bottom: 16px;">Iran has sharply condemned these measures, dismissing the Treasury's actions as "systemic bullying" designed to undermine international law. Meanwhile, Iranian officials have publicly mocked the aggressive U.S. economic posture, pointing to the ongoing complexities of modern global trade networks that cannot be easily shut down by fiat decree.</p>
