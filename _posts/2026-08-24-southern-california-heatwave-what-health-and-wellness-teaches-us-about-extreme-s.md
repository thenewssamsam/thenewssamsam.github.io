<h2 id="what-happened">What Happened: A Historic Heat Dome Settles Over SoCal</h2>
<p>According to the National Weather Service, a potent warming trend has gripped the region, prompting widespread excessive heat warnings and Next Weather Alerts. Daytime temperatures in the Los Angeles area are hovering in the mid-to-high 90s, while inland valleys and the Inland Empire are threatened with punishing triple digits reaching up to 112 degrees by midweek.</p>
<p>What makes this particular weather event especially grueling is the lack of overnight relief. Low temperatures are refusing to drop significantly, breaking record high minimums across multiple neighborhoods. Without cooling nighttime lows, homes without reliable air conditioning act as heat traps, compounding the physiological stress on residents.</p>

<h2 id="health-wellness-perspective">A Health and Wellness Perspective on Extreme Heat</h2>
<p>When the thermometer climbs past 105 degrees, our bodies undergo an intense biological struggle to maintain a stable internal temperature. From a health and wellness standpoint, heat is not just an atmospheric inconvenience; it is a profound physiological stressor that affects everything from cardiovascular function to cognitive clarity.</p>

<h3 id="mental-and-emotional-toll">The Hidden Mental Toll of Sustained Heat</h3>
<p>We rarely talk about how prolonged heat waves affect our psychological well-being. Studies in behavioral health consistently show that sustained high temperatures correlate with increased irritability, anxiety, and interpersonal conflict. When our bodies are locked in a continuous loop of heat stress, our window of tolerance shrinks. Recognizing this internal friction allows us to practice patience with ourselves and those around us.</p>
