    <h2 id="what-happened" style="font-size: 1.5rem; font-weight: 700; color: #1a202c; margin-top: 40px; margin-bottom: 15px;">What Happened in the SEC Vote</h2>
    <p>
        The landscape of collegiate athletics shifted dramatically when university presidents and chancellors across the Southeastern Conference gathered to vote on a strict new deterrent. Under the freshly approved rules, any SEC program fielding former professional players—including veterans of the NFL, NBA, and WNBA—will face draconian penalties. These include a 50% suspension for the head coach and financial penalties equal to half of the sport's entire operating budget.
    </p>
    <p>
        Just a day earlier, LSU athletic director Verge Ausberry and university president Wade Rousse had voted in favor of banning former professionals outright. Yet when the concrete penalty structure came to the floor Wednesday, Rousse abstained. The vote still passed 15-0, but LSU's sudden diplomatic sidestep speaks volumes. 
    </p>
    <p>
        The Tigers have been arguably the most aggressive program in the country attempting to leverage legal injunctions and roster loopholes. Before the vote, LSU had targeted former Ole Miss tight end Dae'Quan Wright, defensive tackle Zxavian Harris, and Notre Dame defensive end Junior Tuihalamaka. Sources indicate LSU leadership even encouraged player representatives to file lawsuits against the NCAA, strategically landing in Louisiana courtrooms. Meanwhile, other power conferences like the Big Ten have enacted similar bans, creating a fractured collegiate landscape holding its breath for an inevitable courtroom showdown on August 31.
    </p>
