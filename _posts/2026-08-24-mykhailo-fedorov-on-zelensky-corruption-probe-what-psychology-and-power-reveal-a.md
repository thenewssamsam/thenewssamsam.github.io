<section id="what-happened" class="ncg-section">
<h2>What Happened: The Fedorov-Zelensky Clash</h2>
<p>Mykhailo Fedorov, a 35-year-old former minister who modernized Ukraine's military and pioneered its drone warfare strategy, was dismissed from his post following reported friction with military leadership. Shortly after his departure, Fedorov released a video calling for wartime elections and demanding that President Volodymyr Zelensky answer direct questions regarding potential corruption within his inner circle.</p>
<p>While Fedorov emphasized that he never witnessed Zelensky engage in personal corruption, he argued that democratic accountability cannot be indefinitely suspended under martial law. Meanwhile, anti-corruption investigators announced a major probe into a multi-million-dollar misappropriation scheme involving former officials. President Zelensky countered that holding elections during an active invasion poses an existential risk, comparing the move to a "tsunami" that could fracture the country from within.</p>

<h2>The Psychology of Wartime Power and Normalcy Bias</h2>
<p>When societies enter prolonged crises like the war in Ukraine, human psychology shifts dramatically. Citizens and leaders alike often lean into <strong>groupthink</strong> and <strong>normalcy bias</strong>—rallying tightly around central figures while suppressing internal dissent to project a unified front against an external threat. For months, questioning wartime governance was viewed as taboo, interpreted almost as a betrayal of the broader defense effort.</p>
