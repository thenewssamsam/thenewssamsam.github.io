<h2 id="what-happened">What Happened at KPMG Australia</h2>
<p>When an audit and consulting giant stumbles, the shockwaves ripple far beyond its glass-fronted corporate towers. KPMG Australia has officially announced it is cutting 27 partners and approximately 360 employees. This sweeping workforce reduction arrives directly on the heels of a damaging audit leaks scandal that effectively severed the firm from lucrative new government contracts.</p>

<p>Reporting its annual financial results, KPMG revealed that overall revenue fell 1% year-on-year down to $2.26 billion for the 2025–26 period. While the core audit arm managed modest revenue growth of 11%, the consulting division took a devastating 16.9% plunge. Consequently, the vast majority of the job losses are concentrated within that struggling consulting sector.</p>

<p>New chief executive John Sams pointed to a confluence of pressures. Speaking candidly about the firm's outlook, Sams noted that weak demand is expected to persist well beyond the next 12 months, exacerbated by a sluggish domestic economy. Crucially, leadership also acknowledged internal missteps. Sams stated that the firm recognizes <em>"the challenges created by our own failings, and the work we must continue to do to rebuild trust."</em></p>
