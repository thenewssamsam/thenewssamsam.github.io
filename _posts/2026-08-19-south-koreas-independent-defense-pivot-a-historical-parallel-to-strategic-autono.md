    <p>In a significant shift for the Korean Peninsula, South Korean President Lee Jae Myung has publicly called for accelerated independent defense capabilities. This move comes as the U.S. military prepares to scale back joint exercises following directives from President Trump. For observers of global security, this moment echoes classic historical transitions where nations have sought to balance alliance commitments with the necessity of sovereign military readiness.</p>

  <section id="what-happened">
    <h2>The Current Situation</h2>
    <p>Following reports that U.S. Forces Korea commander Gen. Xavier Brunson visited the Defense Ministry to discuss reducing joint military drills, the South Korean government has clarified its stance. President Lee emphasized that while the alliance with the United States remains the bedrock of national security, South Korea must prepare for "worst-case scenarios" by bolstering its own military strength. This includes pushing for a nuclear-powered submarine program and enhancing integrated operational capabilities.</p>
  </section>
