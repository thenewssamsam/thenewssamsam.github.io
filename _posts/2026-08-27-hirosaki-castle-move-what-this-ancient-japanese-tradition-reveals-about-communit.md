<h2 id="what-happened">What Happened at Hirosaki Castle?</h2>
<p>The iconic fortified tower of Hirosaki Castle, located in Japan's northern Aomori Prefecture, has begun its final homecoming journey. Originally shifted 80 metres in 2015 to allow structural repairs to its deteriorating stone walls, the three-storey wooden structure is now being returned to its rightful place.</p>

<p>Rather than dismantling the historic building—a masterpiece originating from the Edo period (1603-1868)—engineers hoisted it onto a temporary platform in one piece. Since late March, groups of approximately 80 volunteers have taken turns heaving on ropes to advance the massive structure a few centimetres at a time. Project leaders estimate that heavy machinery will finalize the placement by November, with a full public reopening slated for 2033 following roof and facade restorations.</p>

<h2 id="the-hikiya-tradition">The Hikiya Technique: Preserving More Than Just Wood</h2>
<p>The method being used is called <em>hikiya</em>, a traditional Japanese practice of relocating buildings without demolition that dates back centuries. Project head Kensuke Hayasaka noted that involving the public was a deliberate choice to let people "experience history physically."</p>
