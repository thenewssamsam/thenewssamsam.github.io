  <section class="ncg-section" id="what-happened">
    <h2>What Happened: The Boston University and Mass General Brigham Study</h2>
    <p>A landmark study published in <em>The British Medical Journal</em> by researchers from Massachusetts General Brigham and Boston University has sent shockwaves through the sports world. According to reporting by Jarrett Bell of USA TODAY Sports, the research examined 878 deaths of former NFL players over a six-year period from 2016 to 2021. Among the 231 players who chose to donate their brains for medical examination, an alarming 91.4%—roughly 1-in-4 of all players who died during that span, when adjusted across the broader sample—were found to have suffered from chronic traumatic encephalopathy, commonly known as CTE.</p>

Conversely, when stark statistics like a 91.4% positivity rate among donated brains are published, the pendulum can swing violently toward catastrophic anxiety. Former players experiencing routine memory lapses or everyday mood fluctuations may instantly fear the worst, viewing themselves through the lens of a definitive, untreatable decline.</p>

    <h3>Cognitive Dissonance in the Football Community</h3>
    <p>For millions of fans and participants, football represents discipline, glory, teamwork, and entertainment. Confronting the harsh biological toll of the sport creates intense <em>cognitive dissonance</em>—the mental discomfort experienced by a person holding two contradictory beliefs: loving a game while recognizing its severe neurological risks. Resolving this tension requires neither blind denial nor knee-jerk nihilism, but rather an honest reckoning that demands safer equipment, better rule enforcement, and robust medical support systems.</p>
