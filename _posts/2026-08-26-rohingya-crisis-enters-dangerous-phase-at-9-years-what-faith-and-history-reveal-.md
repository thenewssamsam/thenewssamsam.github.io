<section id="what-happened">
<h2>What Happened: Nine Years in Limbo</h2>
<p>The exodus that created the world&apos;s largest refugee settlement began on August 25, 2017. In a single month, Myanmar&apos;s military and armed Rakhine civilians burned hundreds of villages and killed at least 6,700 people, according to Doctors Without Borders. Between 700,000 and 750,000 Rohingya crossed the border into Bangladesh seeking safety. Today, that number has swelled past one million living in congested camps, with over half being children who have known no other life.</p>

<p>Hopes for a safe repatriation have evaporated. Myanmar&apos;s 2021 military coup plunged the nation into a bitter civil war. Control of Rakhine state has largely shifted to the Arakan Army—an ethnic armed group facing its own accusations of abusing the remaining Rohingya population—while a severe junta blockade has choked off food and medicine. Rather than returning home, desperate refugees continue crossing the border, with the U.N. refugee agency reporting 154,000 new arrivals since 2024.</p>

<h2>The Human Toll of Fraying Lifelines</h2>
<p>Barred from legal employment in Bangladesh, the refugees depend almost entirely on international aid. Yet, donor fatigue and shifting global priorities have caused the safety net to fray dangerously. In April 2026, U.N. World Food Program rations were slashed to as little as $7 a person per month under a new tiered system. Chronic malnutrition rates among children under five remain alarmingly high.</p>
