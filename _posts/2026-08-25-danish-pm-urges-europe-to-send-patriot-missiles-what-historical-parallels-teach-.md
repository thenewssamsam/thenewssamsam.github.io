    <p>Speaking directly to the press in Kyiv, Danish Prime Minister Mette Frederiksen made a blunt assessment of Europe's military capabilities and political will. The missing ingredient, she argued, is not hardware availability, but raw political determination.</p>
    
    <p>Frederiksen's warning comes with an explicit seasonal deadline. Without an immediate influx of interceptors, Ukraine faces a brutal winter campaign in which Russian forces are expected to ruthlessly target civilian infrastructure with ballistic missiles. While European nations have gradually increased their financial and material commitments over the years, the continent still relies heavily on American logistical support and licensing agreements—particularly because the United States holds the primary production licenses for the Patriot missile systems.</p>
    
    <p>This development arrives alongside complementary announcements from other allied leaders. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy recently noted that Kyiv requires up to 360 Patriot interceptors for the winter months, while pointing out that Germany has scheduled deliveries of hundreds of PAC-2 missiles for 2027-2028. Additionally, France has pledged incoming interceptor missiles and new radar systems following direct talks between Zelenskyy and French President Emmanuel Macron.</p>
