<h2 id="health-wellness-perspective" class="ncg-h2">A Health and Wellness Perspective on Extreme Heat</h2>
<p class="ncg-p">When temperatures climb into the triple digits, our bodies undergo significant physiological stress. Thermoregulation—the body's ability to maintain its internal temperature—becomes overworked as humidity and heat trap moisture on the skin. Understanding how heat exhaustion and heatstroke develop can mean the difference between minor discomfort and a medical emergency.</p>

<p class="ncg-p">Public health officials emphasize that high overnight temperatures are particularly dangerous. Without a cooling reprieve at night, the human body cannot adequately recover from daytime heat exposure, placing cumulative stress on the cardiovascular system. This is especially true for older adults, young children, and outdoor workers.</p>

<h2 id="practical-response" class="ncg-h2">How Should Readers Respond?</h2>
<p class="ncg-p">Surviving a severe heatwave safely requires practical, intentional adjustments to your daily routine:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Hydrate Continuously:</strong> Drink water throughout the day before you feel thirsty, avoiding excessive caffeine or alcohol that can lead to dehydration.</li>
<li><strong>Seek Climate-Controlled Spaces:</strong> If your home lacks air conditioning, identify local public cooling centers, libraries, or shopping malls where you can take refuge during peak afternoon hours.</li>
<li><strong>Check on Neighbors:</strong> Reach out to elderly relatives, neighbors, and anyone living alone to ensure they have working cooling systems and adequate supplies.</li>
<li><strong>Modify Physical Activity:</strong> Reschedule outdoor exercise to the early morning hours or postpone it entirely until temperatures drop.</li>
</ul>
