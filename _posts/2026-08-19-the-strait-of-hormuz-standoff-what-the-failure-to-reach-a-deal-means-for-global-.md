<section id="introduction">
<p>The expiration of the 60-day negotiating window between the United States and Iran has left the Strait of Hormuz—the world’s most critical maritime energy artery—in a state of precarious limbo. With no deal in sight to resolve the blockade, energy markets are bracing for prolonged uncertainty. For the average consumer and global investor alike, the failure to reach an agreement is not just a diplomatic setback; it is a direct pressure point on the cost of energy and the stability of global supply chains.</p>
</section>

<section id="what-happened">
<h2>The Breakdown of Negotiations</h2>
<p>The memorandum of understanding (MOU) that aimed to halt hostilities and reopen the Strait of Hormuz has officially lapsed. Despite ongoing discussions, the core impasse remains: the U.S. insists on unrestricted commercial navigation, while Iran continues to exert control over the waterway as leverage for sanctions relief and the release of frozen assets. With the deadline passed, both nations have signaled a return to hardline positions, leaving the transit route—which carries roughly 20% of global oil and liquefied natural gas (LNG)—under significant threat.</p>
</section>
