  <section id="what-happened">
    <h2>What Happened: The 2028 Trial Date Set</h2>
    <p>Military judge Lt. Col. Michael Schrama announced Wednesday that the trial on the merits for Khalid Sheikh Mohammed will begin on Monday, June 5, 2028. Prosecutors had initially pushed for a 2027 timeline, but the judge ruled that schedule was premature given the mountain of procedural hurdles still facing the court.</p>
    <p>Mohammed, who was captured in Pakistan in 2003 and transferred to Guantanamo Bay in 2006, has spent more than two decades in detention. His prosecution—alongside co-defendants—has been mired in legal battles, most notably regarding the admissibility of evidence gathered during his interrogation at CIA "black sites."</p>
    <p>The timeline was further scrambled by a turbulent series of events involving plea agreements. In July 2024, Mohammed and two co-defendants reached a deal with military prosecutors to plead guilty to all charges—including the murder of 2,976 people—in exchange for taking the death penalty off the table in favor of life imprisonment. However, the agreement sparked fierce backlash from 9/11 families and victims' advocates. Days later, then-Defense Secretary Lloyd Austin revoked the deal, asserting that such a momentous decision should rest with the superior convening authority. Subsequent legal challenges wound their way through federal courts, with a D.C. appeals court ultimately ruling 2-1 that Austin acted within his authority.</p>
