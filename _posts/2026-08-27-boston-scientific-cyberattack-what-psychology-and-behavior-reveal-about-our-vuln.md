  <section id="what-happened">
    <h2>What Happened at Boston Scientific?</h2>
    <p>According to an official regulatory filing with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC), Massachusetts-based Boston Scientific began experiencing critical disruptions and limited access to its business applications and IT systems. The incident immediately impacted the company's ability to process and ship orders.</p>
    
    <p>Boston Scientific serves approximately 48 million patients annually, manufacturing life-sustaining medical technology including pacemakers and defibrillators. While the company stated that its investigation is ongoing and order processing is restricted, company representatives have remained tight-lipped regarding whether patient-facing implanted devices are directly compromised. Local reports from Ireland noted that thousands of staff members at the company Cork campus were sent home after widespread network communication failures.</p>

    <h2>The Psychology of Digital Dependency</h2>
    <p>From a psychological perspective, major cyberattacks on critical infrastructure expose a profound behavioral phenomenon known as <strong>automation bias</strong>—our deep-seated tendency to trust automated systems implicitly and underestimate the probability of their failure. Because modern medical technology operates seamlessly in the background of everyday life, society develops a false sense of absolute security.</p>

This cognitive bias causes people—and organizations—to underestimate the possibility of disaster even when warning signs accumulate. With Abbott, Medtronic, and Stryker all facing recent cyber intrusions, the writing has clearly been on the wall for the medical technology sector.</p>
