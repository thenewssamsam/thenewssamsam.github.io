  <section id="historical-parallel">
    <h2>A Historical Parallel: The Cost of Depleted Buffers</h2>
    <p>History teaches us that geopolitical conflicts often hinge on the exhaustion of strategic buffers. Much like the energy crises of the 1970s, today’s reliance on depleting oil stockpiles creates a "brittleness" in the global economy. When a nation’s emergency reserves drop to 40-year lows, the margin for error in diplomatic negotiation effectively vanishes.</p>
    
    <blockquote>
      "When national energy buffers are depleted to historic lows, the geopolitical 'safety valve' is removed, forcing leaders into high-stakes brinkmanship where economic stability becomes the first casualty."
    </blockquote>

    <p>Consider the 1973 oil embargo or the supply shocks of the early 1980s. In both instances, the market's reaction was driven not just by the conflict itself, but by the perceived inability of governments to act as a backstop against price spikes. Today, with the SPR falling below 300 million barrels, we are seeing a modern echo of that vulnerability. The difference today is the speed of global capital; in the 1980s, market reactions were buffered by slower information cycles. Today, the VIX index may be at a multi-year low, but history suggests that such calm is often the precursor to a sudden, sharp correction when energy security is tested.</p>
  </section>
