<h2 id="what-happened">What Happened: The Secrecy Behind Project Osprey</h2>
<p>When Louisiana officials announced a massive $100 billion SpaceX spaceport slated for coastal Vermilion Parish, the headlines focused on big numbers: 3,000 permanent jobs, a sprawling 125,000-acre tract, and a major win for Gov. Jeff Landry’s administration. But behind the celebratory press conferences lies a contentious web of paperwork. According to records obtained by The Times-Picayune | The Advocate, dozens of state and local officials, regulatory agencies, and private companies signed strict nondisclosure agreements (NDAs) to keep Project Osprey under wraps for months.</p>

<p>Louisiana Economic Development (LED) used the code name "Project Osprey" while facilitating at least 31 NDAs with figures ranging from legislative leaders like Senate President Cameron Henry to local parish officials in Vermilion Parish, utility regulators, and energy companies like Entergy. While Landry defended the secrecy as a necessary tactic to prevent other states—like Texas—from poaching the multi-billion-dollar deal, good government advocates argue that locking the public out of major infrastructure and land decisions creates a dangerous precedent.</p>

<h2 id="legal-rights">A Legal Rights Perspective: NDAs, Public Records, and Transparency</h2>
<p>From a legal standpoint, nondisclosure agreements in economic development represent a fascinating tug-of-war between statutory exemptions and the constitutional public right to know. Under Louisiana law, LED is permitted to keep certain prospective business negotiations confidential. Proponents argue these tools are essential in high-stakes corporate recruitment, where premature leaks can derail negotiations or spark bidding wars that inflate public costs.</p>

When top state lawmakers, environmental regulators, and local utility officials bind themselves to secrecy, the traditional system of checks and balances breaks down. Citizens lose their window to review public land sales—such as the 125,000-acre tract sold to SpaceX for $100 million after being acquired from ExxonMobil—before deals are finalized.</p>
