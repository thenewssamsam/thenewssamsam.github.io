<h2>What Happened: The USPS and the Midterms</h2>
<p>The legal tug-of-war over American voting procedures has taken another sharp turn. A federal judge in Boston has handed the Trump administration a temporary win, clearing the path for the U.S. Postal Service to continue preparations for executing the president's controversial executive order on mail-in voting. With the midterms rapidly approaching, this administrative step brings sweeping changes closer to reality.</p>

<p>U.S. District Judge Indira Talwani on Wednesday canceled her earlier nationwide ruling that had temporarily blocked the mailing agency from implementing the presidential directives. The pivot follows a procedural signal from the Supreme Court, which noted that earlier challenges had arrived prematurely before the USPS had formalized its operational rules. Now that the agency has released its finalized plan for federal ballot mail—focusing on delivering ballots exclusively to individuals listed on state-provided absentee rosters—the courtroom battle lines are shifting.</p>

<p>While the administration defends the order as a safeguard against rare non-citizen voting anomalies, critics argue it infringes upon the constitutional separation of powers that grants states primary authority over federal elections. As the countdown to the midterms accelerates, millions of voters are left wondering how these administrative gears will turn in practice.</p>
