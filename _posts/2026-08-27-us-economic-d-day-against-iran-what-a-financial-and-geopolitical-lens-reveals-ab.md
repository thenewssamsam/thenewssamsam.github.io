    <h1>US Economic D-Day Against Iran: What a Financial and Geopolitical Lens Reveals About Global Energy Shocks</h1>
  </header>

  <p class="ncg-intro">As Washington launches its latest coordinated push to cripple the Iranian economy through sweeping secondary sanctions, Tehran is threatening a fierce regional and global retaliation that could send shockwaves straight to consumer wallets worldwide. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent's announcement of what is being framed as an economic "D-Day" targets an intricate evasion network built over years—yet the immediate financial fallout and the potential for severe energy supply disruptions reveal how interconnected global markets truly remain.</p>

  <h2 id="what-happened">What Happened: The Economic D-Day and Tehran's Response</h2>
  <p>In a recent news conference, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent detailed Washington's intensified strategy to plug gaps in existing sanctions, adding strict penalties for Iranian trade partners operating in vital sectors like digital assets, technology, and shipping. The measures arrive on the heels of a six-month stalemate in the ongoing conflict involving the US, Israel, and Iran.</p>
  
  <p>While many analysts anticipated an avalanche of brand-new restrictions, the administration opted primarily to expand enforcement of current measures while dangling the threat of secondary penalties for countries refusing to cut financial ties with Tehran. Rather than backing down, Iranian officials have responded with sharp warnings of reciprocal economic and regional pain—including potential strikes on energy transit routes, threats against European nations supporting allied military efforts, and renewed debates over withdrawing from the Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty (NPT).</p>
