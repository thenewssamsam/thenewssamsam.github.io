<section id="what-happened">
<h2>What Happened: The 2028 Finalists</h2>
<p>The race to host the 2028 Democratic National Convention has officially cleared its latest hurdle. According to Democratic National Committee Chair Ken Martin, the field has been narrowed down to three finalist cities: <strong>Boston</strong>, <strong>Denver</strong>, and <strong>Philadelphia</strong>. The Associated Press first broke the news, signaling the next phase of a multi-month evaluation process managed by the Democratic National Convention Committee.</p>

<p>The announcement leaves notable casualties in its wake. Atlanta and Chicago—which hosted the 2024 gathering—were actively in the running earlier this year but ultimately did not make the cut. The convention itself is scheduled to take place from August 7 to 10, 2028, acting as a high-stakes launchpad for the party's future presidential nominee.</p>

<p>While DNC leadership emphasized the strength of all submitted bids, the exclusion of Atlanta sparked immediate pushback. Atlanta Mayor Andre Dickens expressed sharp disappointment in a letter obtained by CBS News Atlanta, arguing that the decision suggested the DNC undervalued Georgia's role in the party's past, present, and future. As party strategists weigh logistical capabilities, arena sizes, and regional political dynamics, looking backward at how these three finalist cities served past nominees offers a fascinating lens into party planning.</p>
