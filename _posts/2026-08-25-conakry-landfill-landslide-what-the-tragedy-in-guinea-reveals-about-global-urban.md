<section id="what-happened" style="margin-bottom: 32px;">
    <h2 style="font-size: 1.5rem; font-weight: 700; color: #2d2a4a; margin-bottom: 16px; border-bottom: 2px solid #6b46c1; padding-bottom: 6px;">What Happened: The Collapse at Dar Es Salam</h2>
    <p>Early on a Sunday morning in Conakry, Guinea, an industrial-scale catastrophe unfolded quietly in the dark. Heavy seasonal rains, arriving around 2 a.m., saturated a towering, mountainous heap of refuse at the Dar Es Salam landfill—the largest open-air waste site in the capital. The sheer weight of the water-logged trash proved too much for the unstable mass. It gave way in a violent cascade, burying nearby shacks and killing at least 30 people.</p>
    
    <p>According to local authorities and military engineering officials, the site had already been slated for closure that very day. Eviction notices had been issued to nearby residents, forcing many to pack up and leave. Yet, economic desperation and a lack of alternative housing meant that many families were still sleeping in the shadow of the massive garbage heap when the landslide struck. Six people were pulled from the debris with serious injuries, and search-and-rescue teams immediately scoured the ruined settlement for survivors.</p>
