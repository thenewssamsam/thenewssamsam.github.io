<h2>What Happened: The Emergence of Sava's Titanic</h2>
<p>The rusting hull of the steam tugboat known locally as "Sava's Titanic" has risen completely into view near the Serbian town of Sremska Mitrovica. According to local museum director and historian Andrija Popovic, parts of the wreck have occasionally been glimpsed during previous dry spells, but never with such stark exposure. The retreating waters of the Sava River—alongside plunging levels in the Danube, Rhine, and Tiber—are symptoms of a continent-wide climate squeeze.</p>

<h2>Historical Echoes: A Ship Marked by War</h2>
<p>Built in Budapest in 1913 when the city was still part of the Austro-Hungarian empire, the vessel originally bore the name <em>Vag</em>, after Slovakia's longest river. It worked tirelessly, towing heavy cargo along the Danube before geopolitical upheaval reshaped the map of Europe following World War I. Handed over as war reparations to the newly formed kingdom that would become Yugoslavia, it was renamed <em>The Slovenian</em> under mysterious circumstances.</p>
