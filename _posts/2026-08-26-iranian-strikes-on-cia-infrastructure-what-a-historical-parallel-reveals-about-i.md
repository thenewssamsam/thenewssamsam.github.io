<section id="what-happened">
  <h2>What Happened: The Anatomy of the Strikes</h2>
  <p>For decades, the standard narrative of modern intelligence operations assumed that premier spy agencies operated behind impenetrable layers of security, advanced missile defenses, and overseas sanctuary. But recent events in the Middle East have dramatically shattered that assumption.</p>
  
  <p>According to four sources cited by NBC News, Iranian missile and drone attacks have caused unprecedented destruction to buildings and specialized surveillance equipment utilized by the CIA and other American intelligence agencies across the region. The escalation, which followed US and Israeli operations against Iran that began in late February, has struck far deeper into the core architecture of US intelligence gathering than any previous conflict.</p>
  
  <p>Satellite imagery and military assessments have documented widespread hits on radars, satellite communications infrastructure, hangars, and runways. While earlier military tallies estimated repair costs for broader defense infrastructure between $2 billion and $5 billion, this new assessment specifically targets secretive intelligence posts. The staggering price tag has already forced congressional leaders to scramble for emergency funding while defense planners fundamentally rethink how to station personnel in a region saturated with advanced Iranian drone and missile tech.</p>

  <p>Historians often point to the concept of "imperial overstretch," a term popularized by historian Paul Kennedy. The core phenomenon is simple: as a dominant power's global commitments expand, the cost of protecting fixed assets eventually begins to outpace the economic and strategic returns of holding them. Iran’s sophisticated drone and missile campaigns represent a modern evolution of this dynamic. By utilizing relatively low-cost projectiles against multi-million-dollar radar arrays and intelligence hubs, adversaries exploit the financial asymmetry of modern warfare.</p>
