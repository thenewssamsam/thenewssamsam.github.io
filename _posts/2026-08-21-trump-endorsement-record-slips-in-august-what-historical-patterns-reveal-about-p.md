<h2 id="what-happened" class="ncg-section-heading">What Happened?</h2>
<p class="ncg-p">According to reporting by NBC News, President Donald Trump’s endorsement record for the election cycle has experienced some unexpected turbulence this month. While Trump-backed candidates still maintain a commanding overall tally—winning far more primaries than they lost, posting a record so far of 67-6—the month of August brought a string of losses that punctured an otherwise dominant run.</p>
<p class="ncg-p">With two critical primary tests looming next week, political strategists and campaigns are watching closely to see whether this late-month stumble is merely a statistical blip in an otherwise triumphant cycle or an early signal of shifting dynamics at the grassroots level.</p>

<h2 id="historical-perspective" class="ncg-section-heading">The Historical Parallel: The Limits of Presidential Coattails</h2>
<p class="ncg-p">History suggests that presidential influence in midterms and primary seasons is rarely uniform. When we look back at past executive administrations—from Dwight Eisenhower to Ronald Reagan and Barack Obama—a clear historical pattern emerges: a popular or influential leader can clear fields and anoint winners, but local context ultimately exerts its own gravity.</p>
<p class="ncg-p">In midterms and primary cycles, voters frequently balance national allegiance with distinct local grievances, candidate quality, and regional dynamics. A national endorsement acts as a powerful megaphone, but it cannot entirely erase local friction.</p>
