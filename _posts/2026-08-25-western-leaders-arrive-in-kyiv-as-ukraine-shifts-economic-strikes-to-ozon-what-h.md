  <section id="what-happened">
    <h2>What Happened: High-Level Visits Amid Escalating Logistics Warfare</h2>
    <p>This weekend, as Ukraine prepares to mark its national holiday, high-profile international figures have made their way into the capital. Finnish President Alexander Stubb, US Senators Richard Blumenthal and James Lankford, European Council President António Costa, and Luxembourg Prime Minister Luc Frieden have all arrived or are en route to Kyiv. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy utilized these diplomatic meetings to press for critical winter air defenses, specifically asking for a small percentage of US Patriot interceptor stockpiles to counter persistent Russian ballistic missile threats.</p>
    
    <div style="background: #fff8e1; border-left: 4px solid #ffa000; padding: 15px; margin: 20px 0; border-radius: 4px;">
      <p style="margin: 0; font-style: italic;">"Systematic strikes on storage centers holding dual-use goods are disrupting the enemy's logistics and negatively impacting Russia's economy as a whole." — Ukrainian Ministry of Defence</p>
    </div>

    <p>Simultaneously, the military campaign on the ground has taken a distinct economic turn. Following extensive drone operations against the retail giant Wildberries, Ukrainian forces struck facilities belonging to Russia's largest online marketplace, Ozon. Logistics complexes in Orenburg and Samara have taken direct hits, triggering rapid evacuations, fires, and indefinite warehouse suspensions. Meanwhile, Russian forces continued their bombardment of critical infrastructure, striking passenger and freight trains in the Kharkiv and Odesa regions. Amid these pressures, President Zelenskyy firmly rejected a surprise call from his former defense minister for wartime presidential elections, arguing that a national vote during active hostilities would trigger a catastrophic internal split.</p>
