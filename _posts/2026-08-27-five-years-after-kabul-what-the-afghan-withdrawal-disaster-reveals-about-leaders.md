    <h2>What Happened: The Anatomy of a Crisis</h2>
    <p>Five years ago, television screens flashed images that shocked the world: desperate crowds surging against the gates of Hamid Karzai International Airport in Kabul, people clinging to the outside of moving military transport planes, and ultimately, the devastating carnage of a suicide bombing that claimed nearly 200 lives.</p>

    <p>A new Wall Street Journal documentary, <em>The Path to Zero</em>, produced by filmmakers Michael and Thomas Pack, revisits those agonizing final days of America's two-decade engagement in Afghanistan. The film centers on the stark testimony of retired Marines, diplomats, and commanders who bore direct witness to the collapse.</p>
    
    <p>According to the documentary, the tragedy at Abbey Gate—carried out by ISIS-K terrorist Abdul Rahman al-Logari, who had been released from a Taliban-emptied prison during the chaotic early weeks of the drawdown—was merely the most visible symptom of a systemic policy failure. Military leaders had repeatedly urged the administration to maintain a residual force of roughly 2,500 troops and to secure vital infrastructure like Bagram Air Base. Instead, political imperatives to end the "forever war" by a strict deadline overrode on-the-ground counsel.</p>

As the August 30 evacuation deadline loomed, U.S. forces at Kabul airport were overwhelmed by civilians fleeing Taliban rule. CIA-trained Afghan special forces, known as Zero Units, were brought in to manage the crowds but resorted to violent crowd-control tactics out of desperation.</p>
