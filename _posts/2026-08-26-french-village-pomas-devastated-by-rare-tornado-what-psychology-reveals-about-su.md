  <h2 id="what-happened">What Happened in Pomas</h2>
  
  <p>Late Monday afternoon in the Aude department of southern France, an exceptional meteorological event struck without warning. The small village of Pomas, home to approximately one thousand residents, bore the brunt of a tornado defined by officials as being of "rare intensity." The storm brought down stone walls, ripped roofs straight off buildings, and uprooted ancient trees.</p>

"The nursery school is gone… We no longer have a school, we no longer have a town hall," Mayor Gine noted, estimating that roughly 300 of the village's 476 homes suffered damage.</p>

  <h2 id="psychology-perspective">The Psychology of Sudden Disasters: Why "War Zones" Happen in Seconds</h2>

  <p>When people like Mayor Gine compare a natural disaster to wartime bombing, they are describing more than just physical rubble—they are articulating a profound psychological rupture. Human beings rely on an innate sense of environmental predictability. We expect the ground to stay still, the walls of our homes to protect us, and our daily routines to follow a familiar rhythm.</p>

When that assumption is violently disproven, shock and trauma immediately follow.</p>
