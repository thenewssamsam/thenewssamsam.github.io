<section id="what-happened">
<h2>What Happened: Moke Follows Lala</h2>
<p>Just days after Hurricane Lala tore through parts of Hawaii, flooding communities, washing out roadways, and leaving thousands without power, the islands are bracing for a fresh round of severe weather. The National Hurricane Center tracked Tropical Storm Moke passing roughly 320 miles south-southwest of Hilo on Sunday, carrying sustained winds of around 40 mph.</p>

<p>While the storm's winds are relatively modest compared to a major hurricane, the primary danger comes from relentless moisture. Forecasters warn that Moke could dump up to 10 inches of rain across the Big Island, with isolated spots seeing up to 15 inches. For areas like Naalehu and windward slopes already saturated by Lala's historic rainfall—some parts saw over 43 inches last week—this additional water raises immediate concerns over life-threatening flash floods and mudslides.</p>

<p>Child development experts often emphasize that children mirror their parents' emotional temperature. When adults panic, children absorb that fear. Conversely, when parents frame emergency preparation as an adventure or a time of family closeness—playing board games by flashlight or sharing stories—it transforms potential trauma into a memory of safety and protection. Honest, age-appropriate communication ("The storm is bringing a lot of water, but our local leaders and helpers are working hard to keep us safe") builds trust without overwhelming young minds.</p>
