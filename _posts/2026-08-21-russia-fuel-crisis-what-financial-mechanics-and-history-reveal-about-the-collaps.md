<section id="what-happened">
<h2>What Happened: The Anatomy of a National Fuel Drought</h2>
<p>The numbers coming out of the Russian energy market are stark. According to monitoring app Gdebenz, nationwide petrol availability plummeted to just 28% of filling stations, down sharply from 41% only a week prior. Nearly all regions have implemented emergency restrictions, ranging from volume caps per customer to QR-based rationing and odd-even license plate schemes.</p>

<p>In Moscow, where queues at the pump were once practically unheard of, major operators like Gazprom Neft instituted a temporary 40-litre limit per fill-up, while Tatneft capped petrol at 50 litres and diesel at 60. Services like Yandex Fuel reported that AI-92 petrol was accessible at a mere one in 10 stations within the capital. Despite Kremlin reassurances from President Vladimir Putin characterizing the disruptions as "non-critical," the reality on the ground tells a story of systemic stress.</p>
