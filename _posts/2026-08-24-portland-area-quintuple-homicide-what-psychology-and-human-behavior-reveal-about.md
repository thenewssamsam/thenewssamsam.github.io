<section id="what-happened">
    <h2>What Happened in Forest Grove</h2>
    <p>A quiet rural property outside Portland, Oregon, became the center of a massive multi-state death investigation over the weekend. Authorities announced that five bodies, along with several domestic animals, were discovered following a vehicle fire response early Saturday morning in Forest Grove, located about 30 miles west of downtown Portland. By Sunday, the suspected perpetrator—identified as 36-year-old Benjamin Charles Parker—was found dead of an apparent suicide inside a vehicle nearly 180 miles away in Enumclaw, Washington.</p>
    
    <p>Washington County Sheriff Caprice Massey described the crime scene during a Sunday news conference as uniquely complex, noting that in her 22 years in law enforcement, she had never witnessed anything of this magnitude. Investigators are still working tirelessly to identify the victims, determine their relationships to one another and to Parker, and untangle the timeline of events. While officials have stated there is no ongoing threat to the public, the sheer shock of the incident leaves communities searching for answers.</p>

    <h2>A Psychology &amp; Behavior Perspective on Unimaginable Violence</h2>
    <p>When news breaks of a multi-victim tragedy like the Portland area quintuple homicide, our collective psyche immediately demands an explanation. How does a human being cross the threshold from everyday life into committing mass violence? Behavioral science tells us that extreme acts of violence rarely erupt in a vacuum, even when they appear sudden to the outside observer. They are almost always the culmination of prolonged internal distress, grievance, cognitive distortions, and a total collapse of empathetic connection.</p>

Without community touchpoints or casual daily oversight from neighbors, warning signs can easily go unnoticed until it is too late.</p>
