  <section id="what-happened" class="ncg-section">
    <h2>What Happened in South Carolina?</h2>
    <p>Early voting is officially underway across South Carolina for the high-stakes Republican runoff for the state's open U.S. Senate seat. Voters in counties like Anderson and Spartanburg have a narrow three-day window through Friday, August 21, to cast their ballots ahead of the final election day on Tuesday, August 25.</p>
    
    <p>Behind the scenes, local election officials have faced a demanding sprint. With just a thirty-day turnaround following the August 11 primary rather the typical ninety-day preparation window, administrators like Anderson County Director of Elections Laura Booth and Spartanburg County Director of Elections John Baucom scrambled to train new poll workers and streamline polling locations. Yet, despite the hurdles and compressed timeline, something unexpected happened: voter turnout has outpaced expectations. Spartanburg County even surpassed previous peak hourly voting rates, drawing over 1,300 votes on the very first day.</p>
