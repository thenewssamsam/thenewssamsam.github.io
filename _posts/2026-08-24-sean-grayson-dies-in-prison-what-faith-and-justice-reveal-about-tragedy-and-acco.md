  <section id="what-happened" class="ncg-section">
    <h2>What Happened</h2>
    <p>Sean Grayson, the former Sangamon County, Illinois, sheriff's deputy convicted of murdering 36-year-old Sonya Massey in her own home in July 2024, has died in prison. According to his trial attorney, Daniel Fultz, Grayson passed away around noon, roughly six months into his 20-year prison sentence for second-degree murder.</p>

    <p>Grayson, 32, had been battling severe health issues. During his sentencing in January, defense attorney Mark Wykoff noted that Grayson had been diagnosed with Stage 3 colon cancer in 2023, which later progressed to Stage 4 and spread to his liver, lungs, and rectum. In July, the Illinois Prisoner Review Board denied his request for a medical release. Officials stated it was not immediately clear whether cancer was the direct cause of death.</p>
    
    <p>The original tragedy unfolded on July 6, 2024, when Massey called 911 to report a suspected prowler outside her Springfield-area home. Body camera footage captured the fatal encounter inside the kitchen, where Grayson shot Massey three times after she picked up a pot of boiling water and uttered, "I rebuke you in the name of Jesus." The shooting sparked nationwide protests, calls for systemic reform, a $10 million civil settlement with Massey's family, and the passage of the Sonya Massey Act, which mandates stricter background checks for law enforcement hires.</p>
