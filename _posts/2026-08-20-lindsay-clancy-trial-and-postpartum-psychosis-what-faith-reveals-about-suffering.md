  <h2 id="what-happened">What Happened in the Clancy Case?</h2>
  <p>The facts presented in a Massachusetts courtroom are as devastating as they are complex. In January 2023, Patrick Clancy returned home from picking up dinner to find his three young children—Cora, five, Dawson, three, and eight-month-old Callan—dead, and his wife Lindsay severely injured from an attempted suicide. Clancy is not disputing that she carried out the acts, but her defense team maintains she was entirely detached from reality due to postpartum psychosis.</p>

  <p>Testimony from family members and medical experts painted a picture of a spiraling medical crisis. Just weeks prior, Clancy had told her family she was experiencing intrusive thoughts of harming her children, pleading for help and repeatedly stating, "This isn't me, I've never been like this before." Yet, on the very day of the tragedy, her husband testified that she appeared to be having one of her "best days," playing outside with the children in the snow. This sudden, volatile shift—what medical professionals describe as a light-switch effect—underscores the terrifying unpredictability of severe psychiatric emergencies.</p>

Postpartum psychosis is not a moral failing; it is a physical and neurological breakdown that affects the seat of human consciousness and emotion.</p>
