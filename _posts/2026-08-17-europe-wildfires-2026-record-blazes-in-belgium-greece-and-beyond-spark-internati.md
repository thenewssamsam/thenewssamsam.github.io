International cooperation mechanisms are being deployed as officials grapple with deep-seated soil dryness and high wind speeds.</p>

<section>
  <h2 id="belgium-high-fens">Belgium's Record-Breaking High Fens Blaze</h2>
  <p>In eastern Belgium, firefighters have been locked in an intense battle against a massive wildfire consuming the High Fens, a major nature reserve located near the German border. According to data from the European Forest Fire Information System <strong class="eu-fire-claim">[Confirmed — European Forest Fire Information System]</strong>, the fire has scorched approximately 30 square kilometers (12 square miles), making it one of the largest and most destructive wildfires in modern Belgian history.</p>
  
  <p>The current disaster has already far surpassed a 2011 blaze that burned nearly 14 square kilometers (5.4 square miles) in the same geographic area. Nicolas Yernaux, a spokesperson for the Public Service of Wallonia, noted that the severity stems from exceptionally dry subterranean conditions <strong class="eu-fire-claim">[Confirmed — Public Service of Wallonia]</strong>. “We’re dealing with something much deeper because we have very dry soil,” Yernaux stated.</p>
  
  <p>Local authorities in the Liège province municipalities of Waimes and Bütgenbach ordered approximately 600 residents to evacuate as wind shifts pushed smoke through populated districts. Over 250 emergency personnel—including Belgian and German firefighters, police officers, and military units—have been deployed under the direction of Liège provincial governor Hervé Jamar <strong class="eu-fire-claim">[Confirmed — Provincial Authorities]</strong>.</p>
</section>

<section>
  <h2 id="greece-tragedy">Tragedy on the Greek Island of Salamina</h2>
  <p>While northern and central European nations battled forest reserves, conditions turned fatal in southern Europe. Two people lost their lives in a sudden wildfire on the Greek island of Salamina, located west of Athens, according to local fire authorities <strong class="eu-fire-claim">[Confirmed — Greek Firefighters]</strong>.</p>
  
  <p>The victims were recovered in the village of Peristeria. A second major outbreak struck the Selini area, prompting coast guard and firefighting vessels to evacuate roughly 500 beachgoers from vulnerable coastal zones. Brigadier General Vasileios Vathrakogiannis of the Greek Fire Service explained that near gale-force winds propelled the fire dangerously close to residential sectors within minutes of ignition <strong class="eu-fire-claim">[Confirmed — Greek Fire Service]</strong>.</p>
</section>

<section>
  <h2 id="broader-european-impact">Widespread Fires in France and the UK</h2>
  <p>The multi-country crisis is part of a wider continental pattern driven by weeks of unrelenting heat. In France, the southwestern Landes region saw emergency crews managing a 17-square-kilometer (6.6 square miles) pine forest fire that forced the evacuation of approximately 650 people, according to Landes Prefect Gilles Clavreul <strong class="eu-fire-claim">[Confirmed — Landes Prefect]</strong>. This follows devastating earlier-summer fires in the neighboring Gironde department, which burned over 400 square kilometers.</p>
  
  <p>Meanwhile, in the United Kingdom, emergency teams combated 14 simultaneous wildfires across South Wales with military support. The incidents unfolded just days after official figures recorded the hottest day of the year in parts of England, with temperatures hitting 38.1 C (100.6 F), destroying more than a dozen homes in the West Midlands <strong class="eu-fire-claim">[Confirmed — UK Emergency Services]</strong>.</p>
</section>

<section>
  <h2 id="eu-response">International Aid and EU Civil Protection</h2>
  <p>Faced with blazes defying standard municipal responses, Belgium officially activated the European Union’s civil protection mechanism <strong class="eu-fire-claim">[Confirmed — European Commission]</strong>. EU Commissioner for Crisis Management Hadja Lahbib confirmed immediate cross-border deployments. Sweden dispatched two water-bombing aircraft, while the Netherlands and the Czech Republic contributed specialized firefighting helicopters.</p>
  
  <p>On the ground, civilian solidarity matched state action. In border regions, Belgian and German farmers utilized tractor-mounted tanker pumps to draw water from local lakes, directly supporting frontline crews working to protect towns such as Monschau from encroaching flames.</p>
</section>
