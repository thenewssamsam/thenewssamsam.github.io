  <section id="introduction">
    <p>For decades, residential solar energy has come with a massive financial barrier to entry. Traditional rooftop installations often require upfront costs in the tens of thousands of dollars, effectively locking out renters, apartment dwellers, and lower-income homeowners. But a newly passed piece of legislation in California aims to flip that economic model on its head.</p>
    
    <p>Senate Bill 868, which recently sailed through the state legislature with bipartisan support and now sits on Gov. Gavin Newsom’s desk, seeks to legalize and streamline plug-in balcony solar panels. By bypassing cumbersome utility interconnection red tape and expensive contractor fees, this emerging technology promises to bring decentralized clean energy straight to everyday household budgets.</p>

  <section id="what-happened">
    <h2>What Happened: The Details Behind SB 868</h2>
    <p>California lawmakers voted on Wednesday night to pass <strong>SB 868</strong>, legislation designed to make purchasing and operating plug-in solar kits as straightforward as buying an off-the-shelf appliance from Home Depot or Costco. Instead of navigating lengthy utility review processes that can cost hundreds of dollars and take weeks, consumers would complete a free online registration form, unpack a portable kit, and plug it directly into a standard home outlet.</p>

    <h3>The Utility Debate: Who Bears the Infrastructure Burden?</h3>
    <p>Not everyone views the financial mechanics of balcony solar through rose-colored glasses. Major utilities such as PG&E and SDG&E initially opposed the final version of the legislation, arguing that decentralized systems could create cost shifts. Their primary economic concern is that as solar-equipped customers pull less electricity from the centralized grid, the fixed costs of maintaining wires, transformers, and wildfire mitigation will be distributed across a shrinking pool of non-solar ratepayers—disproportionately impacting lower-income residents who cannot afford any solar setup.</p>
