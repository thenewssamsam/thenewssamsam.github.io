  <section class="ncg-section">
    <h2 id="a-legal-rights-explainer-understanding-the-warn-act">A Legal Rights Explainer: Understanding the WARN Act</h2>
    <p>At the center of the newly launched investigation is a piece of federal legislation passed decades ago to protect workers from abrupt economic upheaval: the Worker Adjustment and Retraining Notification (WARN) Act.</p>
    <p>Enacted by Congress in 1988, the WARN Act generally requires employers with 100 or more full-time workers to provide a 60-day advance written notice prior to executing a plant closing or a mass layoff. This mandatory window is designed to give employees time to adjust to prospective job loss, seek alternative employment, or participate in skill retraining programs before their paychecks stop.</p>
    
    <div class="ncg-legal-box">
      <p><strong>Legal Disclaimer:</strong> This is general information, not legal advice. Consult a licensed attorney about your specific situation if you have been affected by workplace layoffs.</p>
    </div>

    <p>When legal firms investigate potential WARN Act violations, they look specifically at whether the company provided adequate advance notice, whether exceptions (such as unforeseeable business circumstances) legitimately apply, and whether employees received appropriate back pay and benefits for any shortfall in the 60-day notice period.</p>

    <div class="ncg-quote-box">
      <p>"Federal labor protections like the WARN Act exist to ensure that workers are never treated as an afterthought when corporate decisions abruptly upend thousands of lives."</p>
    </div>

  <section class="ncg-section">
    <h2 id="how-should-workers-and-communities-respond">How Should Workers Respond?</h2>
    <p>Navigating sudden job loss is overwhelming, but taking methodical steps early can protect your financial and legal standing:</p>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Gather Your Documentation:</strong> Keep copies of your termination letters, severance agreements, employee handbooks, and any written communications from management regarding the shutdown.</li>
      <li><strong>File for Unemployment Immediately:</strong> Do not wait for legal investigations to resolve before filing for state unemployment benefits to bridge immediate financial gaps.</li>
      <li><strong>Connect with Local Support Services:</strong> Look into state dislocated worker programs and rapid response teams that often mobilize assistance, job fairs, and retraining grants in the wake of large-scale closures.</li>
      <li><strong>Seek Professional Guidance:</strong> If you believe your rights under federal or state labor laws were violated, consult with a qualified employment attorney or legal aid organization.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
