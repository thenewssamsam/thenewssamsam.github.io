<section id="what-happened">
<h2>What Happened: The Spike in Forced Recruitment</h2>
<p>Reports emerging from multiple regions across Russia—ranging from Novorossiysk to Birobidzhan—highlight an aggressive intensification of recruitment tactics. Human rights project Idite lesom noted that detentions have shifted from isolated incidents in July to a sustained peak in August. Men are routinely stopped on streets under the guise of document checks, picked up at workplaces, or targeted through job advertisements if they face financial distress or existing debts.</p>

<p>Concurrently, systemic losses on the front lines have driven the crisis. Independent estimates by the Centre for Strategic and International Studies (CSIS) and statements from Western officials indicate that Russian forces are losing upwards of 30,000 personnel per month—exceeding the estimated 27,000 monthly intake of new contract soldiers. With average life expectancies for new front-line recruits measured in minutes according to intelligence briefings, the Kremlin's appetite for manpower appears insatiable, leading directly to the aggressive pressuring of conscripts, prisoners, and unsuspecting civilians.</p>

Citizens find themselves trapped in an environment where routine institutions—police stations, employment agencies, and transit systems—are suddenly weaponised against personal safety.</p>
