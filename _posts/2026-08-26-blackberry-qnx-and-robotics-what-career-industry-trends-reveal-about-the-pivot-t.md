<section id="what-happened">
    <h2>What Happened: BlackBerry Finds a Second Life in Software</h2>
    <p>In a recent interview on CNBC's <em>The Tech Download</em> podcast, BlackBerry CEO John Giamatteo shared a surprising update about the company’s trajectory: robotics is officially one of the fastest-growing business segments within its core software division. While older generations remember BlackBerry as the dominant pioneer of physical-keyboard smartphones, the modern enterprise has undergone a massive reinvention.</p>
    
    <p>At the center of this transformation is QNX, a safety-critical operating system and embedded software portfolio already deployed in 275 million vehicles worldwide. That automotive footprint generates a notable royalty backlog of roughly $950 million, a portion of which is now stemming from emerging applications in industrial robotics, robotic forklifts, and medical instruments. Rather than chasing consumer-facing humanoid trends like those gaining traction in China, BlackBerry is quietly establishing itself as foundational infrastructure for business-to-business <strong>physical AI</strong>.</p>
</section>

    <h2>The Career &amp; Industry Perspective: Moving Beyond the Smartphone Era</h2>
    <p>From a career and industry strategy perspective, BlackBerry’s pivot offers a masterclass in enterprise adaptation. When consumer hardware markets saturate or shift rapidly, companies are often left with two choices: obsolescence or fundamental reinvention. BlackBerry chose the latter, peeling away its handset legacy to double down on what it does best: ultra-reliable, safety-critical embedded operating systems.</p>
