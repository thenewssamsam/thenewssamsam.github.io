<section id="what-happened">
<h2>What Happened: Tariffs, Visas, and Policy Shifts</h2>
<p>The latest economic flashpoint involves a multi-layered series of policy announcements from Ottawa and Washington. Canadian Finance Minister François-Philippe Champagne announced that Canada will apply retaliatory tariffs of 15%, 25%, and 50% on a wide variety of U.S. exports starting September 8. Hitting key sectors including steel, dairy, appliances, agricultural equipment, pulp and paper, and electronics, the counter-tariffs target nearly $20 billion worth of annual U.S. imports.</p>

<h2>The Financial Mechanics of Retaliatory Tariffs</h2>
<p>From a purely financial perspective, understanding how tariffs function clarifies who ultimately bears the burden. Tariffs are not paid by the exporting country; rather, they are taxes levied on imported goods paid by domestic importers—costs that are frequently passed along to consumers and downstream manufacturing businesses.</p>
