  <section id="what-happened" class="ncg-section">
    <h2>What Happened in Rimini?</h2>
    <p>On a Saturday evening that marked the tail end of the European summer vacation season, Pope Leo XIV traveled to the coastal Italian city of Rimini. Concluding a one-day pastoral journey that also touched the Republic of San Marino, the Holy Father celebrated Holy Mass for the Twenty-first Sunday of Ordinary Time before an estimated gathering of 25,000 people at the local port.</p>
    
    <p>The visit carried deep pastoral significance. Earlier in the day, the Pope engaged with participants at the well-known "Meeting for Friendship Among Peoples." But it was his homily during the evening liturgy that captured the essence of his message. Drawing from the day's Gospel reading—which recounts St. Peter’s historic profession of faith in Jesus as the Christ—the Pope anchored his reflections on the very foundation of Christian identity and leadership.</p>

    <h2>A Faith Perspective on Servant Leadership</h2>
    <p>In our modern world, we are conditioned to view authority through the lens of hierarchy, control, and privilege. Whether in corporate boardrooms, political arenas, or even community organizations, climbing the ladder is usually synonymous with acquiring more autonomy and less accountability to those below. Yet the Christian tradition—reiterated with striking clarity by Pope Leo XIV in Rimini—turns this entire paradigm completely upside down.</p>

    <h3 class="ncg-h3">Authority as an Instrument of Grace</h3>
    <p>When the Pope noted that "at every level, authority is service," he was echoing the very heartbeat of the Gospel. Jesus did not come to be served, but to serve. When worldly leaders hoard influence, Christian leaders are called to steward gifts. As the papal reflection on the Old Testament reading regarding Eliakim reminds us, everything we possess—our talents, our positions, our influence—is a pure gift from God meant to be used for the flourishing and salvation of our neighbors.</p>

    <h3 class="ncg-h3">The Disarming Immensity of Divine Love</h3>
    <p>Another striking element of the Pope's homily was his commentary on the "inscrutability" of God’s plans, as described by St. Paul. We often struggle to understand why history unfolds the way it does, why suffering exists, or why justice seems delayed. Pope Leo pointed out that God's plans aren't difficult to grasp because of convoluted logic, but because of the "disarming immensity of His love." That kind of love defies human calculation. It compels us to look at summer vacation not as a selfish retreat into comfort, but as a time to rejuvenate our capacity for authentic human encounter and genuine hospitality toward the vulnerable.</p>
