<section id="what-happened">
    <h2>What Happened: The New NFL Suspension Policy</h2>
    <p>The NFL and the NFL Players Association (NFLPA) announced a major policy shift on Wednesday in Atlanta, agreeing to enforce a strict one-game suspension for players who accumulate three penalties within five designated health and safety categories. Approved by players at a Board of Player Representatives meeting and briefed to team owners, the new standard targets infractions deemed most likely to cause severe injuries: the hip-drop tackle, horse-collar tackle, launching, hits on defenseless players, and blindside blocks.</p>
    
    <p>While players will enter the upcoming season with a clean slate, these violations will carry forward across subsequent seasons. To balance the punitive measures with positive reinforcement, players committing only one infraction will receive 50% of their fine back at season's end. Furthermore, coaches must intervene directly by joining players in review meetings with the league once a player hits two violations—sitting right on the threshold of a suspension. Clubs themselves also face heightened financial penalties for excessive safety violations.</p>

From a psychological standpoint, this policy leverages several established behavioral principles to reshape how athletes calculate risk.</p>

    <h3>Loss Aversion and the Threshold Effect</h3>
    <p>Behavioral economics and psychology show that humans are far more motivated by the pain of losing something than by the pleasure of gaining an equivalent reward—a concept known as loss aversion. Traditional fines often functioned merely as a "cost of doing business" for elite athletes earning millions. However, a potential suspension introduces a vastly different kind of loss: the loss of playing time, team trust, and personal impact on the field.</p>

    <h3>Group Dynamics and Coach Intervention</h3>
    <p>Requiring coaches to sit down with players after two violations introduces social accountability.
