    <section id="what-happened" style="margin-bottom: 40px;">
        <h2 style="font-size: 1.6rem; color: #2d3436; margin-bottom: 15px; border-bottom: 1px solid #eee; padding-bottom: 8px;">What Happened: The Boca Raton Ocean Ditching</h2>
        <p>On a Saturday morning that began like any other routine flight, the aviation community in South Florida faced a moment of intense drama. A 23-year-old female pilot found herself battling sudden mechanical issues while flying a Piper 30A solo. The flight had originated from Page Field in Fort Myers, with an intended destination of Bimini Airport in the Bahamas. However, mechanical failure forced a rapid change in plans.</p>
        
        <div style="background: #fdf2f2; border: 1px solid #f5c6cb; border-left: 4px solid #dc3545; padding: 15px; margin: 20px 0; border-radius: 4px;">
            <p style="margin: 0; font-size: 0.95rem; color: #721c24;"><strong>Quick Take:</strong> As the aircraft approached Boca Raton Airport, the pilot recognized she could not make the runway safely. With remarkable composure, she notified authorities and guided the plane down into the ocean off 250 S. Ocean Boulevard around 10:25 a.m.</p>
        </div>

        <p>Boca Raton Ocean Rescue units responded swiftly to the crash site. To the profound relief of onlookers and emergency responders alike, the young pilot managed to climb out of the sinking aircraft uninjured. She was subsequently transported to a local hospital purely as a precaution. By Sunday morning, Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) investigators were already hard at work, hoisting the wreckage from the water and beginning the painstaking process of disassembling the aircraft at the Silver Palm Boat Ramp for evidence gathering.</p>

Pilots train extensively to break this bias. When the Piper 30A experienced mechanical trouble, the pilot did not waste precious seconds wishing the engine would miraculously heal itself; she accepted the hostile reality instantly and shifted entirely into problem-solving mode.</p>
