Thai defense ministry spokesperson Surasant Kongsiri confirmed that the carrier and its strike group will dock next week, providing the weary crew a much-needed chance to go ashore, rest, and recover before making the final voyage back to their U.S. base.</p>

    <h2>The Psychology of Extended Isolation at Sea</h2>
    <p>Human beings are fundamentally wired for rhythm, horizons, and predictable touchpoints with the familiar world. When individuals are placed in an isolated, high-pressure environment for over eight months without setting foot on solid ground, the psychological toll is profound. From the lens of behavioral psychology, an ordeal like the Lincoln's deployment tests several distinct mental thresholds.</p>

Over time, normalcy bias fades into a state of chronic hyper-vigilance, where the brain remains locked in a subtle, low-grade stress response.</p>
