  <section id="introduction">
    <p>In a dramatic reversal of fortune, the Wyoming Freedom Caucus—which held majority control of the state House as recently as 2024—saw its influence collapse in Tuesday’s primary elections. Voters soundly rejected many of the caucus’s incumbents, signaling a significant shift in the state's legislative trajectory.</p>
  </section>

  <section id="what-happened">
    <h2>What Happened in the Primaries?</h2>
    <p>The results were sweeping. The Freedom Caucus, which aimed to expand its reach, instead lost 13 incumbents in the House, including key figures like former House Speaker Chip Neiman and majority floor leader Scott Heiner. In the Senate, the group failed to gain ground, losing six caucus-aligned lawmakers. The caucus is now projected to have only 10 reliable votes in the House, a sharp decline from its previous majority status.</p>
    <p>This loss of numbers effectively strips the group of its ability to force two-thirds introductory votes, a key tool they had used during budget sessions. As Governor Mark Gordon noted in an interview, the results reflect a electorate focused on the future of Wyoming rather than nationalized talking points.</p>
