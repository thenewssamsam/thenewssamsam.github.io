In low-information or high-stress environments like voting booths, name familiarity is one of the most powerful heuristics available. But what happens when that exact shortcut weaponizes itself against clear decision-making?</p>

    <h3 class="ncg-h3">The Mechanics of the Recognition Heuristic</h3>
    <p>Psychologists note that when voters scan a ballot quickly, their eyes often latch onto familiar words or names. When two candidates share an identical first and last name, the automatic recognition system in the brain stumbles. Instead of triggering a clear association with a specific political platform, record, or ideology, the duplicate name triggers uncertainty.</p>

    <h3 class="ncg-h3">Choice Overload and Decision Fatigue</h3>
    <p>Alaska's ranked-choice system already requires voters to weigh multiple candidates across party lines. Adding a same-name candidate into this mix creates choice overload. Voters experience cognitive dissonance when trying to differentiate between two individuals who occupy the exact same lexical space on paper. The result is often voter drop-off, ballot spoilage, or unintentional vote siphoning driven purely by split-second visual misidentification.</p>
