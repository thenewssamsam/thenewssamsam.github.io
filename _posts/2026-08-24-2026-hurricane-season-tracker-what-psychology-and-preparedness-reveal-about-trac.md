<h2 id="what-happened">What Happened in the 2026 Hurricane Season?</h2>
<p>According to reports from The Weather Company meteorologists Jennifer Gray, Miriam Guthrie, and Rob Shackelford, the tropical basins are currently active across both the Atlantic and Pacific. Forecasters are closely monitoring Tropical Storm Moke as it approaches Hawaii, evaluating its forecast cone, current radar, wind speeds, and potential rainfall. Meanwhile, the Atlantic basin is being shaped by competing atmospheric forces: hostile zones of high wind shear marked in purple, and plumes of dry, dusty air streaming across the ocean from Africa's Sahara Desert that tend to squelch thunderstorm development.</p>

<p>Meteorologists also rely on metrics like Accumulated Cyclone Energy (ACE) to measure the total energy produced by tropical storms and compare current season activity against historical averages. While the technical data helps experts predict landfall and intensity, the human reaction to these shifting colored maps tells an entirely different story about how our minds process severe weather threats.</p>
