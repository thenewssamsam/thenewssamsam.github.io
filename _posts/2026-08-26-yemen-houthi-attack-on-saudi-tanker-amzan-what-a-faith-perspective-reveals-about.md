    <h2>What Happened in the Red Sea?</h2>
    <p>On Monday, August 24, 2026, the Yemeni armed group known as the Houthis claimed responsibility for a targeted strike on the Saudi oil tanker <em>Amzan</em>. According to statements released by the group, the vessel—belonging to the Saudi national shipping company Bahri—was hit by a ballistic missile off the coast of Yanbu, Saudi Arabia’s primary Red Sea oil export hub.</p>

    <p>Maritime authorities first noted the incident earlier in the day when the UK Maritime Trade Operations agency reported an unidentified projectile striking a tanker approximately 63 nautical miles west of Yanbu. Fortunately, Bahri later confirmed that all crew members were safe and no injuries were reported, though the strike caused a fire aboard the vessel and prompted neighboring ships to clear the area.</p>

    <p>This attack is far from an isolated incident. In recent weeks, the Houthis have intensified their campaign against maritime traffic, carrying out strikes that have resulted in casualties at Yemeni ports and targeted critical regional infrastructure. As shipping lanes around Yanbu face mounting disruptions following the declared Houthi blockade on Saudi-linked vessels, the security of international trade hangs in a delicate balance.</p>
