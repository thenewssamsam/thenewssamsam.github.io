<section id="what-happened">
  <h2>What Happened in the Primaries</h2>
  <p>The primary losses piled up quickly. Alexander Vindman, the former National Security Council aide whose testimony galvanized the 2019 House impeachment inquiry, suffered a notable defeat in Florida's Senate primary against democratic socialist Angie Nixon. Similar fates met other prominent names: former impeachment managers and vocal critics like Eric Swalwell and Dan Goldman fell short or stepped aside amid shifting political winds, while figures like Capitol Police officer Harry Dunn and billionaire donor Tom Steyer also failed to secure nominations.</p>
  
  <p>As veteran Democratic strategist David Axelrod noted, memories fade while the daily pressures of inflation, housing, and the cost of living remain front and center for average voters. The political landscape has shifted from generalized anti-administration outrage to a granular, kitchen-table focus on economic survival and institutional delivery.</p>

  <h2>Closing Takeaway</h2>
  <p>The primary setbacks facing prominent resistance Democrats signal a profound evolution in the American electorate. As voters grow weary of perpetual political warfare and prioritize immediate economic realities, candidates of all backgrounds must look forward rather than backward. True political endurance requires addressing the tangible needs of everyday people, not just reliving yesterday's battles.</p>
