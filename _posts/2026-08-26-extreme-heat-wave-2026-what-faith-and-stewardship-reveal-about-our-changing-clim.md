  <section id="what-happened" class="ncg-section">
    <h2>What Happened: The 2026 Heat Dome Unfolds</h2>
    <p>If you have stepped outside in parts of the South-Central United States, the Southwest, or the Lower Mississippi Valley recently, you already know what the thermometers are shouting. According to the National Weather Service, more than 80 million people are trapped under an Extreme Heat Warning or Heat Advisory. Forecasters at the Weather Prediction Center have labeled this current atmospheric setup a "steamy air-mass even by mid-to-late August standards."</p>
    <p>Daytime highs are pushing past the triple-digit mark across the southern plains, while parts of Arizona, New Mexico, and Texas brace for blistering peaks upwards of 110 degrees Fahrenheit. But meteorologists note that the true danger of this particular weather system lies not just in the burning afternoon sun, but in the stubborn persistence of night. After the sun goes down, temperatures refuse to drop, robbing communities of the natural cooling-off period that human bodies and power grids desperately rely upon.</p>
    <div class="ncg-quote-box">
      <p>"The most intense heat waves would be impossible without human-caused global warming—and when night brings no relief, our physical and spiritual resilience is tested to its absolute limits."</p>
