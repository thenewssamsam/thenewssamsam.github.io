    <h1 class="ncg-main-title">Northwest Indiana Power Outages: What a Local Impact Lens Reveals About Community Resilience</h1>
    <p class="ncg-subtitle">When utility grids fail for over a week, spreadsheets and corporate updates matter less than shared generators, box lunches, and neighbors helping neighbors clear the path forward.</p>
  </header>

  <section class="ncg-section" id="introduction">
    <p>When the lights go out for a few hours, it is a minor inconvenience. When they stay out for ten days in the dead of summer, it becomes a test of endurance. Across Northwest Indiana, residents in Lake County, Schererville, Gary, and Lake Station are finding out firsthand what happens when critical infrastructure collapses under the weight of severe weather. More than a week after violent storms tore through the region, thousands of customers remain in the dark, wrestling with spoiled food, disabled medical devices, and stalled daily routines.</p>
    
    <p>Yet, while frustration with utility provider NIPSCO has reached a boiling point—drawing sharp rebukes from state leaders like Indiana Governor Mike Braun—another story is quietly unfolding in the alleys, driveways, and community centers of these affected neighborhoods. It is a story of grassroots solidarity, improvised logistics, and the raw power of local community resilience when institutional systems stutter.</p>
  </section>
