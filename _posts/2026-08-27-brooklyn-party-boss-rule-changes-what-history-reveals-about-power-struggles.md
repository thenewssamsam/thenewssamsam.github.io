<section id="what-happened">
<h2>What Happened in Brooklyn</h2>
<p>In a closed-door meeting marked by heightened security and sharp public division, Brooklyn Democratic Party leadership secured enough votes to pass a series of controversial rule changes. Despite suffering notable losses in the June primaries, party chair Rodneyse Bichotte Hermelyn and her allies managed to expand the executive committee from 42 to 68 members. Critics, including progressive challengers and high-ranking officials such as Mayor Zohran Mamdani and Gov. Kathy Hochul, immediately denounced the move as an insular power grab designed to preserve institutional control.</p>

<p>The vote underscored a deep, ongoing schism within the modern Democratic Party between the moderate establishment and progressive or socialist factions. While allies celebrated the procedural victory, opponents argued that conducting such significant structural overhauls behind closed doors alienates everyday voters and deepens intra-party wounds. With progressives vowing to pursue legal action before the next scheduled vote in September, the conflict highlights enduring questions about how political organizations govern themselves when internal factions collide.</p>
