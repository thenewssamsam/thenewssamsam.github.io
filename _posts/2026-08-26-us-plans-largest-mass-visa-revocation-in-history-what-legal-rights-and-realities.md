<section id="what-happened">
<h2>What Happened: The 200,000 Visa Revocation Plan</h2>
<p>According to administration sources speaking on the record and through official statements, the State Department is actively identifying and revoking visas belonging to foreign nationals who arrived under short-term pretexts but subsequently pursued asylum claims. State Department spokesperson Tommy Pigott framed the move around the principle of national privilege, noting that a U.S. visa is not a right.</p>
<p>Administration officials argue that obtaining a tourist or business visa with the underlying intent of filing for asylum constitutes systemic fraud. To highlight their case, officials pointed to high-profile examples, including the case of deported activist Beto Coral, whose situation Deputy Secretary of State Christopher Landau described on social media as symptomatic of broader asylum system dysfunctions. Rather than executing all 200,000 cancellations at once, the initiative is slated to roll out progressively across the country.</p>

<h2>Legal Rights Explainer: What a Visa Revocation Actually Does</h2>
<p>While the administration presents the sweeping initiative as a major enforcement milestone, legal experts suggest the policy’s practical mechanisms are far more nuanced than the headline figures suggest. Immigration attorney Nicolette Glazer characterized the announcement as largely performative, pointing out a fundamental reality of immigration statute.</p>
