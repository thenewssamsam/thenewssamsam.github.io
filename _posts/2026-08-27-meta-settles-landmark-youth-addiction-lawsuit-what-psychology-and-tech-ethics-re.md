<section id="what-happened" style="margin-bottom: 2.5rem;">
    <h2 style="font-size: 1.5rem; font-weight: 700; color: #1a202c; border-bottom: 2px solid #cbd5e0; padding-bottom: 0.5rem; margin-bottom: 1rem;">What Happened: The $17.1 Billion Settlement</h2>
    <p>In a watershed moment for digital regulation and consumer protection, Meta has agreed to pay as much as $17.1 billion and implement strict restrictions on teen accounts. Filed in court papers, the historic agreement resolves claims brought by 29 states accusing the tech giant of designing Facebook and Instagram to addict children and misleading the public about platform safety.</p>
    
    <p>The settlement structure requires Meta to pay at least $12.1 billion over 10 years, with an additional $5 billion conditional upon major competitors like TikTok and YouTube adopting comparable child-safety measures. The agreement also forces Meta to institute daily time limits, block app access during nighttime hours, mute notifications during school hours, and enhance parental controls. However, Meta noted that several of these features remain contingent on industry-wide adoption, pointing out that teens simply migrate between platforms if only one app enforces restrictions.</p>

Social media applications are masterclasses in applied psychology. At the core of platform engagement is the variable ratio reinforcement schedule—the exact same psychological mechanism that makes slot machines so compelling in casinos.</p>
