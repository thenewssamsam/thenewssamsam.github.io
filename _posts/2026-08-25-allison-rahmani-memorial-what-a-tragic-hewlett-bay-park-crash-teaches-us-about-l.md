        <p style="font-size: 1.125rem; font-weight: 500; color: #1f2937; margin-bottom: 20px;">
            Life can change in a single, unannounced heartbeat. Just days before a long-awaited trip to Paris to celebrate her 40th birthday, Allison Rahmani tragically lost her life when her SUV plunged into a backyard pool in Hewlett Bay Park, Long Island. As her family, students, and community gather to mourn, we are left confronting the fragile, fleeting nature of our days and the heavy questions that linger in the wake of sudden loss.
        </p>
        <p style="margin-bottom: 20px;">
            News reports from outlets like Newsday detail a heartbreaking scene. Allison Rahmani—a devoted mother of four, a cherished math coordinator at the Hebrew Academy of the Five Towns and Rockaway (HAFTR), and a radiant presence in her community—was preparing for a milestone celebration. Instead of boarding a flight to France with her husband, Matthew, loved ones packed into a Hewlett memorial chapel to honor a life cut far too short.
        </p>
    </section>

        </p>

    <section id="ncg-what-happened" style="margin-bottom: 30px;">
        <h2 style="font-size: 1.5rem; color: #1f2937; margin-top: 30px; margin-bottom: 15px; border-bottom: 2px solid #f3f4f6; padding-bottom: 8px;">What Happened in Hewlett Bay Park?</h2>
        <p style="margin-bottom: 20px;">
            According to the Nassau County Police Department, the incident unfolded around 9:30 a.m. on a Friday morning. Allison Rahmani was driving a 2023 GMC in the driveway of a residence on Elinor Road when she somehow lost control of the vehicle. The SUV struck a tree and an unoccupied Honda before coming to rest, fully immersed, in an in-ground backyard pool. 
        </p>
        <p style="margin-bottom: 20px;">
            First responders treated her at the scene before she was rushed to a nearby hospital, where she was tragically pronounced dead. Police have stated that the investigation into the exact mechanical or medical factors leading up to the crash remains ongoing. Yet, while investigators look for physical answers, those who knew Allison are left searching for emotional and spiritual footing.
        </p>

                <li style="margin-bottom: 8px;"><strong>Date &amp; Time:</strong> Friday morning, approximately 9:30 a.m.</li>
                <li style="margin-bottom: 8px;"><strong>Location:</strong> Elinor Road driveway, Hewlett Bay Park, Long Island.</li>
                <li style="margin-bottom: 8px;"><strong>Victim:</strong> Allison Rahmani, 40, mother of four and HAFTR educator.</li>
                <li style="margin-bottom: 0;"><strong>Status:</strong> Ongoing investigation by Nassau County Police.</li>
            </ul>

        <p style="margin-bottom: 20px;">
            At her memorial service held at Boulevard-Riverside Chapels, friends and family painted a portrait of a woman defined by her fierce vitality. Her husband, Matthew Rahmani, shared how deeply intertwined their lives were, speaking candidly about his reliance on her strength and confidence. Friends like Shayna Ungar recalled Allison&rsquo;s joyful competitiveness—recounting a playful memory of racing to give birth and texting &ldquo;I win&rdquo; after welcoming her baby boy, Zachary, just weeks apart from her friend.
        </p>
        <p style="margin-bottom: 20px;">
            Her sister, Jamie Scharaga, reflected on a lifetime of following closely in each other&rsquo;s footsteps, sharing homes in the Five Towns, entering the teaching profession, and sharing milestones side by side. Scharaga movingly noted that she simply never imagined her sister&rsquo;s footsteps would stop following hers so soon. 
        </p>
        <p style="margin-bottom: 20px;">
            As a math coordinator at HAFTR, Allison didn&rsquo;t view teaching as a mere obligation. She taught because she genuinely loved shaping young minds. Her absence leaves an irreplaceable void in the classrooms she illuminated and the home she nurtured for her four young children: Chloe, Jake, Nathan, and Zachary.

        <h2 style="font-size: 1.5rem; color: #1f2937; margin-top: 30px; margin-bottom: 15px; border-bottom: 2px solid #f3f4f6; padding-bottom: 8px;">A Faith Perspective on Sudden Sorrow</h2>
        <p style="margin-bottom: 20px;">
            During the memorial service, Rabbi Binyomin Sloviter raised the timeless, painful question that echoes whenever tragedy strikes a young parent: Why are the vibrant taken far too soon? It is a question that defies easy answers. From a biblical perspective, moments of profound grief remind us of our ultimate reliance on a Creator whose ways are higher than our own.
        </p>
        <p style="margin-bottom: 20px;">
            Scripture invites us to ponder the brevity of human life and the preciousness of every single day we are given. James 4:14 gently reminds us: &ldquo;Why, you do not even know what will happen tomorrow. What is your life? You are a mist that appears for a little while and then vanishes.&rdquo; This realization is not meant to fill us with morbid fear, but rather to awaken us to gratitude, compelling us to cherish our families, love our neighbors deeply, and hold earthly plans with open hands.
        </p>
        <p style="margin-bottom: 20px;">
            When unexpected sorrow shatters our routines, we are also reminded of the comforting presence of God in the darkest valleys. Psalm 34:18 tells us, &ldquo;The Lord is close to the brokenhearted and saves those who are crushed in spirit.&rdquo; While we may never fully understand the mechanics or timing of such heartbreaking accidents in this life, we can find refuge in the enduring promise that love outlasts death and that God bottles every tear shed by grieving children and mourning spouses.
        </p>
