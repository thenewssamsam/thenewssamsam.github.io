<section id="what-happened">
<h2>What Happened: The Growing Russian Fuel Crisis</h2>
<p>In recent weeks, systematic Ukrainian drone and missile strikes targeting Russian oil refineries have triggered a widespread fuel crisis across the Russian Federation. According to reports from independent media and the Reuters news agency, at least 30 regions are now experiencing acute shortages, with major metropolitan hubs like Moscow and the surrounding areas feeling the pinch. Major gas station chains, including Gazprom Neft and Tatneft, have begun restricting fuel sales in the capital, while motorists in southern regions like Krasnodar face hours-long queues just to fill their tanks.</p>

<p>This is not merely a seasonal blip or an inconvenience for holiday travelers on the Black Sea coast. Independent economists and journalists note that the crisis stems from severe structural damage to Russia's oil-refining infrastructure, a drop in overall oil production from 11.5 million barrels per day in 2019 to under 9 million today, and plummeting fuel quality. With garages reporting months-long waitlists for engine repairs caused by fuel impurities, the disruptions are beginning to choke vital supply chains, impacting logistics, construction, courier services, and public transport.</p>

<h2>The Wider Economic and Logistical Fallout</h2>
<p>Beyond the historical symbolism lies a harsh logistical reality. Economist and exiled business journalist Vyacheslav Shiryaev points out that Russia's vast geography makes it utterly dependent on liquid fuels to maintain basic societal cohesion. Without diesel, the country's 6,000-kilometer railway network risks grinding to a halt, tens of thousands of transport trucks cannot deliver food to supermarkets, and agricultural harvests are placed in jeopardy.</p>
