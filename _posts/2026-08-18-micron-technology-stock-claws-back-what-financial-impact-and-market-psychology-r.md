  <h2 id="financial-perspective">The Financial Mechanics and Market Psychology</h2>

  <p>When a company posts earnings growth exceeding 1,000%, it signals a dramatic shift in industry fundamentals rather than a minor quarterly beat. In the semiconductor industry—particularly memory and storage solutions—supply and demand operate in intense cycles. During down periods, overproduction and falling prices squeeze margins. When demand surges, prices rebound aggressively, and profit margins expand at an exponential rate.</p>

  <p>From a behavioral standpoint, explosive rallies like Micron's often trigger powerful psychological reactions among retail and institutional investors alike. Two primary behavioral forces tend to dominate during these momentum phases:</p>
  
  <p>First is <strong>normalcy bias</strong>—the tendency for market observers to underestimate how fast cyclical companies can recover once industry conditions flip from negative to positive. Second is the risk of chasing momentum purely out of fear of missing out (FOMO). Understanding the financial mechanics of cyclical earnings helps separate sustainable business growth from short-term speculative hype.</p>
