<p class="ncg-lead">When Serena and Venus Williams stepped onto the tennis world stage decades ago, they changed the sport forever. Now, in a move that has electrified the tennis community, the legendary sisters have accepted a wildcard entry to play women's doubles together at the 2026 US Open—marking their first Grand Slam outing as a pairing in four years.</p>

<p>For fans who thought they had seen the final chapter of the Williams sister act at Flushing Meadows, this surprise reunion offers a powerful reminder of how resilient sporting greatness can be. But beyond the headlines of wildcards and historic comebacks, this moment invites us to look at the deeper arcs of athletic longevity, family partnership, and what it means to step back into the arena on your own terms.</p>

<strong>Quick Take:</strong> Serena (44) and Venus (46) are reuniting for women's doubles at the 2026 US Open after receiving a USTA wildcard. It is their first Grand Slam appearance together since 2022, reminding the world of an unmatched partnership that has claimed 14 major doubles titles.
</div>

<h2 id="what-happened">What Happened at the US Open Announcement</h2>

<p>According to reports from BBC Sport, the United States Tennis Association (USTA) granted the American greats a wildcard entry for the women's doubles draw at the 2026 US Open. The last time the sisters teamed up at a major was back at the 2022 US Open—the tournament widely understood to be Serena's emotional farewell before she stepped away from professional tennis.</p>

<p>Since then, both sisters have kept busy in different ways. Serena made a brief return in doubles at Queen's and played singles at Wimbledon before a knee injury sidelined her. She also recently partnered with Carlos Alcaraz in the US Open mixed doubles, reaching the quarter-finals. Meanwhile, Venus, now 46, has continued to play sporadically on the WTA Tour and has also accepted a wildcard for the upcoming singles tournament beginning Sunday, August 30.</p>
