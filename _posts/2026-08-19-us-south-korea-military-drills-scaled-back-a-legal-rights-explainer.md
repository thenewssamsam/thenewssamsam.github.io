  <section id="what-happened">
    <h2>What Happened?</h2>
    <p>Following a directive from President Trump, the U.S. military is scaling back the "Ulchi Freedom Shield" exercises. President Trump expressed concerns that these drills send an "inappropriate and hostile" signal to North Korea, while simultaneously criticizing Seoul for a lack of support in U.S. operations regarding Iran. South Korean officials have responded by stating they are reviewing their readiness and domestic legal procedures regarding potential contributions to other U.S. military efforts.</p>
  </section>

  <section id="legal-framework">
    <h2>The Legal Basis of the Alliance</h2>
    <p>The U.S.-South Korea military presence is anchored in the 1953 Mutual Defense Treaty. Under this legal framework, both nations agree to act to meet common dangers in the event of an armed attack in the Pacific area. However, the treaty does not mandate specific, perpetual military exercises. Instead, it creates a broad framework for cooperation.</p>
