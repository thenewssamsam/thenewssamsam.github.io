  <section id="what-happened" class="ncg-section">
    <h2>What Happened: The 15-Minute Airport Dream</h2>
    <p>For decades, travelers navigating between downtown Chicago and O'Hare International Airport have faced a familiar gamble: brave the unpredictable crawl of the Kennedy Expressway or settle in for a slow, multi-stop ride on the CTA Blue Line. That long-standing commuting frustration could soon change.</p>
    <p>Private investors behind a venture called O'Hare Flyer LLC have formally unveiled a $2.25 billion proposal to construct a dedicated express train system. According to project CEO Dave Lundy and chief development officer Mark Walbrun, the battery-powered trains would carry up to 450 passengers from a converted station at Chicago's old main post office straight to O'Hare in a swift 15 minutes. Operating primarily on existing, underutilized freight railway rights-of-way—with only the final mile and a half requiring new elevated concrete support piers—the project promises dependable, quarter-hour departures without massive reliance on public tax dollars.</p>
    <p>While the Chicago Tribune editorial board and Mayor Brandon Johnson have expressed enthusiastic support for the concept, major questions remain regarding municipal bureaucracy, union negotiations, and the historical friction that often accompanies major infrastructure projects in the Windy City.</p>

Valued at $2.25 billion, the project intends to utilize private investment capital alongside federal Railroad Rehabilitation and Improvement Financing (RRIF) loans, minimizing direct tax burdens on city residents.</p>
