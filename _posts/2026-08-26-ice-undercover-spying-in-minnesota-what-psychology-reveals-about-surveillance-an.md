  <section id="what-happened">
    <h2>What Happened: Inside "Operation Puppet Master"</h2>
    <p>According to internal DHS files disclosed in federal court and reviewed by <em>The Guardian</em>, federal law enforcement carried out an extensive surveillance operation dubbed "Operation Puppet Master" in Minnesota. The campaign targeted individuals and groups opposing Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) crackdowns following local protests.</p>
    <p>Undercover Homeland Security Investigations (HSI) agents went to extraordinary lengths to blend into local activist ecosystems. Agent 9843, using the handle “peanutbutterjelly,” infiltrated Signal messaging groups, screenshotted protest plans, attended virtual "ICE watch" trainings, and even offered to help with fundraising and graphic design. Other agents attended community meetings hosted by groups like the Sunrise Movement and the Party for Socialism and Liberation, taking notes, photographing attendees—even documenting ordinary details like snacks, books, and workout classes—and in some cases, offering to supply building materials for direct actions.</p>

    <p>Psychologically, human beings rely on spaces outside of work and home—often called "third places"—where they can drop their guard, share ideas openly, and debate freely. When community library meetings, book tables, and calisthenics classes are secretly monitored by undercover operatives, those safe harbors instantly vanish. Every newcomer is suddenly viewed through a lens of quiet suspicion. People stop speaking freely, not because they are breaking the law, but because they can no longer distinguish a genuine neighbor from a federal informant.</p>

People withdraw from civic participation entirely. Furthermore, the presence of bad actors or agent provocateurs trying to nudge groups toward illegal activity—as noted in the DHS transcripts where agents probed for individuals interested in more extreme actions—fosters an environment of internal paranoia and group fracture.</p>
