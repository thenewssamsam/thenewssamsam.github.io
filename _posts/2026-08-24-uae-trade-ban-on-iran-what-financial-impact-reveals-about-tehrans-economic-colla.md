<p>When geopolitical tensions boil over, the headlines usually focus on naval blockades, soaring oil prices, or tense diplomatic standoffs. But sometimes, the most devastating blow to a nation isn't fired from a warship—it happens in the quiet halls of commerce. The United Arab Emirates recently suspended all trade, commercial exchanges, and financial transactions with Iran following accusations that Tehran fired ballistic missiles toward maritime traffic. For the Iranian economy, this is not just another diplomatic spat; it is the severing of a vital financial artery that bilateral sanctions could never quite reach.</p>

<section id="what-happened">
    <h2>What Happened in the UAE-Iran Trade Freeze?</h2>
    <p>The announcement from the UAE Foreign Ministry came with immediate effect and remains in place until further notice. Emirati officials stated that two ballistic missiles launched from Iran fell into the sea while targeting maritime navigation. Tehran has vehemently denied responsibility, with its foreign ministry dismissing the accusations as baseless and pointing to historical regional tensions.</p>
    
    <p>However, the economic fallout is immediate and measurable. According to World Trade Organization data, the UAE supplied 31% of Iran's imports—valued at roughly $21 billion in 2024—and served as the destination for 13% of its exports. More importantly, Dubai has long functioned as Iran's window to the wider world economy, acting as a re-export hub where third-country goods were routed to bypass direct restrictions.</p>
