<section id="what-happened">
<h2>What Happened: The White House Ballroom Showdown</h2>
<p>The legal battle over a proposed 90,000-square-foot ballroom at the White House has reached the highest court in the United States. Lawyers for the National Trust for Historic Preservation urged the Supreme Court on Tuesday to keep in place a lower court order temporarily blocking construction on the multi-million-dollar project. The Trump administration, which argues that the construction is now roughly 65% complete and vital for national security, is pushing the high court to lift the stay so work can proceed.</p>

<p>At the center of the dispute is a fundamental question of jurisdiction and authorization: Does the executive branch possess the unilateral authority to tear down part of the East Wing and build a massive new event space, or does that power rest exclusively with Congress? Lower courts have repeatedly sided with the National Trust, ruling that the project lacks proper congressional appropriations and bypasses essential reviews by the National Capital Planning Commission and the Commission of Fine Arts. As the legal wrangling intensifies, the case offers a fascinating lens for professionals in project management, architecture, and regulatory compliance.</p>
