<h1>Ohio State and Oregon Claim AP Preseason 1-2: A Historical Look at College Football's New Power Map</h1>
</header>

<p>When the Associated Press released its preseason Top 25 poll, it delivered a striking piece of college football history: Ohio State and Oregon occupy the top two spots. For the first time in 65 years, and only the second time ever, the Big Ten Conference holds both preseason pinnacle positions. This milestone reflects a dramatic shifting of power across the collegiate landscape, echoing seismic era shifts from decades past while setting the stage for an explosive autumn.</p>

<p>The latest rankings feature the Buckeyes pulling in 40 first-place votes, while the Ducks secure 14 following a historic ascent to their highest preseason ranking in program history. Behind them, heavyweights like Georgia, Notre Dame, and Texas round out a formidable top five, while the Southeastern Conference continues its sheer numerical dominance with nine total ranked teams. Yet, all eyes remain fixed on the top two. When conferences concentrate power at this level, history shows that the entire ecosystem of college athletics shifts in response.</p>

<section id="what-happened">
<h2>What Happened in the Preseason Poll?</h2>
<p>The release of the AP preseason Top 25 captured the consensus of 69 media members covering college football, and the resulting tally brought major storylines across multiple powerhouse programs. Ohio State enters the season at No. 1 for the ninth time in school history, tying Alabama for the second-most preseason top rankings behind Oklahoma's ten. Meanwhile, Oregon has climbed to No. 2, marking a new benchmark for a program that previously peaked at No. 3 in preseason expectations.</p>

<p>The top five is rounded out by Georgia at No. 3, Notre Dame at No. 4, and Texas at No. 5. Defending national champion Indiana enters at No. 6, providing a fascinating case study of a reigning titleholder starting outside the top circle despite an unprecedented 16-0 run in the prior campaign. Further down the ledger, the SEC flexes its collective muscle by placing nine teams inside the Top 25, accounting for over a third of the entire poll and five of the top ten spots.</p>

<p>The numbers tell a compelling story of concentration. While programs like Notre Dame, Indiana, and Miami collected scattered first-place votes, the overarching narrative belongs to the massive gravitational pull of the Big Ten and the SEC. As the expanded College Football Playoff era enters its mature phase, these preseason rankings offer a clear blueprint of how voters perceive the modern hierarchy of the sport.</p>
</section>

<section id="historical-parallels">
<h2>Historical Parallels: When the Big Ten Ruled the Polls</h2>
<p>To understand the rarity of Ohio State and Oregon holding down the top two spots, one must look back through the dusty archives of college football history. Prior to this poll, a single conference holding the top two spots in the preseason rankings had not occurred since 1987, when Oklahoma and Nebraska of the old Big Eight accomplished the feat. You have to travel even further back—to 1961—to find the last time the Big Ten achieved this exact distinction, when Iowa and Ohio State sat atop the initial poll.</p>

<p>History provides a sobering reminder that preseason prognostications are rarely definitive prophetic texts. In that 1961 season, Iowa started 4-0 before collapsing to lose four of their last five games, finishing unranked entirely. Ohio State fared better, going 8-0-1 and finishing No. 2 behind an undefeated Alabama squad. The parallel illustrates a timeless sports truth: early praise and lofty expectations are heavy armor to carry through a grueling autumn schedule.</p>

<div class="ncg-history-box">
<h4>The 1961 Preseason Echo</h4>
<p>When Iowa and Ohio State opened the 1961 season at Nos. 1 and 2, it felt like a coronation for Midwestern football. Yet the turbulence of a long season quickly rewrote the script. Today's Buckeyes and Ducks face similar crosshairs, knowing that wearing the target on their backs invites every conference rival's absolute best effort week in and week out.</p>
</div>

<p>Another historical echo resonates in how modern conference realignment has reshaped these traditional boundaries. Oregon, once a regional West Coast powerhouse in the old Pac-10/Pac-12 ecosystem, now shares a conference banner with Ohio State. The last time the Big Ten locked down the top two spots, it was an intra-regional affair; today, it represents a coast-to-coast superpower alliance forged by media rights and playoff expansion.</p>
</section>

<section id="conference-dominance">
<h2>The Conference Power Dynamic: Big Ten vs. SEC</h2>
<p>Beyond the top two teams, the broader conference breakdown reveals a duopoly defining contemporary college athletics. The SEC leads all conferences with nine ranked teams, maintaining a tradition of deep, week-in and week-out attrition that voters reward. Five of the top ten teams hail from the SEC for the first time since 2013, underscoring the relentless depth of programs like Georgia, Texas, Texas A&M, Mississippi, and Oklahoma.</p>

<p>Conversely, the Big Ten counters with elite top-end firepower. Having won three straight College Football Playoff national championships across three different programs—Michigan, Ohio State, and Indiana—the conference is gunning for an unprecedented fourth consecutive national title. This is also the fourth consecutive year that the Big Ten has placed two teams inside the top three of the preseason rankings, demonstrating sustained excellence at the absolute summit.</p>

<p>This dynamic creates a fascinating psychological and competitive contrast. The SEC relies on a sprawling, iron-sharpens-iron collective density where nine fanbases believe they can navigate the gauntlet. The Big Ten leverages elite marquee titans at the absolute peak while building formidable depth behind them. For fans and analysts alike, watching these two distinct philosophies collide on the field forms the primary narrative engine of the modern college football calendar.</p>
</section>

<section id="what-this-reveals">
<h2>What This Reveals About Modern College Football</h2>
<p>Preseason polls are often dismissed as empty summer chatter designed to fill sports talk airwaves, but they reveal a great deal about how the collective consciousness of college football evaluates stability and trust. Voters consistently reward rosters with returning quarterback continuity, elite transfer portal recruitment, and proven coaching infrastructure.</p>

<p>Take Ohio State, for instance. Despite having to replace eight defensive starters for the second consecutive year, voters placed supreme faith in an offense loaded with Heisman finalist Julian Sayin, star receiver Jeremiah Smith, and 1,000-yard rusher Bo Jackson. The willingness to overlook massive defensive turnover in favor of offensive firepower shows a modern bias toward explosive scoring capacity in an era where offensive schemes continually stress-test defenses.</p>

<p>Similarly, the placement of teams like Notre Dame—riding a ten-game winning streak and armed with Heisman candidate CJ Carr—demonstrates how past momentum bleeds into future expectations. The sport has evolved into a continuous loop of high-stakes roster management, where the gap between rebuilding and reloading has vanished for the elite tier.</p>
</section>
