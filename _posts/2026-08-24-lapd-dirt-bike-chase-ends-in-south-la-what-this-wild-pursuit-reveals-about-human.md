<h2 id="psychology-perspective">The Psychology of Evasion: Why Do People Run?</h2>
<p>To an outside observer watching a helicopter broadcast, fleeing from law enforcement on a dirt bike across crowded public parks defies basic rational thought. The odds of escaping a modern metropolitan police force backed by aerial support are exceedingly low. Yet, human beings under acute pressure frequently abandon rational cost-benefit analysis in favor of immediate, impulsive preservation strategies.</p>

<p>Psychologically, high-stakes evasion is often fueled by a severe form of cognitive narrowing and the "fight-or-flight" response gone into overdrive. When confronted with the immediate threat of incarceration or accountability, the brain's emotional center—the amygdala—can hijack executive functioning housed in the prefrontal cortex. Long-term consequences vanish from the mental horizon, replaced entirely by a singular, urgent mandate: <em>get away right now.</em></p>
