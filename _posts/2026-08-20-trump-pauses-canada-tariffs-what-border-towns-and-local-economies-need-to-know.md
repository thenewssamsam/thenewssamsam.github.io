  <section id="what-happened">
    <h2>What Happened: The 72-Hour Tariff Freeze</h2>
    <p>Late Tuesday, just minutes before a midnight deadline, President Trump announced on social media that he was halting the aggressive 50% duties scheduled to hit approximately  billion worth of Canadian imports. The sudden reversal came after intense, high-stakes negotiations between Washington and Ottawa, resulting in what both sides describe as a tentative framework agreement.</p>
    
    <p>Prime Minister Mark Carney confirmed that the postponement would remain in effect until the end of the day Friday, noting that substantial progress has been made while acknowledging that important administrative and document-finalization work remains. The proposed 50% import taxes—invoked under Section 338 of the Tariff Act of 1930—would have touched everything from specialized building materials and alcoholic beverages to hockey sticks and apparel.</p>

    <p>While federal trade representatives and national chambers of commerce debated macroeconomic figures in Washington, the real pulse of this trade drama was beating far away from the capital—in the warehouses, trucking depots, retail shops, and family-owned businesses situated just miles from the northern border.</p>
