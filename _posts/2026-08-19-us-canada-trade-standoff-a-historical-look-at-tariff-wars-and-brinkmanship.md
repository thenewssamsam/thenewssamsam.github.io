    <h2>What is Happening at the Negotiating Table?</h2>
    <p>Canadian negotiators are currently attempting to stave off new tariffs that the Trump administration plans to impose on Wednesday. The demands are specific: the US wants an end to retaliatory tariffs on American autos, adjustments to Canada’s dairy supply management, and the removal of provincial bans on American alcohol. For Canada, the goal is to protect its steel, aluminum, and forestry sectors, which have already suffered significant damage from previous rounds of trade disputes.</p>

  <section id="historical-perspective">
    <h2>Historical Parallels: When Tariffs Become Policy</h2>
    <p>History suggests that trade disputes between the US and Canada are rarely resolved quickly. We can look to the 1930 Smoot-Hawley Tariff Act as a sobering reminder of how protectionist measures can spiral. When the US raised tariffs to record levels in the 1930s, the international trade system collapsed, deepening the Great Depression. While the current situation is far more contained, the psychological dynamic of "retaliatory cycles"—where provinces ban American goods in response to federal-level tariffs—mirrors the tit-for-tat escalations of the early 20th century.</p>
