<p>The legal battle over the controversial White House ballroom project has reached the nation's highest court, with the Department of Justice urging the Supreme Court to greenlight ongoing construction. As the administration points to critical national security features, preservationists argue the project bypasses constitutional boundaries—setting up a fascinating clash between executive authority and public legal rights.</p>

<section id="what-happened">
<h2>What Happened at the Supreme Court?</h2>
<p>The Trump administration filed its final arguments with the Supreme Court on Wednesday, seeking to overturn lower court rulings that ordered a halt to the construction of the massive White House ballroom project. The National Trust for Historic Preservation previously sued the administration, asserting that the executive branch lacks the congressional authorization required to undertake such a massive alteration of a national landmark.</p>

<p>With construction currently reported to be roughly 65% complete, the Justice Department warned that stopping work immediately could leave an unstable 70-foot structure exposed to the elements, threatening both the historic complex and Secret Service operations. Meanwhile, an appeals court ruling ordering a construction standstill is slated to take effect imminently unless the Supreme Court intervenes.</p>
