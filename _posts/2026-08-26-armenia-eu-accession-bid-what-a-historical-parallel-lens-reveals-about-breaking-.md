<section id="what-happened">
<h2>What Happened</h2>
<p>Armenian Prime Minister Nikol Pashinyan announced to parliament that Yerevan will soon launch its formal bid to join the European Union. This decisive move marks a profound acceleration in Armenia's efforts to distance itself from Moscow, its traditional political and military partner. Speaking to the National Assembly, Pashinyan noted that the process may begin in the near future, supported by a planned national referendum once the formal EU accession track gets underway.</p>

<p>This political pivot follows years of mounting friction between Yerevan and the Kremlin. Relations deteriorated sharply after Moscow failed to intervene or protect Armenia during the Nagorno-Karabakh conflict. In response, Pashinyan's government has steadily diversified its diplomatic and security partnerships, forging closer economic and strategic ties with France, the United Kingdom, and the broader West. Meanwhile, the Kremlin has reacted with heavy economic and diplomatic pressure, including threats of retaliatory measures and implicit warnings from Russian leadership.</p>

<h2>The Weight of Empire: A Historical Parallel</h2>

History is replete with empires and dominant powers struggling to maintain control over their traditional spheres of influence as smaller states seek sovereignty, security, and economic survival elsewhere. Armenia's current geopolitical dance shares striking similarities with the gradual realignments seen in Eastern Europe and the Baltic states during the early post-Cold War era, or even further back, when buffer states attempted to navigate competing imperial ambitions.</p>
