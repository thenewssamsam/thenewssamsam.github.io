<p>According to reports by <em>The New York Times</em>, House Democratic Leader Hakeem Jeffries recently held a private meeting with Jared Kushner. The encounter was arranged by a mutual acquaintance with the stated objective of exploring potential areas of "common ground" ahead of the November midterm elections, should Democrats recapture one or both chambers of Congress.</p>

<p>However, once news of the summit broke, the reaction from progressive quarters and party loyalists was swift and fierce. Jeffries took to X (formerly Twitter) to push back against the incoming wave of criticism, declaring bluntly, "No one from the Trump cartel is getting a pass."</p>

<p>To understand why a routine political maneuver—reaching across the aisle to anticipate governance challenges—provoked such visceral anger, we have to look through the lens of human psychology. In moments of intense political polarization, the human brain relies heavily on in-group and out-group mental categorization.</p>
