    <h3>Normalcy Bias and the Trap of Routine</h3>
    <p>In regions like Central Florida, afternoon downpours are as routine as morning traffic. This familiarity breeds a powerful cognitive shortcut known as normalcy bias. When an event happens repeatedly—such as summer afternoon storms—our brains categorize it as safe simply because we have survived it before. We expect a quick twenty-minute shower, not a localized flash flood that stalls traffic on major thoroughfares.</p>

    <h3>Loss Aversion vs. Safety Decisions</h3>
    <p>Behavioral psychology also highlights the role of loss aversion. People naturally hate disrupting their plans or losing time. Staying late at the office or pushing through a scheduled errand feels like an immediate, tangible loss, whereas the risk of getting caught in a dangerous thunderstorm feels abstract and distant until it actually happens. This mental weighing mechanism routinely favors convenience over caution.</p>
