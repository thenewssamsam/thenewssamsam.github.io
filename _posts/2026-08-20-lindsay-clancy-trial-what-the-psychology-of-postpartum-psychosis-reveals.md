As jurors hear testimony regarding Clancy’s mental state, the case forces a difficult confrontation between legal accountability and the profound, often misunderstood nature of severe maternal mental health crises.</p>

  <section id="what-happened">
    <h2>The Latest in the Courtroom</h2>
    <p>Recent proceedings in the Plymouth Superior Court have centered on the testimony of psychologist Paul Zeizel, who has conducted extensive evaluations of Clancy. Zeizel testified that Clancy expresses deep remorse, stating she thinks of her children "almost every moment of the day." This testimony stands at the heart of the defense’s argument that Clancy suffered from a break from reality caused by postpartum psychosis—a condition they claim rendered her unable to appreciate the criminality of her actions.</p>

    <h2>The Psychology of Postpartum Psychosis</h2>
    <p>To understand this trial, one must distinguish between "baby blues," postpartum depression, and the rare, medical emergency known as postpartum psychosis. Psychologically, psychosis involves a fundamental loss of touch with reality—a state where hallucinations or delusions can override a person's core values and survival instincts.</p>

    <h3>The Role of Cognitive Dissonance</h3>
    <p>The prosecution’s skepticism regarding Clancy’s mental state reflects a common psychological challenge: <em>cognitive dissonance</em>. Observers find it difficult to reconcile the image of a loving mother with the tragic reality of the crimes committed. In psychological terms, the defense argues that the "Clancy" who committed the act was not the same "Clancy" who loved her children, suggesting a dissociative state that is difficult for the average person to conceptualize without clinical context.</p>

    <h3>The Danger of Normalcy Bias</h3>
    <p>The trial also highlights our "normalcy bias"—the tendency to believe that because a person appears functional or "fine" in one moment, they cannot be in the throes of a psychiatric crisis the next. Understanding that mental health can deteriorate rapidly, especially under the biological stress of the postpartum period, is crucial for both public awareness and legal fairness.</p>
