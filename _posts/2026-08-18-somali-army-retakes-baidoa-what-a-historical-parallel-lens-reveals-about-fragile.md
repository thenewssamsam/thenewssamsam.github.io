<h1 style="font-size: 2.25rem; line-height: 1.2; margin-bottom: 0.75rem; color: #111;">Somali Army Retakes Baidoa: What a Historical Parallel Lens Reveals About Fragile Power Shifts</h1>

<div style="display: flex; gap: 1rem; font-size: 0.875rem; color: #555; margin-bottom: 1.5rem; align-items: center; border-bottom: 1px solid #eaeaea; padding-bottom: 1rem;">
  <time datetime="2026-08-17">Published: August 17, 2026</time>
  <span>•</span>
  <span>6 min read</span>
  <span>•</span>
  <span style="background: #f3f4f6; padding: 0.2rem 0.6rem; border-radius: 4px; font-weight: 500; color: #374151;">Lens: Historical Parallel</span>
</div>
</header>

<p style="font-size: 1.125rem; line-height: 1.6; color: #222; margin-bottom: 1.5rem;">
  When federal forces in Somalia drove opposition fighters out of Baidoa after a fierce pre-dawn firefight, it marked more than just a tactical victory for the central government in Mogadishu. The clashes—sparked by forces loyal to former South West State president Abdiaziz Hassan Mohamed Laftagareen—lay bare an old, fragile pattern of governance where constitutional extensions, regional fractures, and centralizing authority collide with devastating human consequences.
</p>

<section id="what-happened">
  <h2 style="font-size: 1.5rem; margin-top: 2rem; margin-bottom: 1rem; color: #1a1a1a; border-bottom: 2px solid #e5e7eb; padding-bottom: 0.5rem;">What Happened in Baidoa?</h2>
  <p style="line-height: 1.7; color: #333; margin-bottom: 1rem;">
    According to the Defence Ministry, the violence erupted before dawn on Monday when armed groups using vehicle-borne explosives attacked federal positions in Baidoa, the largest city in Somalia’s South West State. Federal forces repelled the assault, killing 30 attackers, though local residents described terrifying scenes of heavy explosions and gunfire waking families as they began morning prayers.
  </p>
  <p style="line-height: 1.7; color: #333; margin-bottom: 1.5rem;">
    Doctors Without Borders reported treating several casualties at its local facilities as fighting raged on two fronts. The clashes represent the fiercest violence in the city since federal forces initially seized control in March. That military takeover followed a profound political rupture: Laftagareen severed ties with Mogadishu after President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud pushed through controversial constitutional amendments that extended his own term by a year and overhauled Somalia's delicate 2012 power-sharing framework. Though Laftagareen resigned following the March intervention, loyalist fighters have continued efforts to reclaim the territory, with the Defence Ministry alleging links between the assailants and the al-Qaeda-linked militant group al-Shabab.
  </p>
</section>

<section id="historical-parallel-perspective">
  <h2 style="font-size: 1.5rem; margin-top: 2rem; margin-bottom: 1rem; color: #1a1a1a; border-bottom: 2px solid #e5e7eb; padding-bottom: 0.5rem;">A Historical Parallel Perspective: The Perils of Centralizing Power</h2>
  <p style="line-height: 1.7; color: #333; margin-bottom: 1rem;">
    History rarely repeats itself word for word, but it often rhymes with an uncomfortable precision. When national leaders unilaterally rewrite foundational governance charters—extending terms or altering power-sharing pacts—regional peripheries almost invariably push back. 
  </p>
  <p style="line-height: 1.7; color: #333; margin-bottom: 1.0rem;">
    Looking back at classic federalist transitions and post-conflict state-building efforts across the Horn of Africa and beyond, a recurring dynamic emerges: the tension between administrative consolidation and regional autonomy. When central governments attempt to absorb or override regional leadership structures without broad consensus, decentralized federal systems frequently fracture into localized power struggles.
  </p>
  
  <div style="background: #f8fafc; border-left: 4px solid #3b82f6; padding: 1.25rem; margin: 1.5rem 0; border-radius: 0 8px 8px 0;">
    <p style="font-size: 1.1rem; font-style: italic; color: #1e293b; margin: 0; line-height: 1.6;">
      "Constitutional amendments pushed without regional consensus do not consolidate a state; they draw battle lines across provincial capitals."
    </p>
  </div>

  <p style="line-height: 1.7; color: #333; margin-bottom: 1.5rem;">
    However, historical comparisons must also respect their limits. Unlike stable democracies undergoing institutional reform, Somalia operates under an extraordinarily fragile provisional constitution, constantly battling insurgent groups like al-Shabab that thrive in governance vacuums. The danger of the current power struggle in South West State is not simply that political actors are disputing election rules, but that the resulting military friction creates immediate space for radical extremists to exploit internal government divisions.
  </p>
</section>

<section id="humanitarian-cost">
  <h2 style="font-size: 1.5rem; margin-top: 2rem; margin-bottom: 1rem; color: #1a1a1a; border-bottom: 2px solid #e5e7eb; padding-bottom: 0.5rem;">The Human Toll in Baidoa</h2>
  <p style="line-height: 1.7; color: #333; margin-bottom: 1.5rem;">
    Behind the geopolitical maneuvering and constitutional debates lies a severe humanitarian crisis. Baidoa is not merely a regional political hub; it hosts hundreds of thousands of vulnerable people who have already been displaced by decades of war and repeated climate shocks. When fighting erupts in the streets, homes are destroyed, aid delivery is disrupted, and displaced families bear the brunt of political instability. The intersection of political gridlock and acute food insecurity underscores why these regional power struggles carry consequences far beyond the halls of government.
  </p>
</section>

<section id="how-should-readers-respond">

  <p style="line-height: 1.7; color: #333; margin-bottom: 1rem;">

  </p>

    <li style="margin-bottom: 0.5rem;"><strong>Promote inclusive dialogue:</strong> Advocate for diplomatic frameworks that prioritize broad-based power-sharing over unilateral central mandates in fractured states.</li>

<section id="conclusion">
  <h2 style="font-size: 1.5rem; margin-top: 2rem; margin-bottom: 1rem; color: #1a1a1a; border-bottom: 2px solid #e5e7eb; padding-bottom: 0.5rem;">One Last Thought</h2>
  <p style="line-height: 1.7; color: #333; margin-bottom: 1rem;">
    The clashes in Baidoa demonstrate that lasting stability cannot be decreed from a capital city through rushed constitutional changes. True state-building requires listening to regional grievances and honoring the delicate balance of power that keeps nations together. Until leaders prioritize inclusive governance over consolidation, the cycles of conflict will continue to ripple through the most vulnerable communities.
  </p>
  <p style="font-weight: 500; color: #111; margin-top: 1rem;">
    History shows that peace built on exclusion is only a pause between battles.
  </p>
</section>
