    <h3 style="font-size: 1.25krem; color: #2d3748; margin-top: 1.5rem;">The Illusion of Invincibility and Normalcy Bias</h3>
    <p>
      Adolescence and early adulthood are marked by a well-documented neurological phenomenon often termed the illusion of invincibility. The prefrontal cortex—the region of the brain responsible for impulse control, long-term planning, and risk assessment—does not fully mature until a person reaches their mid-twenties. Combined with a heightened sensitivity to dopamine and peer validation, young minds frequently underestimate real-world danger. When coupled with normalcy bias—the psychological state where people underestimate the likelihood of disaster—drivers may assume that high speeds or dangerous maneuvers will simply "turn out fine," right up until the moment physics proves otherwise.
    </p>
