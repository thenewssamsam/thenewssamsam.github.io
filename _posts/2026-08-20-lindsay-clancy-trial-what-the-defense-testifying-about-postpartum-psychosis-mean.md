  <section id="what-happened" class="ncg-section">
    <h2>What Happened in Court</h2>
    <p>The courtroom in Plymouth, Massachusetts, fell quiet as clinical psychologist Paul Zeizel took the stand. Testifying at the murder trial of 36-year-old Lindsay Clancy, Zeizel recounted his evaluation of the mother in the intensive care unit days after she strangled her three young children—5-year-old Cora, 3-year-old Dawson, and 8-month-old Callan—in January 2023, before attempting suicide.</p>
    
    <p>According to Zeizel's testimony, Clancy claimed she heard a male voice ordering her to kill her children and then take her own life, telling her she "didn't have any choice." The defense called four witnesses to demonstrate that Clancy was suffering from severe postpartum psychosis at the time of the slayings and should not be held criminally responsible. Throughout the trial, medical records and expert testimonies have painted a picture of a mother who spiraled through months of severe postpartum anxiety, depression, and insomnia while cycling through numerous medications that allegedly worsened her condition.</p>
    
    <p>While prosecutors argue that the tragedy was premeditated and driven by despair, the defense continues to center its case on the catastrophic collapse of maternal mental healthcare. For parents watching from the outside, the case is an agonizing reminder of how fragile family well-being can be when invisible illnesses go unrecognized or untreated.</p>

For families and partners, this unpredictability makes it agonizingly difficult to assess real danger. A parent might smile through breakfast, only to experience severe auditory hallucinations hours later.</p>
