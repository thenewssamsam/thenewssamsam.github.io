    <h1>Missouri Court Clears Trump-Backed House Map: What Legal Rights Reveal About Gerrymandering</h1>
  </header>

  <section class="ncg-intro-section">
    <p>A Cole County circuit judge has cleared the way for Missouri's controversial, Trump-backed congressional map to be used in the upcoming November elections, rejecting a citizen-led petition to force a statewide referendum. The ruling marks a major victory for Republicans fighting to protect their slim majority in the U.S. House, but voting rights advocates have already promised an immediate appeal to the state Supreme Court. At the heart of this high-stakes legal clash is a fundamental question about constitutional authority: Who gets to draw the lines of democracy, and what legal rights do voters retain when those lines are redrawn?</p>
  </section>

  <div class="ncg-quick-take">
    <h3>Quick Take</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>What happened:</strong> A Missouri judge ruled that state law does not permit a public referendum on congressional redistricting, keeping new Republican-friendly maps in place for November.</li>
      <li><strong>Why it matters:</strong> The redrawn 5th Congressional District targets longtime Democratic Rep. Emanuel Cleaver, shifting the balance of power as part of a broader national redistricting push.</li>
      <li><strong>The legal reality:</strong> Opponents have appealed to the Missouri Supreme Court, setting up a showdown over citizen referendum rights and election timelines.</li>
    </ul>
  </div>

  <section class="ncg-content-section" id="what-happened">
    <h2>What Happened in the Missouri Redistricting Battle?</h2>
    <p>The legal controversy stems from a special legislative session called last year by Republican Gov. Mike Kehoe. Prompted by a push from President Donald Trump for GOP-led states to redraw congressional boundaries ahead of the midterms, the Missouri General Assembly passed a revised map specifically designed to challenge Democratic U.S. Rep. Emanuel Cleaver of Kansas City.</p>

    <h2>A Legal Rights Explainer: Can Voters Challenge Redistricting?</h2>
    <p>For everyday citizens, watching politicians redraw the very districts they vote in can feel profoundly frustrating. It naturally raises questions about where public power ends and legislative authority begins. From a legal standpoint, the Missouri case exposes a tension between representative governance and direct democracy.</p>

Judge Green concurred, noting that the text lacks a "clear" statement shifting that authority.</p>

  <section class="ncg-content-section" id="national-landscape">
    <h2>The Broader National Redistricting Battle</h2>
    <p>Missouri is hardly an isolated battleground. Across the United States, mid-decade redistricting efforts have triggered roughly a dozen major lawsuits over the past year. Following President Trump's call for GOP-led states to maximize their legislative advantage, states like Texas, North Carolina, Ohio, Florida, Tennessee, Louisiana, and Alabama have all engaged in aggressive map-redrawing.</p>
