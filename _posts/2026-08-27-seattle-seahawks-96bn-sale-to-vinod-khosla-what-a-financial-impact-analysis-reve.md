    <p class="ncg-deck" style="font-size: 1.125rem; color: #4b5563; margin: 0;">NFL owners have officially approved a staggering $9.612bn acquisition of the reigning Super Bowl champions by billionaire tech investor Vinod Khosla, redefining the economic ceiling of professional sports assets.</p>

  <section class="ncg-section" id="news-hook" style="margin-bottom: 24px;">
    <h2 style="font-size: 1.5rem; font-weight: 700; color: #1f2937; margin-bottom: 12px; border-left: 4px solid #059669; padding-left: 12px;">What Just Happened in Seattle?</h2>
    <p>When professional sports franchises trade hands, the figures involved routinely push past conventional economic intuition. The latest milestone arrives from the Pacific Northwest, where the reigning Super Bowl champion Seattle Seahawks have officially been approved for sale in a landmark $9.612bn deal led by billionaire venture capitalist Vinod Khosla.</p>
    
    <p>According to reports from BBC Sport, the transaction required and easily secured the necessary support of at least 24 of 32 NFL owners. This massive price tag makes the Seahawks purchase the second-highest amount ever paid for a North American sports franchise, sitting just behind recent astronomical valuations in basketball, such as the reported $12.5bn movement surrounding the Los Angeles Lakers, and outpacing the Washington Commanders' $6.05bn sale in 2023.</p>
    
    <p>The sale stems from the directive of the estate of Paul G. Allen. The late Microsoft co-founder originally saved the franchise from relocation back in 1997. Following his passing in 2018, his sister Jody Allen managed the team with an ultimate mandate to sell both the Seahawks and the NBA's Portland Trail Blazers, dedicating the substantial proceeds entirely to philanthropic causes. Now, under Khosla's stewardship, a new chapter begins—one governed by both high-stakes capital and a mandate to preserve a winning culture.</p>

Furthermore, multi-billion-dollar national television contracts, shared ticket revenues, and lucrative streaming partnerships create a stable baseline of cash flow that protects owners from routine economic downturns.</p>
