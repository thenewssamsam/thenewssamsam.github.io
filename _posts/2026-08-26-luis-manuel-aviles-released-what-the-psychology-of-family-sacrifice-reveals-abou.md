<section id="what-happened">
<h2>What Happened</h2>
<p>Luis Manuel Aviles, a 48-year-old handyman living in Key West, Florida, for nearly two decades, was released from immigration detention just days after his arrest sparked a nationwide outcry. His sudden detention by ICE occurred while his son, Joshua Aviles, was enduring a historic, record-breaking deployment aboard the USS Abraham Lincoln aircraft carrier in the Middle East—working exhausting 12-hour shifts without a break for nine months.</p>

<p>The arrest placed an immense emotional burden on a sailor already operating at the edge of his physical and mental limits. Following rapid media attention and congressional scrutiny, an immigration judge dismissed the case, leading to Aviles's swift release. While the Department of Homeland Security offered no official explanation for either the arrest or the dismissal, the incident highlights a growing friction point between aggressive domestic immigration enforcement and the vulnerable support systems of active-duty military personnel.</p>

<h2>Normalcy Bias and the Breaking Point</h2>
<p>Another psychological concept visible in this situation is normalcy bias—the tendency for systems and bureaucracies to assume that routines will continue smoothly without accounting for catastrophic disruption. For 19 years, the Aviles family built a quiet, lawful life in Florida, with Luis working as a handyman and holding a valid work permit.</p>
