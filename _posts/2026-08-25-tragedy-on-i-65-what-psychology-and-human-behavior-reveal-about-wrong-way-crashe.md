  <h2 id="what-happened">What Happened on I-65</h2>
  <p>According to reports from the Indiana State Police, a devastating tragedy unfolded just after 2:30 a.m. on a Sunday in the southbound lanes of I-65, roughly one mile south of Seymour in Jackson County. Authorities received frantic calls reporting a head-on collision.</p>

  <p>Investigators determined that 51-year-old Theosco Middlebrooks of Louisville, Kentucky, was driving a 2005 Jeep Liberty northbound while traveling in the southbound lanes. Middlebrooks crashed head-on into a 2023 Ford Escape driven by 31-year-old Alyssa Osborn of Scottsburg.</p>

  <p>The impact was catastrophic. Both Middlebrooks and Osborn were pronounced dead at the scene. Osborn’s one-year-old son, Waylon, was critically injured and rushed to a hospital, where he tragically passed away shortly after. State police noted that the young boy was properly restrained in a car seat in the rear passenger side of the vehicle, highlighting that while safety measures are vital, they cannot always shield against extraordinary hazards.</p>

  <h3>Impairment and Cognitive Tunneling</h3>
  <p>When substances like alcohol are introduced into the equation, the brain's executive functioning deteriorates rapidly. Alcohol impairs spatial awareness, reaction time, and decision-making capacity. Psychologists often observe a phenomenon known as cognitive tunneling under severe impairment or high stress: the individual's focus narrows drastically to a single point—such as the road directly in front of them—while peripheral awareness of critical cues, like wrong-way warning signs, completely collapses.</p>

  <h3>Normalcy Bias and Delayed Recognition</h3>
  <p>Another powerful psychological factor at play is normalcy bias—the human tendency to assume that things will function the way they normally do. When a driver enters a highway ramp incorrectly, their brain may struggle to process the sudden contradiction of their expectations. Similarly, oncoming motorists like Alyssa Osborn have mere fractions of a second to process an impossible scenario: headlights approaching in lanes meant for high-speed travel. The sheer cognitive dissonance of seeing headlights where there should be taillights creates a fatal delay in the human startle-and-response cycle.</p>
