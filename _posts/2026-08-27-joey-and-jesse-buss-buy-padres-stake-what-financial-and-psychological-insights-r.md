    <h2 style="font-size: 1.6rem; color: #111; border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb; padding-bottom: 8px; margin-top: 0;">What Happened: The Buss Brothers' Padres Investment</h2>
    <p>In a move that caught the attention of both baseball and basketball worlds, Jesse and Joey Buss—the youngest sons of the late, iconic Los Angeles Lakers owner Jerry Buss—have officially stepped into Major League Baseball. Through their investment firm, Buss Sports Capital, launched in September, the brothers have acquired approximately 5% of the San Diego Padres.</p>
    <p>This strategic acquisition places the duo within a new ownership group spearheaded by husband-and-wife controlling owners José E. Feliciano and Kwanza Jones. While precise financial terms of the deal were not publicly disclosed by the team or the firm, industry estimates place the stake value solidly within the upper echelons of professional sports minor-investor tiers. For Jesse (38) and Joey (41), this represents their first major commercial endeavor outside of the Los Angeles Lakers organization, following a tumultuous period of family restructuring and high-profile sales of their legacy franchise shares.</p>
    <p>The timing of this investment is deeply intertwined with broader ownership shifts in professional sports. Last year, the Buss family sold its controlling stake in the Lakers to Mark Walter for a staggering $10 billion. Although initial agreements promised stability for family members in operations, subsequent high-stakes buyouts—including Joshua Kushner and Bob Iger agreeing to purchase majority ownership for $12.5 billion—triggered internal sibling disputes and the eventual departure of Jesse and Joey from their operational roles as assistant general manager and vice president of research and development, respectively. Rather than exiting the sports landscape entirely, the brothers have chosen to pivot, bringing two decades of basketball operational experience down the coast to San Diego.</p>
