<h2>The Psychology of Postpartum Psychosis</h2>
<p>To understand the testimony surrounding Lindsay Clancy, one must examine the clinical reality of postpartum psychosis through established psychological frameworks. Unlike standard postpartum depression—which involves persistent sadness, anxiety, and fatigue—postpartum psychosis is a rare, terrifying psychiatric emergency affecting roughly one to two in every thousand postpartum women. It typically strikes abruptly within the first few weeks after childbirth, characterized by severe mood swings, confusion, paranoia, and, crucially, hallucinations and delusions.</p>

<p>Psychologists point to concepts such as <strong>command hallucinations</strong> and <strong>severe cognitive dissociation</strong> to explain how a loving mother could experience such an absolute fracture in executive functioning. When the brain's neurochemical balance is violently disrupted by sudden hormonal drops combined with medication adjustments, the internal filter separating reality from delusion collapses. The individual is no longer operating with typical conscious free will; instead, they are trapped inside a psychological state where terrifying commands feel absolute and inescapable.</p>
