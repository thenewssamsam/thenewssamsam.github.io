<h2>What Happened: The Scope of the Breaches</h2>
<p>On Wednesday, U.S. authorities revealed a major counter-cyber operation that dismantled domains tied to two hacking platforms, QScan and QTRouter. According to Department of Justice filings and affidavits, these platforms were operated by Nanjing Xinjiuwei Network Technology Company—a Chinese firm with direct ties to China’s civilian intelligence agency, the Ministry of State Security, and its military, the People’s Liberation Army.</p>

<h2>The Psychology of Cyber Intrusion: Normalcy Bias and Vigilance Fatigue</h2>
<p>When headlines blare about foreign hackers breaching the Federal Reserve or NASA, our first instinct is to look for a technical failure—a weak firewall, an unpatched VPN vulnerability, or an outdated software protocol. Yet, human behavioral patterns often provide the true entry point for sophisticated actors. Understanding the cognitive biases at play helps explain why even the most heavily funded institutions remain perpetually vulnerable.</p>

<h3>Normalcy Bias in High-Stakes Environments</h3>
<p>Normalcy bias is a cognitive state where people and organizations underestimate the possibility of a disaster, or its potential impact, even when warning signs are clear. In large bureaucratic organizations like government agencies or major corporations, employees and IT professionals often operate under the comforting assumption that "our systems are too secure for anyone to successfully breach." This psychological armor makes it difficult to maintain active, hyper-vigilant scrutiny over every minor system alert.</p>

<h3>Vigilance Fatigue and Cognitive Overload</h3>
<p>Another powerful psychological factor is vigilance fatigue. When IT security teams are bombarded with thousands of automated threat warnings every single day, the human brain adapts by filtering out the noise. Over time, genuine anomalies blend in with routine false positives. Hackers rely on this exact psychological wear-and-down effect, launching continuous, quiet probes over years until an exhausted or distracted administrator misses a single anomalous login attempt or virtual private network vulnerability.</p>
