<h2>What Happened in the Q2 Earnings Finale</h2>
<p>The closing stretch of the Q2 earnings calendar has delivered a tale of two distinct economic realities. <p>On the other side, individual corporate updates have sent shockwaves through trading floors. Intuit shares tumbled following soft forward guidance that hinted at a cooling small-business software market. Meanwhile, Dick's Sporting Goods stock plummeted by an astonishing 30%, catching equity analysts and retail investors entirely off guard. Alongside reports from dollar-store bellwethers like Dollar Tree and Dollar General, as well as software stalwarts like Salesforce and Zoom, these turbulent disclosures have forced investors to look past headline index numbers and scrutinize the actual health of consumer spending and corporate margins.</p>

In modern equities, a company does not trade on what it earned yesterday; it trades on the discounted present value of what it expects to earn tomorrow. That forward-looking mechanism explains why a single weak guidance statement can erase billions in market capitalization in a matter of hours.</p>
