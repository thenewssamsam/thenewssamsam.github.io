  <p class="ncg-lead">When natural disasters strike with sudden fury, they challenge our sense of security and test the bonds of our communities. The rapidly expanding Ross Fire in North Texas—surpassing 50,000 acres and threatening historic landmarks like Worth Ranch—reminds us of both the profound fragility of our earthly surroundings and the extraordinary resilience of the human spirit when neighbors come to the aid of one another.</p>

  <h2 id="what-happened">What Happened: The Rapid Spread of the Ross Fire</h2>
  <p>The Ross Fire began on Monday afternoon just west of the town of Palo Pinto, Texas, quickly escalating into a multi-alarm crisis. Fueled by persistent dry conditions, extreme heat, and strong winds blowing steadily to the northeast, the fire has ballooned to over 50,000 acres while remaining only single-digit percentages contained, according to the Texas A&amp;M Forest Service.</p>
  
  <p>Mandatory and recommended evacuation orders have rippled through southern Jack County and northern sections of Palo Pinto County. Families have had to make heartbreaking, split-second decisions to leave behind homes, livestock, and livelihoods. Among the structural losses, historic Worth Ranch—a cherished youth scout camp owned by Scouting America's Longhorn Council—sustained catastrophic damage, though swift emergency actions ensured all camp personnel and scouts were safely evacuated.</p>

  <p>In response, federal aid has begun to mobilize. Texas Governor Greg Abbott announced that the Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA) approved a Fire Management Assistance Grant to bolster state and local suppression efforts. Meanwhile, everyday citizens are stepping up, dropping off cases of water, supplies, and food at distribution points near Mineral Wells to support exhausted firefighters.</p>
