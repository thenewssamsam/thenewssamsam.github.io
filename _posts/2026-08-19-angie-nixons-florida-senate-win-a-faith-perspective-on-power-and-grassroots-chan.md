    <section id="introduction">
        <p>In a result that caught many political observers off guard, Florida state representative Angie Nixon secured a victory in the Democratic US Senate primary, overcoming a massive fundraising deficit against former intelligence officer Alexander Vindman. As Floridians look toward the general election, this outcome invites us to reflect not just on the political mechanics at play, but on the deeper themes of influence, leadership, and the nature of power itself.</p>
    </section>

    <section id="what-happened">
        <h2>The Path to an Upset</h2>
        <p>Nixon, a member of the Democratic Socialists of America, ran a campaign fueled by grassroots organizing rather than the heavy financial backing that characterized her opponent's efforts. While Vindman held a significant funding advantage—with over $16 million compared to Nixon's $975,000—the electorate chose a candidate who emphasized her long-standing history of local activism. Her victory sets the stage for a general election contest against Republican incumbent Ashley Moody.</p>
    </section>
