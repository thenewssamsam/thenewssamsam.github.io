<section id="what-happened">
<p>For millions of Californians living in apartments, condos, and rental homes, rooftop solar has long been an out-of-reach luxury. While homeowners with sprawling roofs could offset rising electricity rates, renters watched from their balconies as utility bills climbed year after year. That dynamic could soon shift.</p>

<p>The California legislature has officially passed Senate Bill 868, widely known as the Plug and Play Solar Act, sending the legislation to Governor Gavin Newsom's desk for a final signature. Authored by Senator Scott Wiener and sponsored by the Environmental Working Group (EWG) and the Abundance Network, SB 868 removes major regulatory roadblocks that have prevented everyday consumers from plugging portable solar panels directly into standard household outlets. With the governor holding a 30-day decision window, the state stands on the brink of a massive shift in localized energy economics.</p>
</section>

While this four-year window gives the market breathing room to establish itself, it also leaves a question mark over the long-term regulatory framework. Will the legislature remove the sunset before 2030, or will utilities successfully claw back exemptions once adoption numbers swell? For early adopters, the financial incentive is immediate, but long-term regulatory certainty remains a moving target.</p>
