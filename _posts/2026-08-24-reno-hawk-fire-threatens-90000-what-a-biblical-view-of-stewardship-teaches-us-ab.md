<section id="what-happened" class="ncg-section">
<h2>What Happened in Reno?</h2>
<p>The Hawk Fire began on a Saturday that started with little indication of the disaster to come. By Sunday, however, shifting winds and extreme dry brush transformed a small patch of flames into a roaring wall of fire that consumed more than 23 square miles of the Humboldt–Toiyabe National Forest. Officials confirmed at least six injuries—including three first responders and three civilians—while Governor Joe Lombardo declared a state of emergency for Washoe County, mobilizing the Nevada National Guard to assist with aerial support and neighborhood security.</p>

<p>For families like the Kieffers, who watched their home burn to the ground on social media, and residents like Rick Arrate, who fled his mountaintop home with only two golden retrievers, the suddenness of the disaster was staggering. Evacuation apps lagged behind the blinding speed of the flames, and residents described an oven-blast wind that gave them mere minutes to grab precious mementos—a Bible, family photos, ashes of loved ones—before fleeing into the smoke.</p>

<p>Disasters like the Hawk Fire instantly strip away the illusions of permanence that modern life encourages us to build. We invest our time, labor, and resources into houses, careers, and possessions, often living as though our earthly footprint is entirely secure. Yet Scripture frequently reminds us of the profound fragility of our material world. James 4:14 gently reminds us: <em>"Yet you do not know what tomorrow will bring. What is your life? For you are a mist that appears for a little time and then vanishes."</em></p>
