    <h2>A Historical Parallel: When Disarmament Meets Political Survival</h2>
    <p>
      History rarely repeats itself neatly, but it often rhymes in the complex arena of Middle Eastern conflict resolution. When outside mediators attempt to impose rigid deadlines on entrenched factions, the dynamics mirror classic post-conflict disarmament efforts from the late 20th century—such as the complex demobilization agreements in Northern Ireland or the Balkan accords of the 1990s. In each of these historical instances, the hardest part was never drafting a roadmap on paper; it was bridging the gap between immediate political survival for local leaders and the grueling, unglamorous mechanics of verification on the ground.
    </p>
    <p>
      Kushner's reliance on an American military verifier—General Jeffers—echoes historical precedents where neither warring party trusted the other to police the transition. Yet, history also teaches a sobering lesson about hardline rhetoric during fragile transitions. When leaders face domestic electoral pressure or ideological defectors within their own cabinets, even well-structured international boards can struggle to maintain momentum against the pull of domestic politics.
