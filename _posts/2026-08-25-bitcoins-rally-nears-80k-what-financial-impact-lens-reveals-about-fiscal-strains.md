<h2 id="what-happened">What Happened: The Spark Behind the Surge</h2>
<p>According to recent reports from Bloomberg, the spark for this sudden price action came largely from the traditional bond market. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent’s plan to at least double long-dated Treasury buybacks initially pulled yields lower and weakened the US dollar while gold surged simultaneously.</p>

<p>That unique combination revived the so-called debasement trade: the financial market thesis that mounting fiscal strains, widening government deficits, and loose monetary policy strengthen the fundamental case for holding scarce assets located entirely outside government-controlled monetary systems. For Bitcoin bulls, it provided fresh macroeconomic ammunition just as bearish market positioning had become severely stretched.</p>

<h2 id="financial-mechanics">The Financial Mechanics of the Debasement Trade</h2>
<p>To understand why a shift in Treasury buybacks can send shockwaves through the cryptocurrency market, we have to examine the underlying plumbing of modern finance. When governments face unsustainable debt spirals—a concern recently echoed even by traditional financial heavyweights like Ray Dalio—investors begin to question the long-term purchasing power of fiat currencies.</p>

<p>In simple terms, fiat money relies entirely on trust in the issuing government. When fiscal deficits expand rapidly and central banks are forced to accommodate massive debt issuance, the supply of currency effectively expands relative to hard, scarce assets. Gold has historically acted as the primary hedge against this risk. However, in the modern digital era, Bitcoin has increasingly captured a share of that debasement-hedging capital.</p>
