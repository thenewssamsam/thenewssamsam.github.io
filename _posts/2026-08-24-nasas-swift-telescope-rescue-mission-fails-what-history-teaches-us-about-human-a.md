    <h2>What Happened to the Swift Rescue?</h2>
    <p>Space exploration has always been a high-stakes gamble against physics, time, and gravity. Recently, that reality struck home for NASA and Arizona-based Katalyst Space Technologies. A bold, first-of-its-kind $30 million rescue mission designed to save the aging Neil Gehrels Swift Observatory from tumbling back into Earth's atmosphere ultimately failed. The rescue spacecraft, dubbed LINK—built at a blistering speed of just nine months—suffered severe altitude-control failures, reaction-wheel malfunctions, and propulsion problems in orbit. Instead of safely grappling the 22-year-old telescope and pushing it into a higher, safer orbit, LINK began spinning out of control.</p>

Yet, increased solar activity accelerated atmospheric drag, dragging the $500 million observatory closer to a fiery demise. Without a successful intervention, Swift is destined to burn up upon re-entering Earth's atmosphere. While the immediate loss is a setback for modern astrophysics, looking back through the lens of history reveals that grandiose engineering failures have always accompanied humanity's greatest leaps into the unknown.</p>
