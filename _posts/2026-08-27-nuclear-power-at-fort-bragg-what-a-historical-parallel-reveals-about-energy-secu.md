  <section id="what-happened">
    <h2>What Happened at Fort Bragg?</h2>
    <p>According to an exclusive report by Axios, the Army's high-stakes Janus program has officially tapped Antares to deploy nuclear microreactors at Fort Bragg—home to the XVIII Airborne Corps and tens of thousands of service members and civilian personnel. The arrangement is part of a broader federal push to secure resilient, fail-safe energy supplies for critical military installations.</p>

    <p>Antares chief executive Jordan Bramble noted that the military has long been caught between a rock and a hard place regarding energy. Civilian power grids and supply chains remain vulnerable to disruptions, cyberattacks, and extreme weather. By utilizing TRISO (tri-structural isotropic) fuel—tested and proven at the Idaho National Laboratory—these compact reactors can sustain vital base operations completely independent of external infrastructure.</p>

    <p>Alongside Antares at Fort Bragg, other defense tech heavyweights secured awards under the Janus initiative: BWXT Advanced Technologies at Fort Campbell, General Atomics at Fort Hood, Radiant at Fort Benning, and Westinghouse Government Services at Fort Drum. Together, these projects represent the spear tip of advanced nuclear deployment in the United States.</p>
