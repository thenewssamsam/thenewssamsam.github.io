  <section id="what-happened">
    <h2>The Rescue That Captured a Nation</h2>
    <p>The incident, which went viral in late July, showed the raw reality of ocean rescue. Waves repeatedly hammered the two as they struggled against the current. Thanks to Williams’ persistence and the eventual assistance of fellow lifeguard Aaron Bohnen, both survived. The video prompted widespread calls for recognition, eventually leading to a meeting in the Oval Office where Donald Trump praised Williams' bravery, quipping, "I don’t know if I’d do it."</p>

  <section id="lens-perspective">
    <h2>A Lesson in Heroism: What It Means to Act</h2>
    <p>Beyond the politics of the meeting, the story of Ryder Williams offers a profound lesson on the nature of courage. Heroism is often romanticized, but at its core, it is simply the willingness to act when others might hesitate. Williams did not wait for a title or a designation; he saw a need and fulfilled it.</p>
    
    <div class="ncg-quote-box" style="border-left: 4px solid #6c5ce7; padding: 20px; background: #f9f7ff; margin: 20px 0; font-style: italic;">
      "True heroism isn't about waiting for a formal designation or a reward; it is the quiet, reflexive choice to prioritize another person's safety over your own comfort when the waves are crashing."
    </div>

    <p>This story teaches us that we don't need to be in a position of authority to impact a life. However, it also highlights a societal gap: we admire these acts of heroism while often failing to provide the institutional frameworks that support those who perform them daily.</p>
  </section>

  <section id="world-pattern">
    <h2>The First Responder Debate</h2>
    <p>The conversation surrounding this rescue has turned toward California's potential legislation to officially label lifeguards as "first responders." While the label seems intuitive to many, state senator John Laird points out the complexity: "How does it work? How does it get designated? What training comes with it? Who pays for it?"</p>
    <p>This reveals a common friction in our world: the gap between our emotional desire to honor someone and the practical, fiscal, and regulatory requirements of formalizing that honor. Recognizing a profession isn't just about a title; it is about ensuring that the resources, training, and worker protections match the level of risk these individuals face every day.</p>
  </section>
