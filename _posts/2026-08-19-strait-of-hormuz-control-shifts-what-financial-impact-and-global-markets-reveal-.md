<section id="what-happened">
<h2>What Happened in the Strait of Hormuz?</h2>
<p>Over the past several months, the narrow waterway connecting the Persian Gulf to the Gulf of Oman has become the epicenter of heightened geopolitical tension and military maneuvering. Both Tehran and Washington have repeatedly claimed the upper hand, creating an information environment thick with conflicting rhetoric and market uncertainty.</p>
<p>However, recent shipping tracking data provides a clearer picture. According to data from Kpler, more than 80% of vessel transits through the Strait of Hormuz over a recent two-week period utilized the Omani route—a United Nations-authorized shipping channel that Iran strongly opposes. Despite ongoing security risks and threats from Iranian forces that have targeted dozens of vessels attempting to travel via the northern coast, commercial operators are increasingly prioritizing US naval protection to ensure their cargoes move.</p>

The sudden pivot away from Iran's preferred northern waters has dealt a direct blow to Tehran's financial strategy.</p>
