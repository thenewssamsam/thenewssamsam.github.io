<section id="what-happened">
  <h2>What Happened: Hard Hats on the South Lawn</h2>
  <p>In a break from traditional White House press conferences, President Donald Trump recently turned the executive mansion's lawn into a construction site walkthrough. Pointing out the fine details of a newly laid white granite helipad and driveway, the president expressed immense pride in his handiwork. He leaned over to autograph one of the paving stones—marking it with his presidential numbers for future generations to uncover—and spoke passionately about the durability of natural stone over asphalt.</p>
  
  <p>Alongside the helipad, construction continues on a massive $400 million ballroom project. Despite legal challenges and competing national headlines involving foreign policy and economic pressures, the president remained entirely focused on the physical transformation of the grounds. “This will be here long after we’re gone,” Trump remarked to reporters, emphasizing his desire to mold Washington in a durable, lasting way.</p>

<section id="the-pursuit-of-permanence">
  <h2>The Human Drive for Permanent Monuments</h2>
  <p>Throughout history, leaders, builders, and ordinary citizens alike have sought ways to cheat transience. We construct buildings meant to endure for centuries, carve our names into cornerstones, plant gardens, and write books, all driven by a quiet, persistent fear of being forgotten. Granite that can withstand 35,000 pounds of pressure per square inch sounds unbreakable, offering a comforting illusion of absolute permanence in a fragile world.</p>

  <p class="ncg-body-highlight">When we admire a piece of architecture or praise the strength of natural stone, we are often expressing a longing that goes deeper than engineering: we want our existence to matter, and we want proof that we were here.</p>

Nature, time, and changing tides have a way of humbling even the most carefully engineered human designs.</p>

<section id="conclusion">
  <h2>A Prayer for Wisdom and Hope</h2>
  <p>Heavenly Father, we look at the shifting landscapes of our world and recognize how easily we chase after temporary comforts and visible monuments. Thank You for blessing human ingenuity, creativity, and the desire to build and improve our surroundings. Help us, however, not to place our ultimate hope in things that rust, fade, or wear away. Grant our leaders wisdom, humility, and a clear vision for stewardship. Direct our hearts toward treasures that endure in Your kingdom, and teach us to number our days so that we walk in wisdom and grace. In Jesus' name, Amen.</p>
