    <section id="what-happened" style="margin-bottom: 30px;">
        <h2 style="font-size: 1.6rem; color: #2d3748; border-bottom: 1px solid #edf2f7; padding-bottom: 8px; margin-bottom: 15px;">What Happened: Mosseri Takes the Stand</h2>
        <p>The high-stakes legal showdown between a coalition of 29 state attorneys general and tech giant Meta reached a dramatic turning point on Tuesday. Instagram chief Adam Mosseri stepped up to the witness stand at the Oakland federal courthouse in California, facing aggressive questioning in a landmark social media addiction trial.</p>
        
        <p>Led by California Attorney General Rob Bonta alongside counterparts from Colorado, New Jersey, and Kentucky, the sweeping lawsuit alleges that Meta violated the federal Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA) and various consumer protection laws. The coalition is seeking penalties that could reach a staggering $200 billion. At the core of the plaintiffs' argument is the claim that Meta executives—including Mosseri and CEO Mark Zuckerberg—publicly misrepresented the severity of child-related mental health harms linked to platforms like Facebook and Instagram while minimizing internal safety red flags.</p>

        <p>During his first day of testimony, Mosseri pushed back against assertions that he actively worked to shield himself from damning internal data. When questioned by trial attorney Jason Slothouber regarding whether he directed employees to withhold potential liabilities, Mosseri answered plainly: <q>I'm not trying to encourage my team to hide anything.</q></p>
