<section id="what-happened">
    <h2>What Happened in the July PCE Report?</h2>
    <p>Prices continued their upward march this summer according to the latest figures from the Commerce Department. The personal consumption expenditures (PCE) price index—the Federal Reserve’s preferred barometer for measuring inflation—increased by a seasonally adjusted 0.2% in July, pushing the annual headline inflation rate to 3.7%. Both figures edged slightly higher than the Dow Jones consensus forecasts, coming in 0.1 percentage point above expectations.</p>
    
    <p>Meanwhile, the core PCE index, which strips out the volatile food and energy sectors to give policymakers a clearer view of long-term trends, posted a 0.2% monthly gain and an annual increase of 3.3%, right in line with expectations. Alongside these price metrics, personal income rose 0.4% while consumer spending increased by 0.2%, signaling that households continue to spend even as the cost of living remains stubbornly above the central bank's elusive 2% target.</p>

    <p>Beneath the headline figures, the details paint a nuanced picture of consumer life. Goods prices actually dipped 0.1% for the month, largely aided by a 2.7% drop in gasoline and other energy-related goods, alongside lower prices for long-lasting household equipment. However, services prices moved in the opposite direction, rising 0.3% due to notable increases in financial services, insurance, and housing costs. Stock market futures pulled back slightly in response, and Treasury yields climbed higher as investors digested the ongoing challenges facing the broader economy.</p>

With no formal meeting scheduled for August, policymakers have a brief window of respite before gathering on September 15–16. Financial markets currently price in only about a one-in-three probability of a rate cut or hike at the September meeting, with many looking toward December as a more likely turning point.</p>
