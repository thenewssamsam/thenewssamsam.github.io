  <section id="what-happened">
    <h2>What Happened: Inside the Bernina Express Route</h2>
    <p>According to recent travel reporting, the Bernina Express continues to capture global attention as the premier alpine rail experience. Departing from Switzerland's oldest town, Chur, the train ascends past towering glaciers and high mountain passes before descending softly into Italian landscapes around Poschiavo and Tirano. Along the way, passengers encounter engineering marvels like the iconic Brusio Spiral Viaduct, where the train loops in a 360-degree turn designed to manage steep alpine elevation changes without disrupting the natural topography.</p>
    
    <p>Travel experts note that the route offers stark seasonal contrasts. While summer brings lush green valleys, winter transforms the Engadin region into a world of frozen lakes and snow-draped forests before arriving in milder Italian climates where palm trees thrive. With ticket prices ranging around $162 for second class and $279 for first class—plus mandatory seat reservations—the journey champions a philosophy of immersive, unhurried exploration rather than rapid transit.</p>

    <h3>The Antidote to Normalcy Bias and Cognitive Fatigue</h3>
    <p>When travelers step aboard the Bernina Express, they experience what psychologists call environmental contrast. The physical design of the train—featuring panoramic windows that wrap upward toward the ceiling—forces our gaze outward and upward. This expansive view engages our soft fascination, a cognitive state studied by environmental psychologists where our attention is held effortlessly by natural patterns like mountains, snow, and forests.</p>
