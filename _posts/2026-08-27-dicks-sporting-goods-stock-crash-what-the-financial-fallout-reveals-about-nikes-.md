<h2 id="what-happened">What Happened: The Dick's Sporting Goods Shock</h2>
<p>On Tuesday, Dick's Sporting Goods delivered a sobering financial update that stunned investors. The company reported second-quarter adjusted earnings per share (EPS) of $3.53, falling short of Wall Street estimates calling for $3.76. More alarming than the earnings miss was the dramatic guidance cut: Dick's slashed its full-year EPS outlook to a range of $10.94 to $11.94, down sharply from its previous forecast of $13.27 to $14.27.</p>

<p>The market reaction was swift and merciless. Dick's stock crashed 30.7% on Tuesday, plunging below all key moving averages according to Yahoo Finance AlphaSpace data. While a single retail miss can sometimes be dismissed as company-specific friction, executive commentary from the earnings call made it clear that a much broader industry headwind is blowing through the athletic retail space.</p>

<h2 id="footwear-hangover">The Footwear Hangover and Pricing Pressures</h2>
<p>The core of the issue lies in what Dick's Sporting Goods Executive Chair Ed Stack described to analysts as a "footwear hangover." For years, lifestyle sneakers featuring minimal fashion looks—predominantly driven by Nike—have been dependable, high-margin anchor products for major sports retailers. Today, those legacy styles are rapidly falling out of favor with consumers.</p>
