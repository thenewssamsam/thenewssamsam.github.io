  <section id="the-news">
    <h2>A Crisis in the Basin</h2>
    <p>Lake Powell and Lake Mead, the two largest reservoirs in the United States, have officially hit record-low water levels. As the primary water and power sources for Arizona, Nevada, and California, their decline signals a critical moment for the Colorado River basin. For millions of residents, this isn't just a headline—it is a reality that touches daily life, utility bills, and the stability of the power grid.</p>
    
    <p>The decline is the result of a three-decade drought, persistent overuse, and a shifting climate. Lake Powell has dropped over 20 feet this year alone, placing it dangerously close to the threshold where hydroelectric turbines would cease production. Meanwhile, Lake Mead has hit its lowest point since records began. Experts like Sarah Porter of the Kyl Center for Water Policy note that while this was anticipated, it underscores that the system is in deep trouble.</p>

When we see the water levels of our great reservoirs receding, it serves as a sobering reminder of our dependence on God’s creation and the fragility of the resources we often take for granted.</p>
