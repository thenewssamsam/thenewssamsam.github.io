<h2 id="what-happened">What Happened in San Jose</h2>
<p>According to updates from the San Jose Police Department, the incident began with an armed robbery call that quickly escalated into gunfire in the area of Lick Avenue and Goodyear Street. The injured officer was rushed to Santa Clara Valley Medical Center, where Mayor Matt Mahan later visited to check on his condition and offer support to the officer's family.</p>
<p>As the investigation unfolded, residents reported a heavy police presence near Almaden Avenue and Floyd Street involving the San Jose Police Department, the Santa Clara County Sheriff’s Office, and potential university police units. Witnesses recounted seeing a suspect down and expressed deep concern over potential barricade situations. Meanwhile, local families reported terrifying encounters, including claims of a suspect entering a nearby home before escaping—though authorities are still working to verify the exact timeline and connections between these harrowing events.</p>

<h2 id="psychology-of-trauma">The Psychology of Frontline Trauma</h2>
<p>News of an officer shot in the line of duty forces us to confront the intense psychological toll carried by first responders. When police officers walk into a volatile armed robbery call, their brains experience an instantaneous activation of the sympathetic nervous system—the classic fight-or-flight response. Adrenaline surges, heart rates skyrocket, and auditory exclusion can occur.</p>

Normalcy bias—the psychological tendency to underestimate the possibility of disaster—gets shattered, replaced temporarily by hypervigilance.</p>
