<h2>What Happened on a Dark Florida Road</h2>
<p>On a cool February evening in 2022, Jared Bridegan made the fatal mistake of stepping out of his SUV. The Microsoft design manager had just dropped off his twins at the home of his ex-wife, Shanna Gardner, and was heading home with his toddler daughter safely buckled in her car seat. But as he drove down a quiet Jacksonville Beach road, a discarded tire blocked his path. When he got out to move it, he was ambushed and shot dead in cold blood.</p>

<h3>Malignant Entitlement and Tunnel Vision</h3>
<p>In prolonged legal and custody battles, parties often develop what psychologists term <em>malignant entitlement</em>. Over time, the dispute ceases to be about legal rights or the best interests of the children; it transforms into an existential crusade. The opponent is no longer viewed as a co-parent with a differing perspective, but as an existential threat to one's happiness, autonomy, or ego.</p>

<h3>The Danger of Groupthink and Third-Party Enablers</h3>
<p>Murders-for-hire rarely happen in an emotional vacuum. They almost always require an enabler—someone who can distance themselves slightly from the physical act while serving as an emotional echo chamber. In psychological terms, groupthink within a closed relationship can amplify destructive impulses. When two people continuously validate each other's grievances without external reality-checking, extreme ideas that would normally be discarded in civilized society begin to look like viable solutions.</p>
