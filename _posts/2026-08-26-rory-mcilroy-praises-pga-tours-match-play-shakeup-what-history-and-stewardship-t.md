    <p>Professional golf is staring down a radical evolution. After initially questioning sweeping structural adjustments earlier this year, golf star Rory McIlroy is now enthusiastically backing the PGA Tour's planned 2028 overhaul, which introduces a dynamic match-play format and potentially opens the door to historic, intimate venues like Baltusrol and the Olympic Club. But beyond the television ratings and strategic course layouts, this seismic shift in sports entertainment offers a compelling lens through which to examine how we navigate change, honor tradition, and practice stewardship in our daily pursuits.</p>

  <section id="what-happened" class="ncg-section">
    <h2 class="ncg-h2">What Happened: A Bold New Vision for the Tour Championship</h2>
    <p>According to reports from CBS Sports, Rory McIlroy met with the media to discuss newly revealed details regarding the PGA Tour's upcoming competitive restructuring slated for 2028. Following discussions with PGA Tour CEO Brian Rolapp and the Future Competition Committee, McIlroy expressed newfound optimism for a two-week, two-venue season finale centered entirely around match play.</p>
    <p>Under the proposed structure, the season-long points race would crown its primary champion through traditional stroke-play metrics, while the subsequent Tour Championship would transform into an exclusive match-play spectacle. Because match play renders traditional aggregate scoring to par irrelevant, the tour can bypass the massive infrastructure requirements usually reserved for sprawling modern courses. This opens up possibilities to stage events at legendary, tighter historic venues—such as Merion, Baltusrol, or the Olympic Club—that typically lack the massive acreage required for modern PGA Tour spectator crowds.</p>
