  <h2 id="what-happened">What Happened in the Latest Middle East Escalation?</h2>
  
  <p>The delicate diplomatic framework established by a memorandum of understanding signed in June has officially hit a wall. That agreement set an ambitious 60-day deadline to halt active hostilities and forge a lasting accord regarding Iran's nuclear ambitions. Last week, that window closed without a compromise, leaving the U.S. and Iran at an impasse—particularly over the contested reopening and management of the vital Strait of Hormuz.</p>

  <p>The rhetoric has rapidly sharpened in the wake of the deadline. Mohsen Rezaei, the hard-line head of Iran's Supreme National Security Council and a former Revolutionary Guard commander, issued a stark warning on state media and social platforms. Rezaei declared that Tehran would view any nation supporting new U.S. economic sanctions as an active participant in an "act of war," threatening seismic retaliation that could target alternative Persian Gulf oil-shipping routes.</p>

  <p>Meanwhile, practical pressures are mounting on multiple fronts. While direct military engagements around the Strait of Hormuz remained relatively quiet over a recent 48-hour window, the U.S. naval blockade has effectively redirected dozens of commercial vessels. At the same time, newly formed Iranian maritime authorities have issued transit restrictions on ships deemed in violation of local arrangements. Amidst these high-stakes maneuvers, regional diplomats—including Pakistan's army chief, Field Marshal Asim Munir—are scheduled to visit Tehran in an urgent attempt to reopen communication channels and prevent further regional contagion.</p>
