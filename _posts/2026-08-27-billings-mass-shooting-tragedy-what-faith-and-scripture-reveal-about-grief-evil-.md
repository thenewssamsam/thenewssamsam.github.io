  <p class="ncg-lead">When eight members of a single family across four generations gathered for a Sunday evening dinner in a quiet Billings neighborhood, no one could have imagined that a horrific act of violence would shatter their lives forever. As Yellowstone County reels from what is reportedly the worst mass shooting in modern Montana history, communities everywhere are left asking a heavy, aching question: How do we process profound evil, and where is God when the unthinkable happens?</p>

  <h2 id="what-happened">What Happened on White Star Circle</h2>
  <p>According to Yellowstone County Sheriff Kent O'Donnell, deputies and Billings police officers responded to a distressing 911 call from a home on White Star Circle on Sunday evening, August 23. Inside the residence, 44-year-old Alan Michael Smith had opened fire on his family during a Sunday gathering before setting fire to the 4,000-square-foot home.</p>

  <p>The victims include four children ranging in age from 7 to 15, their mother Megan Ireland-Ghekier (39), Megan's parents Dana and Barbara Smith, and the family matriarch, 92-year-old Evelyn Smith. When law enforcement confronted the suspect in the front yard, he retreated inside, where officers heard a final gunshot. First responders later found the suspect mortally wounded in the backyard; he was pronounced dead at a local hospital.</p>
