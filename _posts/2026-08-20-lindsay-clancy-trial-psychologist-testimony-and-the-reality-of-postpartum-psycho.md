<section id="ncg-what-happened">
    <h2>What Happened in Court</h2>
    <p>The high-profile murder trial of Lindsay Clancy entered a crucial phase in Plymouth Superior Court as defense witnesses took the stand to present evidence regarding her mental state. Clancy, 36, has admitted to strangling her three young children—5-year-old Cora, 3-year-old Dawson, and 8-month-old Callan—in January 2023 at their Duxbury, Massachusetts home before attempting suicide by jumping from a second-story window.</p>
    
    <p>She has pleaded not guilty to three counts of first-degree murder, arguing she should not be held criminally responsible due to severe postpartum psychosis. During the recent court proceedings, forensic psychologist Paul Zeizel testified about evaluating Clancy shortly after the tragedy and detailed phone calls she made to her husband, Patrick, where she described hearing a commanding internal voice.</p>
    
    <p>The prosecution contends that the killings were premeditated, arguing that Clancy was depressed, intended to end her own life, and took her children because she mistrusted others to care for them. With dozens of witnesses having testified, the central legal and medical battleground centers on whether Clancy experienced a psychotic break brought on by extreme postpartum psychiatric deterioration.</p>

    <h2>The Medical and Psychological Reality</h2>
    <p>Understanding postpartum psychosis requires distinguishing normal postpartum blues or standard anxiety from a complete break with reality. Postpartum depression affects many new mothers, but postpartum psychosis is a rare, psychiatric medical emergency occurring in roughly 1 to 2 out of every 1,000 deliveries.</p>
