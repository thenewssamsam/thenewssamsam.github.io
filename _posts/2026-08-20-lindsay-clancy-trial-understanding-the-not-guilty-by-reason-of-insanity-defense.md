  <p>Zeizel recounted listening in on a phone call between Clancy and her then-husband, Patrick, during which she stated she heard a male voice telling her she had no choice and had to kill her children and herself. Defense attorneys presented testimony from four witnesses total, aiming to establish that Clancy was suffering from severe postpartum psychosis and should not be held criminally responsible for her actions.</p>

  <h2 id="legal-framework">A Legal Rights Perspective: The Insanity Defense</h2>

  <p>From a legal standpoint, the defense of "not guilty by reason of insanity"—or lack of criminal responsibility, as it is formally structured in many jurisdictions—is one of the most misunderstood concepts in American jurisprudence. It is not a legal "loophole" or a simple get-out-of-jail-free card; rather, it is a foundational acknowledgment that criminal punishment requires a concurrence of a guilty act and a culpable, functioning mind.</p>

  <p>In general criminal law, the prosecution must prove beyond a reasonable doubt not only that a defendant committed the physical act (the <em>actus reus</em>), but also that they possessed the necessary mental state or intent (the <em>mens rea</em>) at that exact moment. When a defendant asserts a lack of criminal responsibility due to mental disease or defect, the legal inquiry centers on whether that illness was so severe that the individual was rendered unable to appreciate the wrongfulness of their conduct or conform their behavior to the requirements of the law.</p>

  <h2 id="prosecution-vs-defense">The Burden of Proof and Psychiatric Evidence</h2>

  <p>A critical element in any insanity defense trial is the burden of proof. While the state always carries the ultimate burden of proving every element of the crime beyond a reasonable doubt, jurisdictions vary on how the insanity defense is raised and proven. Typically, the defense must present sufficient credible evidence—such as expert psychological testimony and extensive medical histories—to put the defendant's sanity genuinely in issue.</p>
