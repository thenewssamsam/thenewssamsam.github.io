For parents, this high-stakes primary—featuring frontrunners Byron Donalds for the Republicans and David Jolly for the Democrats—is more than just a headline; it is a real-world opportunity to teach children about the mechanics of democracy and the importance of civic engagement.</p>

  <section id="what-happened">
    <h2>The State of the Race</h2>
    <p>The August 18 primary features a massive field of hopefuls. Republican Congressman Byron Donalds has emerged as a frontrunner, backed by a strong fundraising edge and an endorsement from Donald Trump. On the Democratic side, former Congressman David Jolly, who switched parties in 2025, leads in polling, focusing his campaign on affordability and infrastructure. With 11 Republicans and six Democrats on the ballot, voters are deciding who will advance to the November general election.</p>

    <h2>Turning News Into a Teachable Moment</h2>
    <p>When elections dominate the news, children often pick up on the tension. Instead of shielding them, use this time to build their understanding of how communities make decisions. The goal isn't to impart political bias, but to explain the <em>process</em> of choosing leaders.</p>

    <h3>Age-Appropriate Engagement</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Young Children (Ages 4-7):</strong> Keep it simple. Explain that we are choosing a "helper" for our state to make rules about schools and parks. Focus on the concept of voting as a fair way to decide things, like choosing a game to play.</li>
      <li><strong>Tweens (Ages 8-12):</strong> Discuss the "why." Explain that candidates have different ideas about how to solve problems like housing costs or insurance. Encourage them to ask, "What problems do you think are important to solve?"</li>
      <li><strong>Teens (Ages 13+):</strong> This is the time to explore critical thinking. Look at the candidates' platforms together and discuss how different policies might impact families in Florida.</li>
    </ul>
