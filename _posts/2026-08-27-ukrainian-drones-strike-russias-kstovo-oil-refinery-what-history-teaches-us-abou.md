<h2>What Happened at the Kstovo Refinery?</h2>
<p>On the night of August 25–26, 2026, deep-strike units from Ukraine's Special Operations Forces, alongside the Unmanned Systems Forces and other defense units, launched a successful drone attack against Russia's fourth-largest oil refinery. Located in Nizhny Novgorod Oblast, the Lukoil-Nizhegorodnefteorgsintez facility—commonly known as the Kstovo oil refinery—sustained confirmed strikes and subsequent fires, according to the General Staff of the Armed Forces of Ukraine.</p>

<p>While the full extent of the physical and operational damage is still being evaluated by military and industrial analysts, the strategic weight of the target is well-established. The Kstovo facility boasts a crude oil processing capacity of approximately 17 million tonnes per year, producing essential products including petrol, diesel, aviation fuel, and bitumen. Crucially, it accounts for up to 11% of Russia's total petrol production and directly supplies the logistical needs of the Russian armed forces. Alongside the refinery, local reports noted that a facility belonging to online retailer Wildberries also came under fire during the overnight barrage.</p>

Furthermore, striking deep within an adversary's territory inevitably sparks escalatory cycles, widening the scope of collateral impact. The fundamental difference today is the technology: asymmetric drone swarms offer a precision and cost-effectiveness that massive bomber formations in the 1940s could only dream of, altering the arithmetic of attrition entirely.</p>
