  <section id="what-happened" style="margin-bottom: 40px;">
    <h2 style="font-size: 1.6rem; font-weight: 700; color: #1a202c; border-bottom: 2px solid #3182ce; padding-bottom: 8px; margin-bottom: 20px;">What Happened: The SEC Drops the Hammer</h2>
    <p>In a decisive move that reshapes the landscape of collegiate athletics, the Southeastern Conference (SEC) announced Tuesday night that it will officially bar former professional athletes from returning to college sports rosters. The unanimous decision by SEC presidents, chancellors, and athletic directors establishes a strict policy: any athlete who has declared for the NFL, NBA, or WNBA draft without proper withdrawal, signed a professional contract, or been listed on a professional roster is permanently prohibited from competing at an SEC institution.</p>
    
    <div style="background: #ebf8ff; border-left: 4px solid #3182ce; padding: 15px 20px; border-radius: 0 8px 8px 0; margin: 20px 0;">
      <p style="margin: 0; font-style: italic; color: #2b6cb0;">"College athletes, not former professional athletes, have been at the heart of the Southeastern Conference. Individuals who choose to leave college athletics, sign professional playing contracts, and compete professionally should not then be permitted to return to college competition." — SEC Official Statement</p>
    </div>

    <p>This policy mirrors a parallel initiative from the Big Ten, while the Big 12 and Notre Dame have quickly voiced unanimous support for similar measures. The swift and coordinated regulatory action comes in direct response to recent courtroom rulings—most notably in Louisiana—that opened temporary legal pathways for players who had exhausted or bypassed traditional eligibility limits, including athletes who participated in NFL training camps before being released.</p>
    
    <p>For institutions like LSU, which recently navigated high-profile roster additions involving players with professional camp experience, the new rule creates an immediate compliance challenge. Commissioner Greg Sankey has been granted explicit authority to enforce the mandate with institutional penalties, signaling that collegiate conferences are willing to use regulatory muscle to counter judicial intervention.</p>
