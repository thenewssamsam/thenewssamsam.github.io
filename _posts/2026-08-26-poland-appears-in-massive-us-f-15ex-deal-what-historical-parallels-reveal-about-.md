<h2>What Happened: The F-15EX Framework Contract</h2>
<p>When the U.S. Department of War announced a staggering $131.23 billion indefinite-delivery/indefinite-quantity (IDIQ) contract with Boeing for the "F-15 Eagle Crest" program, military analysts immediately scanned the fine print for international partners. Buried within the list of Foreign Military Sales (FMS) customers was a surprising entry: Poland. The inclusion instantly sparked speculation that Warsaw was preparing to acquire the advanced F-15EX Eagle II.</p>

<p>However, Poland's Ministry of National Defence moved swiftly to cool the excitement. Officials in Warsaw stated clearly that the Polish Armed Forces "do not plan to acquire F-15EX aircraft" and that inclusion in the U.S. framework contract does not equate to an active procurement process. An IDIQ contract merely establishes a legal and logistical mechanism for future orders; it does not represent an immediate purchase. Yet, defense watchers experienced in reading between the lines of military bureaucracy know that an official denial is rarely the final word on the matter.</p>
