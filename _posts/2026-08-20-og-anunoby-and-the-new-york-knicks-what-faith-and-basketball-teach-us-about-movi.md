  <div class="ncg-intro">
    <p>When New York Knicks forward OG Anunoby casually remarked that he was already "over" his legendary, championship-clinching shot, the sports world paused. After ending a grueling 53-year championship drought for New York basketball, most athletes would happily swim in the adulation for months. Yet Anunoby's quiet refusal to get trapped by his own highlight reel offers a surprising parallel to a timeless spiritual truth: the danger of living in yesterday's victories and the Christian call to press on toward what lies ahead.</p>
  </div>

  <section id="what-happened" class="ncg-section">
    <h2>What Happened in New York?</h2>
    <p>The summer of 2026 has been a whirlwind for the New York Knicks. Following an improbable, heart-pounding run through the NBA Finals—culminating in an unforgettable Game 4 comeback against the San Antonio Spurs and Anunoby's clutch game-winning tip-in—the franchise claimed its first title in over half a century. The aftermath has brought the typical New York media circus: morning show appearances, a massive championship parade, the ESPYs, and various brand partnerships.</p>
    
    <p>Amidst the fanfare, Anunoby has remained his trademark stoic self. Sitting down for interviews in a black hoodie, his demeanor is closer to a monk in meditation than a conquering sports hero. When asked about the whirlwind summer and the massive attention surrounding his heroic shot—which some fans playfully dubbed the "Hand of God"—Anunoby clarified that his declaration of being "over" it wasn't about disrespecting the moment. Rather, it was a practical acknowledgment that the NBA calendar waits for no one. Training camp looms, a title defense awaits, and the relentless pressure of New York sports means yesterday's glory expires quickly.</p>
