<h1 class="ncg-title">NIPSCO Storm Recovery Meals: What Local & Community Impact Reveals About Disaster Relief</h1>
<p class="ncg-subtitle">When severe weather knocks out power across northwest Indiana, recovery is about more than just repairing lines—it is about keeping neighbors fed while the lights come back on.</p>
</header>

<section class="ncg-section" id="what-happened">
<h2>What Happened in Northwest Indiana?</h2>
<p>Severe storms recently tore through northwest Indiana, leaving behind a trail of significant property damage, tangled debris, and frustratingly lengthy power outages. For thousands of residents, the immediate aftermath meant spoiled groceries, dark homes, and disrupted daily routines.</p>
<p>Amid the ongoing cleanup—which even brought the National Guard to places like LaPorte County—utility provider NIPSCO has stepped beyond traditional infrastructure repair. The company is actively coordinating free hot meal distributions across multiple regional hubs, offering both lunch and dinner to community members who need a helping hand while power is restored.</p>

<section class="ncg-section" id="distribution-schedule">
<h2>Where and When to Get Relief</h2>
<p>The distribution schedule runs through Tuesday, targeting some of the hardest-hit pockets of northwest Indiana. Each site is prepared to serve up to 500 people per event, with meals set for noon and 4:30 p.m. Central time daily:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Sunday, August 16:</strong> Portage Strack &amp; Van Til and Hammond Sportsplex</li>
<li><strong>Monday, August 17:</strong> LaPorte Civic Auditorium and Gary YWCA</li>
<li><strong>Tuesday, August 18:</strong> Dean &amp; Barbara White Community Center (Merrillville) and Highland Lincoln Center</li>
</ul>
<p>Events wrap up daily at 6 p.m., providing a brief window of immediate relief for families navigating spoiled kitchens and prolonged outages.</p>
</section>

<section class="ncg-section" id="local-impact">
<h2>The Local &amp; Community Impact Perspective</h2>
<p>From a local and community impact standpoint, events like these highlight the fragile ecosystem of regional resilience. When a storm knocks out electricity, the ripple effects hit vulnerable households hardest—particularly families living paycheck to paycheck who lose hundreds of dollars in perishable food overnight.</p>
<div class="ncg-quote-box">
<p class="ncg-quote-text">"Infrastructure recovery isn't just about poles and wires; it's about making sure the people sitting in the dark have a hot meal on the table."</p>
</div>
<p>Municipalities across Portage, Hammond, LaPorte, Gary, Merrillville, and Highland demonstrate that community recovery is inherently collaborative. It takes utility crews, local civic centers, grocery partners, and emergency personnel working in sync to bridge the gap between disaster and normalcy.</p>
</section>

<section class="ncg-section" id="how-to-respond">
<h2>How Should Residents Respond?</h2>
<p>If you or someone you know is currently navigating the recovery process in northwest Indiana, here are a few practical steps to stay safe and informed:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Check Food Safety:</strong> Discard refrigerated perishable food if power has been out for more than four hours. When in doubt, throw it out to avoid foodborne illness.</li>
<li><strong>Utilize Local Resources:</strong> Take advantage of community food distributions and local civic centers offering aid, but arrive early given the 500-meal capacity caps.</li>
<li><strong>Stay Updated on Utility Work:</strong> Keep tabs on NIPSCO outage maps and official municipal channels for real-time restoration estimates and safety alerts.</li>
</ul>
</section>

<section class="ncg-section" id="closing-thought">
<h2>One Last Thought</h2>
<p>Disasters have a way of revealing the invisible threads that hold our neighborhoods together. While downed lines and ruined groceries are frustrating, the swift mobilization of hot meals across northwest Indiana proves that communities are built on mutual care long before the power switch is flipped back on. When the lights finally return, it is the human connection forged in the dark that we remember best.</p>
</section>
