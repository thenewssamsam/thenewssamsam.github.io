  <p>Florida voters are heading to the polls today to narrow down a massive field of candidates vying to replace term-limited Governor Ron DeSantis. With 17 candidates across two major-party primaries and a total of 29 individuals on the ballot, confusion is inevitable. In a race this crowded, rumors often outpace reality. Here is a look at the facts behind the headlines.</p>

  <div style="border-left: 4px solid #d4af37; background-color: #f9f9f9; padding: 15px; margin: 20px 0;">
    <p><strong>Did you know?</strong> While 17 candidates are fighting for the major party nominations, the November general election will feature a total of 29 candidates, including independents and write-ins.</p>
  </div>

  <section id="myth-vs-fact">
    <h2>Myth vs. Fact: Clearing the Air</h2>
    
    <h3>Myth: "All voters can participate in today's primary."</h3>
    <p><strong>Fact:</strong> Florida maintains a closed primary system. This means that only voters registered with the Republican Party can vote in the GOP primary, and only those registered as Democrats can vote in the Democratic primary. If you are registered as "No Party Affiliation" (NPA) or with a minor party, you are ineligible to vote in these specific partisan contests.</p>
