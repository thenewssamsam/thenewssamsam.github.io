  <p style="font-size: 1.15rem; font-weight: 500; color: #2b6cb0; margin-bottom: 20px;">
    Just hours before a sweeping 50% tariff on Canadian goods was set to take effect, President Donald Trump announced a tentative trade agreement with Canada, averting an immediate economic collision. But beyond the shifting political rhetoric and hurried negotiations, this high-stakes diplomatic pivot raises profound questions about cross-border commerce, economic stability, and how nations manage mutual trust.
  </p>

  <h2 id="ncg-what-happened" style="color: #2c5282; border-left: 4px solid #3182ce; padding-left: 10px; margin-top: 30px;">What Happened in the U.S.-Canada Tariff Showdown?</h2>
  <p>
    The dramatic eleventh-hour announcement came late Tuesday evening, less than two hours before steep new duties on roughly $20 billion worth of Canadian imports—including wine, hockey sticks, and various manufactured goods—were scheduled to activate. President Trump took to Truth Social to state that the tariffs would be paused for three days, declaring that the U.S. and Canada had reached a tentative deal pending the finalization of documents.
  </p>
  <p>
    According to reports from CNBC, the framework includes potential breakthroughs on multiple fronts. Trump hinted at reviving the Keystone XL pipeline—the cross-border oil transport project scrapped in 2021—and signaled adjustments to U.S. tariffs on Canadian steel, aluminum, and automobiles. Meanwhile, Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney struck a more measured tone initially, noting substantial progress before later highlighting that Canada entered discussions with strong strategic leverage. U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer echoed cautious optimism, emphasizing that the framework aims to eliminate long-standing trade irritants and secure comprehensive market access for American workers.
  </p>
