  <section class="ncg-section" id="what-happened">
    <h2>What Happened in the Comey Seashell Case?</h2>
    <p>The United States Department of Justice has formally defended its decision to prosecute former FBI Director James Comey over a social media post featuring an image of seashells arranged to spell out <strong>“8647”</strong>. In court documents filed in the eastern district of North Carolina, federal prosecutors argued that there is "no serious dispute" that an objective observer could interpret the post as a violent threat against President Donald Trump.</p>
    
    <p>Comey was indicted by a grand jury last April on charges of making and transmitting a threat against the US president across state lines. The controversy erupted last May when Comey posted the seashell photo on Instagram while vacationing in North Carolina. Supporters and allies of the president immediately denounced the numbers as a thinly veiled message: "86" is common slang for getting rid of or ejecting something, while Trump is the 47th president.</p>

    <p>To understand why a photograph of beach shells has landed a former FBI director in federal court, we have to look past the legal briefs and examine how human brains process ambiguous information in times of intense societal division. Psychologists refer to several well-documented mental frameworks when explaining how otherwise mundane objects become flashpoints for massive cultural friction.</p>

    <h3 class="ncg-h3">Confirmation Bias and Pattern Recognition</h3>
    <p>Human beings are evolutionary wired to find patterns. When we look at a complex, uncertain world, our brains actively seek out connections that confirm what we already believe to be true. For critics of James Comey who have watched years of political warfare between him and Donald Trump, the idea that he might send a coded jab is entirely plausible. When they saw "8647," confirmation bias instantly filled in the blanks, transforming a ambiguous numerical pun into a sinister plot.</p>

    <h3 class="ncg-h3">The Danger of Moral Panic and Groupthink</h3>
    <p>Groupthink and collective outrage often amplify minor events into major crises. When a social media mob or a political movement seizes on a symbol, individual members face immense social pressure to adopt the group's interpretation. Nuance is flattened. In this environment, an ambiguous seashell post ceases to be evaluated through common sense or linguistic context and instead becomes an existential test of loyalty.</p>
