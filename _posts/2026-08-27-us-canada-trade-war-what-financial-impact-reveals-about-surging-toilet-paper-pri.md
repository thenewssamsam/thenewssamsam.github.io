  <section id="what-happened">
    <h2>What Happened: The Breakdown of US-Canada Trade Relations</h2>
    <p>The latest economic friction began after trade talks broke down between Washington and Ottawa. Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney quickly vowed to match American levies “dollar for dollar,” unveiling retaliatory tariffs targeting nearly 900 American goods with hikes ranging from 25% to 50% starting September 8. In response to earlier hikes from Washington, paper products—specifically toilet paper and face tissue stock—face severe retaliatory duties.</p>
    
    <p>Despite assurances from US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer that trade disputes would not affect everyday consumers, market analysts and industry insiders point to deeply interconnected supply chains that suggest otherwise. The US imported $328 million worth of toilet paper from Canada in 2024, making our northern neighbor the largest exporter of paper goods to the American market. Retail giants like Costco and major manufacturers such as Procter & Gamble have repeatedly warned that maintaining pre-tariff pricing structures under these conditions is simply unsustainable.</p>

    <h2>The Financial Mechanics of Tariffs on Paper Products</h2>
    <p>To understand why toilet paper prices are surging, we have to look past the retail price tag and examine the underlying manufacturing ecosystem. While many American-branded paper products are assembled domestically, the raw materials required to produce them often cross international borders. The US paper industry heavily relies on lumber-rich Canada for raw pulp and tissue stock.</p>
