  <section id="what-happened" class="ncg-section">
    <h2>What Happened: The 200,000 Visa Revocation Plan</h2>
    <p>The administration of US President Donald Trump has unveiled plans for what experts describe as the largest mass visa cancellation in American history. The US Department of State announced it will revoke B-1 and B-2 business and tourist visas issued between 2016 and 2026 for foreign nationals who have previously sought or are currently seeking asylum in the United States.</p>
    
    <p>State Department officials defended the maneuver, arguing that tourist and business visas are being improperly utilized as a backdoor mechanism to bypass standard immigration controls. While exact figures remain fluid, Department documents and internal estimates cited by the Associated Press suggest that up to 200,000 people could find their underlying non-immigrant status abruptly dissolved.</p>

    <div class="ncg-callout-box" style="border-left: 4px solid #b45309; background: #fffbeb; padding: 16px; margin: 20px 0; border-radius: 8px;">
      <p style="margin: 0; font-style: italic; color: #92400e;"><strong>Quick Take:</strong> "Policy shocks of this magnitude are rarely designed solely for immediate physical deportation; rather, they function as powerful psychological instruments meant to reshape expectations and deter future legal navigation."</p>
    </div>

    <p>Migration experts, however, point out a critical legal distinction: revoking a temporary entry visa does not inherently invalidate a pending asylum claim, which derives its legal standing from separate administrative processes. Even so, the policy thrusts tens of thousands of individuals into a tense administrative purgatory, altering the emotional and legal landscape for vulnerable applicants overnight.</p>

    <h2>The Psychology of Policy Shocks and Systemic Uncertainty</h2>
    <p>When sweeping government actions alter the ground rules of daily existence for hundreds of thousands of people, behavioral science observes predictable patterns of collective anxiety and hyper-vigilance. The human brain craved stability and predictability; sudden, large-scale bureaucratic shifts strip away that foundational sense of psychological safety.</p>

For foreign nationals who entered the United States through standard, approved tourist or business channels, believing they were acting in full accordance with the law, the retroactive rebranding of their presence as problematic generates acute mental friction.</p>
