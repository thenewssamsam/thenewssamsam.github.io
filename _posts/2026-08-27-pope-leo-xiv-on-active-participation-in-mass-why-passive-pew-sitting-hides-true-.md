  <div class="ncg-quick-take">
    <strong>Quick Take:</strong> Pope Leo XIV recently emphasized during his Wednesday general audience that the Catholic liturgy is not a spectator sport. Drawing from the Second Vatican Council document <em>Sacrosanctum Concilium</em>, the Holy Father reminded believers that physical attendance without spiritual engagement falls short of fostering a genuine relationship with God.
  </div>

  <p>For many regular churchgoers, Sunday Mass can easily settle into a familiar routine. We walk through the heavy wooden doors, find our usual pew, stand, sit, and kneel almost by muscle memory. But during a recent general audience in St. Peter’s Basilica, Pope Leo XIV challenged this comfortable habit with a sharp reminder from Church history: when it comes to divine worship, passive observation is simply not enough.</p>

  <p>Concluding his catechetical series on the documents of the Second Vatican Council, the pope focused squarely on <em>Sacrosanctum Concilium</em>, the constitution on the sacred liturgy. His message was direct: the post-conciliar reforms were never meant to turn the faithful into silent spectators watching a performance at the altar. Instead, they were designed to open the door for every believer to enter into a living, breathing encounter with the Creator.</p>

  <h2 id="what-happened">What Happened at the Vatican Audience</h2>

  <p>Speaking to pilgrims gathered in St. Peter’s Basilica on August 26, 2026, Pope Leo XIV delivered a comprehensive reflection on the liturgical reforms initiated by Vatican II. He noted that the council's push for renewal grew from an urgent need for Catholics to celebrate the liturgy more consciously and actively, rather than remaining like "strangers and silent spectators."</p>
