  <h2 id="what-happened">What Happened at the Kennedy Center?</h2>
  <p>The latest salvo in the ongoing legal war over the John F. Kennedy Center for the Performing Arts arrived in a 21-page court filing submitted by administration lawyers. The document responds directly to efforts by U.S. Representative Joyce Beatty (D-Ohio) and preservation groups seeking to block the Kennedy Center board from prominently featuring President Trump's name on the structure.</p>

  <p>According to the Justice Department's filing, the future of the performing arts hub boils down to a stark binary choice: accept the financial and structural rescue championed by the Trump administration—complete with renaming the venue to include "Restored and Renovated by President Donald J. Trump"—or watch the building deteriorate into an unsafe structure destined for demolition. The administration even floated a potential replacement: a simpler outdoor amphitheater overlooking the Potomac River.</p>

  <p>This dramatic escalation caps months of intense turmoil. Early in his second term, President Trump appointed himself chairman after shaking up the board, prompting artist and staff departures. Although Federal District Court Judge Christopher R. Cooper halted both the two-year shutdown and the mandatory renaming in a May ruling—leaving a tarp covering the disputed signage—the board voted once more this month to press forward with both the construction plans and the naming tribute. Meanwhile, preservationists remain deeply anxious, pointing to the swift demolition of the White House’s East Wing earlier this year as evidence that physical alterations can happen before legal challenges can fully mount.</p>
