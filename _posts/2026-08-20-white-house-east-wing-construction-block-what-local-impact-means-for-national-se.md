<h2 id="what-happened">What Happened: The Legal Battle Over the East Wing</h2>
<p>The Department of Justice has formally petitioned Supreme Court Chief Justice John Roberts to stay an order from the D.C. Circuit Court of Appeals. That lower court ruling upheld a strict, rococo construction schedule imposed by U.S. District Judge Richard Leon regarding the above-ground and underground replacement structure of the White House East Wing.</p>

<p>Originally built in 1902 and substantially expanded by Franklin D. Roosevelt in 1942—an expansion that included the creation of the Presidential Emergency Operations Center—the East Wing has grown outdated. The ongoing renovation and rebuilding effort, approved by President Trump in consultation with the Secret Service and the Pentagon, aims to update command-and-control facilities and emergency bunkers to counter modern threats.</p>

<p>However, the National Trust for Historic Preservation challenged the project in court, arguing for strict adherence to historic preservation reviews. Lower courts sided in part with micromanaging the construction process, prompting the DOJ to seek urgent Supreme Court relief before a response deadline of August 18, 2026.</p>
