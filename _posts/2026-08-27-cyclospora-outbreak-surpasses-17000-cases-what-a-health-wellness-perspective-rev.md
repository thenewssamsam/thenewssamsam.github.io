  <section id="what-happened" class="ncg-section">
    <h2>What Happened: The 17,000-Case Milestone</h2>
    <p>According to the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC), confirmed infections with <em>Cyclospora cayetanensis</em> reached 17,180 across 48 states and Washington, D.C., as of late August 2026. While the overall weekly growth rate is showing signs of a downward swing compared to previous spikes of 2,000 to 3,000 new cases a week, the cumulative toll remains staggering: 922 hospitalizations and two deaths.</p>
    
    <p>Laboratories have yet to confirm nearly 12,000 additional suspected cases, largely due to a typical six-week reporting lag between initial illness onset and federal verification. Michigan and Ohio remain the hardest-hit states, with Michigan alone accounting for over 14,000 cases in its local tallies due to a massive cluster linked to recalled iceberg lettuce grown by produce giant Taylor Farms in central Mexico.</p>

  <section id="health-perspective" class="ncg-section">
    <h2>A Health &amp; Wellness Perspective on Cyclospora</h2>
    <p>To understand why this outbreak feels so unsettling for many people, we have to look past the raw numbers and examine how we relate to our food supply. Cyclosporiasis is caused by a microscopic parasite transmitted through contaminated food or water. Unlike bacteria that multiply rapidly in food left sitting on a counter, this parasite brings a different kind of challenge: a long incubation period and unique resilience that make tracing its origin exceptionally difficult.</p>

    <div class="ncg-quote-box" style="border-left: 4px solid #2e7d32; background: #f1f8f5; padding: 15px 20px; margin: 25px 0; border-radius: 6px;">

      </p>

    <h3>Understanding Symptoms and Body Resilience</h3>
    <p>Health-wise, cyclosporiasis is known for causing gastrointestinal symptoms that can completely derail daily life. The hallmark symptom is watery, frequent, and sometimes explosive diarrhea that can cycle through periods of improvement and relapse. Other common signs include extreme fatigue, nausea, stomach cramps, bloating, and low-grade fever.</p>
