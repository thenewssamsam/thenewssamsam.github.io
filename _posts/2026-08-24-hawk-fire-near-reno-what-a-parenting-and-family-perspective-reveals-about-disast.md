<section id="what-happened">
<h2>What Happened: The Rapid Advance of the Hawk Fire</h2>
<p>Flames advanced rapidly down hillsides toward residential neighborhoods near Reno, Nevada, as the uncontained human-caused Hawk Fire scorched more than 15,000 acres by Sunday evening. Igniting on Saturday, the fast-moving blaze quickly overwhelmed containment efforts, driven by dry brush, low humidity, and persistent gusty winds that failed to calm overnight.</p>

<p>Nevada Governor Joe Lombardo declared a state of emergency in Washoe County and activated the Nevada National Guard, while around 90,000 residents faced evacuation orders and warnings. At least two medical centers evacuated patients out of an abundance of caution, and the Washoe County School District canceled classes for Monday. Amid the chaos, heartbreaking stories emerged of homes lost in minutes, contrasting with the tireless efforts of roughly 1,000 firefighting personnel battling the inferno from the ground and air.</p>
</section>

<h3>Age-Banded Communication Tips During Wildfires</h3>
<p>Talking to kids about disasters requires nuance depending on their developmental stage. Young children need simple, concrete reassurances: "The firefighters are working hard to protect our home, and our job right now is to stay together and go to a safe place." Avoid overly graphic descriptions of destruction that can trigger acute anxiety.</p>
