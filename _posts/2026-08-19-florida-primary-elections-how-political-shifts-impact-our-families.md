<section id="what-happened">
<h2>What Happened in Florida?</h2>
<p>Florida’s primary election day has arrived, marking a significant moment in the state’s political landscape. These are the first primaries held since the Florida legislature implemented a new congressional map. This redistricting effort has redrawn district lines, potentially flipping four seats and altering the political representation for millions of families across the state.</p>
<p>Key races include competitive primaries in both parties, with high-profile candidates like Rep. Byron Donalds in the gubernatorial race and shifting district lines affecting incumbents like Rep. Debbie Wasserman Schultz and Rep. Jared Moskowitz. For families, these aren't just names on a ballot; they represent the voices that will advocate for local schools, healthcare, and economic policies that directly impact our daily lives.</p>

<h3>3. Age-Appropriate Engagement</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Young Children (Ages 5-9):</strong> Focus on the act of voting as a community event. Explain that voting is how we work together to solve problems.</li>
<li><strong>Pre-Teens (Ages 10-13):</strong> Discuss how lines on a map can change who represents a community and why it is important to pay attention to local government.</li>
<li><strong>Teens (Ages 14+):</strong> Encourage them to look at the issues that affect their future, such as education funding or the economy. Ask them, "If you could change one thing in our town, what would it be?"</li>
