    <h2>A Sudden Loss in Samburu County</h2>
    <p>A routine scenic flight intended to showcase the natural beauty of Kenya’s Samburu county turned into a scene of unspeakable tragedy this Wednesday. A Eurocopter EC130 B4, operated by Lady Lori Helicopters, crashed near Mount Ololokwe, resulting in the deaths of seven individuals. Among those lost were Ecuador’s intelligence chief, Michele Sensi-Contugi, and his wife, Stephany Hollihan, along with five American citizens.</p>

While this bias helps us function, it can also blind us to the importance of living with intention. The loss of these seven people serves as a quiet invitation to re-evaluate our priorities. Are we spending our time on things that matter? Are we treating our relationships with the care they deserve before the opportunity passes?</p>

I am reminded of the words in <strong>James 4:14</strong>: <em>"Why, you do not even know what will happen tomorrow. What is your life?   <section id="practical-response">
    <h2>How Should We Respond?</h2>
    <p>While we cannot prevent every accident, we can choose how we carry ourselves in the wake of such news. First, we should practice <strong>radical appreciation</strong>. Tell your loved ones you value them today, not tomorrow. Second, engage in <strong>purposeful living</strong>. If you feel called to a career or a hobby that brings light to others, pursue it with vigor, knowing that time is a finite resource. Finally, offer <strong>grace to the grieving</strong>. Whether it is the families of the victims or a friend going through a smaller personal trial, your presence is often the most profound thing you can offer.</p>
