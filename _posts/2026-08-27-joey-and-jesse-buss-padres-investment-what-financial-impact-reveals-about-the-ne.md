<p class="ncg-intro">Following the historic sale of the Los Angeles Lakers, brothers Joey and Jesse Buss are officially stepping out of the NBA spotlight and into Major League Baseball. Through their newly minted investment vehicle, Buss Sports Capital, the former Lakers executives have reached a deal to acquire a roughly 5 percent stake in the San Diego Padres.</p>

<p>This strategic move comes on the heels of a massive ownership shakeup that saw the Padres recently sold for a staggering $3.9 billion to a group led by Kwanza Jones and José Feliciano. For sports fans and business observers alike, this transition highlights a massive paradigm shift in how professional sports franchises are bought, sold, and operated in an era of soaring team valuations.</p>

<h2 id="what-happened-the-buss-brothers-mlb-transition" class="ncg-heading">What Happened: A New Chapter After Decades in LA</h2>
<p>The Buss family name has been synonymous with Los Angeles basketball for nearly half a century. However, following the family's sale of a controlling stake in the Lakers to billionaire Mark Walter last year, and subsequent family votes to cash out remaining holdings amid internal legal friction involving Jeanie Buss, Joey and Jesse Buss found themselves looking for a new arena.</p>

When legacy sports owners liquidate family-held assets during record valuation cycles—such as the Lakers fetching a reported $12.5 billion valuation under Kushner and Iger—they are faced with massive capital gains events and the challenge of redeploying liquid wealth into appreciating alternative asset classes.</p>
