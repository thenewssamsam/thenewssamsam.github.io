    <h2>Understanding Candidate Rights and Eligibility</h2>
    <p>In the wake of this primary, citizens often ask: What are the legal requirements to run for the U.S. Senate? Under Article I, Section 3 of the United States Constitution, a candidate must be at least 30 years old, a U.S. citizen for at least nine years, and an inhabitant of the state they represent at the time of the election.</p>

    <h3>The Primaries and Party Autonomy</h3>
    <p>Political parties have the legal right to recruit and support candidates, as seen with the DSCC’s backing of Vindman. However, these endorsements do not grant legal immunity from the primary process. Voters retain the absolute right to choose their nominee through the ballot box, regardless of party endorsements or funding levels.</p>
