<p>On Tuesday, regional Governor Veniamin Kondratyev confirmed that at least 10 private homes were damaged and fires erupted following a drone attack on the Afipsky Refinery in Krasnodar Krai. Processessing roughly 7.9 million tons of crude oil annually, the facility stands as one of the largest and most critical refining hubs in southern Russia. Falling drone debris also impacted the nearby Afipskaya railway station, compounding logistical bottlenecks.</p>

<p>This incident did not occur in a vacuum. Just days earlier, a separate early-morning strike damaged two U-272 gas separation units at the Astrakhan Gas Processing Plant, a major facility operated by energy titan Gazprom. Ukrainian military officials confirmed that the plant produces gas products utilized by Russian armed forces, alongside sulfur outputs crucial for manufacturing explosives.</p>

<p>According to reports from independent outlets like the <em>Moscow Times</em>, these ongoing operations have accumulated to roughly 70 confirmed strikes this year alone. Major processing complexes—including Permnefteorgsintez, Orsknefteorgsintez, Zapsibneftekhim, Saratov, and Volgograd—have faced significant operational disruptions.</p>
