<h2>What Happened in Court Today?</h2>
<p>A Massachusetts federal judge has formally ruled that the Trump administration violated a nationwide injunction by pushing forward with a controversial rule designed to overhaul mail-in voting requirements. The decision, handed down Tuesday afternoon by U.S. District Judge Indira Talwani in a five-page filing, centers on the administration's decision to finalize new criteria that critics warn could disqualify millions of mail ballots.</p>

<p>The core dispute revolves around the boundaries of judicial authority and federal rulemaking. While Judge Talwani's ruling allows the administration to formally publish the final rule, she firmly reiterated that the U.S. Postal Service remains strictly prohibited from making the dramatic changes to mail-in ballots before the upcoming 2026 midterms. "Defendants cannot contend that they misunderstood the scope of the court's order," Judge Talwani wrote, rejecting the government's argument that merely publishing the text bypassed the restriction.</p>

<p>The legal friction began when the League of Women Voters of Massachusetts filed a motion urging the court to enforce its injunction. Although the administration countered that publishing was merely a preparatory step so states could comply if higher courts lifted the freeze, the district court found this explanation insufficient.</p>
