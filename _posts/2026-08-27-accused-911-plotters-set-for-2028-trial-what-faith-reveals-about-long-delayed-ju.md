  <section id="what-happened">
    <h2>What Happened in the Military Commissions</h2>
    <p>On Wednesday, Air Force Lt. Col. Michael Schrama, serving as the military commissions judge, officially scheduled a tentative trial date of June 5, 2028. According to reports from Axios, this court date comes roughly 20 years after Mohammed and his co-defendants were first arraigned back in 2008, and an astounding 27 years after the tragic events of September 11, 2001.</p>

    <p>Military prosecutors had initially pushed for an earlier trial in January 2027, but Judge Schrama determined that timeline was simply unrealistic given the complex legal maneuvers, classified evidence disputes, and procedural hurdles that have plagued the tribunal system for decades. The path to this 2028 date has been notoriously turbulent. Plea agreements that might have spared Mohammed and two of his co-defendants the death penalty were abruptly overturned by a divided federal appeals court in July 2025. Currently, the U.S. Supreme Court is weighing whether to review that consequential decision, keeping the final parameters of the proceedings in limbo.</p>
    <p>Alongside Khalid Sheikh Mohammed, the defendants scheduled to stand trial include Walid bin Attash, Ali Abdul Aziz Ali, and Mustafa Ahmed al-Hawsawi. For the families of victims and a public yearning for final closure, the protracted delay remains one of the most frustrating chapters in American judicial history.</p>
