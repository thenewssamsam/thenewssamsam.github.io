<h2>What Happened: The Rapid Explosion of the Hawk Fire</h2>
<p>On Sunday, a human-caused wildfire dubbed the Hawk fire erupted in the Sierra foothills and raced toward the heart of Reno, Nevada. Fueled by dry vegetation, low humidity, and powerful, erratic winds, the blaze ballooned to more than 15,000 acres in less than 48 hours. Local authorities issued urgent "GO NOW" evacuation orders impacting nearly 90,000 residents, with 42,000 under strict mandatory evacuation instructions as flames crept dangerously close to the University of Nevada, Reno campus just as students were preparing to return for classes.</p>

<p>Mayor Hillary Schieve urged residents not to hesitate, noting that structures had already been lost and emphasizing that human life must always take precedence over property. Governor Joe Lombardo declared a state of emergency for Washoe County, deploying the Nevada National Guard and aerial firefighting resources to assist local ground crews. Despite these efforts, shifting winds complicated containment, leaving thousands of homes and businesses without power and forcing major highway closures.</p>
