  <section id="what-happened" class="ncg-section">
    <h2>What Happened: The New USPS Ballot Rules</h2>
    <p>The U.S. Postal Service recently published a new rule introducing mandatory verification requirements for all mail-in ballots across the nation. According to reports from ABC News, the policy stipulates that states must collect and report basic voter details—such as a voter's name and residential address—attached to a unique barcode on every distributed ballot.</p>

    <p>Under the proposed framework, state election authorities would be required to input this data into a centralized "federal ballot mail portal" prior to the postal service accepting the ballots into its stream. The agency stated it would refuse delivery for ballots originating from any state failing to comply with the mandate.</p>
    <p>However, the changes are currently on hold. A federal appeals court recently upheld a lower court's injunction, keeping the USPS regulations suspended while broader litigation moves forward. The agency indicated the rules would take effect immediately should the legal barrier be lifted.</p>

    <p>Legal analysts point out that while the USPS possesses broad authority to regulate the mail stream to ensure security and efficiency, using postal regulations to enforce voter registration oversight touches upon areas traditionally managed by state legislatures. Voting advocacy organizations, such as the Campaign Legal Center, have expressed concern that centralizing voter information into federal databases could alter the traditional balance of election administration.</p>

    <h3>Understanding the Boundaries of the Rule</h3>
    <p>The finalized USPS rule stops short of more expansive executive proposals from earlier in the year, which sought to require widespread citizenship verification lists and direct law enforcement coordination through immigration databases. The postal service clarified that no sensitive details such as full birthdates or Social Security numbers will be uploaded to the portal, limiting data collection primarily to names, addresses, and tracking barcodes.</p>
