  <section id="what-happened" class="tpf-section">
    <h2>What Happened: The 11th-Hour Tariff Pause</h2>
    <p>Late Tuesday evening, just minutes before a midnight deadline that would have upended North American commerce, President Donald Trump slammed the brakes on a sweeping wave of 50 percent tariffs targeting Canadian imports. Announcing the sudden shift on social media, Trump revealed that a three-day pause had been enacted because the United States and Canada had reached a tentative trade agreement, pending final document reviews.</p>
    
    <p>The threatened 50 percent duties—which would have marked the inaugural deployment of Section 338 of the Tariff Act of 1930—stood to impact roughly $20 billion worth of U.S. imports from our northern neighbor. Everything from hockey sticks and building materials to specific liquors and apparel faced immediate price spikes. Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney confirmed the temporary reprieve, noting that substantial progress had been made during intense, delicate negotiations, though vital administrative work remains before the deal is sealed.</p>

    <p>While trade representatives iron out details regarding market access, digital trade alignment, and revived energy infrastructure like the Keystone XL pipeline, the broader economic ecosystem catches a brief collective breath. Yet for everyday households trying to plan grocery budgets, back-to-school shopping, and long-term savings, the 48-hour drama serves as a stark reminder of how quickly global headlines can translate into domestic financial stress.</p>

    <h3>1. For Young Children (Ages 4–8): Focus on Stability</h3>
    <p>Young children do not need to hear about international trade disputes or looming tariff deadlines. Their sense of security is tied directly to their immediate environment. If grocery prices fluctuate or household routines require adjustments, keep explanations simple and reassuring: "Sometimes the cost of things at the store changes a little bit, but our family is safe, and we always figure out our budget together." Avoid using catastrophic language.</p>
