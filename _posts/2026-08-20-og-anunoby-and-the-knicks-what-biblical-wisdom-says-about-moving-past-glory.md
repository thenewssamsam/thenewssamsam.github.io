  <p class="ncg-lead">When New York Knicks forward OG Anunoby casually remarked that he was already "over" his legendary NBA Finals game-winning shot, it sounded like quintessential sports indifference. But beneath his trademark stoicism lies a profound spiritual truth about the human struggle to celebrate success without becoming trapped by yesterday's victories.</p>

  <h2 id="what-happened">What Happened: The Summer After the Shot</h2>
  <p>It has been a whirlwind summer for the New York Knicks. Following a grueling 53-year championship drought, the Knicks captured the NBA title in June 2026, punctuated by OG Anunoby's miraculous, series-saving tip-in during Game 4 against the San Antonio Spurs—a play playfully dubbed the "Hand of God."</p>
  
  <p>Yet, while fans and media across the five boroughs continue to luxuriate in the euphoria of a championship parade, key ring-bearers like Anunoby have pivoted sharply toward the horizon. Amid a dizzying offseason of brand partnerships, late-night television appearances, and civic honors, Anunoby made headlines by admitting he is ready to move on from the historic moment. Rather than swimming endlessly in the warm waters of instant adoration, the soft-spoken forward is entirely focused on the grueling burden of defending the crown when training camp opens in just a few short weeks.</p>

  <h2 id="the-weight-of-yesterdays-glory">The Spiritual Danger of Dwelling on Past Triumphs</h2>
  <p>Human beings are naturally wired to build monuments to our past successes. Whether it is a career milestone, a financial windfall, or a long-sought personal victory, we love to linger in the warm glow of what we once accomplished. Yet, scripture offers a persistent warning against letting yesterday's triumphs dictate today's identity.</p>
