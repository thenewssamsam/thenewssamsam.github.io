<section id="what-happened">
    <h2>What Happened: The Tariff Pause and Trade Talks</h2>
    <p>President Trump announced on Truth Social late Tuesday that he was delaying the scorching 50% tariffs on a wide range of Canadian goods—including dairy, alcohol, furniture, and industrial supplies—that had been set to take effect at midnight. Speaking from the White House lawn on Wednesday morning, Trump confirmed that the administration has come to a deal with Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney, though official documents remain subject to finalization.</p>
    
    <p>The proposed duties, which would have impacted about 5% of total US imports from Canada and utilized a little-known 1930s trade law known as Section 338, created a tense backdrop for North American commerce. Canadian officials, including Prime Minister Carney and Trade Minister Dominic LeBlanc, engaged in intensive, delicate talks. Carney described the ongoing discussions as constructive, noting that substantial progress has been made before the August 21 deadline.</p>
