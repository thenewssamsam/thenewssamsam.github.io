  <section id="news-hook">
    <p>The recent announcement by Pennsylvania Attorney General Dave Sunday regarding a massive cocaine distribution ring at Penn State University has sent shockwaves through the community. With 14 individuals—including students and fraternity members—facing charges, the allegations go beyond mere drug trafficking; they suggest a disturbing culture of "indoctrination" where illegal activity was presented as a rite of passage for fraternity pledges.</p>
  </section>

  <section id="what-happened">
    <h2>The Allegations: A Coordinated Operation</h2>
    <p>According to the Attorney General’s office, the operation involved members of the Delta Upsilon and Sigma Chi fraternities. The alleged ringleaders, Agostino Abbatiello and Thomas Robinson, reportedly sourced large quantities of cocaine from New York and Philadelphia. The most chilling detail, however, is the claim that cutting and packaging the drugs was used as a form of "indoctrination" for new pledges.</p>
    <p>Penn State has placed Delta Upsilon on interim suspension, while Sigma Chi was already operating without university recognition. The charges include felony corrupt organizations, conspiracy, and drug-related offenses. As the investigation unfolds, the university and national fraternity organizations are scrambling to address a culture that allowed such criminal activity to flourish under the guise of brotherhood.</p>
