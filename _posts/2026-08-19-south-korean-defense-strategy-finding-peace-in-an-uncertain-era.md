    <section id="introduction">
        <p>In a rapidly shifting geopolitical landscape, South Korean President Lee Jae Myung has signaled a strategic pivot toward stronger independent defense capabilities. Following reports of scaled-back joint military exercises with the United States, Seoul is recalibrating how it ensures national security while remaining committed to regional stability. This shift invites us to consider the deeper, timeless questions of how nations—and individuals—balance the duty of protection with the pursuit of peace.</p>
    </section>

    <section id="what-happened">
        <h2 style="color: #2c3e50;">A Shifting Security Landscape</h2>
        <p>The recent discourse stems from President Trump’s directive to reduce the scope of joint U.S.-South Korea military drills. In response, President Lee has emphasized that while the U.S.-South Korea alliance remains a cornerstone of security, South Korea must accelerate its own defense initiatives, including the development of nuclear-powered submarines and more integrated operational capabilities. The administration maintains that these moves are defensive, intended to protect citizens rather than provoke neighbors.</p>
    </section>
