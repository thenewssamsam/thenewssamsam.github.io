<h2>What Happened in the 2026 Primaries</h2>
<p>The 2026 primary election cycle delivered a series of high-stakes surprises across the United States, with Florida taking center stage. State Representative Angie Nixon, running as a democratic socialist, secured a projected victory in the Democratic Senate primary over Alex Vindman, advancing to face Republican incumbent Senator Ashley Moody in November. Meanwhile, in the crowded race to replace outgoing Florida Governor Ron DeSantis, Republican Representative Byron Donalds won his party's nomination and will face Democrat David Jolly.</p>

<p>Beyond the top-tier matchups, the primaries exposed severe vulnerability among incumbent politicians and establishment-backed candidates. In Florida's 7th District, Representative Cory Mills lost his GOP primary following a House Ethics Committee investigation. Simultaneously, Wyoming delivered another blow to presidential endorsements, as State Senator Eric Barlow defeated Donald Trump's preferred gubernatorial candidate, Megan Degenfelder. For political observers, campaign strategists, and ambitious office seekers, these results offer a masterclass in how modern political careers are built, tested, and abruptly ended.</p>

<p>Another striking trend visible across these primaries is the steep career cost of ethical controversies and institutional drag. Representative Cory Mills' primary defeat illustrates that legal or ethical investigations create an almost insurmountable headwind for incumbent retention. In the political services industry, risk management has become the primary job requirement. Candidates who draw negative scrutiny from oversight committees quickly find themselves abandoned by donors, PACs, and party apparatuses.</p>
