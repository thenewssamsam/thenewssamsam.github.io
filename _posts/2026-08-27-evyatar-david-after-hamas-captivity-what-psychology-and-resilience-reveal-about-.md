  <section id="what-happened">
    <h2>What Happened: The Ordeal of Evyatar David</h2>
    <p>Evyatar David was just 22 years old when his life abruptly fractured on October 7, 2023. Abducted alongside his childhood friend Guy Gilboa-Dalal from the Nova music festival during the Hamas-led attack on Israel, David spent the next two years underground in Gaza. Two other friends in their immediate group were killed that catastrophic day.</p>
    
    <p>In February 2025 and again in August 2025, David was thrust into international view via harrowing Hamas propaganda videos. In the latter video, reduced to a skeletal frame, he was forced to hold a shovel and dig a shallow trench in a concrete tunnel. He was among the last 20 living Israeli hostages released on October 13, 2025, under a US-brokered ceasefire agreement.</p>

    <p>When examining an ordeal as extreme as David's, psychology offers a lens to understand how the human mind processes and survives systemic degradation. We are not analyzing any individual's private mental state, but rather the broad, well-documented psychological mechanisms of captivity, trauma, and recovery.</p>

    <h3>1. Psychological Terror and Manufactured Helplessness</h3>
    <p>Captors often use psychological manipulation—such as staging fake executions, forcing prisoners to dig their own graves, or creating extreme sensory deprivation and starvation—to induce total psychological surrender. This mirrors what psychologists call learned helplessness, where an individual feels completely powerless to change their circumstances. David noted that the mental agony of starvation—not knowing if food would arrive in an hour or never—was often worse than the physical hunger itself.</p>

    <h3>2. The Reframing Mechanism: Finding Agency in the Abyss</h3>
    <p>Despite relentless attempts by his captors to break his will, David discovered a critical psychological turning point. He recalled telling himself: <em>"No matter how bad our conditions were, it could always get worse, so you can always find good things."</em> In psychological terms, cognitive reframing under extreme duress is a vital survival tool. By shifting focus from total despair to micro-moments of endurance (such as thinking of his family waiting outside), prisoners can preserve a core sense of identity and agency that their captors cannot confiscate.</p>

    <h3>3. Post-Traumatic Stress and the Complex Road to Rehabilitation</h3>
    <p>Release is not an instantaneous return to normalcy; it is often the beginning of a complex, long-term psychological journey. David has been open about his struggles with PTSD, random flashbacks, and night terrors that disrupt his daily life. Recovery requires rebuilding safety, processing intrusive memories, and finding external tethers—such as music, which David uses as a refuge to quiet his thoughts and reconnect with his humanity.</p>
