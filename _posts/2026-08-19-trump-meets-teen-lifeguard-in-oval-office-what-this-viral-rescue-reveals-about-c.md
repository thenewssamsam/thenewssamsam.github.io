  <section id="ncg-what-happened">
    <h2>What Happened in the Oval Office</h2>
    <p>When headlines are dominated by political friction and institutional gridlock, an ordinary act of human heroism offers a refreshing break. Recently, President Donald Trump welcomed 16-year-old California State Parks lifeguard Ryder Williams and 10-year-old Nathaniel Rai to the Oval Office. The meeting followed a dramatic July ocean rescue caught on video, which quickly went viral across social media platforms.</p>
    
    <p>Back in July, young Nathaniel Rai was swept away by a powerful wave off the California coast, caught in rough surf that threatened to pull him under. Williams, on duty and relying on his rigorous training, plunged into the water, reaching the boy just as secondary help arrived to pull both to safety. The dramatic footage caught the attention of millions—including Eric Trump—ultimately leading to an official White House invitation. Praising Williams as an inspiration for young Americans, President Trump highlighted a simple truth often overlooked in daily life: <em>"Maybe lifeguards don't get the credit they deserve."</em></p>
  </section>

    <p>Most of us will never have to pull a drowning child out of rough ocean waves. We will not have cameras rolling to capture our split-second decisions, nor will we receive an invitation to the White House to shake the hand of the president. Yet, the story of Ryder Williams offers concrete lessons about how preparation meets opportunity when crises strike.</p>
