  <section id="what-happened" class="ncg-section">
    <h2>What Happened: The Florida Primary Upset</h2>
    <p>In political terms, the Democratic US Senate primary in Florida looked like a classic mismatch long before the ballots were cast. On one side stood Alexander Vindman, a retired army lieutenant colonel and national security whistleblower whose campaign amassed an impressive $16.3 million war chest by the end of July. On the other side stood Angie Nixon, a state representative and member of the Democratic Socialists of America based in Jacksonville, working with a modest budget of roughly $975,000.</p>
    
    <p>Yet, when the primary results rolled in on Tuesday night, it was Nixon who clinched the nomination, besting Vindman to face Republican incumbent Ashley Moody in the upcoming November general election. Moody, appointed by Governor Ron DeSantis to fill Marco Rubio's seat when he became secretary of state, breezed through her own primary.</p>
    
    <p>Nixon's campaign focused heavily on her nearly two decades of grassroots advocacy in Florida, including high-profile demonstrations against state redistricting efforts that led to her arrest on trespassing charges—a legal battle she continues to fight. While Vindman largely directed his messaging toward the general election opponent and declined Nixon's public debate challenges, Florida voters delivered a clear message against establishment-anointed frontrunners. National Democratic leaders, including Senate leader Chuck Schumer, quickly rallied behind Nixon, praising her background as a union organizer and tireless fighter for local communities.</p>
