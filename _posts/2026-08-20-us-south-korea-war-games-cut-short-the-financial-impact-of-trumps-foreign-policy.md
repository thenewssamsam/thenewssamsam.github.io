  <section id="what-happened">
    <h2 style="font-size: 1.6rem; color: #2b6cb0; margin-top: 35px; margin-bottom: 15px;">What Happened: The Shortened Ulchi Freedom Shield</h2>
    <p>South Korea's annual joint military drills with the United States, known as the Ulchi Freedom Shield, have been officially cut short by six days at the direct request of Washington. According to the South Korean defense ministry, both the period and the overall scale of the 2026 exercises were adjusted following a formal proposal from the US side. Instead of concluding on August 27, the drills will now wrap up on August 21, alongside a partial reduction in combined field training exercises.</p>
    
    <p>The decision caught South Korean and US officials largely by surprise. Foreign Minister Cho Hyun told a parliamentary hearing that neither side's diplomatic corps had been fully briefed in advance about the scale-back. The catalyst for the move, as articulated by US President Donald Trump, is tied directly to broader foreign policy expectations and financial burden-sharing disputes—specifically, South Korea's refusal to join US-led actions regarding Iran.</p>
    
    <p>"We have 39,000 soldiers over there guarding you from Kim Jong Un, your next door neighbour, and you're not going to help us on a very easy military operation in Iran," Trump remarked during an Oval Office briefing, explicitly linking alliance commitments to economic and military reciprocity.</p>
