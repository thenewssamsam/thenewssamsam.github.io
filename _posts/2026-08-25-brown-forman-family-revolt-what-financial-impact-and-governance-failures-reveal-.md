<div class="ncg-intro">
<p>When the founding family behind one of the world's most iconic spirits companies publicly turns on its own executive suite, Wall Street pays attention. Weeks after talks to combine with Pernod Ricard collapsed, two members of the Brown family—descendants of the pioneer who first bottled whiskey in sealed glass 156 years ago—drafted a blistering letter to the board of Brown-Forman Corp. Their critique exposed a staggering drop in market value, questionable executive payouts, and a growing crisis for the maker of Jack Daniel's, Woodford Reserve, and Herradura tequila.</p>

<section id="what-happened">
<h2>What Happened: Inside the Brown-Forman Boardroom Battle</h2>
<p>The conflict came to light following a scathing letter dated July 10, written by W. L. Lyons Brown III and his brother Stuart Brown. Addressed to the Brown-Forman board, the letter took leadership to task for deteriorating financial results, questionable compensation practices, and a bungled acquisition dialogue with European spirits giant Pernod Ricard SA.</p>

<p>The timing of the missive was explosive. Just three days after the letter was drafted, Brown-Forman announced that Chief Executive Officer Lawson Whiting had decided to retire. For a company historically known for steady, generational stability, the public airing of grievances signaled deep internal friction.</p>

<p>The root of the discord lies in a dramatic market contraction. At its peak in 2020, Brown-Forman commanded a valuation of $38 billion. For the family and public shareholders alike, the erosion of wealth has been swift and painful.</p>

<h2>One Last Thought</h2>
