  <h2 id="what-happened">What Happened: Bill Gates Breaks His Silence</h2>
  
  <p>Bill Gates has spent decades championing technological innovation. Yet, his recent remarks mark a notable shift from unbridled optimism to urgent caution. Speaking with Anderson Cooper, Gates expressed shock that stronger global safeguards are not already in place to manage the exponential growth of AI.</p>

  <p>Gates argued that simply blocking local data centers will not slow down the global trajectory of artificial intelligence, as infrastructure will simply migrate elsewhere. Instead, he called for explicit public tradeoffs between the "goods" and "bads" of the technology. His proposals include taxing AI tokens or robots to fund worker safety nets and reserving specific human-centric roles for people.</p>

  <p>While computer science experts like Oren Etzioni have debated the specifics of Gates' policy prescriptions—such as whether a token tax is the right mechanism—there is broad agreement that the diagnosis of rapid, unmonitored disruption is accurate.</p>

  <h2 id="the-psychology-of-silence">The Psychology of Technological Silence</h2>

  <p>Why has the global conversation around AI felt so muted until recently, despite its unprecedented velocity? From a psychological perspective, several well-documented cognitive biases explain our collective hesitation to confront massive, systemic threats.</p>

  <p>First is <strong>normalcy bias</strong>. When faced with a paradigm-shifting innovation, human brains tend to default to past experience, assuming that technological revolutions will follow the historical curve of the industrial or internet ages. Gates himself noted that the impact on the job market is "very different than any technology that I've been involved in or that has come across in human history." Yet, because we have survived past transitions, normalcy bias lulls society into a false sense of security.</p>

Silicon Valley accelerates forward under competitive pressure, while governments wait for public outcry, creating a dangerous feedback loop of mutual silence.</p>
