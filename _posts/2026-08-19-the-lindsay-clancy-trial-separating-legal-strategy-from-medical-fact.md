  <p>The trial of Lindsay Clancy, who is charged with the 2023 strangulation of her three young children, has reached a critical turning point. As the prosecution rests its case, the courtroom has become a battleground of conflicting narratives: is this a case of calculated, premeditated murder, or the tragic outcome of an undiagnosed psychotic break?</p>

  <section id="what-happened">
    <h2>What Happened?</h2>
    <p>Lindsay Clancy has admitted to killing her children—Cora, 5; Dawson, 3; and Callan, 8 months—before attempting to take her own life, which left her paralyzed. The defense argues that she was suffering from severe, undiagnosed postpartum psychosis and was acting under the command of voices. The prosecution, meanwhile, maintains that Clancy was acting with intent and control, citing evidence of her behavior on the day of the incident.</p>

However, psychiatric experts note that mental health deterioration, particularly regarding psychosis, is rarely a "straight line." Defense experts argue that behavior can fluctuate significantly, and an individual may appear functional to outside observers even while experiencing profound internal distress.</p>

    <h3>Myth: The Prosecution Has Proven Premeditation</h3>
    <p><strong>Fact:</strong> Prosecutors highlight digital evidence, such as Apple Maps lookups, as proof of calculation. However, legal analysts point out that this is circumstantial. Proving "premeditation" requires establishing a specific intent to kill, which the defense argues was impossible due to the alleged state of psychosis. The jury must decide if these actions were deliberate planning or the erratic movements of a mind in collapse.</p>
