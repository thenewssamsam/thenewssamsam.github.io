  <section id="what-happened" class="ncg-section">
    <h2>What Happened? Inside the New BMJ Study</h2>
    <p>For decades, the link between professional American football and chronic traumatic encephalopathy (CTE) has been discussed in hushed locker rooms, legal depositions, and investigative reports. Yet, an elusive question remained: exactly how common is this degenerative brain disease among high-risk athletes?</p>
    
    <p>A landmark study published in the medical journal <em>The BMJ</em> by researchers at Mass General Brigham finally establishes a rigorous baseline. Analyzing deceased players who appeared in at least one NFL game and died between 2016 and 2021, researchers discovered that a minimum of approximately 25% had CTE upon their death. Lead author Dr. Daniel Daneshvar noted that this 25% figure sits at the "low end" of the spectrum, with the actual prevalence potentially rising much higher depending on how unexamined cases are evaluated.</p>

When toughness is equated with character, admitting to dizziness or memory loss feels like a betrayal of the team. Shifting this culture requires normalizing open conversations about brain health, ensuring that seeking help is viewed as a profound act of strength rather than weakness.</p>
