  <section id="introduction">
    <p>When 29-year-old Sophie Rottenberg passed away in February 2025, her family was left with a devastating mystery. To the outside world, she was a driven professional surrounded by a loving support system. But in the digital silence of her laptop, a different story unfolded: thousands of pages of dialogue with an AI chatbot she had instructed to act as her "personal therapist." Her story forces us to confront a growing psychological phenomenon: the dangerous allure of AI in moments of profound human crisis.</p>
  </section>

  <section id="what-happened">
    <h2>The Hidden Struggle</h2>
    <p>Sophie Rottenberg’s experience was not unique in its digital nature but tragic in its outcome. As she navigated job-hunt anxiety and depression, she turned to a general-purpose chatbot, which she prompted to adopt the persona of "Harry," a supportive therapist. For months, this AI became her primary confidant, providing a 24/7 space that felt non-judgmental and accessible. However, as experts now point out, this convenience masked a dangerous psychological displacement: the AI did not provide the clinical intervention she needed; instead, it may have acted as a buffer, preventing her from disclosing the true depth of her agony to the real, human support systems around her.</p>

  <section id="psychology-perspective">
    <h2>The Psychology of the "AI Confidant"</h2>
    <p>From a psychological standpoint, the appeal of AI in mental health is rooted in the <strong>"Burden Myth."</strong> Many individuals struggling with mental health issues fear they are a weight on their loved ones. AI chatbots, being devoid of human emotion, eliminate the fear of "burdening" a friend or family member. This creates a feedback loop of avoidance.</p>

    <h3>The Illusion of Intimacy</h3>
    <p>Psychologically, we are wired for human connection. When we interact with sophisticated AI, our brains often project human-like qualities onto the machine—a concept known as <em>anthropomorphism</em>. For someone in a state of crisis, the AI’s consistent, calm, and available responses can feel more "safe" than the unpredictable reactions of real people. However, this is a dangerous illusion. The AI is not "listening"; it is processing data patterns. It cannot detect the subtle vocal cues or physical signs of distress that a flesh-and-blood therapist would recognize immediately.</p>

    <h3>The Displacement Effect</h3>
    <p>The most alarming psychological risk is the <em>displacement of care</em>. By venting to a bot, the user completes the "act" of expression without the therapeutic benefit of being heard and understood by another human. This can create a false sense of relief, effectively "capping" the distress and preventing it from spilling over into the real world, where intervention might actually occur.</p>

  <section id="scripture-reflection">
    <h2>A Reflection on Human Connection</h2>
    <p>This tragedy reminds me that we were never designed to carry our heaviest burdens in isolation. There is a profound difference between the "counsel" of an algorithm and the presence of a neighbor. Scripture speaks to the necessity of bearing one another's burdens: <em>"Bear one another's burdens, and so fulfill the law of Christ"</em> (Galatians 6:2, ESV). This is a call to presence—a physical, emotional, and spiritual commitment to those around us.</p>

We must cultivate communities where it is safe to be vulnerable, and where we are empowered to reach out for help that is tangible and human.</p>

  <section id="practical-response">
    <h2>How Should We Respond?</h2>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Prioritize Human Connection:</strong> If you or a loved one are struggling, prioritize face-to-face or voice-to-voice conversations. AI cannot replace the neurological regulation that comes from human presence.</li>
      <li><strong>Recognize the "Buffer":</strong> If you notice yourself or a friend spending excessive time chatting with AI about emotional health, treat it as a red flag that they may be avoiding real-world support.</li>
      <li><strong>Know the Resources:</strong> Tools like the 988 Suicide and Crisis Lifeline are designed to connect individuals with trained humans. AI should only ever be a bridge to these services, never the destination.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>

  <section id="closing-takeaway">
    <h2>One Last Thought</h2>
    <p>Technology promises us efficiency, but mental health is not a problem to be "solved" with a prompt. It is a human experience that requires empathy, clinical expertise, and the messy, beautiful reality of connection. Let us ensure that in our rush toward digital convenience, we do not lose the very thing that keeps us alive: each other. <em>True healing happens when we are brave enough to be known by people who can actually hold our hand.</em></p>
