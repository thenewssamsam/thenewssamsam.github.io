    <section style="margin-bottom: 30px;">
        <p style="font-size: 1.15rem; font-weight: 500; color: #4a5568; margin-bottom: 20px;">
            The tranquil hills above Port-au-Prince were shattered when armed gangs launched a devastating assault on Kenscoff, leaving at least 47 people dead and over 50 kidnapped. This horrific event pierce the fragile hope that security in Haiti was finally stabilizing.
        </p>
        <p style="margin-bottom: 15px;">
            News reports confirmed that the tragedy included the execution of 22 people inside a church compound where they sought sanctuary. As families mourn and survivors recount harrowing escapes over the bodies of loved ones, the international community watches in horror. But beyond the immediate geopolitical failures and military strategies, events of this magnitude force us to confront deeper questions about human brokenness, societal vulnerability, and where hope can be found when earthly systems fail.
        </p>
    </section>

    <section id="what-happened" style="margin-bottom: 30px;">
        <h2 style="font-size: 1.5rem; color: #2d3748; border-left: 4px solid #2b6cb0; padding-left: 12px; margin-bottom: 15px;">What Happened in Kenscoff?</h2>
        <p style="margin-bottom: 15px;">
            According to United Nations reports and local authorities, the attack struck the once-peaceful community of Kenscoff on Sunday. Armed groups burned 25 homes and ruthlessly targeted civilians. Among the dead were at least five children and two government employees. 
        </p>
        <p style="margin-bottom: 15px;">
            In the wake of the massacre, gang leaders released chilling social media threats warning police leadership that hostages would be executed if security forces retaliated. Humanitarian organizations note that this attack highlights the alarming reality: armed groups control roughly 70 percent of Port-au-Prince and are aggressively expanding into vulnerable rural and hillside corridors ahead of scheduled general elections.
        </p>
    </section>

    <div style="background-color: #f7fafc; border-left: 4px solid #d69e2e; padding: 20px; border-radius: 6px; margin: 30px 0;">

            "The atrocity of this crime reminds us that we have to continue working stronger every day to bring peace to the community." — Kenscoff Mayor Jean Massillon
        </p>
    </div>

    <section id="faith-perspective" style="margin-bottom: 30px;">
        <h2 style="font-size: 1.5rem; color: #2d3748; border-left: 4px solid #2b6cb0; padding-left: 12px; margin-bottom: 15px;">A Spiritual Lens on Unimaginable Loss</h2>
        <p style="margin-bottom: 15px;">
            When violence shatters a community—especially within the sacred refuge of a church—it naturally stirs profound spiritual anguish. How can such darkness prevail? Scripture does not offer easy platitudes for moments of mass suffering, but it does speak directly to the reality of human cruelty and the cry for divine justice.
        </p>
        <p style="margin-bottom: 15px;">
            When reflecting on the suffering in Haiti, I am reminded of the words in Psalm 34:18: <em>"The Lord is close to the brokenhearted and saves those who are crushed in spirit."</em> This verse invites us to consider that God is not indifferent to the terror experienced by families in Kenscoff. He sees the innocent lives cut short and the anguish of those fleeing for their lives.
        </p>
        <p style="margin-bottom: 15px;">
            Furthermore, the relentless cycle of violence and greed displayed by armed groups illustrates the destructive power of unchecked human corruption. As James 4:1-2 reminds us, conflicts and fights among us stem from unbridled desires and the pursuit of power at the expense of others. The gangs coveting Kenscoff for strategic control demonstrate a tragic earthly reality: when societal structures weaken, human selfishness often rushes in to fill the vacuum.
        </p>
    </section>
