  <section id="what-happened">
    <h2>What Happened in Chocó?</h2>
    <p>Last week, a powerful 7.4 magnitude earthquake struck western Colombia, leaving unimaginable devastation in its wake. At least 287 people lost their lives, over 26,000 homes were destroyed, and countless communities were plunged into sudden grief. The hardest-hit epicenter was the province of Chocó—one of Colombia's poorest regions, historically marginalized and largely populated by Indigenous and Afro-Colombian communities where infrastructure is fragile and rivers serve as the primary roads.</p>
    <p>Amidst the rubble and ongoing rescue assessments, global music icon Shakira made a surprise visit to the provincial capital of Quibdó. Walking through damaged classrooms and comforting displaced children, the Grammy-winning artist didn't just offer words of sympathy. Through her Fundación Pies Descalzos (Barefoot Foundation), alongside philanthropist Howard Buffett—son of billionaire Warren Buffett—she committed to reconstructing 10 schools. Backed by additional funding from international partners like the FIFA Global Citizen Education Fund and the CAF Development Bank, Shakira pledged to ensure that the children of Chocó are not permanently robbed of their education.</p>
    <p>As the initial shock waves fade, the deeper question remains: How do societies—and the individuals blessed with influence—respond when the cameras turn away and the hardest work of rebuilding begins?</p>

Shakira’s foundation has maintained a presence in Chocó for over two decades, running schools long before the earthquake brought cameras to the region. This distinction points to a profound biblical principle: true stewardship is marked by consistency rather than convenience.</p>
