  <section id="introduction">
    <p>In the high-stakes world of federal politics, a seat in Congress is often viewed as the pinnacle of a professional trajectory. However, the recent primary defeat of Republican Rep. Cory Mills in Florida’s 7th District serves as a stark reminder that professional longevity is not guaranteed by incumbency alone. As voters move toward the general election, Mills' loss to challenger Ryan Elijah highlights how investigations and public perception can fundamentally alter a career path, regardless of past endorsements or political pedigree.</p>
  </section>

  <section id="what-happened">
    <h2>The Anatomy of an Upset</h2>
    <p>NBC News projects that former journalist Ryan Elijah has secured the Republican nomination, defeating incumbent Cory Mills. Despite receiving an endorsement from President Donald Trump—a factor that historically secures many GOP primaries—Mills fell short, garnering 34% of the vote compared to Elijah’s 46% with over 80% of precincts reporting.</p>

    <h3>Campaign Finance and Viability</h3>
    <p>Mills’ struggle to raise funds—despite being an incumbent—is a classic indicator of a cooling professional market. Donors, much like investors, look for reliability. When an individual becomes a "high-risk" asset, capital flows elsewhere. Even though Mills raised more than Elijah, he significantly underperformed compared to the average House member, indicating that his political base was losing confidence in his ability to deliver results.</p>

  <section id="scripture-reflection">
    <h2>A Reflection on Integrity</h2>
    <p>This situation brings to mind the wisdom found in Proverbs 22:1: <em>"A good name is rather to be chosen than great riches, and loving favor rather than silver and gold."</em> (KJV). In the heat of a career, it is easy to focus on the "gold"—the power, the position, or the campaign funds—but the foundation of any lasting contribution is a person's character. Similarly, Colossians 3:23 reminds us: <em>"Whatever you do, work at it with all your heart, as working for the Lord, not for human masters."</em> (NIV). This teaches us that the ultimate standard for our work is not just whether we can win a primary or secure a contract, but how we conduct ourselves in the process. It invites us to consider whether our professional ambitions are aligned with the quiet, steady work of integrity, which often sustains us long after the noise of a campaign has faded.</p>
