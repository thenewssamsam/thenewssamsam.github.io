  <h2 id="what-happened">What Happened: The Warning From Moscow</h2>
  
  <p>In a recent appearance on the BBC's <em>Newsnight</em>, former Russian Deputy Foreign Minister Andrei Fedorov issued a stark warning to the United Kingdom. Amid mounting public pressure inside Russia for a firmer response to Western military aid, Fedorov suggested that defense enterprises in Britain producing drones and munitions for Ukraine might soon find themselves targeted. Crucially, he framed these potential actions not as official Russian military strikes, but as operations carried out by "semi-military" or "unknown sources," including possible cyberattacks.</p>

  <p>This fiery rhetoric follows a high-stakes diplomatic development: British Prime Minister Andy Burnham visited Kyiv to finalize an agreement enabling Ukraine to domestically manufacture advanced cruise missile technology. Under this pact, Ukraine will utilize French SCALP missiles containing British components, significantly bolstering its long-range strike capabilities against Russian command infrastructure. The Kremlin swiftly condemned the agreement, with spokesman Dmitry Peskov accusing London of direct escalation and confirming that Russian intelligence is actively tracking potential production sites.</p>

During the mid-20th century, major powers routinely engaged in proxy conflicts where direct military engagement was too politically volatile, relying instead on saboteurs, volunteers, and covert disruptions to bleed their adversaries without triggering all-out war.</p>
