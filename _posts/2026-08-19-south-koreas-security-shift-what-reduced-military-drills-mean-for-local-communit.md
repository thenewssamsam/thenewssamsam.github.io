  <section id="introduction">
    <p>The geopolitical landscape of the Korean Peninsula is shifting once again. With President Trump ordering a reduction in joint U.S.-South Korea military exercises to avoid appearing "hostile" toward North Korea, citizens across Seoul and the border regions are left questioning what this means for their day-to-day safety. While high-level diplomacy makes headlines, the reality for local communities is a complex mix of uncertainty and the need for renewed vigilance.</p>
  </section>

  <section id="what-happened">
    <h2>The Current Situation</h2>
    <p>President Lee Jae Myung has publicly urged the nation to brace for a "worst-case scenario" following the U.S. decision to scale back the annual <em>Ulchi Freedom Shield</em> exercises. These drills, which involve roughly 18,000 South Korean personnel, are traditionally viewed by Seoul as a defensive necessity. However, President Trump’s recent comments, citing his relationship with Kim Jong Un and his frustration regarding South Korea's lack of support in Iran, have injected a new layer of friction into the alliance.</p>
