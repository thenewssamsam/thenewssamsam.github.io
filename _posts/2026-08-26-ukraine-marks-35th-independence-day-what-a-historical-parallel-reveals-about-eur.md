  <section id="what-happened">
    <h2>What Happened in Kyiv</h2>
    <p>On Monday, August 24, 2026, Ukraine marked its 35th Independence Day. The mood in Kyiv was far from a conventional celebration, unfolding against the grim backdrop of a four-year-old Russian invasion with no immediate end in sight. European Council President António Costa, British Prime Minister Andy Burnham—making his first overseas trip since taking office—and several other international leaders arrived in the Ukrainian capital to stand shoulder-to-shoulder with President Volodymyr Zelenskyy.</p>
    
    <p>The meetings yielded concrete security commitments. British Prime Minister Burnham confirmed the approval of shared classified technology regarding the Storm Shadow and SCALP long-range missile systems, paving the way for domestic Ukrainian and French co-production. Meanwhile, the human toll of the conflict continued to exact a devastating price on both sides, punctuated by escalating long-range drone and missile attacks.</p>

Yet the closest mirrors to the current struggle in Eastern Europe are found in the mid-20th century. History shows that when a larger regional power seeks to absorb a neighbor under the banner of historical restoration, the conflict rarely resolves quickly; instead, it settles into a war of industrial capacity, coalition-building, and sheer national resolve.</p>
