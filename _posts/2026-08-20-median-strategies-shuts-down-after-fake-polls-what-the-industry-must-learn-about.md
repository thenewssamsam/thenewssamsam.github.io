  <section id="what-happened">
    <h2>What Happened?</h2>
    <p>Median Strategies announced this week that it has ceased all operations following widespread fallout over fabricated public opinion surveys. The firm initially published polling data indicating that Los Angeles Mayor Karen Bass held a commanding 12-point lead over city council member Nithya Raman ahead of the primary. In a separate instance, Median Strategies released numbers suggesting Wisconsin Democratic gubernatorial candidate Francesca Hong was leading her primary rival, David Crowley, by a staggering 20 points.</p>
    <p>These numbers quickly infiltrated the political information ecosystem. Local television broadcasts, digital reporting outlets, and countless social media commentators picked up the metrics, treating them as legitimate insights into voter sentiment. However, reality proved starkly different. Bass edged out Raman by a much tighter five-point margin, while Crowley ultimately defeated Hong by a razor-thin margin. Following intense scrutiny, Median Strategies posted a statement admitting the entire project was a manufactured hoax meant to examine how unverified polling spreads across modern digital networks.</p>

    <h2>A Career & Industry Perspective: The Fragility of Professional Trust</h2>
    <p>From a career and industry standpoint, the Median Strategies stunt highlights a deeply unsettling vulnerability within the data and analytics profession. Modern polling is built on a foundational currency: trust. When organizations rely on reputation, methodology, and historical accuracy to validate their work, a bad actor can easily exploit the system's reliance on fast-moving news cycles.</p>
