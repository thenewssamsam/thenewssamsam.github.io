<section id="what-happened">
<h2>What Happened in the Primaries</h2>
<p>As the long, contentious primary season draws to a close, voters across Florida, Wyoming, and Alaska have headed to the polls to shape the upcoming midterms. In Florida, high-stakes races set the stage for a dramatic fall campaign. U.S. Rep. Byron Donalds secured the Republican nomination for governor, moving closer to a historic potential milestone, while former Rep. David Jolly claimed the Democratic nod. At the same time, progressive state lawmaker Angie Nixon scored a major upset victory over well-funded establishment rival Alex Vindman in a race for the U.S. Senate nomination.</p>

<p>Beyond these marquee matchups, the primaries featured a chaotic mix of redrawn congressional districts, political comebacks, and hard-fought battles that highlighted the unpredictable nature of modern American politics. For adults following the news cycles, these results signal shifts in congressional power and party direction. But for children watching television broadcasts or listening to adult conversations around the dinner table, these intense political contests can feel overwhelming, confusing, or abstract.</p>

<h2>A Parenting Perspective: Ambition and Resilience</h2>
<p>When major primary results flash across our screens, parents have a unique opportunity to look past the political strategy and focus on the human behaviors underneath. Campaigns are ultimately human stories filled with ambition, hard work, unexpected victories, and stinging defeats. For children, observing how candidates succeed or fall short provides a powerful mirror for their own experiences in school, sports, and friendships.</p>

<h2>One Last Thought</h2>
<p>As the primary season gives way to the general election noise, our homes can remain steady anchors for our children. Let us model grace, curiosity, and steadfast character around our dinner tables.</p>
