  <section id="what-happened">
    <h2>What Happened: The White House Construction Stoppage</h2>
    <p>Federal infrastructure projects routinely face permitting delays, regulatory hurdles, and budget friction. But when those delays hit the executive mansion, the stakes scale instantly from local zoning disputes to a high-level constitutional showdown. As reported by legal scholar and media host Hugh Hewitt, the U.S. Supreme Court is facing an urgent application from the Department of Justice to stay a lower court ruling that effectively micromanages the ongoing construction of the new East Wing of the White House.</p>
    
    <p>The legal friction stems from lower court rulings upholding a restrictive construction schedule ordered by U.S. District Judge Richard Leon following a challenge by the National Trust for Historic Preservation. The original East Wing, built in 1902 and expanded under FDR in 1942 to house facilities like the Presidential Emergency Operations Center, now requires modernized security upgrades. With modern threat matrices including hypersonic missiles, advanced drones, and rapidly evolving surveillance capabilities, the 84-year-old subterranean bunkers have become obsolete.</p>
    
    <p>The Department of Justice, pointing to persuasive dissents from D.C. Circuit Judge Neomi Rao, has asked Chief Justice John Roberts to intervene and lift the construction freeze. For project managers, engineers, and industry professionals navigating complex regulatory environments, this public standoff provides a fascinating lens into the tension between statutory oversight and executive command-and-control operations.</p>
