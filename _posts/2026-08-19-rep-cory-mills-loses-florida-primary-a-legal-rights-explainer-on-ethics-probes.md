  <section id="news-hook">
    <p>Republican Representative Cory Mills has been defeated in Florida’s 7th District primary, a rare loss for an incumbent backed by President Donald Trump. His campaign was overshadowed by ongoing scrutiny from both the House Ethics Committee and the Department of Justice regarding his conduct and finances. As voters look toward the general election, many are left asking: what exactly does a federal investigation into a sitting member of Congress entail?</p>
  </section>

  <section id="what-happened">
    <h2>The Context of the Defeat</h2>
    <p>Mills, who secured the Republican nomination in 2022, faced a primary challenge from former journalist Ryan Elijah. With over 80% of the vote counted, Elijah led with 46% to Mills' 34%. This result follows months of negative publicity, including a House Ethics Committee probe into alleged sexual misconduct and potential campaign finance violations, alongside a separate Justice Department inquiry into his personal finances. Mills has consistently denied any wrongdoing.</p>

    <h3>The Role of the House Ethics Committee</h3>
    <p>The House Committee on Ethics is a bipartisan body tasked with investigating allegations of misconduct by members. Unlike a criminal court, its primary jurisdiction involves the rules of the House itself. If the committee finds evidence of rule violations, it can recommend sanctions ranging from letters of reproval to expulsion. It is important to note that this process is administrative, not judicial.</p>

    <h3>Department of Justice Inquiries</h3>
    <p>When the Department of Justice (DOJ) investigates a member of Congress, the process shifts from internal House rules to federal law. If a member is suspected of financial crimes, such as campaign finance violations, the DOJ follows standard federal criminal procedure. This includes the right to counsel, the presumption of innocence, and the requirement that the government meet the burden of proof to secure a conviction.</p>
