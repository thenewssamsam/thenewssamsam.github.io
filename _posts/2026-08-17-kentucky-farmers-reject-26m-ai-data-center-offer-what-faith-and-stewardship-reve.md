  <p style="box-sizing:border-box; font-size:1.15rem; font-weight:500; margin:0 0 20px 0;">When an anonymous AI company flashed a staggering $26.4 million payout to buy out their multi-generational Kentucky family farm, most people would have signed on the dotted line without a second thought. But self-described "country hicks" Delsia Bare and her mother Ida Huddleston had a much shorter, far more memorable answer for the corporate reps: "Kick rocks and don't come back."</p>

  <section id="what-happened" style="box-sizing:border-box; margin-bottom:30px;">
    <h2 style="border-bottom:2px solid #3182ce; color:#1a202c; font-size:1.5rem; font-weight:700; margin:0 0 15px 0; padding-bottom:5px;">When $26 Million Meets a 200-Year Legacy</h2>
    <p style="box-sizing:border-box; margin:0 0 15px 0;">According to reports from the <em>Wall Street Journal</em>, the mother-daughter duo initially agreed to sell their plots in Maysville, Kentucky, for a combined total of $26.4 million when approached by corporate representatives. At $48,000 per acre for Bare's 463 acres and $60,000 per acre for Huddleston's 71 acres, the offer sat well above market value, presenting life-changing wealth for a rural family.</p>
    <p style="box-sizing:border-box; margin:0 0 15px 0;">However, the moment the duo learned that the land—which has been in their family for roughly 200 years—was intended to host massive data centers designed to power artificial intelligence bots, they swiftly walked away from the deal.</p>
    <p style="box-sizing:border-box; margin:0 0 15px 0;">Bare, who is nearly blind, and Huddleston, who walks with a cane, suddenly found themselves at the center of a raging community debate over rural land use, corporate expansion, and the relentless march of technological infrastructure.</p>
  </section>

  <section id="the-faith-perspective" style="box-sizing:border-box; margin-bottom:30px;">
    <h2 style="border-bottom:2px solid #3182ce; color:#1a202c; font-size:1.5rem; font-weight:700; margin:0 0 15px 0; padding-bottom:5px;">A Biblical Perspective on Land and Stewardship</h2>
    <p style="box-sizing:border-box; margin:0 0 15px 0;">What drove two women to walk away from enough money to secure several lifetimes of earthly comfort? For Delsia Bare, the decision was not merely about zoning, community politics, or property values. It was a matter of spiritual accountability before God.</p>
    <p style="box-sizing:border-box; margin:0 0 15px 0;">As Bare explained to reporters regarding her decision to protect the family homestead: <em>"If I step off of this piece of property and it becomes something for an AI hub, then I have failed to occupy and I can be held accountable for that on the Judgment hour."</em></p>
    
    <div style="background:#ebf8ff; border-left:4px solid #3182ce; box-sizing:border-box; margin:20px 0; padding:15px 20px;">
      <p style="box-sizing:border-box; font-style:italic; margin:0 0 8px 0;">"The earth is the Lord's, and everything in it, the world, and all who live in it..."</p>
      <p style="box-sizing:border-box; font-size:0.9rem; font-weight:600; margin:0; text-align:right;">&mdash; Psalm 24:1 (NIV)</p>
      <p style="box-sizing:border-box; font-size:0.95rem; margin:10px 0 0 0;">This verse reminds believers that we are ultimately caretakers, not absolute owners, of the ground beneath our feet. Property is a trust passed down through generations.</p>
    </div>

    <p style="box-sizing:border-box; margin:0 0 15px 0;">Scripture consistently places a high value on faithful stewardship and the preservation of our God-given responsibilities. When human innovation seeks to pave over creation simply to feed the bottomless computational appetite of digital networks, stories like this force us to re-examine what we trade away for a paycheck.</p>

    <div style="background:#f7fafc; border:1px solid #cbd5e0; border-radius:8px; box-sizing:border-box; margin:25px 0; padding:20px; text-align:center;">
      <p style="color:#1a202c; font-size:1.2rem; font-weight:700; line-height:1.4; margin:0;">"When corporate ambition offers millions to pave over a 200-year legacy, true stewardship remembers that some things carry an eternal value that no check can ever cash."</p>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section id="what-this-reveals" style="box-sizing:border-box; margin-bottom:30px;">
    <h2 style="border-bottom:2px solid #3182ce; color:#1a202c; font-size:1.5rem; font-weight:700; margin:0 0 15px 0; padding-bottom:5px;">The Deeper Tension: Progress Versus Preservation</h2>
    <p style="box-sizing:border-box; margin:0 0 15px 0;">The standoff in Maysville highlights a broader cultural fault line. As the tech industry races to build sprawling data centers to fuel the artificial intelligence boom, rural communities across the nation find themselves caught in the crossfire.</p>
    <p style="box-sizing:border-box; margin:0 0 15px 0;">On one side, local economic developers and neighbors argue that data centers bring much-needed jobs, tax revenue, and modern infrastructure investment. On the other side, longtime residents fear the quiet erosion of their communities, the transformation of pastoral landscapes into humming fortresses of server racks, and the permanent loss of local heritage.</p>
    <p style="box-sizing:border-box; margin:0 0 15px 0;">Bare and Huddleston's refusal has escalated beyond a simple property dispute; they have joined lawsuits challenging the construction of the centers, dragging complex legal battles into a small-town courtroom where neighbors are increasingly pitted against neighbors.</p>
  </section>
