<section id="what-happened">
<h2>What Happened: The Storm That Left Thousands in the Dark</h2>
<p>On August 11, a violent storm system tore across northern Illinois and northwestern Indiana, unleashing straight-line winds ranging from 70 mph to over 100 mph. The National Weather Service has preliminarily identified the event as a derecho—often called an "inland hurricanes"—though final confirmation may take months. At its peak, the resulting damage left approximately 374,500 customer accounts without electricity as trees crashed into homes, power lines snapped, and local infrastructure buckled.</p>

<p>While power was gradually restored to many over the following days, nearly two weeks later, thousands of residents remain stranded without electricity. As of August 23, roughly 9,000 customers in Gary, Indiana, and over 16,000 across the broader utility service area were still waiting for the grid to come back to life. Local leaders, including Gary Mayor Eddie Melton and Indiana Gov. Mike Braun, have voiced intense frustration over the prolonged delays, prompting the mobilization of the Indiana National Guard to help clear blocked roads so utility crews can finally reach damaged lines.</p>
