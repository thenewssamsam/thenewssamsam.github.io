  <section style="margin-bottom: 30px;">
    <p style="font-size: 1.15rem; font-weight: 500; color: #2d3748; margin-bottom: 20px;">
      When a tight-knit New England neighborhood is rocked by unimaginable tragedy, the ripples extend far beyond the courtroom walls. The Lindsay Clancy murder trial in Massachusetts has forced local communities, healthcare providers, and families to confront a devastating question: How did a system meant to protect mothers fail someone who was actively begging for help?
    </p>
    <p style="margin-bottom: 15px;">
      As testimony continues in the high-profile Massachusetts trial, the focus has shifted from simple criminal attribution to a broader, more uncomfortable conversation about maternal mental health resources at the local level. For residents across Massachusetts and beyond, the case is a painful reminder that the infrastructure supporting new mothers often crumbles precisely when it is needed most.
    </p>
  </section>

  <div style="background-color: #f7fafc; border-left: 4px solid #3182ce; padding: 20px; margin: 25px 0; border-radius: 4px;">
    <h3 style="margin-top: 0; color: #2b6cb0; font-size: 1.1rem;">Quick Take: The Local Reality</h3>
    <p style="margin-bottom: 0;">
      While prosecutors argue that Lindsay Clancy made a calculated decision to kill her three children, family testimony paints a picture of a desperate mother experiencing severe postpartum psychosis. For local communities, the case underscores an urgent need for accessible, emergency psychiatric care for new parents.
    </p>
  </div>

  <section id="what-happened" style="margin-bottom: 30px;">
    <h2 style="font-size: 1.5rem; color: #2d3748; border-bottom: 1px solid #e2e8f0; padding-bottom: 8px; margin-bottom: 15px;">What Happened in the Testimony</h2>
    <p style="margin-bottom: 15px;">
      During recent proceedings in the Massachusetts courtroom, defense attorneys presented personal anecdotes and testimonies from family members following the conclusion of the prosecution's case. Among the most striking accounts came from Susan Clancy, the defendant's former mother-in-law, who testified that Lindsay Clancy was "begging for help" in the weeks leading up to the January 2023 tragedy.
    </p>
    <p style="margin-bottom: 15px;">
      According to family testimony, Clancy—a registered nurse—experienced severe insomnia, a loss of appetite, anxiety, and profound sadness following the birth of her youngest son, Callan. Paula Musgrove, Clancy's mother, testified that her daughter had explicitly stated she was having thoughts of harming the children a month before the incident, telling family members, "This isn't me, I've never been like this before."
    </p>
    <p style="margin-bottom: 15px;">
      Defense attorneys maintain that Clancy was suffering from acute postpartum psychosis, a rare and severe mental health illness distinct from the standard "baby blues." Prosecutors, however, contend that the killings of Cora (5), Dawson (3), and Callan (8 months) were intentional acts. Following the incident, Clancy jumped from a second-floor window, leaving her paralyzed and wheelchair-bound throughout the trial.
    </p>
  </section>

      <p style="margin-top: 10px; margin-bottom: 0;">
        Postpartum psychosis is a rare and severe mental health illness that can affect women soon after childbirth. Symptoms include hallucinations, delusions, severe insomnia, mania, and extreme confusion. It is considered a medical emergency requiring immediate psychiatric treatment.
      </p>
    </details>

      <p style="margin-top: 10px; margin-bottom: 0;">
        Lindsay Clancy has been charged with three counts of first-degree murder in connection with the deaths of her three children in January 2023. Her defense argues she was not criminally responsible due to postpartum psychosis at the time.
