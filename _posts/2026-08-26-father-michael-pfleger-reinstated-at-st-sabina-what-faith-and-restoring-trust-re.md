<p>After weeks of painful absence and intense scrutiny, long-serving Chicago pastor Father Michael Pfleger has been officially reinstated to ministry at St. Sabina Catholic Church. Chicago Cardinal Blase J. Cupich announced on Monday that an independent review board concluded there is no reason to believe Pfleger is guilty of a historical sexual abuse allegation dating back more than 30 years. For a community that has weathered decades of polarizing activism, pastoral departures, and deep emotional ties to their leader, the decision brings both profound relief and complex questions about justice, reputation, and the fragile nature of institutional trust.</p>

<h2 id="what-happened">What Happened at St. Sabina</h2>

<p>The Archdiocese of Chicago confirmed that Father Pfleger stepped aside last month in accordance with standard child protection policies after an individual raised a historical claim regarding an incident in the late 1980s. Pfleger consistently denied the accusation, calling it an absolute fabrication in statements to local media. Following a thorough review, the independent review board found the allegations to be unfounded. Cardinal Cupich accepted the board's findings and restored Pfleger to his senior pastor duties at St. Sabina, effective immediately.</p>

<p>This is not the first time Pfleger has faced severe allegations and subsequent exoneration. In 2021, an investigation cleared him of separate historical claims made by three men. Yet, regardless of where observers stand on his public persona, the canonical process has once again cleared him of the specific misconduct alleged.</p>
