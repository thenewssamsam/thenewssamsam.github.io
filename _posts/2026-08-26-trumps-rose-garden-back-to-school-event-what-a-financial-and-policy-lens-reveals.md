<section id="what-happened">
    <h2>What Happened at the Rose Garden Event?</h2>
    <p>As tens of millions of American children head back to classrooms, the political battle lines over education policy have sharpened once again. President Donald Trump marked the opening of the new school year with a high-profile Rose Garden gathering, joined by Education Secretary Linda McMahon, students, parents, and educators from across the nation. While the White House framed the event as a celebration of the upcoming academic year, the remarks quickly pivoted to a sweeping defense of the administration's aggressive education agenda.</p>
    
    <p>The president used the platform to contrast his administration's goals with the policies of past White House occupants. Central to his speech was a strong push for "school choice," alongside sharp critiques of federal oversight, gender identity policies in classrooms, and public school performance metrics. According to administration figures cited during the event, government-run schools continue to face steep challenges in basic academic proficiencies, serving as a primary justification for opening up alternative educational pathways.</p>

    <p>Yet, beyond the political rhetoric and cultural debates, the administration's back-to-school announcements included concrete financial policy shifts that directly impact household budgets, state tax structures, and long-term savings for American families. To understand the true weight of these announcements, it helps to examine the underlying financial mechanics.</p>

    <h2>Expanding 529s and the New Trump Accounts</h2>
    <p>Beyond tax credits for donations, the administration's education strategy relies heavily on expanding tax-advantaged savings vehicles. The recent legislative changes notably doubled the annual withdrawal cap for K-12 expenses under 529 education savings accounts, raising the limit to $20,000 per year. Crucially, families can now utilize these accounts to support homeschooling costs as well as private and religious school tuition.</p>

Families and children can later draw from these funds for qualified expenses, ranging from higher education to purchasing a first home or launching a small business.</p>
