  <section id="what-happened" class="ncg-section">
    <h2>What Happened: The Anatomy of the SoCal Heatwave</h2>
    <p>According to recent meteorological reports from Daniel Swain of Weather West, a robust late-summer high-pressure ridge is building over northern Mexico and southern Arizona, extending its most anomalous lobe directly across Southern California. While Northern California largely escapes the brunt of this thermal dome, Southern California and parts of southern Arizona are bracing for significant, and potentially locally extreme, heat.</p>
    
    <p>Meteorologists note that this week's event is characterized by two distinct challenges. First, exceptionally warm ocean temperatures along the Southern California coast are locking in a shallow layer of high coastal humidity. This means that while immediate coastal zones enjoy a modest marine buffer, areas just a few miles inland will experience a stressful "battleground" where intense inland heat converges with coastal moisture, driving up apparent temperatures and heat index values.</p>

    <p>Second, there is a 30 to 40 percent chance that the event could turn significantly more extreme if weak-to-moderate offshore flow develops between Wednesday and Friday. Such a pattern could trigger downslope warming across inland valleys—potentially pushing temperatures near or above 110 degrees Fahrenheit—while near-century heat stretches all the way to the beaches, accompanied by critical fire weather concerns.</p>

    <h3>The Cognitive Friction of Sleepless Nights</h3>
    <p>From a behavioral psychology standpoint, uninterrupted nighttime cooling is not merely a comfort; it is a biological necessity for cognitive restoration. When overnight temperatures remain stubbornly elevated, human circadian rhythms and sleep architecture are disrupted. Sleep deprivation directly impairs emotional regulation, lowers frustration thresholds, and heightens the brain's reactivity to stress.</p>
