    <h2 style="color: #2d3748; border-bottom: 2px solid #e2e8f0; padding-bottom: 0.5rem; margin-top: 2rem;">What Happened: A Delicate Diplomatic Thaw</h2>
    <p style="line-height: 1.7; color: #4a5568; margin-bottom: 1.25rem;">After two years of a cautious thaw, Beijing and New Delhi are preparing for a pivotal diplomatic test. Chinese President Xi Jinping is expected to cross paths with Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi at the Shanghai Cooperation Organisation (SCO) summit in Bishkek, Kyrgyzstan, setting the stage for a much anticipated fuller sit-down in New Delhi during the upcoming BRICS summit.</p>
    
    <p style="line-height: 1.7; color: #4a5568; margin-bottom: 1.25rem;">This upcoming journey marks Xi's first visit to India in seven years. Ahead of the high-stakes meetings, both nations have confirmed eight concrete steps designed to ease long-standing border tensions. However, regional experts and diplomatic sources caution against overplaying expectations, noting that current offers on the table fall well short of a grand bargain or an immediate, comprehensive breakthrough.</p>

    <div style="background: #fff; border: 1px solid #e2e8f0; border-left: 4px solid #3182ce; padding: 1.25rem; border-radius: 6px; margin: 1.5rem 0;">
        <h4 style="margin: 0 0 0.5rem 0; color: #2d3748;">Quick Take</h4>
        <p style="margin: 0; color: #4a5568; font-size: 0.95rem;">While Xi Jinping's upcoming New Delhi visit symbolizes a significant diplomatic normalization after seven years, analysts emphasize that incremental border management steps should not be mistaken for a permanent resolution of deeper geopolitical rivalries.</p>
    </div>

    <p style="line-height: 1.7; color: #4a5568; margin-bottom: 1.25rem;">The disputed Himalayan border remains high on the agenda alongside trade, investment, and people-to-people connections, including visa regulations. The eight steps agreed upon to ease border friction represent a return to tactical risk-reduction rather than a permanent strategic alignment.</p>
