<h2>What Happened: The 2026 Super El Niño Forecast</h2>
<p>According to recent climate reports, a historically intense Super El Niño is taking shape in the Pacific Ocean, threatening to bring extreme weather conditions to the West Coast. Reminiscent of the devastating 1997-1998 event that dropped nearly a year's worth of rain on Los Angeles and battered the Santa Monica Pier, current projections indicate that California will once again serve as an epicenter for major winter hazards.</p>

<p>Experts point out that the upcoming season carries a dual threat: predictable coastal hazards driven by ocean physics, and less predictable precipitation tracks that could trigger heavy rainfall, mountain snows, and dangerous landslides. While scientists like Patrick Barnard from the University of California, Santa Cruz, emphasize the near-certainty of extreme coastal flooding—threatening an additional 170,000 people and $60 billion in property—meteorologists remind the public that every El Niño behaves differently due to background climate change trends.</p>
</section>
