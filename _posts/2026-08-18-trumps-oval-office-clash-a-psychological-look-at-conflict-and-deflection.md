When faced with a line of questioning that threatens to derail a preferred message, the brain often defaults to <strong>deflection</strong>. By shifting the focus from the content of the question to the character of the questioner, the individual creates a psychological "pivot" that avoids the discomfort of addressing the original topic.</p>

By involving a child in the critique of the reporter, the President was attempting to frame the reporter’s behavior as objectively inappropriate through the eyes of a neutral, innocent party. From a behavioral standpoint, this is a classic move to isolate the perceived "aggressor" (the journalist) and align the audience (the boy and the public) with the speaker's emotional reality.</p>

Understanding these psychological tactics allows viewers to remain objective, preventing the emotional intensity of the interaction from clouding our ability to track the actual policy discussions—such as the status of military exercises in South Korea—that were buried beneath the conflict.</p>
