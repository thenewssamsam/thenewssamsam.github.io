  <h2 id="what-happened" style="font-size: 24px; font-weight: 700; color: #1a202c; margin-top: 32px; margin-bottom: 16px;">What Happened in South Carolina</h2>
  <p>Political primary battles often leave deep fractures in their wake, and the 2026 Republican Senate primary runoff in South Carolina proved to be no exception. As precinct returns rolled in through a tense Tuesday evening, incumbent Senator Darline Graham emerged victorious over challenger Ralph Norman in a closely watched contest that captured national attention. According to vote data via the Associated Press and projections by the NBC News Decision Desk, Graham captured roughly 52.6 percent of the vote compared to Norman's 47.4 percent.</p>
  
  <p>The race was billed as a pivotal test for incumbent momentum against insurgent conservative pressure in the Palmetto State. With nearly 95 percent of expected votes accounted for late in the evening, the contest remained close enough in key counties to keep election watchers glued to live tracking maps. Yet, as the remaining ballots from early voting and Election Day precincts were tabulated by county election officials, Graham's coalition held firm.</p>
