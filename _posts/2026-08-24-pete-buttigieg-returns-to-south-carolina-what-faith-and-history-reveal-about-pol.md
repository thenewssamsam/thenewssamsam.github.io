<h2 id="what-happened">What Happened: A Return to the Palmetto State</h2>
<p>In mid-August 2026, just days after the Democratic National Committee reaffirmed South Carolina's position as the first-in-the-nation primary state, Pete Buttigieg traveled to Florence and Orangeburg, South Carolina. Eying a potential second White House run as a private citizen, his weekend itinerary included a speech at the Spratt Issues Conference, a roundtable with students at a historically Black college discussing reparations, and an address to the local NAACP branch.</p>

<p>During his remarks, Buttigieg addressed the deep-seated challenges of modern civic life, drawing a direct line between contemporary political struggles and the historic civil rights battles fought in places like Orangeburg in the 1960s. He emphasized that the path forward requires tackling both fundamental questions of democracy and pressing economic concerns, rejecting the idea that leaders must choose between civil rights and economic relief.</p>
