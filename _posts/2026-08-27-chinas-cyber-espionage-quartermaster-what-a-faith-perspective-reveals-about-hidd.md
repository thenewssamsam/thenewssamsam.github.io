  <section id="what-happened" class="ncg-section">
    <h2>What Happened: Unveiling the Quartermaster</h2>
    <p>In the modern digital landscape, the tools used to gain unauthorized access are only part of the story; the infrastructure that conceals those activities matters just as much. Recently, Lumen’s Black Lotus Labs published a compelling threat intelligence report tracking a sophisticated infrastructure provider supporting Chinese cyber espionage activities. Functioning much like a military "quartermaster," this entity provides a specialized, reusable service layer that integrates reconnaissance, proxy orchestration, and operational routing.</p>
    <p>Rather than building every piece of malicious architecture from scratch, advanced threat actors are increasingly relying on shared, externally managed infrastructure brokers. According to the report, this Nanjing-originating entity supplies pre-packaged stealth, target verification telemetry, and non-attributable transit layers to multiple malicious consumers simultaneously. By commercializing and industrializing the construction of operational relay box (ORB) networks—exploiting commercial internet circumvention architectures known as "Airport" networks—these operators can route traffic, blend with normal internet noise, and bypass traditional security defenses with remarkable efficiency.</p>

    <ul>
      <li><strong>QScan:</strong> Conducts systematic reconnaissance to identify and profile high-value targets across global networks.</li>
      <li><strong>Fast Labyrinth:</strong> The operational proxy layer that co-opts commercial proxy infrastructure into an encrypted relay network, obfuscating traffic to and from victim entities.</li>
      <li><strong>QTRouter:</strong> A preconfigured physical access device that manages operator and customer access to the underlying proxy infrastructure.</li>
