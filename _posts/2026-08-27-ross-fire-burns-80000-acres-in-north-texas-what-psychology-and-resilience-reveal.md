<h2 id="what-happened">What Happened: The Anatomy of the Ross Fire</h2>

<p>The Ross Fire began on a Monday afternoon roughly 60 miles west of Fort Worth in Palo Pinto County, quickly escalating into a sprawling emergency. Fueled by shifting winds and dry terrain, the blaze crossed northward into Jack County, swelling to an immense 80,000 acres. As of mid-week updates from the Texas A&M Forest Service, containment stood stubbornly at just 12%.</p>

<p>Yet, amidst the destruction of remote structures, a youth camp, and solar installations, frontline workers achieved critical victories. Prepositioned firefighting crews successfully defended the town of Bryson as flames neared overnight, while Oncor utility crews worked around the clock to manage damaged power lines. Governor Greg Abbott secured crucial federal support via a FEMA disaster grant, giving emergency responders much-needed reinforcement. Still, for hundreds of displaced residents, the numbers matter less than the immediate scramble to safety.</p>
