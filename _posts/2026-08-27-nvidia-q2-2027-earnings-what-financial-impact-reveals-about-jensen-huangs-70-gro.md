  <section id="what-happened" class="ncg-section">
    <h2>What Happened: The Q2 Numbers</h2>
    <p>In a report that once again defied market skeptics, Nvidia announced its fiscal second-quarter results for 2027, outperforming Wall Street expectations across nearly every major metric. The company reported adjusted earnings per share of $2.22 against an estimated $2.10, while revenue reached $96.22 billion compared to the anticipated $92.17 billion.</p>
    <p>Net income more than doubled year-over-year, soaring to $53.95 billion from $24.76 billion. Driven by these results and a bold forecast for fiscal 2028—where CFO Colette Kress projected 70% revenue growth—Nvidia's stock jumped roughly 4% in after-hours trading. The company also highlighted a massive deal with Amazon Web Services (AWS), which includes orders for 2 million GPUs and millions of proprietary Vera CPUs.</p>

    <h3>The Capital Return Engine</h3>
    <p>Nvidia continues to return massive amounts of cash to its shareholders. During the quarter alone, the company spent $26 billion on share repurchases and dividends, backed by an authorized $80 billion buyback program. This aggressive return strategy highlights management's confidence in near-term free cash flow generation. However, it also illustrates how deeply tied tech sector valuations are to sustained, uninterrupted demand from top-tier cloud providers.</p>
    
    <h3>The Margin Pressure Point</h3>
    <p>Gross margins are expected to dip slightly, bottoming out between 71% and 72% in the fourth quarter of fiscal 2027. CFO Colette Kress pointed directly to memory scarcity as the primary culprit. As the AI buildout accelerates, the raw components required to manufacture advanced chips are driving up input costs. For everyday consumers and enterprise buyers alike, this scarcity ripples outward, demonstrating how microeconomic supply constraints can impact macro-level pricing.</p>

    <h2>Supply Chains, Debt, and the Cost of Expansion</h2>
    <p>Perhaps the most revealing line items in Nvidia's recent quarterly filing involve liabilities and procurement commitments. These figures offer a sobering look at what it takes to maintain dominance in a capital-intensive industry.</p>
    
    <h3>Surging Supply Commitments and Debt Risks</h3>
    <p>Nvidia reported that its supply commitments more than doubled, jumping from $119 billion to $279 billion, primarily driven by the procurement of memory chips. Securing manufacturing capacity on this scale requires heavy financial provisioning.</p>
