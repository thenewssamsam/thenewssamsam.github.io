      Islamabad Hospital Nursery Fire: What Faith and Scripture Reveal About Injustice and Grief
    </h1>
    <p style="font-size: 1.15rem; color: #4a5568; margin: 0;">
      A devastating air conditioner explosion inside a government hospital nursery in Islamabad claimed the lives of 14 newborns, leaving families shattered and a nation demanding answers. Beyond the political fallout, this unthinkable tragedy forces us to confront deep questions about systemic failure, human vulnerability, and where God is when the most innocent suffer.
    </p>
  </header>

  <section id="what-happened" style="margin-bottom: 40px;">
    <h2 style="font-size: 1.5rem; font-weight: 700; color: #2b6cb0; border-bottom: 1px solid #e2e8f0; padding-bottom: 8px; margin-bottom: 20px;">
      What Happened at the Pakistan Institute of Medical Sciences?
    </h2>
    <p>
      In the early hours of a Wednesday morning, joy turned into unimaginable horror at the Pakistan Institute of Medical Sciences (PIMS) in Islamabad. A fire, reportedly triggered by an exploding air conditioner, swept rapidly through the third-floor hospital nursery where 15 newborns were receiving intensive care. Out of the 15 fragile infants, only one baby was miraculously rescued by a brave attending doctor. Fourteen families went to sleep anticipating the day they would take their newborns home, only to wake up to caskets and unanswered questions.
    </p>
    <p>
      Television footage captured the heartbreaking scenes of rescue workers scaling ladders to reach the smoke-choked ward through shattered windows. Grieving parents rushed to the facility, weeping openly and demanding accountability. Among them was Mrs. Mohammad Sami, who had given birth just three days earlier. Through tears that resonated across the globe, she expressed the universal helplessness of the moment: &ldquo;I don’t know what to say or whom to blame. I have lost my child.&rdquo;
    </p>
    <p>
      In response to the public outcry, the government moved swiftly. Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif ordered the removal of Health Secretary Aslam Ghauri, the ministry's highest civil servant, and established a high-level fact-finding committee instructed to report within 24 hours. President Asif Ali Zardari condemned the tragedy as &ldquo;heartbreaking and an irreparable loss,&rdquo; calling for strict measures to ensure institutional safety across the nation's public healthcare infrastructure. Yet, for the families mourning their little ones, bureaucratic inquiries offer small comfort against the permanent silence left in their homes.
    </p>
