<section id="what-happened" style="margin-bottom: 2.5rem;">
    <h2 style="font-size: 1.75rem; color: #1a202c; border-bottom: 2px solid #e2e8f0; padding-bottom: 0.5rem; margin-bottom: 1rem;">What Happened: The Otis Lane Tragedy</h2>
    <p style="line-height: 1.7; color: #2d3748; margin-bottom: 1rem;">
        The nightmare began in the early morning hours of Saturday, August 22, 2026. According to the Washington County Sheriff's Office, a neighbor near 47350 Northwest Otis Lane called 911 just after 5:00 a.m. to report a vehicle fire. As Forest Grove Fire & Rescue rushed to the scene, firefighters discovered an engulfed car in the driveway and quickly uncovered a grim reality: multiple individuals had been shot.
    </p>
    <p style="line-height: 1.7; color: #2d3748; margin-bottom: 1rem;">
        Law enforcement officers swarmed the rural property, which featured numerous outbuildings and residential structures. Inside and around the main home, deputies discovered the bodies of five victims: Stella Kimsey, 26; her mother Rowan Kimsey, who owned the property; Stella's grandmother Mary Kimsey; Stella's best friend Linsey Mae Haraldsen, 27; and Haraldsen's partner Alvin Raymond Chu, 27. Tragically, five household animals—dogs and cats—were also found shot to death on the property.
    </p>
    <p style="line-height: 1.7; color: #2d3748; margin-bottom: 1rem;">
        Investigators revealed that the suspect attempted to conceal the horrific crime by setting fires to vehicles, bodies, and structures using tarps, though exhaustive forensic work confirmed that none of the victims had been bound or duct-taped as initial reports feared.
    </p>
</section>

<section id="investigation-timeline" style="margin-bottom: 2.5rem;">
    <h2 style="font-size: 1.75rem; color: #1a202c; border-bottom: 2px solid #e2e8f0; padding-bottom: 0.5rem; margin-bottom: 1rem;">The Investigation Timeline and Phone Data</h2>
    <p style="line-height: 1.7; color: #2d3748; margin-bottom: 1rem;">
        Detective Mark Povolny of the Washington County Sheriff's Office outlined a meticulous investigation during subsequent press briefings. Early suspicion fell immediately upon Benjamin Parker, Stella Kimsey's boyfriend who lived on the property. However, due to the intense fire damage inside one of the vehicles, identifying whether a body belonged to the suspect or a victim required advanced digital forensics.
    </p>
    <p style="line-height: 1.7; color: #2d3748; margin-bottom: 1rem;">
        Cell phone location data proved critical. Records indicated Parker's phone was active on the Otis Lane property Friday evening through early Saturday morning. At 4:42 a.m. Saturday—just minutes before emergency services were alerted—his phone traveled south toward Forest Grove, east into Cornelius, and was powered off at 5:15 a.m.
    </p>
    <p style="line-height: 1.7; color: #2d3748; margin-bottom: 1rem;">
        The breakthrough arrived Sunday evening when Parker powered his phone back on near Enumclaw, Washington, initiating a lengthy call with his mother. During this conversation, Parker confessed to the premeditated murders, detailing how he fled the scene, passed responding emergency vehicles, and eventually ran out of gas. Following the call, Parker switched off his phone permanently. By Sunday afternoon, Enumclaw police located his truck, where Parker was found dead of an apparent self-inflicted gunshot wound.
    </p>
</section>

The attempt to destroy evidence through arson often reflects a desperate psychological urge to erase not just physical proof, but the psychological weight of their own actions.
