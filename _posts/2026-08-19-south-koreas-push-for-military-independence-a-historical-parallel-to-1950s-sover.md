    <h2>Historical Parallels: Sovereignty vs. Security</h2>
    <p>The struggle for Opcon is not new; it is a recurring theme that mirrors the anxieties of post-armistice Korea. In the 1950s, President Syngman Rhee famously grappled with the United States over the terms of the armistice, fearing that the US might prioritize global stability over South Korean interests. Today’s friction reflects a similar tension: the fear that the "security umbrella" might be folded to suit the diplomatic whims of a distant ally.</p>

During that era, as in today’s climate, the prospect of reduced US engagement served as a catalyst for South Korea to modernize its indigenous defense industry. Just as then, the current situation serves as a "sovereignty check," reminding the public that national security is ultimately a domestic responsibility.</p>
