  <section id="what-happened" class="ncg-section">
    <h2>What Happened in Florida</h2>
    <p>In a stunning upset that reverberated across national political circles, Florida state representative and democratic socialist Angie Nixon won the Democratic nomination for the US Senate, defeating former army intelligence officer and national security figure Alexander Vindman. Nixon secured 56.1% of the vote to Vindman's 43.9%, overcoming a staggering fundraising deficit that saw her raise roughly $975,000 compared to Vindman’s $16.3 million. Nixon now advances to face Republican incumbent Ashley Moody in the general election.</p>
    
    <p>The race highlights a fascinating dynamic in contemporary American politics. While establishment figures and well-funded campaigns typically dominate high-profile primaries, Nixon’s victory demonstrates the power of long-term grassroots engagement. Having spent nearly two decades fighting local policy battles in Florida—including a high-profile sit-in at the governor's office—Nixon positioned herself as an authentic voice deeply connected to everyday community struggles. Vindman, meanwhile, focused much of his messaging outward toward the general election opponent rather than engaging directly in primary debates.</p>
