  <section id="what-happened">
    <h2>What Happened: A Nation at a Crossroads</h2>
    <p>Inside a bustling waterfront factory in Reykjavik, workers in hairnets and blue overalls sort freshly caught cod and haddock as machines fillet the catch, mostly bound for European markets. It is an everyday scene in Iceland, but this weekend, those fillets carry the weight of a national existential question.</p>
    
    <p>Voters in this island nation of nearly 400,000 people are deciding whether to resume stalled accession talks to join the European Union. According to recent polling, the referendum is virtually neck-and-neck, hovering around 51% for the Yes camp and 48% for the No side. Prime Minister Kristrún Frostadóttir’s center-left government pledged the vote by 2027 following a 2024 snap election, but rapid geopolitical shifts—including Russia's ongoing war in Ukraine, sharpening Arctic rivalries, and US policy pressures—forced the timeline forward.</p>

    <p>Yet, while politicians in Reykjavik and diplomats across Europe focus on security alliances and shifting global tides, average citizens are weighing a much more immediate set of pressures: a stubborn cost of living, high interest rates, and the fiercely guarded waters that built the modern Icelandic economy.</p>

Proponents of joining the EU argue that closer European integration and the eventual adoption of the euro could finally tame Iceland's volatile currency and soaring inflation. Analysis by the academic union Viska revealed that prices in Iceland were an eye-opening 84% higher than the EU average, while interest rates have climbed to 8%.</p>
