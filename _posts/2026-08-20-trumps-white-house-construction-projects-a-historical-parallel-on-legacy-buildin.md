  <section id="introduction">
    <p>President Donald Trump recently turned the South Lawn of the White House into a makeshift construction site, personally guiding reporters through the progress of a new granite helipad and a controversial  million ballroom. While the president framed the work as a durable addition to the nation’s most famous residence, the event highlights a recurring tension in American leadership: the desire to leave a physical, tangible mark on the capital while navigating significant political and economic headwinds.</p>
  </section>

  <section id="what-happened">
    <h2>The View from the South Lawn</h2>
    <p>During the Wednesday tour, President Trump touted the technical specifications of the new granite helipad, emphasizing its ability to withstand extreme pressure. He described the project as a legacy piece that would outlast his presidency, even as ongoing work on a massive ballroom continues to face legal and congressional scrutiny. The president appeared in his element, adopting the persona of a developer-in-chief, signing a commemorative stone and praising the materials used to replace asphalt driveways.</p>
