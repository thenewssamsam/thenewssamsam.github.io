    <h2>What Happened: The Shot Heard Round New York</h2>
    <p>For the New York Knicks and their faithful, the summer of 2026 feels surreal. After more than five decades of heartbreak, false dawns, and relentless misery, the Knicks captured the NBA title in dramatic fashion. Down 29 points in a pivotal Game 4 against the San Antonio Spurs, New York completed an improbable resurrection, capped off by forward OG Anunoby’s game-winning tip-in—a moment quickly dubbed the "Hand of God" by captivated fans.</p>
    
    <p>Yet, while the city remains intoxicated by the historic triumph, Anunoby has made headlines for a different reason: he says he is simply "over" the big moment. Sitting in a black hoodie on a brown leather sofa during an August promotional tour, the star forward isn't swimming in endless nostalgia. Instead, he is quietly preparing for the grueling reality of defending a crown. In a sports culture that demands endless celebration of fleeting moments, Anunoby's calm, almost indifferent gaze forward offers a fascinating window into how humans process triumph, expectation, and the passage of time.</p>
