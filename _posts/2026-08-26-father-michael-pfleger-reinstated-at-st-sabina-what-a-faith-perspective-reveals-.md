    <h2 style="font-size: 1.5rem; font-weight: 700; color: #2d3748; border-bottom: 1px solid #edf2f7; padding-bottom: 8px; margin-bottom: 20px;">
      What Happened at St. Sabina?
    </h2>
    <p style="margin-bottom: 15px;">
      Father Michael Pfleger, the longtime and high-profile pastor of St. Sabina Church on Chicago's South Side, has been officially reinstated to active ministry. This decision comes after the Archdiocese of Chicago’s Independent Review Board concluded its investigation into allegations made by a woman claiming she was abused by Pfleger between 1993 and 1995, while she was a student at the parish school.
    </p>
    <p style="margin-bottom: 15px;">
      Cardinal Blase Cupich announced the findings in a letter to parishioners, confirming that the independent board determined there is "no reason to believe Father Pfleger is guilty of this allegation." Pfleger, who had been temporarily removed from his Auburn Gresham post on July 11, responded on social media with a simple message: "Thank you God and thanks to all who stood by me," accompanied by an image of Michael Jordan declaring, "I'm back."
    </p>
    <p style="margin-bottom: 15px;">
      This marks the fifth time in five years that Pfleger, now 77, has faced and been cleared of similar historical allegations. Each time, the archdiocese's investigative procedures have ultimately cleared him of wrongdoing, yet the recurring cycles of removal and reinstatement have profoundly impacted both the clergyman and his deeply devoted congregation.
    </p>
  </section>

      Why This Case Resonates Across Chicago and Beyond
    </h2>
    <p style="margin-bottom: 15px;">
      The ongoing situation at St. Sabina is far more than a localized administrative update within the Catholic Church. It sits at the delicate intersection of institutional accountability, the imperative to protect vulnerable individuals, and the fundamental right to a fair name. St. Sabina has long functioned as more than a Sunday house of worship; under Pfleger's leadership since 1981, it has served as an epicenter for social justice activism, gun violence advocacy, and community support in a historically marginalized neighborhood.
    </p>
    <p style="margin-bottom: 15px;">
      When a pastor of this magnitude is repeatedly removed and subsequently cleared, it creates an intense friction within the community. Parishioners have loudly voiced their frustrations, arguing that the automatic removal policy before an investigation concludes casts an unfair shadow of guilt over the accused cleric. Conversely, church leadership maintains that independent inquiries are essential for preserving public trust and ensuring that every claim—especially those involving historical abuse—is met with rigorous, impartial scrutiny.
    </p>
    <p style="margin-bottom: 15px;">
      This tension forces observers to confront a difficult modern reality: how do religious institutions balance the vital duty of protecting victims of abuse with the biblical and civil principles of justice, due process, and the protection of the innocent?
    </p>
  </section>
