<h1 class="ncg-title">Evyatar David After 783 Days in Gaza: What a Faith Perspective Reveals About Captivity and Hope</h1>
<div class="ncg-intro">
For 783 days, Evyatar David endured unimaginable darkness in Hamas captivity after being abducted from the Nova music festival. In his first international media interview since his release, his harrowing account of starvation, staged propaganda, and psychological torture confronts us with the deepest questions of human endurance and spiritual resilience.
</div>
</header>

<section class="ncg-section">
<h2 class="ncg-h2" id="what-happened">What Happened: The 783-Day Ordeal</h2>
<p class="ncg-p">
Evyatar David was among the thousands of young people whose lives were shattered on October 7, 2023, when Hamas militants launched a brutal surprise attack on the Nova music festival. While two of his close friends were killed in the assault, David and his friend Guy Gilboa Dalal were taken hostage and dragged into the underground tunnel network of Gaza.
</p>
<p class="ncg-p">
In a recent, jaw-dropping interview with CNN published on Wednesday, David broke his silence to recount the horrific realities of spending 783 days in captivity. Among the most disturbing revelations was the background behind a notorious propaganda video shared by Hamas during his imprisonment. In the footage, David was forced to dig his own grave—an experience he described to CNN as "almost impossible."
</p>

<h2 class="ncg-h2" id="the-psychological-torture">The Weight of Mental and Physical Abuse</h2>
<p class="ncg-p">
Beyond the physical deprivation of months without proper nutrition, David detailed a relentless campaign of psychological torment. He described the ordeal as "really like a mental abuse all those three months." On the day of the grave-digging video, just a 50-meter walk nearly caused him to collapse. 
</p>
<p class="ncg-p">
Perhaps most chillingly, David recounted the casual cruelty of his captors. While he and his fellow hostages withered away like skeletons, hearing their guards laughing, eating cakes, and drinking tea just a few feet away highlighted a stark moral divide. It was a daily confrontation with raw, unchecked malice that tested the absolute limits of human endurance.
</p>
