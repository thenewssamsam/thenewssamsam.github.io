<section id="what-happened">
<h2>What Happened: The Recall Filing and Court Date</h2>
<p>The political landscape in Washington's largest city is facing a formal test. Last week, Seattle residents Melinda Jacobson and Dale Osterud filed paperwork seeking to initiate a recall election against Mayor Katie Wilson. The primary grievances listed in the filing center on municipal public safety issues: specifically, the administration's handling of the Bite of Seattle shooting that left three people dead, persistent crime concerns in downtown, Aurora Avenue, and the Chinatown International District, and a controversial administrative decision to temporarily turn off CCTV cameras following the World Cup.</p>

<p>In response, Mayor Wilson has retained legal counsel through attorney Dmitri Iglitzin. Representatives for the mayor emphasized that the administration's focus remains squarely on delivering tangible progress for residents—pointing to expanded community crisis responder hours, accelerated shelter expansion, universal school meals, and transit investments as proof of ongoing dedication to the city's welfare.</p>

<section id="legal-framework">
<h2>The Legal Framework: Malfeasance, Misfeasance, and the Oath of Office</h2>
<p>For everyday observers, political frustration often feels like sufficient grounds to challenge an elected leader. Under Washington state law, however, emotional dissatisfaction is not enough. A successful recall petition must clear a demanding legal threshold defined strictly as “malfeasance,” “misfeasance,” or a distinct violation of the official’s oath of office.</p>

<p>In legal terms, <em>misfeasance</em> refers to the wrongful performance of a normally legal act, while <em>malfeasance</em> involves the commission of an unlawful act in an official capacity. A violation of the oath of office means the official has failed to faithfully perform duties mandated by law. Proving these elements requires demonstrating that the official intentionally or improperly breached specific legal duties—not simply that voters dislike policy outcomes or administrative choices.</p>
