<p>President Donald Trump slammed the brakes late Tuesday on a sweeping set of 50 percent tariffs targeting Canadian imports just hours before they were scheduled to take effect. For the 13 million American workers whose livelihoods depend on North American trade and the integrated cross-border supply chains governed by the USMCA, the sudden three-day reprieve offers a collective, if temporary, sigh of relief.</p>

<p>As negotiations continue under the guidance of U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer and Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney, professionals across manufacturing, logistics, agriculture, and energy find themselves bracing for what comes next. While a tentative framework has been reached—complete with mentions of a resurrected Keystone XL pipeline and broader digital trade alignment—the cliffhanger underscores a volatile new normal for international commerce and industrial employment.</p>

<p>The dramatic eleventh-hour announcement came via social media late Tuesday, with President Trump confirming that the 50 percent duties scheduled for midnight had been paused for three days. The proposed tariffs would have impacted approximately $20 billion worth of U.S. imports from Canada, touching everyday products ranging from hockey sticks and building materials to specific categories of clothing and liquor.</p>
