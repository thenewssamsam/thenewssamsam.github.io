</header>

<p class="ncg-intro">A viral video capturing three headlamp-wearing individuals emerging from a steamy manhole near Manhattan's Penn Station has reignited public fascination with New York City's hidden subterranean world. <h2 id="what-happened">What Happened in the Early Morning Hours</h2>

<p>At roughly 4 a.m. on a Tuesday near 32nd Street and Seventh Avenue, just blocks away from Madison Square Garden and the bustling Penn Station commuter hub, a TikTok video captured a bizarre urban spectacle. Three men, dressed in overalls and equipped with headlamps, were filmed hastily pulling themselves out of a city manhole and disappearing into the morning light.</p>

<p>According to the New York City Police Department and the Department of Environmental Protection (DEP), this incident is not an isolated event. Similar sightings were caught on camera two months prior in Williamsburg and Gravesend, prompting ongoing investigations. Law enforcement and municipal sources indicate that these underground excursions are largely driven by scavenging—treasure hunters searching the sprawling 7,500-mile labyrinth of sewers for lost jewelry, wayward wallets, or other valuables that slipped through street grates.</p>

<p>While an inspection by the DEP confirmed no structural damage to the municipal infrastructure, authorities have issued stern warnings. City officials emphasize that entering manholes is both illegal and extremely hazardous due to noxious gases, sudden flooding, unstable surfaces, and confined spaces.</p>
