    <h2>The Psychology of Power and Risk</h2>
    <p>When high-profile figures are mired in scandal, the public often asks the same question: <em>Why would they risk everything?</em> While we cannot diagnose specific individuals, psychological research into the "power paradox" offers a window into why these patterns occur.</p>
    
    <div class="ncg-quote-box" style="border-left: 5px solid #6a0dad; padding: 15px; background: #f9f4ff; margin: 20px 0;">
      <p style="font-size: 1.2em; font-style: italic;">"Power can create a psychological 'blind spot' where individuals prioritize immediate impulses over long-term stability and professional duty."</p>
    </div>

    <p><strong>The Illusion of Exceptionalism:</strong> Research suggests that individuals who rise to high-status positions may begin to suffer from a form of <em>cognitive bias</em> where they feel the standard rules of social conduct do not apply to them. This isn't necessarily a calculated choice, but rather a gradual shift in perspective where personal desires are conflated with entitlement.</p>
    
    <p><strong>Groupthink and Environment:</strong> In high-intensity environments like Capitol Hill, <em>groupthink</em> can suppress the natural "alarm bells" that might stop inappropriate behavior. When an individual is surrounded by a culture that validates their status, the psychological barriers that usually prevent impulsive, risky behavior can erode.</p>
