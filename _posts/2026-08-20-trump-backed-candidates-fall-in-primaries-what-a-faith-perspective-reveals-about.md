    <h1>Trump-Backed Candidates Fall in Primaries: What a Faith Perspective Reveals About Political Power</h1>
  </header>

  <p class="ncg-lead">When nearly 300,000 Republican voters turned out for primary elections in Wyoming and Florida, just 28 percent cast their ballots for the candidates endorsed by President Donald Trump. This brutal night for presidential picks marks a broader, accelerating trend of GOP voters quietly pushing back against central political control, inviting observers to step back and reflect on the shifting, temporary nature of earthly power.</p>

  <h2 id="what-happened">What Happened in the 2026 Primaries?</h2>
  
  <p>According to a CNN analysis of primary results and endorsements compiled by Ballotpedia, Republican primary voters have rejected 10 Trump-endorsed candidates for statewide office or Congress during the 2026 season. That matches the total number of losses from 2018, 2020, and 2024 combined, and approaches the record 12 primary defeats from 2022.</p>

  <p>The latest wave hit hard on a single Tuesday night, claiming high-profile figures such as Wyoming gubernatorial candidate Megan Degenfelder (who landed under 30% of the vote) and Florida US House Rep. Cory Mills, alongside Catalina Lauf. These defeats follow earlier primary losses in August for Minnesota gubernatorial candidate Mike Lindell, Tennessee Rep. Andy Ogles, and Michigan hopeful Amir Hassan.</p>

  <p>Even as the incumbent president attempts to downplay or rewrite the narrative—claiming online that he advised Cory Mills to step aside before the race—the statistics point to a notable loosening of political grip among grassroots voters. As public polling reveals rising voter discontent on several key policy fronts, political loyalties are proving far more fluid than many establishment figures expected.</p>
