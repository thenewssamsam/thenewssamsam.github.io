<section id="ncg-what-happened">
<h2>What Happened inere in the Comey Seashells Case?</h2>
<p>Few legal battles in recent memory have featured a plot twist quite as meta as the one unfolding in federal court in North Carolina. Former FBI Director James Comey is currently facing felony charges alleging he "knowingly and willingly" made a threat against the life of President Donald Trump. The centerpiece of the government's case is an Instagram post from May 2025 featuring a photograph of seashells arranged on a shore to spell "8647" alongside the caption, "cool shell formation on my beach walk."</p>

<p>Prosecutors argue that "8647" serves as a coded message—combining the restaurant slang "86," which they interpret using a specialized dictionary definition to mean "to kill," with the number 47, designating the president. But in newly filed court documents defending their case against accusations of vindictive prosecution, the government introduced an entirely unexpected piece of evidence: Comey's newly released crime novel, "FDR Drive."</p>

Legal experts and observers alike are left scratching their heads over a prosecution that treats a work of fiction as a psychological blueprint for a real-world crime.</p>

<h2>The Psychology of Fiction and Intent</h2>
<p>When legal arguments begin relying on the plotlines of novels written by the defendants themselves, we cross from standard jurisprudence into the fascinating realm of human psychology. Why do prosecutors find the connection between Comey’s fiction and reality so compelling? And why does the human mind find it so easy to merge an author with their characters?</p>

<p>Psychologically, humans are hardwired to look for narrative coherence. When we encounter an ambiguous action—such as an unusual arrangement of seashells on a beach—our brains instantly search for a coherent story to make sense of it. This tendency is known in cognitive psychology as narrative transportation or the narrative fallacy: we prefer a neat, compelling story over random, disconnected facts.</p>
