<h2>What Happened: The Mechanics of the New Edict</h2>
<p>The presidential edict, which took effect immediately upon signing, targets private enterprises in critical sectors including fuel, energy, nuclear facilities, transport, communications, and logistics. Under the decree, the Russian government can impose external state management simply through a presidential instruction—a directive that legal experts note can be issued verbally and requires no judicial review.</p>
<p>The trigger for state intervention is loosely defined: a company's alleged failure to adopt adequate security measures against drone attacks, violations of security requirements, or steps judged ineffective by authorities. With Ukrainian drone strikes hitting Russian economic infrastructure with increasing frequency—including dozens of refinery strikes and widespread logistics disruptions—the decree links wartime operational defense directly to property rights.</p>

When a private company loses operational control due to an alleged security failure, its current owners lose the ability to steer corporate strategy, restructure debt, or negotiate fair valuations.</p>
