<section id="what-happened">
<h2>What Happened: Inside the June Ethics Disclosure</h2>
<p>According to financial disclosures published by the Office of Government Ethics, President Trump's accounts completed more than 1,000 individual purchases and sales throughout June. While the standard federal disclosure forms do not list exact dollar amounts—instead utilizing broad valuation ranges—the breadth of the activity highlights an extraordinary level of market participation during a sitting presidency.</p>

<p>Among the most notable disclosures was a June 22 sale of Vanguard exchange-traded fund shares, offloading between $5 million and $25 million, representing the single largest transaction on the form. On the acquisition side, the portfolio picked up between $1 million and $5 million in Berkshire Hathaway stock, alongside similarly valued positions in financial stalwarts like Visa, Mastercard, and Cintas.</p>

<p>Most eye-catching for market watchers, however, was the inclusion of SpaceX stock. Trump acquired shares valued between $15,001 and $50,000 on June 23—roughly eleven days after the aerospace giant's initial public offering. Because SpaceX maintains lucrative federal contracts for satellite and defense infrastructure, the investment drew immediate scrutiny regarding the intersection of executive governance and private corporate growth.</p>

The White House has consistently maintained that the president's assets are managed by independent financial institutions via automated mechanisms. In statements to financial media, administration representatives emphasized that trades are executed through "computer-based model portfolios that automatically replicate recognized indexes, such as the Schwab 1000."</p>
