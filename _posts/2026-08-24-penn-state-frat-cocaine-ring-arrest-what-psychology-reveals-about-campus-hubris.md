The operation was allegedly anchored by two primary suppliers: Thomas Robinson, a student living in the Delta Upsilon house, and Agostino Abbatiello, a member of Sigma Chi.</p>

    <h3>1. The Personal Fable and Imaginary Audience</h3>
    <p>Psychologists note that young adults often operate under a subconscious narrative where rules, consequences, and statistical realities apply to "other people," but somehow bend around them. Operating out of a Greek life chapter house surrounded by peers can magnify this delusion. When an environment normalizes high-risk behaviors—whether heavy drinking, financial recklessness, or illicit substances—the baseline for what feels "dangerous" shifts dramatically. Risk becomes routine.</p>

    <h3>2. Normalcy Bias Under Threat</h3>
    <p>Normalcy bias causes people to underestimate the possibility of disaster even when warning signs flash red. Because Robinson was insulated within the comfortable confines of a fraternity house, his brain reflexively rejected the terrifying possibility of federal and state prosecution in favor of a familiar, benign alternative: a prank. It is a psychological defense mechanism designed to stave off panic, albeit one that shattered violently the moment physical force was applied.</p>
