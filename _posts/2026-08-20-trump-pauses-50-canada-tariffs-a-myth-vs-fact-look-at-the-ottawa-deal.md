<section id="what-happened">
    <h2>What Happened: The Three-Day Tariff Pause</h2>
    <p>President Donald Trump late Tuesday announced a three-day pause on scheduled 50 percent tariffs targeting roughly $20 billion worth of Canadian imports, just hours before the duties were set to take effect at midnight. Writing on Truth Social, Trump stated that the postponement was implemented because Canada and the United States have reached a "DEAL," subject to the finalization of official documents. The announcement followed intense trade talks between Trump and Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney.</p>
    
    <p>The proposed duties were originally set to cover an array of consumer and commercial goods—including hockey sticks and wine—under Section 338 of the Tariff Act of 1930, a rarely used Great Depression-era law. In addition to the tariff suspension, Trump hinted that the controversial Keystone XL pipeline could potentially be revived. As the dust settles on this fast-moving trade development, sorting the rhetoric from reality is essential for understanding what is actually happening between Ottawa and Washington.</p>
