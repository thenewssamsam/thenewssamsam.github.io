<section id="what-happened">
    <h2>What Happened?</h2>
    <p>According to reports from The Associated Press, U.S. District Judge Indira Talwani in Boston agreed on Wednesday to lift an order prohibiting the U.S. Postal Service from carrying out President Trump's executive order regarding mail-in ballots for the November midterms. Judge Talwani stated she felt "compelled" to make the move following a recent Supreme Court procedural ruling in a separate case, which found that an initial injunction requested by Democratic states was premature.</p>

<section id="legal-rights-explainer">
    <h2>A Legal Rights Perspective: Who Controls the Ballot Box?</h2>
    <p>To understand the core constitutional tension here, we have to look at the division of power enshrined in the U.S. Constitution. Under the Elections Clause (Article I, Section 4), states hold primary authority to set the "Times, Places and Manner" of holding elections for Senators and Representatives, though Congress holds the ultimate power to make or alter such regulations.</p>

    <p>When an executive branch agency—in this case, the U.S. Postal Service—issues operational rules that effectively regulate how state ballots are collected, formatted, and tracked, it enters a constitutional gray area. Historically, election administration has been fiercely guarded as a state prerogative. The ongoing litigation hinges on a fundamental legal question: Did Congress grant the Postal Service statutory authority to impose uniform envelope requirements and voter list sharing as a condition of mail delivery?</p>
