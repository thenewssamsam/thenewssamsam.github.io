    <h1>US-Canada Trade Deal Reached: What Faith and Scripture Reveal About Fair Dealing and Neighborly Trust</h1>
  </header>

  <p class="ncg-lead">When nations step back from the brink of economic conflict, the relief is palpable &mdash; but true peace involves far more than signed papers. Following intense negotiations and a threatened 50 percent tariff, United States President Donald Trump announced that the US and Canada have reached a "very fair" trade agreement, offering a moment to reflect on how nations negotiate, trade, and treat their neighbors.</p>

  <h2 id="what-happened">What Happened in the US-Canada Trade Negotiations</h2>
  
  <p>The breakthrough came swiftly after a period of intense economic brinkmanship. Late Tuesday, President Trump announced a three-day pause on planned 50 percent tariffs against Canadian imports following a constructive conversation with Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney. By Wednesday, Trump reported to reporters that a deal had been secured, highlighting that tariffs would be non-existent for US farmers and businesses exporting north.</p>

The proposed tariffs, which would have impacted roughly bn worth of imports, created immediate anxiety across North American supply chains. Key friction points, particularly around the auto sector and historic Section 232 tariffs on vehicles, were central to the discussions led by US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick.</p>

  <p>With nearly 72 percent of Canada's goods exports heading to the United States, stability across the northern border is vital for both economies. Yet, beyond the charts, shipping manifests, and political announcements, international trade is fundamentally an exchange between human beings &mdash; a reality that invites deeper moral and spiritual reflection.</p>
