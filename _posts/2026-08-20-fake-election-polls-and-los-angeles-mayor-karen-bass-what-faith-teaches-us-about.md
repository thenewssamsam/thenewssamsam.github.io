  <section class="ncg-section" id="news-hook">
    <h2>What Happened in the Los Angeles Mayoral Race</h2>
    <p>In the high-stakes arena of modern politics, good news is currency. Last week, Los Angeles Mayor Karen Bass shared exciting news on social media: a fresh poll suggested her reelection campaign was gaining significant momentum, showing her leading her progressive challenger, councilmember Nithya Raman, by roughly 12 percentage points. For a campaign locked in a competitive race, it was a welcome sign.</p>
    
    <p>There was just one catch—the poll was completely fabricated.</p>
    
    <p>The numbers originated from a previously unknown organization called Median Strategies, which later admitted to the <em>Los Angeles Times</em> that the purported results were entirely fake. The company shuttered its website after posting a brief confession claiming the stunt was a "social experiment" examining how unverified polling spreads through the political ecosystem. Though the Bass campaign quickly deleted its posts once the truth emerged, the episode has left political analysts stunned and raised urgent questions about trust, verification, and human credulity.</p>
