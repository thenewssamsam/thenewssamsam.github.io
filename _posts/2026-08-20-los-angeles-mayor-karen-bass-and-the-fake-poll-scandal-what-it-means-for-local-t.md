  <p class="ncg-intro">When Los Angeles Mayor Karen Bass shared social media data showing her reelection campaign gaining momentum, residents trusted the numbers—until a mysterious entity admitted the entire poll was completely fabricated. In an era where local news is consumed on the go, this fake polling scandal hits closer to home than many realize, shaking the foundational trust that holds neighborhoods and civic communities together.</p>

  <h2 id="what-happened">What Happened in the Los Angeles Polling Controversy?</h2>
  <p>The controversy centers around an obscure entity calling itself "Median Strategies," which materialized online to publish purported election polling results across three states, including a high-profile mayoral race in Los Angeles. The bogus Los Angeles poll claimed that incumbent Democratic Mayor Karen Bass held a commanding 12-percentage-point lead over progressive challenger and councilmember Nithya Raman. Believing the numbers reflected legitimate media reporting, the Bass campaign amplified the chart on social media before independent fact-checkers and news organizations exposed the data as entirely fictitious.</p>
  
  <p>As the Associated Press reported, Median Strategies quickly shuttered its website after admitting the exercise was designed as a "social experiment" to test how unvetted polling information propagates through the political ecosystem. Despite the prompt deletion of the posts by the campaign—which defended its actions by noting the figures had circulated in news reports—the episode laid bare profound vulnerabilities in how local political information travels from invisible online actors straight to our local community screens.</p>
