<section id="what-happened" class="ncg-section">
<h2>What Happened: The 11th-Hour Reprieve</h2>
<p>Trading partners on both sides of the northern border breathed a cautious sigh of relief when US President Donald Trump and Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney announced a last-minute agreement to pause steep 50 percent tariffs on Canadian goods. The levies, which were slated to hit roughly $20.2 billion worth of exports ranging from electronics and industrial machinery to wine and dairy, were suspended just hours before taking effect.</p>

<p>According to official reports, the temporary truce lasts until August 22, giving negotiators a brief window to finalize documentation. Yet, as social media feeds light up with speculation about a permanent trade breakthrough, it is easy for rumors, political posturing, and economic anxiety to obscure what was actually agreed upon. When high-stakes international negotiations collide with 24-hour news cycles, clarity is often the first casualty.</p>

<h2>Beyond the Headlines: The Real Stakes</h2>
<p>Understanding why this negotiation is so tense requires looking at the deeper structural pressures at play. Canada depends heavily on the US market, with approximately 70 percent of its exports heading south. Conversely, the US sends about 30 percent of its exports northward. When tariffs are threatened on USMCA-protected goods, supply chains for automobiles, machinery, and energy face immediate disruption.</p>
