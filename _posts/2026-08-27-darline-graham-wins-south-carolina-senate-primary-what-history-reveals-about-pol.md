<p>When voters in South Carolina cast their ballots to select a Republican nominee for the US Senate, they chose a name with deeply familiar political resonance. Darline Graham, sister of the late Senator Lindsey Graham, secured the primary victory with 52.5% of the vote, defeating right-wing challenger Representative Ralph Norman. Bolstered by a decisive endorsement and personal campaign visits from President Donald Trump, Graham's victory offers a striking window into both modern party dynamics and a centuries-old political tradition.</p>

<h2 id="what-happened">What Happened in South Carolina?</h2>

<p>The primary contest concluded with Darline Graham edging out Congressman Ralph Norman, who attempted to frame her as an inexperienced Washington insider despite her never previously holding elected office. Graham is currently serving as a temporary replacement in the Senate following the sudden passing of her brother in July. With counting nearly finished, CBS News data confirmed her victory at 52.5% of the vote.</p>

<p>The race carried high stakes beyond the Palmetto State. President Trump personally traveled to South Carolina to campaign for Graham, cementing the primary as a test of his continued influence over the Republican Party apparatus ahead of the broader midterm elections. Political analysts widely expect Graham to triumph in November, given that South Carolina has not elected a Democratic senator in decades. Meanwhile, similar succession themes played out elsewhere, such as in Georgia, where the daughter of late Democratic Congressman David Scott unsuccessfully sought to fill his seat.</p>
