  <section id="what-happened" class="ncg-section">
    <h2>What Happened: Texas Senate Bill 12 Struck Down</h2>
    <p>On the same day the world mourned the loss of country music icon Dolly Parton at age 80, a federal courtroom in Texas echoed her enduring cultural footprint. U.S. District Judge David Hittner issued a decisive ruling striking down Texas Senate Bill 12 (SB 12)—a 2023 state law designed to restrict drag performances by threatening businesses with fines up to $10,000 and performers with misdemeanor criminal charges carrying up to a year in jail.</p>
    
    <p>Judge Hittner blocked the Texas attorney general from enforcing the statute, ruling unequivocally that the ban constitutes an unconstitutional restriction on free speech. The legal battle over SB 12 has bounced through the federal court system since its passage, including an intervention by the 5th U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals in November 2025. Yet, upon reconsideration, Judge Hittner’s core conclusion remained unchanged: the state law fundamentally violates protections guaranteed by the First and Fourteenth Amendments.</p>

    <h2>A Legal Rights Perspective: Vagueness and the First Amendment</h2>
    <p>From a legal standpoint, Judge Hittner’s decision hinges less on personal artistic taste and more on a fundamental pillar of American constitutional jurisprudence: the void-for-vagueness doctrine and the breadth of the First Amendment. Statutes that seek to regulate speech or public expression must provide clear, objective standards so that ordinary citizens know what is prohibited and law enforcement officials cannot arbitrarily apply the law.</p>
