        <p>The trial of Lindsay Clancy, the Duxbury mother accused of killing her three children in 2023, has reached a critical juncture as the prosecution rests its case. For many parents, this tragedy is not just a legal headline—it is a haunting reminder of the fragility of maternal mental health and the terrifying gap between seeking help and finding safety.</p>

    <section id="the-facts">
        <h2>What Happened in the Duxbury Case?</h2>
        <p>Lindsay Clancy has admitted to the deaths of her children—Cora, 5; Dawson, 3; and Callan, 8 months. Her defense team argues she was suffering from severe mental illness, exacerbated by prescribed medications, and should not be held criminally responsible. Conversely, prosecutors have presented evidence, including digital forensics and testimony from the children’s father, Patrick Clancy, to suggest the actions were premeditated.</p>
        <p>The testimony has been harrowing, involving detailed accounts of Clancy’s struggle with depression, insomnia, and suicidal thoughts leading up to the tragedy. As the defense begins calling witnesses, including family members and psychiatric experts, the jury must weigh complex medical records against the evidence of the evening in question.</p>

        <h3>Recognizing the Signs</h3>
        <p>Postpartum depression and psychosis are often shrouded in shame. Parents, particularly mothers, may fear that admitting to "scary thoughts"—like the ones Patrick Clancy described—will lead to judgment or the loss of their children. However, early intervention is the only path to safety.</p>

        <h3>Communication Tips for Families in Crisis</h3>
        <ul>
            <li><strong>Create a "No-Judgment" Zone:</strong> Partners should encourage open dialogue about intrusive thoughts without immediate alarm, which can help lower the barrier to seeking professional help.</li>
            <li><strong>Involve the Extended Village:</strong> As noted in the trial, family members were present, yet the gravity of the situation was misjudged. If a parent is struggling, do not hesitate to bring in professional, specialized help immediately.</li>
            <li><strong>Monitor Medication Compliance:</strong> When mental health medication is involved, it is a family responsibility to ensure consistency, as withdrawal or improper dosage can trigger severe behavioral shifts.</li>
        </ul>
