<section id="what-happened">
    <h2>What Happened in Gary, Indiana?</h2>
    <p>Nearly two weeks after a sudden and destructive storm tore through the US Midwest on August 11, thousands of residents in Gary, Indiana, found themselves grappling with an unimaginable reality: 12 days without electricity. Peaking wind gusts of 99 mph (159 km/h) ripped down power lines, brought down massive trees, and triggered severe flooding and tornadoes across the region. While the worst of the meteorological fury lasted only five to ten minutes, its aftermath paralyzed the former industrial heartland.</p>
    
    <p>According to the Northern Indiana Public Service Company (Nipsco), power has been restored to more than 345,000 customers, but roughly 30,000 remained in the dark as of Sunday morning. For 77-year-old resident Terry Dubois, surviving meant relying on friends for non-perishable food and a son-provided power bank to keep her phone charged. Others faced much graver dangers: individuals unable to refrigerate critical medication, a wheelchair user forced to crawl down stairs without working lifts, and patients like Jack Tipold, who had all his teeth removed during dental surgery just as the power failed, leaving him discharged without adequate pain medication for 48 hours.</p>
</section>

<section id="community-impact">
    <h2>A Local and Community Impact Perspective</h2>
    <p>Examining this crisis through a <strong>Local and Community Impact Perspective</strong> reveals how infrastructure failures do not hit a population equally. Gary, Indiana—once home to the world's largest steel mill—carries distinct socio-economic vulnerabilities. With roughly one-third of its 70,000 residents living in poverty according to US Census data, an extended utility outage quickly snowballs from an inconvenience into a profound public health and humanitarian emergency.</p>

As the outage dragged into its second week, global food charity World Central Kitchen stepped in to provide meals, filling a glaring void left by local supply chain failures.
