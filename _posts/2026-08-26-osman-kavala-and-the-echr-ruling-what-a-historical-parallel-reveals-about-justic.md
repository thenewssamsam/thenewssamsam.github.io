<h2 id="what-happened">What Happened: The ECHR Landmark Ruling</h2>
<p>In a decisive ruling delivered on Tuesday, the European Court of Human Rights ordered Turkey to release Osman Kavala, an activist and cultural philanthropist who has spent nearly a decade behind bars. Kavala, now 68, was initially arrested in October 2017 and later sentenced to an aggravated life term in 2022. Prosecutors accused him of orchestrating the 2013 Gezi Park demonstrations—which grew from Istanbul park protests into a nationwide challenge against then-Prime Minister Recep Tayyip Erdogan—and later implicated him in a failed 2016 military coup attempt.</p>

<p>The ECHR's latest judgment went far beyond ordinary procedural criticism. The court explicitly declared that Kavala's continued detention was unlawful, politically motivated, and designed to "punish him for his role in the Gezi Park demonstrations and for expressing his opinions as a human rights defender." Furthermore, the tribunal labeled his severe sentence as a form of "inhuman and degrading treatment," while cautioning that his ongoing imprisonment exposes a systemic weaponization of Turkey's judiciary against journalists, activists, and political opponents.</p>

As the Committee of Ministers approaches a pivotal meeting in mid-September, the organization faces its own historical precedent.</p>
