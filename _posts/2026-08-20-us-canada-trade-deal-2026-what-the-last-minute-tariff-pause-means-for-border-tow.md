    <h2>What Happened at the Border</h2>
    <p>In a dramatic 11th-hour breakthrough on Tuesday, the United States and Canada reached a temporary trade agreement to delay a punishing 50 percent tariff on roughly $20.2 billion worth of Canadian goods. Announced by US President Donald Trump just before midnight, the three-day pause until August 22 spares exporters from an immediate economic shock while negotiators work to finalize documents.</p>

    <p>Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney confirmed the suspension following intensive talks, noting that while substantial progress was made, important work remains. The proposed tariffs, invoked under Section 338 of the Tariff Act of 1930, targeted electronics, industrial machinery, furniture, dairy, and wine. Intriguingly, the breakthrough appears tied to renewed discussions regarding the long-dormant Keystone XL Pipeline, adding an unexpected layer of cross-border political maneuvering to an already tense negotiation.</p>
