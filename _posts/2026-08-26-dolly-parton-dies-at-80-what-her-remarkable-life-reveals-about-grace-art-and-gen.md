  <section id="what-happened">
    <h2>What Happened: Remembering Dolly Parton</h2>
    <p>Music star, actress, and beloved cultural figure Dolly Parton passed away peacefully on Tuesday at the Vanderbilt-Ingram Cancer Center in Nashville, Tennessee, surrounded by her loved ones, according to a statement from her publicist, Marcel Pariseau. Parton had been bravely facing a brief battle with cancer.</p>

    <div class="ncg-quote-box" style="border-left: 4px solid #d4af37; background: #fdfbf7; padding: 15px; margin: 20px 0; border-radius: 4px;">
      <p><em>"Dolly Parton proved that extraordinary talent wrapped in authentic humility and fierce business independence could change the cultural landscape without ever losing its soul."</em></p>
    </div>

    <p>Born into a large, impoverished family in the Smoky Mountains of East Tennessee, Parton rose to become one of the most successful and enduring artists in entertainment history. With a career spanning over six decades, she authored hundreds of timeless songs—including enduring classics like "Jolene," "Coat of Many Colors," and "I Will Always Love You"—amassing more than 100 million worldwide sales and billions of streams.</p>

    <h2>From a Mountain Cabin to Global Stardom</h2>
    <p>Parton often described her childhood as "dirt poor," growing up as one of 12 children in a tiny cabin. Her early musical influences were deeply rooted in her faith and family; her grandfather was a preacher, and her first performances took place in church. By age 10, she was playing guitar and singing on local television, and at just 13, she stepped onto the Grand Ole Opry stage, introduced by Johnny Cash.</p>
