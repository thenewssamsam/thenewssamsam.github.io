<section id="what-happened">
    <h2>What Happened</h2>
    <p>The recall effort targeting Seattle Mayor Katie Wilson is officially moving forward to its first major judicial test. Following a technical review by the King County Prosecuting Attorney's Office confirming that petitioners Melinda Jacobson and Dale Osterud are registered local voters and that their paperwork met procedural standards, the case is now headed to King County Superior Court Judge Patrick Oishi for a legal sufficiency hearing scheduled for August 31.</p>
    
    <p>While clearing the prosecutor's technical desk is a routine procedural step—confirming little more than valid signatures and a concise summary of claims—the upcoming court hearing is an entirely different battle. KIRO Newsradio's Jake Skorheim and Spike O'Neill recently dissected the upcoming legal showdown on their afternoon program, highlighting that the core question before Judge Oishi is whether the petition's allegations rise to the strict legal definition of official malfeasance.</p>

<section id="legal-explainer">
    <h2>The Legal Mechanics of a Mayoral Recall</h2>
    <p>Under Washington state law, recalling an elected official requires more than mere policy disagreements or voter dissatisfaction. Petitioners must clear a high judicial bar by alleging specific acts of malfeasance, misfeasance, or a violation of the oath of office while in discharge of official duties. A judge must determine whether the charges, if taken as true, legally constitute grounds for a recall before the petition can ever be printed on a ballot for public vote.</p>

If Judge Oishi finds that the allegations meet the legal threshold for malfeasance, the recall campaign moves to the next phase—gathering thousands of physical signatures from registered Seattle voters within a statutory timeframe to secure a spot on the ballot.</p>
