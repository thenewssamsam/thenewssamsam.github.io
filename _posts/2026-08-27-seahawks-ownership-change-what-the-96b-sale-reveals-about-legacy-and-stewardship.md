<h1>Seahawks Ownership Change: What the $9.6B Sale Reveals About Legacy and Stewardship</h1>
</header>

<p>For nearly three decades, the Seattle Seahawks have been synonymous with a single family name, anchoring a community through championship highs and heartbreaking defeats. When NFL owners unanimously approved the sale of the franchise to the Khosla family at a staggering $9.612 billion valuation, it marked more than a simple corporate transaction; it closed a legendary 29-year chapter built under Paul G. Allen and later his estate. As veteran players like Cooper Kupp and Jarran Reed reflect on the transition, the massive shift in ownership invites us to look past the astronomical financial figures and consider a timeless question: what does it truly mean to inherit, manage, and pass on something of great value?</p>

<h2 id="what-happened">What Happened: A Historic Handoff in the Pacific Northwest</h2>
<p>The 29-year era of Paul Allen's family ownership of the Seattle Seahawks officially concluded when league owners gave their unanimous blessing for the franchise's sale. Valued at $9.612 billion, the sale transfers stewardship of one of the NFL's most stable and successful modern organizations into the hands of a group led by the Khosla family.</p>
<p>Speaking to reporters after practice, veteran wide receiver Cooper Kupp—who grew up watching the Seahawks in Yakima—noted that outside of his earliest childhood years, the Allen family is all he has ever known this franchise to represent. Yet, as the team prepares to meet its new leaders, players voiced cautious optimism. Defensive lineman Jarran Reed emphasized that the organizational culture is "top tier" and expressed hope that the new owners will immerse themselves in the unique environment built by the "12s" and the team's internal standard of excellence.</p>
