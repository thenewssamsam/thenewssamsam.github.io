<section id="what-happened">
  <h2>What Happened in Alaska?</h2>
  <p>Elections are rarely boring, but the 2026 midterms in Alaska have just delivered a twist straight out of a political satire. Incumbent Republican Senator Dan S. Sullivan is running for a third term in a neck-and-neck battle against Democrat Mary Peltola. However, election officials have confirmed that he will share the November ballot with a challenger bearing a nearly identical name: retired school teacher and US Forest Service employee Dan J. Sullivan, who is also running as a Republican.</p>
  <p>The situation is far from an accidental coincidence. The second Sullivan entered the race earlier this year, prompting the incumbent senator to condemn him as a sham candidate designed to split conservative votes and confuse the electorate. After a legal battle that saw state election officials briefly disqualify the challenger in June, the Alaska Supreme Court ordered him restored to the ballot. With 16 candidates total in the primary, the incumbent pulled nearly 43% of the vote while Peltola secured over 48%, leaving the similarly named challenger with roughly 2.5%. Now, both Sullivans are heading toward a high-stakes general election where Alaska's ranked-choice voting system will be put to a very unusual test.</p>

Name recognition is traditionally the most powerful heuristic in politics.</p>
