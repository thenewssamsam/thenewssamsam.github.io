    <h1>Leah Stewart Speaks Out After Sydney Shark Attack: A Psychology Look at Trauma and Resilience</h1>
    <p class="ncg-subtitle">When 35-year-old teacher Leah Stewart broke her silence more than two months after a brutal white shark attack at Coogee Beach, her words offered a profound glimpse into the human capacity to endure, heal, and refuse bitterness.</p>
  </header>

  <section class="ncg-section">
    <h2 id="what-happened">What Happened at Coogee Beach?</h2>
    <p>On the morning of June 13, 2026, a routine morning swim turned into a life-altering tragedy. Leah Stewart, a 35-year-old mother and teacher, was swimming between the flags and close to the shore at Coogee Beach in Sydney's east when she was mauled by a white shark. The attack left her with severe bites to her leg and arms, multiple fractures, and massive blood loss. Rushed to St Vincent's Hospital in a critical condition, Ms. Stewart was placed in an induced coma for days, enduring multiple surgeries, including the amputation of one of her arms.</p>
    
    <p>More than two months later, Ms. Stewart finally spoke publicly for the first time through a fundraising page set up to support her family. Expressing profound gratitude, she noted that while her recovery remains ongoing, she feels she has been granted a "second chance at life" and is determined not to waste it. She also announced the closure of the donation page after raising over $500,000, stating that the overwhelming generosity of her local community and supporters worldwide has given her family the financial breathing room and medical options they need.</p>
  </section>

    <h2 id="psychology-perspective">A Psychology &amp; Behavior Perspective: The Architecture of Resilience</h2>
    <p>From a psychological standpoint, surviving a catastrophic physical trauma involves navigating extreme cognitive dissonance. The brain struggles to reconcile the ordinary reality of a morning swim with the abrupt, life-threatening violence of a predatory attack. For weeks, survivors often cycle through shock, hyper-vigilance, and grief over the sudden loss of bodily integrity.</p>
