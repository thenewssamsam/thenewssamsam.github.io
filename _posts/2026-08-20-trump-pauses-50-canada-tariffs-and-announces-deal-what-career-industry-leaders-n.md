    <h2>What Happened: The Tariff Pause and Trade Deal</h2>
    <p>Late Tuesday stateside, President Trump took to Truth Social to announce that the looming 50% duties on an array of Canadian goods—invoked under the rare Depression-era Section 338 of the Tariff Act of 1930—were being put on hold for three days. The pause follows high-stakes trade talks between Trump and Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney.</p>

    <p>As Dan Kelly, president of the Canadian Federation of Independent Business, pointed out to CNBC, a 50% tariff makes specific goods instantly uneconomic to sell. For thousands of independent suppliers, the threat alone was enough to cause U.S. buyers to pause future orders and freeze supply chains. Professionals working in procurement, international trade compliance, and logistics have faced immense pressure trying to advise leadership teams when the rules of engagement shift overnight.</p>

    <p>Behind the multi-billion-dollar figures and trade acronyms like USMCA lie millions of livelihoods. Neil Herrington of the U.S. Chamber of Commerce noted that trade policies affecting North American integration risk approximately 13 million American jobs tied directly to the trilateral pact. Career stability in manufacturing, energy, and transportation is intimately linked to these diplomatic negotiations. Workers and mid-level managers are left evaluating how to plan career trajectories when regional supply chains experience chronic policy friction.</p>
