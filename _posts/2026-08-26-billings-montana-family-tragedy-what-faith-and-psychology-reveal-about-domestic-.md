  <h2 id="what-happened">What Happened in Billings: A Community Shattered</h2>
  <p>On Sunday evening, August 23, 2026, a routine family gathering turned into a nightmare in a residential neighborhood outside Billings, Montana. According to law enforcement and family accounts, 44-year-old Alan Smith opened fire on eight of his relatives, including four children ranging from ages 7 to 15, before setting fire to the home. The suspect ultimately died from a self-inflicted gunshot wound.</p>
  
  <p>The victims represented four generations of a close-knit, blended family that had relocated from the Seattle area years prior. Among those killed were Smith’s elderly grandmother, his parents, his younger sister, and four children. Amidst the terror, 12-year-old Charlotte Ghekiere managed to dial 911, an act of incredible bravery that family members have highlighted as a heroic attempt to save her loved ones.</p>

  <p>Investigators from the Billings Police Department and fire officials battled the blaze well into the night, hampered by structural safety concerns and the rural location's infrastructure limits. As the community mourns, criminologists note that this heartbreaking incident marks the 22nd mass killing in the United States this year—a stark reminder of how frequently domestic disputes escalate into catastrophic violence.</p>
