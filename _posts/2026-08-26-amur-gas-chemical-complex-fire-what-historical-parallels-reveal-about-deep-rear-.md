<h2 id="what-happened">What Happened at the Amur Gas Chemical Complex?</h2>

<p>According to reports originating from the Exilenova+ Telegram channel and local accounts, a powerful explosion rocked the Amur Gas Chemical Complex (AGHK) in Russia’s far eastern Amur Region. The blast wave caused significant structural damage across the facility grounds, specifically targeting a pyrolysis unit that serves as a cornerstone for ethylene and propylene production. Local residents flooded social media with images and videos of a towering plume of black smoke billowing over the installation, while numerous fire trucks and ambulances rushed to the scene.</p>

<p>To put the geographic scale into perspective, 6,000 kilometers represents a vast expanse across the Eurasian landmass, challenging conventional assumptions about where industrial infrastructure remains safe during wartime contingencies. Furthermore, this incident did not occur in isolation. On the night of August 24, local reports also highlighted fiery attacks and explosions at the Novoshakhtinsk Oil Refinery in the Rostov Region and the Afipsky Oil Refinery in the Krasnodar Krai, demonstrating a coordinated pressure point across multiple energy and chemical nodes.</p>

<p>The AGHK facility itself holds immense strategic value for domestic petrochemical supply chains, currently sitting in the delicate commissioning phase prior to full commercial operation. Disruptions at this stage ripple outward, delaying critical industrial scaling and forcing logistical recalculations.</p>
