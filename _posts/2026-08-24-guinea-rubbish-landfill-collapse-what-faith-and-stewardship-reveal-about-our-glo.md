    <section id="what-happened" style="margin-bottom: 40px;">
        <h2 style="font-size: 1.75rem; color: #2d3748; border-bottom: 1px solid #edf2f7; padding-bottom: 8px; margin-bottom: 20px;">What Happened in Conakry</h2>
        <p>In the quiet, dark hours around 02:00 local time, following a night of relentless heavy rain, disaster struck the Dar-es-Salam district of Conakry, the capital of Guinea. A massive, towering mound of rubbish at the city's primary waste disposal site abruptly gave way. The resulting avalanche of refuse cascaded downward, burying nearby homes while families slept.</p>
        <p>According to government statements and local reports, at least 30 people lost their lives in the sudden catastrophe. Another 22 individuals sustained injuries, with six reported to be in serious condition. The Guinean government mobilized significant resources, deploying military personnel and emergency rescue workers equipped with excavators to sift through the unstable mountain of trash in a desperate search for survivors.</p>
        <p>Prime Minister Mamadou Oury Bah visited the disaster zone to assess the unfolding tragedy and coordinate relief efforts. Yet, amidst the rumbling machinery and the grieving community, a grim reality settled in: local leaders and residents fear the death toll will climb even higher as search teams clear the debris. Heartbreaking accounts have already begun to emerge, including one mother who told local media that she lost all five of her children in the collapse. This tragedy is not an isolated anomaly; a similar landslide at the very same site tragically claimed 10 lives back in 2017, highlighting deep-seated systemic vulnerabilities in urban waste management.</p>

Informal settlements frequently spring up near massive open-air landfills because land values force society's most economically vulnerable populations to the absolute margins. People do not choose to live beneath mountains of garbage out of preference; they live there because it is all they can afford.</p>
