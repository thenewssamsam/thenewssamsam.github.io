<section id="what-happened">
    <h2>What Happened at Centennial Mills?</h2>
    <p>When flames finally tore through Portland's historic Centennial Mills, it wasn't just the physical loss of a landmark that shook the city. The dramatic fire drew crowds of spectators and immediate safety concerns regarding asbestos and lead exposure. But beyond the smoke and charred timber lies a much older, quieter fiscal disaster—one defined by decades of bureaucratic paralysis, millions of taxpayer dollars spent with nothing to show for it, and a cautionary tale about municipal real estate ventures.</p>
    
    <p>According to reports from KATU, public investment in the Willamette River property stretches back decades. A September 2006 city report revealed that more than $12 million in tax increment financing and public funds had already been sunk into acquiring the site and making initial improvements. A city spokesperson later confirmed that this multi-million-dollar expenditure occurred over a concentrated six-year period. Despite that heavy upfront investment, successive redevelopment plans stalled out, leaving prime waterfront land sitting vacant while the city navigated complex regulations, environmental hurdles, and shifting political priorities.</p>
</section>
