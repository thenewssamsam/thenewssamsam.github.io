<section id="what-happened">
    <h2>What Happened: The Kansas Outbreak</h2>
    <p>Health authorities in Kansas reported 748 cases of cyclosporiasis as of Monday, with numbers climbing past 100 in Sedgwick County alone. The timing could hardly be worse, coinciding directly with the return to classrooms.</p>

    <p>Cyclospora is a microscopic parasite that infects the small intestine, typically causing watery diarrhea, stomach cramps, nausea, and fatigue. What makes this outbreak particularly tricky for families is its incubation period. Symptoms can take anywhere from one to two weeks to appear. As health officials point out, a child who consumes contaminated food on the very first day of school might not show outward signs of illness until the following week.</p>
    <p>While federal and state agencies, including the USDA, continue tracing the outbreak to its source, the exact origin remains unclear. Although recalled lettuce linked to the initial spread has been pulled from retail shelves, officials emphasize that the investigation is ongoing and the source has not yet been entirely cleared from the food chain.</p>
</section>

<section id="parenting-perspective">
    <h2>A Parent's Perspective: Protecting Kids Without Panic</h2>
    <p>When public health warnings hit right at the start of the school year, it is easy for parents to feel overwhelmed. Packing school lunches is already a daily juggling act of nutrition, convenience, and what kids will actually eat. Adding foodborne parasite worries to the mix feels like one stressor too many.</p>
    <p>However, health experts stress that there is no reason to abandon fresh fruits and vegetables altogether. The goal is not fear-mongering or cutting healthy foods out of a child's diet entirely; rather, it is about shifting toward smart, low-risk preparation methods until the outbreak subsides.</p>
    
    <div class="ncg-quote-box">
        <p class="ncg-quote-text">"The goal isn't to banish fresh produce from the lunchbox, but to understand which foods require extra caution and which ones offer a naturally safer bet."</p>
    </div>
</section>

<section id="lunchbox-guide">
    <h2>Safe vs. Risky: What to Pack Right Now</h2>
    <p>Understanding how the Cyclospora parasite clings to food makes navigating the grocery aisle much simpler. The parasite thrives in microscopic crevices that are difficult to wash clean.</p>
    
    <div class="ncg-food-grid">
        <div class="ncg-food-card ncg-warning-card">
            <h3>Think Twice About: High-Risk Items</h3>
            <ul>
                <li><strong>Bagged lettuce and salad mixes:</strong> Deep folds and cuts trap moisture and microscopic organisms.</li>
                <li><strong>Fresh herbs:</strong> Difficult to scrub effectively due to delicate structures.</li>
                <li><strong>Raspberries and delicate berries:</strong> Textured surfaces make thorough washing nearly impossible.</li>
            </ul>
        </div>
        
        <div class="ncg-food-card ncg-safe-card">
            <h3>Safer Bets: Low-Risk Produce</h3>
            <ul>
                <li><strong>Bananas and avocados:</strong> Protected by a thick peel that is discarded before eating.</li>
                <li><strong>Firm produce (carrots, watermelon):</strong> Safer when scrubbed thoroughly under running water.</li>
                <li><strong>Cooked vegetables:</strong> The absolute safest option, as heat is a proven method for eliminating the parasite.</li>
            </ul>
        </div>
    </div>
</section>

<section id="communication-tips">
    <h2>Age-Appropriate Communication Tips</h2>
    <p>When routines change, children notice. Keeping kids informed without causing anxiety is an important part of the back-to-school transition.</p>
    
    <h3>For Younger Children</h3>
    <p>Keep explanations simple and focused on habits they can control. Remind them why we wash our hands before eating and let them know that mom or dad has packed special, safe foods for their lunchbox today.</p>
    
    <h3>For Pre-Teens and Teens</h3>
    <p>Older children can handle basic facts about food safety. Explain that health departments track outbreaks to keep communities safe, and discuss why certain lunchbox swaps—like trading pre-packaged bagged salads for whole fruits—are helpful right now.</p>
</section>

<section id="faq">
    <h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
    
    <details class="ncg-faq-item">
        <summary>Should I stop buying fresh lettuce and berries for my family?</summary>
        <p>No. Health officials emphasize that you do not need to eliminate fresh produce entirely. Focus on thorough washing for firm produce, choosing thick-peeled fruits like bananas, or serving vegetables cooked.</p>
    </details>
    
    <details class="ncg-faq-item">
        <summary>What are the main symptoms of a Cyclospora infection?</summary>
        <p>The primary symptom is watery diarrhea, often accompanied by cramping, loss of appetite, weight loss, bloating, increased gas, nausea, and fatigue. Symptoms can take one to two weeks to appear after exposure.</p>
    </details>
    
    <details class="ncg-faq-item">
        <summary>Does ordinary soap kill Cyclospora?</summary>
        <p>Standard produce washes and soap do not guarantee the removal of parasites from textured surfaces with deep crevices. Thorough cooking with heat is the only fully proven method of killing the organism.</p>
    </details>
</section>

<section class="ncg-closing-section">
    <h2>One Last Thought</h2>
    <p>Back-to-school season always brings an adjustment period, from new schedules to changing morning routines. While navigating a foodborne illness outbreak adds an extra layer of vigilance, it is also an opportunity to practice mindful food preparation as a family. By making simple, intentional swaps in the lunchbox—focusing on thick skins, firm textures, and cooked meals—parents can keep their children nourished and safe while public health agencies work to resolve the source. A little extra care in the kitchen goes a long way toward peace of mind in the classroom.</p>
    <p class="ncg-signoff">Smart choices today build healthy habits for tomorrow.</p>
