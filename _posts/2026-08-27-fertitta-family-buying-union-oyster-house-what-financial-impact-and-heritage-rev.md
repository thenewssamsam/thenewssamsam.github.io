  <section class="ncg-section" id="what-happened">
    <h2>What Happened at the Union Oyster House?</h2>
    <p>Boston's iconic <a href="https://www.wcvb.com/article/fertitta-family-buying-union-oyster-house-boston/73526922" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Union Oyster House</a>—recognized officially as America's oldest restaurant—is changing hands. A family spokesperson confirmed to NewsCenter 5 that the billionaire Fertitta family from Houston, Texas, has reached an agreement to purchase the historic landmark.</p>
    <p>The landmark establishment, situated along Boston's famous Freedom Trail, had been owned and operated for over 50 years by Joe Milano, a Brigadier General of the U.S. Army and Honorary Consul General to Thailand in Boston. Milano's half-century tenure cemented the restaurant's reputation not just as a tourist destination, but as a living museum of American culinary history where historical figures like Daniel Webster once dined.</p>
    <p>According to the family spokesperson, the Fertitta family intends to maintain the restaurant's historic character, running it similarly to how it has operated since opening its doors in the 19th century. However, an acquisition of this magnitude by a sprawling hospitality and entertainment empire raises fascinating questions about the economics of operating a centuries-old culinary institution in modern downtown Boston.</p>
