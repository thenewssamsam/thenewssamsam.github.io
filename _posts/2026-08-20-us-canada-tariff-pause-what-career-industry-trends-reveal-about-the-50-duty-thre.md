  <h2 id="what-happened">What Happened: A Midnight Reprieve and a Fragile Trade Framework</h2>
  <p>The sweeping 50% import duties—targeting everyday consumer goods such as dairy, alcohol, furniture, industrial equipment, plastics, and clothing—were halted following intense diplomatic talks between Washington and Ottawa. Prime Minister Mark Carney described negotiations as "very delicate and intense," while Canadian Minister for Dominic LeBlanc confirmed that efforts toward a finalized agreement are actively moving forward.</p>

  <p>Unlike previous trade actions that offered exemptions under the United States-Mexico-Canada Agreement (USMCA), these proposed tariffs targeted goods regardless of regional trade pact compliance, leveraging a little-known 1930s statute known as Section 338. Although the duties are currently paused subject to the finalization of documents, the episode highlights the profound unpredictability facing modern supply chains and the millions of workers whose livelihoods depend on seamless international trade.</p>
