    <p>The recent legislative changes to Florida's congressional map are not merely lines on a page; they are financial catalysts. By altering district boundaries, the legislature has effectively shifted the "return on investment" for political donors. Seats that were once considered safe—and therefore cheaper to defend—have suddenly become competitive, forcing incumbents to scramble for additional funding to secure their positions.</p>
