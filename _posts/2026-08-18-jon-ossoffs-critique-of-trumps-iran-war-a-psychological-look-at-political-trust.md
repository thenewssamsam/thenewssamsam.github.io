  <section id="psychology-perspective">
    <h2>A Psychological Perspective: The Erosion of Trust</h2>
    <p>When political leaders are perceived as prioritizing personal gain—or "re-rigging" a system for themselves—it triggers a profound psychological response in the electorate known as <strong>moral injury</strong>. This occurs when citizens feel that the foundational values of their society have been betrayed by those in power.</p>
    
    <p>Ossoff’s language, which focuses on "persistence in deceit," taps into the psychological concept of <strong>cognitive dissonance</strong>. Voters who once supported a candidate on the promise of "draining the swamp" may experience significant mental discomfort when faced with evidence that the system remains rigged. To resolve this, voters often either double down on their support to avoid admitting error or experience a complete collapse of trust in the institution itself.</p>

    <blockquote class="ncg-quote-box" style="border-left: 4px solid #6a0dad; padding: 15px; background: #f9f4ff; margin: 20px 0;">
      "When political rhetoric shifts from policy debate to the moral character of leadership, it transforms a standard election into a psychological referendum on the voter's own reality."
    </blockquote>

    <p>By framing the administration’s actions as "despicable," Ossoff is not just attacking a policy; he is highlighting a <strong>violation of social contract expectations</strong>. Humans have an innate psychological need for equity in leadership. When that equity is perceived as destroyed, the resulting cynicism acts as a barrier to civic engagement, making "truth" feel subjective and political discourse feel like a zero-sum game.</p>
  </section>
