<h2 id="what-happened">What Happened: The Big Menu Shift</h2>
<p>Burger King has officially announced that entirely new chicken nuggets will land on menus nationwide starting September 1. According to corporate press releases, the recipe has been rebuilt from the ground up using all-white meat, an improved marinade, and a significantly crispier breading. To accompany the revamped protein, the chain is also rolling out an updated lineup of dipping sauces, including sweet and sour, honey mustard, and buffalo. Suggested pricing ranges from $1.99 for a 4-piece order up to $7.49 for a 20-piece shareable box.</p>

<p>This menu update is not happening in a vacuum. It directly follows a multiyear turnaround plan that previously targeted the brand's flagship Whopper—updating everything from burger construction to packaging (transitioning to a clamshell box to prevent smushing). That earlier burger remake clearly paid off, helping Burger King report a remarkable 8.5% increase in US same-store sales last quarter, temporarily outpacing rival McDonald's by the widest margin seen in over a decade while edging past Wendy's to claim the spot as the second-largest US burger chain.</p>

<h2 id="financial-impact">Financial Mechanics: Why Chicken Beats Beef Right Now</h2>
<p>To understand why a massive fast-food enterprise invests heavily in reforming a side item like chicken nuggets, one must look at the underlying economic pressures facing the restaurant industry. Over recent years, beef prices have experienced sustained volatility and upward pressure, squeezing restaurant operating margins. Poultry, by contrast, generally offers a more stable and cost-effective supply chain.</p>
