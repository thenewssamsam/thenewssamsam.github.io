  <p>As the 2026 hurricane season hits its active late-summer stride across both the Atlantic and Pacific basins, millions of residents find themselves glued to satellite loops, spaghetti models, and forecast cones.   <h3>Normalcy Bias and the Cone of Uncertainty</h3>
  <p>Two primary mental frameworks battle for dominance whenever a severe weather threat looms. The first is <strong>normalcy bias</strong>—our tendency to underestimate the likelihood of a disaster and its potential effects. It is the voice that whispers, "It always misses us; this time will be no different." While normalcy bias helps prevent us from panicking over every passing cloud, it can dangerously delay evacuation preparations when a real threat materializes.</p>
