    <h2>What Happened: The Strikes Resume</h2>
    <p>The sequence of events reads like a tense chapter from a Cold War thriller, yet it is unfolding in real-time across Eastern Europe. CIA Director John Ratcliffe made an unannounced visit to Moscow on Tuesday morning, arriving via Vnukovo airport. During his time on the ground, a delicate, unpublicized diplomatic arrangement held firm: according to a Ukrainian official speaking to ABC News, Ukraine agreed to a U.S. request not to launch drone or missile strikes against Moscow while the American intelligence chief was in town.</p>

    <p>Yet, the moment Ratcliffe's plane departed, the fragile calm evaporated. Moscow Mayor Sergey Sobyanin reported that at least 10 drones were shot down while flying toward the capital in the early hours of Wednesday morning. Russia's federal air transport agency, Rosaviatsiya, instituted temporary flight restrictions across Domodedovo, Zhukovsky, and Vnukovo airports, alongside restrictions stretching across regional hubs from Kaluga to Kazan.</p>
    
    <p>The overnight bombardment was not restricted to the capital. The Russian Defense Ministry claimed a staggering 426 Ukrainian craft were intercepted over 15 regions and occupied Crimea. Among the verified impacts was a massive fire sweeping across a 100,000-square-meter Wildberries e-commerce logistics facility in Kotovsk, Tambov, alongside a coordinated strike on a major Lukoil-operated oil refinery in Kstovoa, Nizhny Novgorod. On the other side of the ledger, Ukraine's air force reported that Russia launched 162 drones inward, resulting in tragic civilian casualties in the Chernihiv region, underscoring the relentless, reciprocal toll of the ongoing conflict.</p>
