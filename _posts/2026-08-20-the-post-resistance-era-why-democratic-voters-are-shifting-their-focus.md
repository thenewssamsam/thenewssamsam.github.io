  <section id="introduction">
    <p>The political landscape within the Democratic Party is undergoing a profound psychological shift. Recent primary results—most notably the defeat of high-profile figures like Alex Vindman, Diana DeGette, and Dan Goldman—signal that the "resistance" era, defined primarily by opposition to Donald Trump, is losing its potency. Voters are no longer satisfied with candidates whose primary identity is tethered to the former president; instead, they are demanding a forward-looking, policy-heavy agenda.</p>
  </section>

  <section id="the-psychology-of-the-shift">
    <h2>The Psychology of the Shift</h2>
    <p>From a behavioral perspective, we are witnessing a phenomenon akin to <strong>habituation</strong>. When a stimulus—in this case, the political friction caused by Donald Trump—is repeated constantly, the emotional response to that stimulus eventually dulls. After years of high-intensity "resistance" messaging, many Democratic primary voters have reached a point of saturation.</p>

  <section id="what-happened">
    <h2>The Anatomy of the Primary Losses</h2>
    <p>The recent defeats of Vindman, DeGette, and Goldman provide a clear case study. These candidates centered their campaigns on their roles in Trump’s impeachments. However, their challengers—such as Angie Nixon and Brad Lander—largely bypassed the "anti-Trump" narrative, focusing instead on "Medicare for All," housing, and the failure of the political establishment to address working-class needs.</p>
