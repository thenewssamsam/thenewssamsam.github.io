    <h2>What Happened: California Advances Balcony Solar</h2>
    <p>Following a unanimous vote in the state Assembly, a landmark renewable energy bill is heading to the state Senate for a final procedural vote before landing on Governor Gavin Newsom's desk. If signed by September 30, California will join a growing wave of states legalizing plug-in solar technology.</p>

Unlike traditional rooftop installations that require complex permits, expensive professional setup, and intricate utility approvals, plug-in solar is designed for everyday consumers.</p>

    <h2>The Utility Pushback and the 2030 Sunset Clause</h2>
    <p>Unsurprisingly, major utility providers have expressed reservations. Pacific Gas & Electric (PG&E), California's largest utility, opposed aspects of the legislation, citing safety and electrical grid integration concerns. While the bill passed, it included a crucial concession: a sunset clause giving residents only until January 1, 2030, to install plug-in solar without direct utility permission.</p>
